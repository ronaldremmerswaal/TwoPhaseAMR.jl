- **Behaviour:** the CMDGFM projection infers the phase velocities of its jump rows by linear extrapolation, `CMDGFMProjection(; jump_inference = LinearJumpInference())`, the new default (Fixes #171). The published one-donor rows infer each phase from one same-axis donor and create a tangential phase-velocity jump `±2hU'` in a resolved shear layer; they remain available as `jump_inference = ConstantJumpInference()` (the thesis reference, used by the reference tests and the techref classification checks). `LinearJumpInference()` (i) extrapolates each phase from two donors, `u₁ + θ(u₁ − u₂)`, in the velocity, jump and pressure-gradient columns (third-order inferred jump for `θ = 1`); (ii) gives a mixed face without a regular row the row that removes its interpolated normal jump where `|n_t| ≤ |n_a|` and the `β = 0` inference row otherwise; (iii) gives a face with an exactly tangential normal the `β = 0` row, imposed as an increment of its own jump (a continuous shear across a flat interface stays exactly continuous); (iv) sets the slope weight `θ = 1 − smoothstep` of the interface crossing speed `w/U` (normal component of the mixture velocity at the face over the local mixture speed) over the ramp `[1/16, 3/16]`, evaluated once from the state the projection receives (a step's predictor) and frozen with the rows of that reconstructed interface, so a repeated projection is idempotent; (v) keeps the jump block weakly diagonally dominant: the first donors carry their mass fractions with the weight `1 + θ`, so `θ` is capped at `(1 − s)/s` (`s` the cross mass fractions of the first donors) and `β` follows the published rule for the inference sum `(1 + θ)s`. The rows of each contact add up to its measure times its centre distance, so every staggered control volume receives the shear flux of its own contact lengths: the forces of an affine velocity now cancel at transitions (the truncation error near a transition no longer grows as `1/h`), and the shifted manufactured solve on fixed and adapted composite trees converges at orders 2.01/2.01 (L2) and 2.00/1.95 (max), previously 0.99/1.01 and 0.62/0.80. Next to a Cartesian embedded wall a cell quarter is left to the wall rows only where the row's contact crosses the wall. The stiffness stays `E'WE` (symmetric positive semidefinite, momentum-conserving), and uniform meshes keep the vertex rows. This also fixes a stencil bug on every mesh with a periodic direction and a wall: the former shear stencil search did not wrap wall faces across the periodic seam, so the shear rows of the corner vertices fell back to a least-squares fit that coupled `u(0, y)` to `v` at distant `x` and put the stiffness off by about 1 % of the largest force at the seam. The construction is exact on the integer lattice and no longer needs the shear-patch and template caches, so `cache_layer` and the cache counters are gone.
- The temporal refinement series at a fixed mesh gate on temporal self-convergence instead of on the error against the exact solution: the difference between a run and the finest-step run of the same series, sampled at the coarse run's step times, must not increase, and the capillary series halve the step three times for both velocity models. The error against the exact solution converges to the spatial error of the fixed mesh and grows once a temporal error of opposite sign stops cancelling it, which the correct one-velocity capillary wave at density ratio one shows beyond a quarter step (0.01257, 0.00455, 0.00302, 0.00418, 0.00501 for the steps 1 to 1/16) while its self-convergence is non-increasing; the previous gate failed that model (#178). At density ratio one the test also requires the step-converged two-velocity capillary wave to reproduce the one-velocity wave: the CMDGFM inference term holds a 0.098 tangential jump on cut faces (#171), so the errors at a step of one eighth are 0.0133 against 0.0042, and that check is marked as a known failure with a NUMERICS CONCERN note (#178).
- More of the mesh-change rebuild is sort- and hash-free and threaded, with bitwise unchanged results for every thread count. The elliptic stiffness `(A + A')/2` of `A = J'MJ` is assembled directly on its complete pattern (count, prefix sum and fill over face columns for `J'M` and over cell columns for the product, threaded over fixed column chunks), replacing two sparse products, a transpose sum, `dropzeros!` and the pattern-completion `sparse` call; a reference test compares it bitwise with that SparseArrays assembly. The inverse face metric orders vertex endpoints by two stable counting sorts on the lattice of the finest active level instead of `sortperm`, so its cost does not grow with an unused `max_level`, and reuses its scratch arrays from `GradientConstructionCache`. The height-function halo cache stores its donors in one flat array and is built by count and fill over blocks, with read-only tree searches, instead of one vector per halo cell; its block keys are set only once the cache is complete.
- Face displacements, and with them every entry of the pressure gradient's Riesz map, are derived from the integer lattice of the block keys and from level spacings instead of from a difference of two absolute cell centres (#163). A centre difference is a small difference of large numbers: on an eight-cell mesh of extent 1.2 translated to `(1e12, -1e12)` the normal distance read 6.5e-4 relative error (9.8e-4 at a coarse–fine contact), the tangential offsets that are exactly zero at the origin moved by 1e-4, the assembled map changed its sparsity pattern with the origin, and the area-weighted jumps of an affine pressure mapped to a gradient that was off by 4.1e-4 instead of round-off. The normal component is now the mean cell width that `_face_dual_volumes` already uses and the tangential component twice the lattice centre offset scaled by the finest spacing, so the displacement is exact and an operator built on a translated mesh is bitwise the operator of the untranslated one at every origin; the periodic half-period handling of #36 is unchanged, and a behaviour test holds both. Meshes at the origin change in their last bits only where the two spellings of one width differ: bitwise unchanged for dyadic extents and for the AMR, stretched and two-cell meshes of the gradient tests, up to 8.0e-16 relative (302 of 384 entries of a uniform 1.2-wide mesh) for non-dyadic ones.
- Orphaned transverse contacts of the cut-cell viscous operator are linear-exact for affine velocities (#99). A level transition that ends at a domain wall leaves a transverse contact without a mirrored partner. Where the wall prescribes its normal velocity -- `NoSlip`, `FreeSlip`, and now also `Inflow` -- the far unknown is interpolated to the wall distance of the near one, with the prescribed value: that form is exact for wall data constant along the wall (the orphan rows of the field `1 + 3y` are reproduced to `3.6e-15` at `n = 16`) and first order for data that vary along it (the field `(1 + 3x)(1 + 3y)`: `0.126`, `0.0641`, `0.0323`, orders `0.975`, `0.989`). Everywhere else -- `Outflow` and `ZeroGradientVelocity`, which prescribe no wall value -- the contact is differenced on a level-matched pair of unknowns (the endpoint that has a neighbour on its own tangential level spanning the contact, and that neighbour) instead of on the offset pair. Differencing the offset pair carried the tangential derivative of the offset into the normal gradient with weight `offset/separation`, which does not vanish under refinement: on a manufactured bilinear field the largest transverse row error was 18.1, 18.6 and 18.8 for `n = 16, 32, 64` (order zero, it grows) and the rms 3.44, 2.44 and 1.73 (order 0.5), against 0.140625, 0.0703125 and 0.03515625 with the level-matched row, order 1.000 in both norms. The level-matched row fires for every orphan that is not closed against a wall and its coefficients sum to zero; where no level-matched face lies within two contact distances the contact takes the weighted minimum-norm derivative over the two-ring face graph that the unembedded transition already uses (0 of 240 pathological sliver obstacles next to a wall at a level transition reach that fallback, and none of them makes the constructor throw). Both closed forms are non-negative squares, so the stiffness stays symmetric positive semidefinite and conserves a constant; the stiffness changes at those few rows only. New tests pin the affine exactness of every transverse row and the use of the level-matched row where it exists, the exactness and the first-order residual of the inflow closure for constant and varying wall data, the shared closure of the two open sides, the symmetry, zero row sum and smallest eigenvalue of the flux core, and the row-error orders above.
- Mesh-changing steps rebuild the variable-viscosity stiffness and the AMR dual advection schedules without sorting or hashing. `_symmetric_stiffness!` forms `E'WE` and its coefficient-refresh map in one merge over the strain rows of each face column (count, prefix sum, fill; threaded over fixed chunks of columns), replacing the two sparse products, the `getindex` symmetrisation and the `sortperm` of the refresh map (Stokes-3 at level 5: 45 → 6 ms and 44.5 → 5.6 MB at one thread). The dual schedules collect their flux-weight contributions per face (count, prefix sum, fill), group them by a stable counting sort and sum each key in face order, replacing the `Dict` keyed by connection (16 → 5 ms per bounded phase schedule). `_grouped_output_schedule` is a stable counting sort, also used for the multigrid transfer schedules, and three-point stencils are threaded per connection. Results are bitwise unchanged for every thread count.
- Cut-cell no-slip walls of the constant-viscosity viscous operator take their geometry from each cut cell's reconstructed PLIC segment (#110). A component face's wall conductance is the piece of that segment it owns — the pieces partition the segment by the two faces' tangential strips and the cell mid-plane — divided by the perpendicular distance from the dual node to the reconstructed plane, replacing half the open face fraction times the whole segment length over the distance to the segment midpoint, so the closure is continuous in the grid-aligned limit. The pieces are emitted per dual face, so a cut cell at a non-periodic boundary in the component direction keeps its segment, and the component-direction partition is half open, so a segment that lies on the cell mid-plane is not counted twice; the wall distance is floored at one sixty-fourth of the smallest cell width, which bounds the largest conductance and keeps the assembled stiffness scaling as `1/h²` as the mesh refines. The normal contact of a cut cell is measured by the open area of the cell mid-plane instead of the aperture-weighted advection schedule, whose average over the two flanking primal faces halves whenever one of them is closed, and a clipped open trace is bisected at the cell centre instead of at the midpoint of the surviving open part. The grid-aligned normal wall connection that completed the halved aperture-weighted contact is removed because that contact now carries the full Dirichlet flux; its integrated force moves from `wall_connections` to the eliminated face's reaction, with the same total. Every cut-wall row of a fluid-side unknown is now exact for a linear velocity, and in the tilted-wall manufactured Helmholtz comparisons the errors at 64 cells fall by factors 1.1 to 2.6 (L2 from 0.00562 to 0.00232 at slope 0.1 on the uniform grid) while the observed L2 orders rise to 1.1–1.4, and 0.74 on the composite grid. A node whose centre lies inside the solid keeps its wall piece, so every positive-aperture face remains a degree of freedom of the cut-cell projection; those rows stay first order. The inclined `embedded_wall_rows` case becomes that manufactured convergence test at slopes 0.1 and 0.5 on uniform and composite meshes, with an independent fluid-side mask, symmetry and non-negative-dissipation checks, and a NUMERICS CONCERN note recording the shortfall from the second order that #110 targets.
- The staggered transport of the one-velocity momentum, the two-velocity momentum and the staggered phase scalars limits the outflow of a draining control volume at a level transition: where the phase volume leaving an unknown exceeds the phase volume its staggered content holds, the excess now leaves at the mean of the values that enter it instead of at its own value (`_phase_drain_limit!`). The `capillary_mimetic` oracle case, the mimetic example variants, tests, documentation and technical-reference sections were removed with it.
- **Breaking:** removed the legacy `ProjectedLiquidVelocity` two-velocity interface carrier with its liquid-extension Poisson operator, projected time-step bound, auxiliary gas carrier and projected transport diagnostics. Two-velocity flows always transport the interface through the frozen liquid/gas subfaces, so `TwoVelocityModel`, `TwoPhaseModel`, `NavierStokesSystem` and `FlowSimulation` no longer accept `interface_transport`, and `ProjectedLiquidVelocity`, `FrozenInterfaceFluxes` and `AbstractInterfaceTransportMethod` are no longer exported. `PhaseFluxDiagnostics` keeps the three face-partition defects; the projected divergence, iteration and gas-reset fields are gone. The `projected` benchmark variant, tests, documentation and technical-reference sections were removed with it.
- One-phase `step!` now checks the advective bound at the top of the accepted `cfl` range (`cfl = 1`) rather than at `cfl = 1/2`, so `simulate!(…; cfl = 0.8)` no longer stops at its first advection-limited step and any step `stable_timestep` returns is accepted (#121). `simulate!` and the flow-level `stable_timestep` reject a `cfl` outside `(0, 1]` (one phase) or `(0, 1/2]` (two phases) up front, also when a prescribed `dt` makes it unused.
- Made single-phase composite-grid viscous diffusion dissipative in the dual-volume norm, using shared geometric transition contacts instead of nonsymmetric corrected rows, and gave a split boundary side's wall strips their own half-wall rows, matching the embedded operator (#80).
- `step!(flow, dt)` now performs a complete flow step, including time and counter updates, post-step adaptivity, diagnostics, and the `:step` observer event; `simulate!` repeats these steps and retains run lifecycle events and continuation.
- `FlowSimulation` accepts `force` and stores it as `flow.force`, the default body acceleration for its stepping and simulation calls. Field-level `step!` remains available for the physical solve alone.
- Bound WY cut-donor timesteps by the actual swept strips, including hanging faces and frozen phase subfaces; freeze subface ordering with apertures in solid geometry to keep opposing donating regions disjoint, snap round-off phase apertures to an exact single phase, and report an empty donor strip by face and cell instead of returning a zero step (#52, #56).
- Prevented CG from using asymmetric multigrid V-cycles and selected CG for volume-weighted symmetric embedded viscous systems (#128).
- Shared the stepping controls, force bounds and surface-tension/gravity stages across flow models, and consolidated topology-dependent workspace rebuilding.
- `NavierStokesSystem` is now the concrete equation-system type, and `FlowSimulation` exposes it as `flow.system`; numerical options and physics are retained on the system.
- Fixed premature termination of ILU-preconditioned CMDGFM projection solves and eliminated warm staggered elliptic solver allocations.
- Avoided per-face allocations in the single-phase pressure predictor.
- The phase interface band no longer refines a wetted solid boundary (#119). Its near test and its parent guard took extrema of the conditional liquid fraction over every cell of the block and its buffer ring, and a covered cell's conditional fraction is zero by construction, not gas, so liquid touching an embedded solid looked like a liquid–gas interface: an all-liquid domain with an embedded slab refined all 16 of its level-1 blocks to the target level. Cells whose open part is empty are now skipped, so that fixture coarsens every block, while a contact line still refines: the wedge on a sloped wall refines exactly the blocks that hold a cell mixed in its open part, the wetted blocks below it coarsen. The solid band (`GeometryRefinementIndicator`) is unchanged, because it measures the solid's own fraction and has no open fraction to consult; the interface indicator on `geometry.solid` still matches it.
- Interface and solid refinement bands protect coarsening families whose parent may be near the boundary, preventing the buffer-induced static-band refine/coarsen cycle.
- Curvature refinement detects mixed liquid–gas cells using the open-fluid conditional fraction, rather than treating liquid-filled solid cut cells as unresolved interfaces. A block that holds a mixed cell in its cells or its one-cell halo counts its liquid-filled cut cells as interface cells, because the interface meets the solid inside their wetted part; the contact line of a droplet on an embedded wall keeps its curvature refinement instead of being coarsened into an unresolved surface-tension face (#148); coarsening families that hold such a cell are kept when their prospective parent would span both phases, which stops the contact-line refine/coarsen cycle without treating covered cells as gas (#118).
- The conditional liquid fraction's halo is now the restriction of the liquid and open fractions, their weighted ratio, instead of the mean of the per-cell ratios. This is a physics change as well as a refinement change: covered cells carry conditional fraction zero by construction, not gas, so a coarse slot over three covered children and one liquid child read 0.25 where the restriction reads one. Transport fluxes, the interface reconstruction and the surface-tension force evaluate cut cells at a level jump with the corrected value, and the interface criterion no longer treats a nearly covered block as bordering the interface (#148).
- Two-velocity projection tolerances now compare dimensionless local velocity-jump and residual defects, using the documented local mesh spacing.
- Standalone one-velocity projections require the pressure operator and `velocity_boundary` to have the same open sides; either an open boundary with a Neumann-only operator or an open operator with a mismatched boundary now raises `ArgumentError`.

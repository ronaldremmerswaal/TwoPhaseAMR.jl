# Changelog

## Unreleased

- The per-step two-velocity face loops are threaded deterministically: inactive phase-velocity marking and the WY velocity checks validate faces in fixed chunks of 1024 (one output flag per chunk, owned by the calling workspace), the frozen-interface bound takes fixed-chunk face maxima folded in chunk order (exact, so the timestep does not depend on the thread count), and the phase-face extension and the projection's phase-velocity correction are threaded per face. The face maxima, the extension and the correction capture flat arrays and scalars; the marking and the WY checks read cell data through the conditional-fraction block vectors, which are boxed per launch; the frozen bound's per-column loop hoists the workspace's fields into array and scalar captures instead of capturing the owner structs. Error precedence is unchanged and independent of the thread count: the marking reports its first failing face, liquid before gas on that face, after the remaining interior faces have been marked, while the WY checks run per phase (liquid interior, liquid boundary, gas interior, gas boundary) with interior before boundary within a phase. The cut-cell variants, which read the solid reconstruction, stay serial. Results are bitwise identical for every thread count.
- The per-solve and per-iteration solver loops are threaded deterministically: component sums, strong-residual and roundoff-floor maxima and the Krylov residual loops use fixed chunks of 1024 items whose boundaries depend only on the cell count, so one thread and many give bitwise identical results and a solve repeats exactly. Sums change round-off once against earlier versions, on levels above 1024 cells only (the oracle's levels are smaller, so its records are unchanged); maxima are equal to the old serial maximum up to the `NaN` payload, because `NaN` terms are canonicalised. Restriction and prolongation always run the package kernels (row-scheduled prolongation, column-dot restriction), also unthreaded, so their results no longer depend on whether SparseArrays' `mul!` fuses (Julia 1.12) or not (1.10/1.11).
- Threaded loop bodies no longer throw: inputs are validated before the loop, so errors are the package's own exceptions rather than a Polyester worker's `TaskFailedException`. User callbacks (`initialize!` functions, velocity initializers, level sets, `PhaseScalar` initializers, `Dirichlet` halo and inflow values) are evaluated serially on the calling task, so they need not be thread-safe and give thread-count-independent results.
- Data-dependent checks in threaded kernels record the failure in an owned, preallocated flag and the caller throws after the launch, rather than costing a serial pass over every cell each step. The WY sweep and the conditional-fraction refresh fuse their bounds check into the threaded update, the elliptic matrix locates its diagonal slots once at assembly (also for the reaction update), the variable-diffusion refresh adds its diagonal inertia in one fused pass, and `fill_halo!` iterates a precollected list of physical halo entries instead of scanning the schedule. A rejected conditional fraction is no longer restored cell for cell, because its check shares the clamp's pass.
- **Breaking:** exported animations now play time linearly in simulation time and never map video frames one-to-one to stored frames. `export_animation` samples the candidate frames (`frames = :keyframes` or `:all`, optional `window`) on a uniform simulation-time grid and shows the nearest frame (ties go to the earlier one); `fps` (default 20) and `duration` set the output count; without `duration` it scales with the candidate count as `clamp(count / fps, 2, 20)` seconds, and `nframes` sets it directly. The `framerate` and `every` keywords and the live `skipped` counter are gone. Live recordings use the same rule with `Δt = sim_per_second / fps` and one frame of look-ahead; without `sim_per_second`, ten seconds of video span the run's remaining simulation time (or the stored frame span when the end is unknown). The live recording handle also reports `ids` and `times`. New core API: `VideoSchedule`, `video_frame_schedule`, `video_frame_count`, `LiveVideoSampler`, `push_live_frame!`, and `flush_live_frames!`.
- Studio's header **Record ▾** menu is removed; the Export dialog is the only recording entry point and now records either stored frames or a live stream, showing the uniform simulation-time spacing, output-frame count, distinct stored frames, and a sparse-recording warning. The protocol adds `duration`, `fps`, `sim_per_second`, a `schedule` preflight action, and exposes completed live frame times; `framerate`, `every`, and stored start/stop sessions are removed.
- Studio renders again when the browser reports a locale `Intl` rejects, such as Chromium's `en-US@posix` under the C/POSIX locale: the reported tag is repaired at startup, before uPlot formats its first tick, so the page no longer stalls on "Connecting to TwoPhaseAMR Studio…".
- A stored export requested from a paused run starts immediately on a single-threaded session; it used to wait for the run to leave `:paused`, so the job stayed in `starting` until the run ended. On such a session Resume waits until a running stored export finishes, because the encoder and the solver share one thread.
- A live export freezes its stop time when the recording is stopped, before the frame queue drains, so the written video never extends past the simulation time reached at stop even when the run keeps stepping; `LiveRecording.stop_time` reports that time and `ids`/`times` are lock-protected snapshots that are safe to read while recording. The deferred encoder also renders its seed frame only after the run leaves the stepping states.
- Export frame counts are capped at 100 000 (`video_frame_count`, `nframes`, and thus `export_animation` and Studio), and the Export dialog limits its duration input to that cap. The dialog no longer stays on "Computing the time-linear schedule…" after a discarded preflight response or a source change, and refreshes a stale summary when the stored frame count changes or a preflight fails.
- Threaded loops go through one launch function (`_parallel_for`, with the loop form `@_parallel`); the separate column and task-scheduler (`Threads.@threads`) paths are gone. `@_parallel` keeps a plain serial loop and a `Polyester.@batch` loop over the same body, because Polyester boxes a closure argument (32 B per threaded launch) while a `@batch` body is allocation-free. The threading grain is a work estimate in cell updates and can be lowered in tests to force the threaded path on small meshes; block-level loops thread from four blocks, as before.
- **Breaking:** removed the legacy mimetic two-velocity pressure projection. `MimeticProjection`, `MimeticProjectionSolver`, `MimeticProjectionWorkspace`, `MimeticProjectionStats` and `mimetic_gradient!` are gone; `CMDGFMProjection` is the only `AbstractTwoVelocityProjection` and still the default. The `capillary_mimetic` oracle case, the mimetic example variants, tests, documentation and technical-reference sections were removed with it.
- **Breaking:** removed the legacy `ProjectedLiquidVelocity` two-velocity interface carrier with its liquid-extension Poisson operator, projected time-step bound, auxiliary gas carrier and projected transport diagnostics. Two-velocity flows always transport the interface through the frozen liquid/gas subfaces, so `TwoVelocityModel`, `TwoPhaseModel`, `NavierStokesSystem` and `FlowSimulation` no longer accept `interface_transport`, and `ProjectedLiquidVelocity`, `FrozenInterfaceFluxes` and `AbstractInterfaceTransportMethod` are no longer exported. `PhaseFluxDiagnostics` keeps the three face-partition defects; the projected divergence, iteration and gas-reset fields are gone. The `projected` benchmark variant, tests, documentation and technical-reference sections were removed with it.
- One-phase `step!` now checks the advective bound at the top of the accepted `cfl` range (`cfl = 1`) rather than at `cfl = 1/2`, so `simulate!(…; cfl = 0.8)` no longer stops at its first advection-limited step and any step `stable_timestep` returns is accepted (#121). `simulate!` and the flow-level `stable_timestep` reject a `cfl` outside `(0, 1]` (one phase) or `(0, 1/2]` (two phases) up front, also when a prescribed `dt` makes it unused.
- Made single-phase composite-grid viscous diffusion dissipative in the dual-volume norm, using shared geometric transition contacts instead of nonsymmetric corrected rows, and gave a split boundary side's wall strips their own half-wall rows, matching the embedded operator (#80).
- `step!(flow, dt)` now performs a complete flow step, including time and counter updates, post-step adaptivity, diagnostics, and the `:step` observer event; `simulate!` repeats these steps and retains run lifecycle events and continuation.
- `FlowSimulation` accepts `force` and stores it as `flow.force`, the default body acceleration for its stepping and simulation calls. Field-level `step!` remains available for the physical solve alone.
- Bound WY cut-donor timesteps by the actual swept strips, including hanging faces and frozen phase subfaces; freeze subface ordering with apertures in solid geometry to keep opposing donating regions disjoint, snap round-off phase apertures to an exact single phase, and report an empty donor strip by face and cell instead of returning a zero step (#52, #56).
- Prevented CG from using asymmetric multigrid V-cycles and selected CG for volume-weighted symmetric embedded viscous systems (#128).
- Shared the stepping controls, force bounds and surface-tension/gravity stages across flow models, and consolidated topology-dependent workspace rebuilding.
- `NavierStokesSystem` is now the concrete equation-system type, and `FlowSimulation` exposes it as `flow.system`; numerical options and physics are retained on the system.
- Fixed premature termination of ILU-preconditioned CMDGFM projection solves and eliminated warm staggered elliptic solver allocations.
- Avoided per-face allocations in the single-phase pressure predictor.
- Interface and solid refinement bands protect coarsening families whose parent may be near the boundary, preventing the buffer-induced static-band refine/coarsen cycle.
- Curvature refinement detects mixed liquid–gas cells using the open-fluid conditional fraction, rather than treating liquid-filled solid cut cells as unresolved interfaces. A block that holds a mixed cell in its cells or its one-cell halo counts its liquid-filled cut cells as interface cells, because the interface meets the solid inside their wetted part; the contact line of a droplet on an embedded wall keeps its curvature refinement instead of being coarsened into an unresolved surface-tension face (#148); coarsening families that hold such a cell are kept when their prospective parent would span both phases, which stops the contact-line refine/coarsen cycle without treating covered cells as gas (#118).
- The conditional liquid fraction's halo is restricted from the liquid and open fractions with the same weights, instead of averaging per-cell ratios: a covered cell no longer reads as gas when a coarse block receives its halo from finer neighbours, which had made nearly covered halo cells look mixed (#148).
- Two-velocity projection tolerances now compare dimensionless local velocity-jump and residual defects, using the documented local mesh spacing.
- Standalone one-velocity projections require the pressure operator and `velocity_boundary` to have the same open sides; either an open boundary with a Neumann-only operator or an open operator with a mismatched boundary now raises `ArgumentError`.

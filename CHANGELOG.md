# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Face displacements, and with them every entry of the pressure gradient's Riesz map, are derived from the integer lattice of the block keys and from level spacings instead of from a difference of two absolute cell centres (#163). A centre difference is a small difference of large numbers: on an eight-cell mesh of extent 1.2 translated to `(1e12, -1e12)` the normal distance read 6.5e-4 relative error (9.8e-4 at a coarse–fine contact), the tangential offsets that are exactly zero at the origin moved by 1e-4, the assembled map changed its sparsity pattern with the origin, and the area-weighted jumps of an affine pressure mapped to a gradient that was off by 4.1e-4 instead of round-off. The normal component is now the mean cell width that `_face_dual_volumes` already uses and the tangential component twice the lattice centre offset scaled by the finest spacing, so the displacement is exact and an operator built on a translated mesh is bitwise the operator of the untranslated one at every origin; the periodic half-period handling of #36 is unchanged, and a behaviour test holds both. Meshes at the origin change in their last bits only where the two spellings of one width differ: bitwise unchanged for dyadic extents and for the AMR, stretched and two-cell meshes of the gradient tests, up to 8.0e-16 relative (302 of 384 entries of a uniform 1.2-wide mesh) for non-dyadic ones.
- Mesh-changing steps rebuild the variable-viscosity stiffness and the AMR dual advection schedules without sorting or hashing. `_symmetric_stiffness!` forms `E'WE` and its coefficient-refresh map in one merge over the strain rows of each face column (count, prefix sum, fill; threaded over fixed chunks of columns), replacing the two sparse products, the `getindex` symmetrisation and the `sortperm` of the refresh map (Stokes-3 at level 5: 45 → 6 ms and 44.5 → 5.6 MB at one thread). The dual schedules collect their flux-weight contributions per face (count, prefix sum, fill), group them by a stable counting sort and sum each key in face order, replacing the `Dict` keyed by connection (16 → 5 ms per bounded phase schedule). `_grouped_output_schedule` is a stable counting sort, also used for the multigrid transfer schedules, and three-point stencils are threaded per connection. Results are bitwise unchanged for every thread count.
- Cut-cell no-slip walls of the constant-viscosity viscous operator take their geometry from each cut cell's reconstructed PLIC segment (#110). A component face's wall conductance is the piece of that segment it owns — the pieces partition the segment by the two faces' tangential strips and the cell mid-plane — divided by the perpendicular distance from the dual node to the reconstructed plane, replacing half the open face fraction times the whole segment length over the distance to the segment midpoint, so the closure is continuous in the grid-aligned limit. The pieces are emitted per dual face, so a cut cell at a non-periodic boundary in the component direction keeps its segment, and the component-direction partition is half open, so a segment that lies on the cell mid-plane is not counted twice; the wall distance is floored at one sixty-fourth of the smallest cell width, which bounds the largest conductance and keeps the assembled stiffness scaling as `1/h²` as the mesh refines. The normal contact of a cut cell is measured by the open area of the cell mid-plane instead of the aperture-weighted advection schedule, whose average over the two flanking primal faces halves whenever one of them is closed, and a clipped open trace is bisected at the cell centre instead of at the midpoint of the surviving open part. The grid-aligned normal wall connection that completed the halved aperture-weighted contact is removed because that contact now carries the full Dirichlet flux; its integrated force moves from `wall_connections` to the eliminated face's reaction, with the same total. Every cut-wall row of a fluid-side unknown is now exact for a linear velocity, and in the tilted-wall manufactured Helmholtz comparisons the errors at 64 cells fall by factors 1.1 to 2.6 (L2 from 0.00562 to 0.00232 at slope 0.1 on the uniform grid) while the observed L2 orders rise to 1.1–1.4, and 0.74 on the composite grid. A node whose centre lies inside the solid keeps its wall piece, so every positive-aperture face remains a degree of freedom of the cut-cell projection; those rows stay first order. The inclined `embedded_wall_rows` case becomes that manufactured convergence test at slopes 0.1 and 0.5 on uniform and composite meshes, with an independent fluid-side mask, symmetry and non-negative-dissipation checks, and a NUMERICS CONCERN note recording the shortfall from the second order that #110 targets.
- The staggered transport of the one-velocity momentum, the two-velocity momentum and the staggered phase scalars limits the outflow of a draining control volume at a level transition: where the phase volume leaving an unknown exceeds the phase volume its staggered content holds, the excess now leaves at the mean of the values that enter it instead of at its own value (`_phase_drain_limit!`). Every partial volume still carries one value for its sender and its receiver, so the transport conserves the phase amount as before, and the coefficients of the old value and of the entering values are non-negative; a limited unknown takes the term-wise flux in the bulk branch of the CTU switch too, because the bulk state moves the face's net volume at one value while the limiter counts the partial volumes by sign. A staggered liquid value 0 among neighbours 1 became 1.98 in one step on the composite fixture and a one-velocity momentum 1.95 at the density ratio 1000; both stay in the range of the old values now, at every `ctu_threshold` and for a non-uniform velocity, which no state choice avoided (#70). The mean the excess leaves at is taken over the neighbours' values at the start of the sweep, so a neighbour that is itself limited makes the emitted value a convex combination of the raw values: observed, no new extremum in 3000 random hanging configurations (5e-14), not proved for that case. Conforming patches take the unchanged path.
- The dual Courant number of the two-phase transport is bounded by the WY bound's `cfl` next to a solid too, so the measured bound of the earlier audit (up to 3.3 times) no longer applies: the cut-donor strip bound limits every outgoing flux by its donor's open strip and the phase schedules weigh the fluxes by those same strips. Measured over 40 random cut meshes with a level transition at `cfl = 0.5` and `0.3`, the largest ratio to `cfl` is one to round-off, and a behaviour test holds it on the cut fixture (#72). No code change was needed beyond the shared open-strip schedule of the entry above; the technical reference's "no bound is claimed" note is gone.
- The two-velocity momentum and the staggered phase scalars factor their phase fluxes with the open-strip fractions of the face's strip in the open cell, as the one-velocity momentum already did, instead of the geometric fraction of the cell side. At a split coarse cut side the geometric factorization does not commute with the open-strip interpolation that forms the staggered fraction, so the discrete mass-momentum identity missed by 9.8e-2 per unit volume there; it now holds to 4.4e-16 for all three schedules, which share one builder (`_open_strip_phase_schedule`). Results change only on cut coarse cells with a split side (#71).
- **Breaking (behaviour):** the two-phase variable-viscosity diffusion operator now averages the phase viscosities with the harmonic (series) mean instead of the geometric mean, in the cells and at the shear-stress vertices: `mu = 1/(c/mu_liquid + (1-c)/mu_gas)` from the conditional fraction and `1/sum(w/mu_cell)` over the cell quarters a stress row spans. The geometric coefficient `sqrt(2)` at the vertex of an aligned `mu = (2, 1)` interface overestimates the tangential traction flux by 6.07 % (`4/3` is the continuous-traction value) and left 1-D shear across such a layer at order 0.8-1.0; the harmonic rule makes it second order (orders 2.03, 2.01, 2.00, 2.00 for `n = 16 -> 128`), and equal-viscosity flows are bitwise unchanged. The thesis' volume-fraction-weighted geometric mixture is kept as `NavierStokesOptions(viscosity_averaging = GeometricViscosityAveraging())` (`HarmonicViscosityAveraging()` is the default); both keep the stiffness symmetric positive semidefinite and the midpoint step energy-contractive (#113).
- Issue #76 is verified against the current code and its promised balances are now pinned as behaviour, at round-off, in both places they can regress: `test/two_phase/two_velocity/projection/cmdgfm_projection.jl` gains a group that checks the two maps of the two-velocity step directly (the one-velocity cut-cell coefficient `aperture^2/rho` against the projection's `aperture/rho`, face by face) and requires both that the hydrostatic rest state with a solid in either phase on the composite mesh stays at rest to `1e-9 dt|g|` (was `1e-8`) and that the same jumps through the one-velocity map leave more than `1e-3`; the constant-curvature drop test in `test/two_phase/forces.jl` requires `1e-9` of the increment on the cut composite mesh and the former map to fail there by more than `1e-3`. No numerics change: the map fix itself is PR #81 (`497a401`), and the chapter-8 criterion it implies is now stated exactly (`R^1 = A^{1/2} R^tv A^{1/2}`, balanced iff `A^{1/2} R^tv A^{1/2} J` lies in the range of `R^tv D`), with the `a_f`-per-face shortcut removed. The figure generator gains a `forces` mode that regenerates only Chapter 8's macros and checks the one-velocity counterfactual (`hydrostatic_former_step`, `chkStepCmdgfmFormer`), and `proof_review/eq-forces-two-velocity/scripts/code_05_current_map.jl` re-verifies the CMDGFM half of that audit on the current tree; the chapter-13 part of the generator still aborts on a `stab_track!` that passes the pre-refactor argument list to `_enforce_current_velocity_boundary!`, which is unrelated and left for its owner.
- The simulation summary reports the pressure-projection work of the whole `simulate!` call instead of only its final step. A developed flow usually starts a solve within its absolute target `tolerance / dt^2`, and that solve legitimately reports no iterations and an unchanged residual, so single-phase runs with a small automatic step printed `iterations: 0` and `residual: 0.574 -> 0.574` even though the run projected and converged (#158). `simulate!` now accumulates a `ProjectionRunStats` in the `projection` field of its result — projected steps, total iterations, the solves that needed none, the largest single-solve count, and the iteration count plus residual pair of the most recent solve that iterated or reduced its residual — and the summary prints those for one-phase, two-phase one-velocity and two-velocity runs, next to the latest solve's convergence flag, `compatible` flag, method, preconditioner and removed-RHS fields.
- Level-set initialisation accepts a box once the area it can misattribute is within the cell's absolute `atol` budget. The old rule compared the box area with a tolerance that was quartered along with the area, so it never fired and a level set touching a grid line tangentially at a point none of the nine samples hits threw for any `maxdepth`. With a Lipschitz bound the misattributed area is now certified by the samples themselves: a sample of the accepted sign rules the other phase out of the ball of radius `abs(phi)/lipschitz` around it, so the largest such ball bounds what is left, with its overlap with the box evaluated in closed form. A box that the pure-cell test and the height graph leave unresolved is accepted at the depth where that bound meets `atol`, and still throws when neither `maxdepth` nor `atol` allows it. A circle of radius 0.27 centred at (0.52,0.47) on a 16x16 unit square with `lipschitz=1` now initialises to the same fractions as without the bound (volume error 6e-17), while `maxdepth=14` or `atol=1e-13` still throw for it (#49).
- The pressure gradient supports a periodic direction with exactly two cells. `_face_displacement` took the centre-to-centre difference modulo the period, which is a tie at half a period (`round(-0.5) == -0.0`), so the seam face of such a direction was displaced the wrong way: the vertex-patch tensor was not positive definite and `GradientOperator` threw `ArgumentError: degenerate vertex patch in gradient construction` on every such mesh (base `(2,2)` with both directions periodic, `(2,4)` periodic in x, `(4,2)` periodic in y, `(2,6)` periodic in x). The normal component now takes its sign from the face orientation — `plus` is always the cell on the `+axis` side of the shared face — and only its magnitude from the periodic wrap, while the tangential component keeps its wrapped sign, which is unambiguous because two centres are at most half a coarse cell apart tangentially. A two-cell direction therefore carries the covering-space displacement `h` on its seam and interior faces alike, the metric reduces to the ordinary diagonal MAC coefficient `1/|ω_f|` there (the diagonal kernel on dyadic extents), the area-weighted jumps of an affine pressure map to its face-normal gradient exactly (0.0, previously a throw), and the pressure operator keeps exactly the constant null mode. The multigrid coarsest level still keeps four cells per periodic axis, which is now a coarse-level resolution choice rather than a gradient restriction.
- Thread-count determinism is now tested directly. The new `threading_equivalence` group runs five short flows (a two-phase CMDGFM capillary wave, two-phase CMDGFM on an embedded sloped wall, the two-velocity Stokes-3 wave, which must regrid during the run, the cylinder, and Taylor–Green vortices on an adaptively refined mesh) in fresh Julia processes with one, two and four threads and the threading grain at one, so small meshes take the threaded branch of every loop, and requires bitwise equal SHA-256 hashes of every state field (velocity components per phase, pressure, liquid fraction) and of every recorded diagnostic history (names, sample times and steps, values), equal step, mesh-update and leaf-block counts, and warm steps of the Taylor–Green and capillary cases within a recorded allocation budget at one and two threads (a separate budget on Julia 1.11, whose Polyester boxes a closure argument per launch). The group is not in the default suite; the new `threading-equivalence` CI job runs it on Julia 1.12 on every pull request and is part of `ci-ok`. A `scaling` dispatch tier (`gh workflow run CI.yml -f tier=scaling`) runs `benchmark/threading/scaling.jl` for the ten target cases at one, two and four threads and uploads the logs as an artifact; it is not part of `ci-ok`.
- Copying a velocity component into a `FaceField` (and back), as the staggered advection does every step, is a range copy of the contiguous faces of that axis instead of a filter over every face, and is threaded; so is the velocity copy at the start of a step. Bitwise unchanged for every thread count.
- The unit CI job now runs its three shards on Julia 1.11 as well as 1.12, so the lower compat bound is checked on every pull request; the push-only unsharded `unit-minimum` job is gone. `SimulationStepSelection` and `TransferRowSchedule` declare their inner constructors explicitly, because the implicitly generated ones left a type parameter that only occurs inside a union unbound, which Aqua reports as "Unbound type parameters" on Julia 1.11. A threaded `@_parallel` launch keeps allocating nothing per item, but on Julia 1.11 Polyester boxes its closure argument (16-32 bytes per launch, nothing from 1.12 on); the documented contract and the threading test now say so.
- The two-velocity CMDGFM projection threads its BiCGSTAB products deterministically: each projection keeps a stored transpose of the assembled system (pattern-checked, threaded value gather) and multiplies one output row per item with SparseArrays' serial `mul!` arithmetic (`muladd` from Julia 1.12 on, multiply-then-add on 1.11), so the products are bitwise the former serial ones on every supported Julia version and thread count. The diagonal Krylov scalings, the true residuals, the solution update (fixed chunks with one change flag each), the matrix-asymmetry diagnostic (a merge against the stored transpose, fixed-chunk exact maximum) and the reduced-pressure preconditioner's cell scatter and gather are threaded as well; the gauge anchors still collect their cells' values serially in index order. A full projection (assembly, the scaled operator and block preconditioner, the reduced-pressure `ldiv!`, the jump ILU and two successive BiCGSTAB solves) is tested bitwise at the serial grain against grain 1.
- The cylinder example's pressure drag and lift diagnostics sum over the cut cells' wall segments, collected once per geometry revision instead of searching every cell for a segment at every step; the values are bitwise unchanged. A geometry's revision for those caches now adds the fixed solid's own reconstruction count (`_interface_geometry_revision`), because `initialize!` and `reconstruct!` on `geometry.solid` rebuild its PLIC planes without a `refresh_geometry!`; the phase-interface PLIC caches, the CMDGFM pressure caches and the topology snapshots key on the same combined version. The adaptive example test forces an adapt, checks that the version changed and that both sums still equal a scan of every cell, and repeats that after a direct `initialize!` of the solid; the geometry tests also cover two geometries over one solid.
- Multigrid level refreshes no longer re-sort the whole level matrix with `transpose!`: each level keeps a value map of its stored row form (`SparseTransposeMap`), checks the matrix pattern against it on every refresh and copies the values through it in a threaded gather, rebuilding the map only when the pattern changes. `_phase_face_average!` is threaded over the interior faces of its axis, using that `mesh.faces` and `mesh.boundary_faces` list every axis-1 face before every axis-2 face, which `_check_face_schedules` now enforces. Results are bitwise unchanged for every thread count.
- The per-step two-velocity face loops are threaded deterministically: inactive phase-velocity marking and the WY velocity checks validate faces in fixed chunks of 1024 (one output flag per chunk, owned by the calling workspace), the frozen-interface bound takes fixed-chunk face maxima folded in chunk order (exact, so the timestep does not depend on the thread count), and the phase-face extension and the projection's phase-velocity correction are threaded per face. The face maxima, the extension and the correction capture flat arrays and scalars; the marking and the WY checks read cell data through the conditional-fraction block vectors, which are boxed per launch; the frozen bound's per-column loop hoists the workspace's fields into array and scalar captures instead of capturing the owner structs. Error precedence is unchanged and independent of the thread count: the marking reports its first failing face, liquid before gas on that face, after the remaining interior faces have been marked, while the WY checks run per phase (liquid interior, liquid boundary, gas interior, gas boundary) with interior before boundary within a phase. The cut-cell variants, which read the solid reconstruction, stay serial. Results are bitwise identical for every thread count.
- The per-solve and per-iteration solver loops are threaded deterministically: component sums, strong-residual and roundoff-floor maxima and the Krylov residual loops use fixed chunks of 1024 items whose boundaries depend only on the cell count, so one thread and many give bitwise identical results and a solve repeats exactly. Sums change round-off once against earlier versions, on levels above 1024 cells only (the oracle's levels are smaller, so its records are unchanged); maxima are equal to the old serial maximum up to the `NaN` payload, because `NaN` terms are canonicalised. Restriction and prolongation always run the package kernels (row-scheduled prolongation, column-dot restriction), also unthreaded, so their results no longer depend on whether SparseArrays' `mul!` fuses (Julia 1.12) or not (1.10/1.11).
- Threaded loop bodies no longer throw: inputs are validated before the loop, so errors are the package's own exceptions rather than a Polyester worker's `TaskFailedException`. User callbacks (`initialize!` functions, velocity initializers, level sets, `PhaseScalar` initializers, `Dirichlet` halo and inflow values) are evaluated serially on the calling task, so they need not be thread-safe and give thread-count-independent results.
- Data-dependent checks in threaded kernels record the failure in an owned, preallocated flag and the caller throws after the launch, rather than costing a serial pass over every cell each step. The WY sweep and the conditional-fraction refresh fuse their bounds check into the threaded update, the elliptic matrix locates its diagonal slots once at assembly (also for the reaction update), the variable-diffusion refresh adds its diagonal inertia in one fused pass, and `fill_halo!` iterates a precollected list of physical halo entries instead of scanning the schedule. A rejected conditional fraction is no longer restored cell for cell, because its check shares the clamp's pass.

- Fixed premature termination of ILU-preconditioned CMDGFM projection solves and eliminated warm staggered elliptic solver allocations.
- Avoided per-face allocations in the single-phase pressure predictor.
- Interface and solid refinement bands protect coarsening families whose parent may be near the boundary, preventing the buffer-induced static-band refine/coarsen cycle.
- Curvature refinement detects mixed liquid–gas cells using the open-fluid conditional fraction, rather than treating liquid-filled solid cut cells as unresolved interfaces. A block that holds a mixed cell in its cells or its one-cell halo counts its liquid-filled cut cells as interface cells, because the interface meets the solid inside their wetted part; the contact line of a droplet on an embedded wall keeps its curvature refinement instead of being coarsened into an unresolved surface-tension face (#148); coarsening families that hold such a cell are kept when their prospective parent would span both phases, which stops the contact-line refine/coarsen cycle without treating covered cells as gas (#118).
- The conditional liquid fraction's halo is now the restriction of the liquid and open fractions, their weighted ratio, instead of the mean of the per-cell ratios. This is a physics change as well as a refinement change: covered cells carry conditional fraction zero by construction, not gas, so a coarse slot over three covered children and one liquid child read 0.25 where the restriction reads one. Transport fluxes, the interface reconstruction and the surface-tension force evaluate cut cells at a level jump with the corrected value, and the interface criterion no longer treats a nearly covered block as bordering the interface (#148).
- Two-velocity projection tolerances now compare dimensionless local velocity-jump and residual defects, using the documented local mesh spacing.
- Standalone one-velocity projections require the pressure operator and `velocity_boundary` to have the same open sides; either an open boundary with a Neumann-only operator or an open operator with a mismatched boundary now raises `ArgumentError`.

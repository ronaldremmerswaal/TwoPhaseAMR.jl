Incompressible Navier–Stokes solver

One solver advances face velocity and cell pressure on a static or dynamically adapted uniform/mixed-level tree. Supplying an interface selects two-phase coefficients and compatible geometric transport. A single FaceVelocity selects the one-velocity model; a (liquid_velocity, gas_velocity) tuple selects the two-velocity model. The outer time-step sequence stays the same.

Common step

Each step executes transport, midpoint body forcing, implicit diffusion, optional phase forces, and one pressure projection. Dispatch changes the kernel inside a stage rather than selecting a second time integrator.

For single-phase flow, the transport and user-force state is

\[u^c = u^n - \Delta t\,C_{LW}(u^n) + \Delta t\,f^{n+1/2}.\]

The bulk scheme defaults to LaxWendroff() and the scheme-aware AMR-interface default selects MonotonizedCentral() on non-regular coarse–fine connections. Global Upwind retains Upwind at the interface, and another limited bulk scheme is used unchanged. Interface-only Upwind is rejected because its unmatched first-order closure has poor mixed-level accuracy. amr_interface_scheme can still be overridden with any compatible non-Upwind scheme.

For two-phase flow, directionally split Weymouth–Yue interface advection first computes the liquid and gas volumes crossing every centred face. The momentum stage interpolates those same volume fluxes to staggered control volumes and advects the two intrinsic phase velocities with CTU upwinding near the interface. Only intrinsic velocity is stored. Compatible phase scalars follow the same construction, so a scalar equal to one in either phase is recovered exactly. Intrinsic recovery divides only above sqrt(eps(T)) phase fraction.

The generic user force f is sampled at the time midpoint and added to the transported state before diffusion in both models. Single-phase viscosity is then centred by Crank–Nicolson/implicit midpoint:

\[\left(I-\frac{\Delta t\nu}{2}L\right)u^{**} = u^c + \frac{\Delta t\nu}{2}Lu^n + \Delta t\nu b^{n+1/2}_{wall}.\]

The affine wall term contains both half-step contributions. The shifted system is solved independently on the x- and y-dual grids by StaggeredEllipticSolver. It selects external Krylov.jl CG for a symmetric operator and BiCGSTAB otherwise, with the package's geometric V-cycle preconditioner. The V-cycle objects contain no Krylov logic. The unchanged operator, preconditioner, and workspaces are reused for a fixed dt, and LU appears only in the two geometric bottom solves.

For two phases, the viscous term is the coupled stress divergence div(2*mu*D(u)). At cell-centred normal stresses and vertex-centred shear stresses, the dynamic viscosity is the geometric mixture

\[\bar\mu=(\mu^\ell)^c(\mu^g)^{1-c},\]

where c is interpolated to the stress location before evaluating the formula. Density appears in the staggered inertia matrix, not in this dynamic viscosity. The midpoint system is

\[\left(M_\rho+\frac{\Delta t}{2}K_\mu\right)u^{**} =M_\rho u^*-\frac{\Delta t}{2}K_\mu u^*,\]

where K_mu is the positive-semidefinite integrated stress stiffness. Here u* is the state after compatible momentum transport and any generic body force applied before diffusion, matching the split stress mu*symgrad(u* + u**). This staggered variable-density/variable-viscosity construction and the two-velocity diffusion model are documented by Remmerswaal (2023); related finite-volume Stokes solver structure is described by Cai et al. (2014).

Balanced CSF or CGFM surface tension and gravity are added after diffusion. They use the same compatible weighted-gradient operator as the following pressure correction. Finally, both models perform one projection:

\[-DGp^{n+1} = -\frac{1}{\Delta t}Du^{**}, \qquad u^{n+1}=u^{**}-\Delta t\,Gp^{n+1}.\]

For the one-velocity two-phase model, G includes the face coefficient 1/<rho>, where <rho>=rho_l*c_f+rho_g*(1-c_f). There is no projection between convection and diffusion and no second projection after the correction above. The same primary finite-volume D and its dual-derived G are used in the Poisson matrix and velocity update.

Two-velocity projection

TwoVelocityModel selects either CMDGFM or the symmetric three-block mimetic formulation through its pressure_projection field. The default is CMDGFMProjection().

The mimetic formulation solves for cell pressure p, the phase-gradient jump xi on mixed faces, and a cell multiplier lambda:

\[\begin{bmatrix}-A_p&C&0\\C^T&K_\xi&B^T\\0&B&0\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}p\\\xi\\\lambda\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\Delta t^{-1}D\langle au^{***}\rangle\\0\\ -\Delta t^{-1}D([a][u^{***}])\end{bmatrix}.\]

A_p uses the existing AMR Riesz gradient and cut-cell pressure operator. B uses the same conservative face/cell incidence and the aperture jump. Only faces on which both staggered phase fractions exceed the interface's global fraction_floor retain xi; the method does not divide by an individual phase mass or by the aperture jump. MINRES uses a symmetric positive block preconditioner. Surface tension and gravity are added to the predictor before this solve and therefore enter its right hand side.

CMDGFMProjection() instead solves the nonsymmetric two-block system

\[\begin{bmatrix}L_{pp}&L_{p\xi}\\L_{\xi p}&L_{\xi\xi}\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}p\\\xi\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}b_p\\b_\xi\end{bmatrix}.\]

It uses the same conservative D, AMR Riesz gradient, pressure block, staggered phase fractions, PLIC apertures, and cut-cell geometry. The local jump interpolant uses transverse incident faces and a bounded coefficient. Writing R=|n_t/n_f|, gamma_lg=gamma^l_(h^g), and gamma_gl=gamma^g_(h^l), the implemented choice is

\[\beta_f=\begin{cases} 1,&R\leq1,\\ \dfrac{1-\gamma_{lg}-\gamma_{gl}} {R-\gamma_{lg}-\gamma_{gl}},&R>1. \end{cases}\]

Both inference faces are neighboring same-axis faces. This leaves the jump block diagonal equal to one and makes every row weakly diagonally dominant. BiCGSTAB uses a lower triangular block preconditioner. The pressure action is one application of the existing rediscretized tree-geometric GMG hierarchy, mapped between the full compatible pressure space and CMDGFM's gauge-reduced unknowns. The jump action is a Crout ILU of the active jump block. A jump is active only when both staggered phase fractions exceed fraction_floor and its local interpolation stencil is resolved.

On a fixed pressure space the GMG hierarchy is retained as a valid lagged preconditioner. The jump ILU is retained while its active-face sparsity pattern is unchanged. A mesh update creates a new workspace and rebuilds both. Set refresh_pressure_coefficients=true or reuse_jump_factorization=false in preconditioner_options to force numeric refreshes. CMDGFMProjection(preconditioner=:ilu) remains available as a whole-system ILU comparison.

Momentum and pressure-work diagnostics use the weak face inner product induced by the same Riesz map. This equals the familiar diagonal dual-volume kinetic inner product on a uniform Cartesian mesh; at a coarse–fine interface it includes the vertex-patch couplings used by the AMR gradient.

Two-velocity diffusion

Viscosity uses an equivalent-one-velocity construction. With the liquid fraction c_f in an open staggered dual volume, the transported intrinsic velocities first form

\[\bar u^* = \frac{\rho^\ell c_f u^{\ell,*} + \rho^g(1-c_f)u^{g,*}} {\rho^\ell c_f + \rho^g(1-c_f)}.\]

The existing one-velocity two-phase midpoint solver advances this field using the same variable-density inertia and geometric dynamic-viscosity average. Both phases then receive the common increment

\[u^{\pi,**}=u^{\pi,*}+(\bar u^{**}-\bar u^*).\]

The phase-weighted momentum therefore matches the one-velocity solve, the intrinsic jump u_g-u_liquid is preserved, and equal phase velocities recover the one-velocity result exactly. On embedded geometry, c_f is weighted over the open part of the staggered dual volume. Only intrinsic velocities are stored; phase-weighted values are local temporaries.

The integration suite also advances the finite-viscosity La = 3000 capillary wave over a quarter period on 8, 16, and 32 cells per wavelength. It compares the complete amplitude history with Prosperetti's linear solution and with the one-velocity solver on the same meshes and time steps:

Dimensionless solver tolerance

step! and simulate! expose one dimensionless tolerance, shared by the pressure and diffusion solves. They do not expose algebraic rtol or dimensional atol values.

For pressure, the implementation follows Basilisk. If r_p is the strong Poisson residual, the requested stopping condition is

\[\Delta t^2\lVert r_p\rVert_\infty \leq \mathtt{tolerance}.\]

This quantity is the maximum fractional cell-volume defect that the remaining velocity divergence would produce during one timestep. It remains meaningful when the provisional velocity is already nearly divergence free and does not depend on the initial pressure residual.

For diffusion, the integrated momentum residual is divided by the local staggered inertia. This gives a velocity defect. Its infinity norm is divided by the corresponding strong right-hand-side velocity scale, so the same dimensionless tolerance applies to both single- and variable-density solves. This normalization depends on the equation data, not on the initial guess. Both criteria are raised to a computed floating-point roundoff floor when the requested tolerance cannot be represented reliably.

LinearSolverStats reports the initial and final L2 and infinity norms, the effective infinity-norm target, the residual scale, iterations, removed pressure-RHS mean, Krylov method, and preconditioner. A failed high-level solve includes all of these fields, the requested tolerance, achieved dimensionless residual, iteration cap, timestep, and simulation time in its exception.

Boundary conditions

The default wall conditions on non-periodic sides impose no permeation, u dot n = 0. The pressure has homogeneous Neumann data, so the projection cannot change the prescribed boundary-normal velocity. The all-Neumann pressure nullspace is fixed by a volume-weighted zero mean.

The viscous closure is independent:

  • NoSlip() is the default and uses zero tangential wall velocity;
  • NoSlip(wall_velocity) supports a moving wall such as a cavity lid; and
  • FreeSlip() imposes zero tangential shear.

For open boundaries, VelocityBoundaryConditions(; left, right, bottom, top) selects a condition per side (unspecified sides default to NoSlip()). Inflow(f) prescribes velocity from f(x, y, time, axis). ZeroGradientVelocity() extrapolates the velocity with zero normal gradient. Outflow() uses area- and aperture-weighted zero-gradient extrapolation, then applies a uniform outward correction over outflow faces to make the total boundary flux zero. Without an open outflow, prescribed boundary velocities must already have zero net flux. Pressure remains homogeneous Neumann on these sides. WY interface transport requires impermeable boundaries.

This is a non-incremental (Chorin-type) projection that solves for the whole pressure at each step. Accurate physical pressure boundary values, especially at no-slip walls, require a separate boundary-accuracy study before pressure itself is used as a validation observable. Brown, Cortez, and Minion explain this distinction for second-order projection methods.

API

mesh = BlockTreeMesh(block_size=(32, 32), periodic=(false, false))
lid = NoSlip((x, y, time, axis) ->
    axis == 1 && y > 0.5 ? 1.0 : 0.0)

system = NavierStokesSystem(
    mesh; density=1.0, dynamic_viscosity=0.01, boundary=lid,
    scheme=LaxWendroff()) # MonotonizedCentral is selected at AMR interfaces
workspace = NavierStokesWorkspace(system)
velocity = FaceVelocity(mesh, (x, y, time, axis) -> 0.0)
pressure = CellField(mesh)

result = simulate!(
    velocity, pressure, system, 1.0, workspace;
    dt=0.002, tolerance=1e-8, max_cycles=80)

The corresponding two-phase setup supplies a physics object:

interface = InterfaceField(
    mesh, (x, y) -> hypot(x - 0.5, y - 0.5) - 0.15)
physics = TwoPhase(
    interface;
    density=(1000.0, 1.0),
    dynamic_viscosity=(1e-3, 1.8e-5),
    surface_tension=0.072,
    gravity=(0.0, -9.81),
)
flow = FlowSimulation(
    mesh, physics; velocity_model=OneVelocityModel(),
    boundary=FreeSlip(), dt=1e-4)

The two-velocity form supplies two intrinsic velocity initializers and uses the same material-property API:

flow = FlowSimulation(
    mesh, physics;
    velocity_model=TwoVelocityModel(
        interface_transport=FrozenInterfaceFluxes(),
        pressure_projection=CMDGFMProjection(),
    ),
    velocity=(liquid_velocity, gas_velocity),
    boundary=FreeSlip(),
)

examples/interfacial_instability.jl uses this API for a combined Kelvin–Helmholtz/Rayleigh–Taylor wave with surface tension. The example is parameterized by the liquid-to-gas density ratio, Weber number, and Bond number:

include("examples/interfacial_instability.jl")
run = run_example(
    density_ratio=1000.0,
    weber=0.25,
    bond=0.5,
    max_level=1,
    final_time=0.1,
)

It uses the capillary frequency as its time scale. With wavenumber k, the derived coefficients satisfy sigma*k^3/(rho_liquid + rho_gas) = 1. Consequently the dispersion relation for a heavy liquid above the gas has offset sqrt(1 - weber - bond). The dimensional density tuple, phase velocity jump, surface tension, and gravity passed to FlowSimulation are derived internally from those nondimensional inputs.

Pressure and coupled two-phase stress diffusion default to preconditioned Krylov.jl CG because their operators are symmetric positive (semi-)definite. Single-phase staggered diffusion selects CG for a symmetric operator and BiCGSTAB when the AMR consistency correction makes it nonsymmetric. Pressure and diffusion use rediscretized tree-geometric hierarchies for both single-phase and two-phase flow. The single-phase diffusion hierarchy keeps separate x/y component operators; the two-phase hierarchy retains the coupled stress operator. Both can be configured independently. The root-block sparse operator is the bottom solve even when its unknown count exceeds coarse_size:

import AlgebraicMultigrid, IncompleteLU, Krylov

system = NavierStokesSystem(
    mesh; density=1.0, dynamic_viscosity=0.01, boundary=lid,
    pressure_krylov=Krylov.CgWorkspace,
    pressure_krylov_options=(;),
    pressure_preconditioner=:amg,
    diffusion_krylov=Krylov.BicgstabWorkspace,
    diffusion_krylov_options=(;),
    diffusion_preconditioner=:ilu,
    diffusion_preconditioner_options=(; drop_tolerance=1e-3))

The two *_krylov_options named tuples forward method-specific settings; for example, a GmresWorkspace factory can use diffusion_krylov_options=(; restart=true, reorthogonalization=true). The dimensionless convergence tolerance and iteration ceiling come from step! or simulate!.

Available specifications and their symmetry restrictions are listed in Geometric multigrid and pressure projection.

step! advances one supplied dt; simulate! repeats the same operation to a final time. If dt is omitted, the solver combines the staggered convective CFL bound with the two-phase capillary and gravity-wave restrictions when those forces are present. The implicit-midpoint diffusion stage imposes no diffusion-based timestep restriction; pass dt explicitly when a particular temporal accuracy resolution is required. Crank–Nicolson is second-order but does not strongly damp unresolved stiff modes, so reduce the time step when a viscous transient oscillates even though the implicit solve converges.

Pass a VisualizationSession with visualization=session to receive copied live frames and retain scheduled checkpoints. Nearest and non-intrusive exact checkpoint policies are described in Interactive visualization. simulate! initializes the observer with an independent-step procedure but does not inspect its checkpoint schedule or modify the main time-step sequence.

Dynamic refinement is enabled by passing refinement_indicators and optional additional adaptive_fields to the solver constructor. Velocity and pressure are always transferred. Adaptation happens after a completed physical step, subject to mesh.adaptation.options.update_interval; it performs no additional time step or pressure correction. Topology-dependent operators and workspaces are rebuilt only when the leaf set changes. See Dynamic adaptive refinement for the API, transfer invariants, cooldown, benchmarks, and exact-checkpoint limitation.

Accuracy notes

The complete time step is verified with periodic Taylor–Green flow on uniform and mixed-level meshes. The lid-driven-cavity example provides a familiar viscous reference with moving and stationary no-slip walls. As with other projection methods, velocity and divergence are the primary observables; accurate physical pressure at a no-slip boundary requires a separate boundary treatment and refinement study.

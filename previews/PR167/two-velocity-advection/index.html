Two-velocity interface advection

Intrinsic phase velocities exist only where their phase occupies positive open staggered volume. FlowSimulation and the two-velocity projection and momentum update store NaN on absent-phase faces, including closed solid volumes. This is an exact zero-volume decision, not a density-dependent threshold. Phase-weighted reductions skip the absent phase before doing arithmetic; they must not evaluate 0 * NaN.

Transport and mesh transfer obtain missing stencil entries by explicit local extension of valid same-phase, same-component values in working buffers. The owned velocity is not a persistent extension into the other phase. Register both velocities with their interface for AMR:

amr = AMRWorkspace(mesh, velocity, interface)  # velocity = (liquid, gas)
# Or: register_adaptive_field!(amr, velocity, interface)

For phase vorticity, pass interface and phase=:liquid or :gas to VorticityRefinementIndicator or visualization_values(velocity, Vorticity()). AMR automatically supplies this association for registered phase velocities. The curl stencil uses a local affine extrapolation from valid phase cells (constant extrapolation if that fit lacks rank). Vorticity in absent-phase cells remains NaN and does not prevent coarsening. An unresolved active cell is not allowed to trigger coarsening.

The two-velocity model transports the physical interface with the frozen liquid and gas subfaces of each face, each extruded by its corresponding intrinsic velocity. The underlying model and its mass/momentum-compatible transport are developed by Remmerswaal (2023) and Remmerswaal and Veldman (2022).

Directional WY balance

Let $V_c^o$ be the geometry-open volume of cell $c$. Every entry in the mesh face schedule is a physical leaf-level face segment, including the fine segments of a coarse–fine connection. A signed, time-integrated face volume is positive from face.minus to face.plus. Define

\[({\cal D}^k F)_c = \frac{1}{V_c^o} \sum_{f\in\partial_k c}\sigma_{cf}F_f,\]

where $\sigma_{cf}=+1$ for face.minus and $-1$ for face.plus. For one directional sweep, the Weymouth–Yue (WY) update is

\[C_{\ell,c}^{[k+1]} = C_{\ell,c}^{[k]} - {\cal D}^k L_c + \chi_{\ell,c}^n {\cal D}^k Q_c. \tag{1}\]

Here $C_\ell$ is the liquid fraction relative to the open volume, $L_f$ is the liquid swept volume, $Q_f$ is the complete carrier volume, and $\chi_{\ell,c}^n = [C_{\ell,c}^n>1/2]$ is frozen for the complete time step. Equation (1) uses time-integrated fluxes and the package's face orientation.

The PLIC plane is reconstructed after every directional sweep. The indicator, velocity, and time-step-start data used by dilation remain frozen. Alternating the sweep order between time steps is independent of these balance identities.

If liquid and gas fluxes partition the carrier,

\[L_f+G_f=Q_f, \tag{2}\]

the matching gas equation is

\[C_{g,c}^{[k+1]} = C_{g,c}^{[k]} - {\cal D}^k G_c + (1-\chi_{\ell,c}^n){\cal D}^k Q_c. \tag{3}\]

Equations (1)–(3) preserve $C_\ell+C_g=1$ after every sweep. They reduce exactly to the existing one-velocity implementation when $Q_f=\Delta t\,A_f a_f^o u_f$ and the same donating region is split by the PLIC plane.

Compatible centred scalars

Persistent scalar fields store intrinsic values. During a sweep only, define the phase amount $M_c^\pi=C_c^\pi s_c^\pi$. Its update is

\[M_c^{\pi,[k+1]} = M_c^{\pi,[k]} - {\cal D}^k(F^\pi s_f^\pi)_c + \chi_c^{\pi,n}s_c^{\pi,n}{\cal D}^k Q_c. \tag{4}\]

The phase-interface interpolant in $s_f^\pi$ is upwind. Higher-order bulk interpolation is allowed only when all cells in its stencil contain that phase. Applying (4) to $s^\pi=1$ gives the phase-volume equation bit for bit; this is the constant-one compatibility requirement. Recovery divides by the new phase fraction only in an active cell. A newly active cell receives the upwind/extension value selected while forming the flux; no artificial small denominator is inserted. The global interface fraction_floor remains the single rule that removes wisps and determines activity.

Compatible staggered scalars and phase momentum

The same centred phase-volume fluxes are algebraically interpolated onto each staggered dual grid. For phase $\pi$ and one velocity component, the local conservative update is

\[(C_f^\pi u_f^\pi)^{[k+1]} = (C_f^\pi u_f^\pi)^{[k]} - {\cal D}_{f}^{k}(F^\pi u^\pi) + {\cal I}_f(\chi^\pi {\cal D}^k Q)u_f^{\pi,n}.\]

The implementation stores only $u_f^\pi$. It forms the products above inside the update and divides by the staggered phase fraction only when that phase is active according to the interface fraction_floor. At an interface, the CTU state is selected separately for every partial interpolated flux; the configured higher-order interpolant is used only when both adjacent dual volumes contain at least ctu_threshold of the phase. The default is $C_{\min}=1/2$.

The requested scheme applies to regular staggered dual faces on periodic, bounded, and embedded meshes. amr_interface_scheme applies at non-regular coarse–fine connections and connections to physical boundary faces. Cut-cell connections use the selected bulk scheme unless they are also non-regular; their fluxes already carry the open-aperture geometry. Three-point schemes fall back to upwind when an upstream stencil is unavailable.

This recovery/stencil policy is distinct from the intrinsic velocity storage mask: only an exactly zero open phase volume is marked NaN. A stencil tolerance must not classify a positive-volume velocity as absent.

For constant per-phase densities, multiplying this update by $\rho^\pi$ and dividing by the new phase mass cancels the density algebraically. The API still accepts the density pair because it defines the conserved momentum and is needed when the two phase results are recombined or diagnosed. Liquid and gas velocities remain the transported state; advection does not replace them with a mass-weighted mixture. The diffusion step forms its equivalent one-velocity field only in reusable scratch storage.

momentum_workspace = MomentumAdvectionWorkspace(interface, velocity)
passive_x = PhaseFaceScalar{1}(
    interface; liquid=liquid_tracer, gas=gas_tracer)

advect!(
    interface, velocity, dt, workspace;
    staggered_scalars=(passive_x,),
    momentum=velocity,
    density=(rho_liquid, rho_gas),
    momentum_workspace,
    scheme=Fromm(),
)

The workspace snapshots both old intrinsic velocity fields, so the source and destination tuple may be the same object. Both staggered phase updates consume the complementary frozen-subface flux pair. Applying the update to a one-function reproduces the interpolated staggered phase-volume balance exactly. phase_flux_diagnostics(workspace) exposes the face-partition defect of the complementary phase fluxes. The tuple entry point always uses this separate phase-momentum update. Equal phase velocities are therefore a validation limit of the two-velocity discretization rather than a runtime dispatch to the one-velocity operator. Callers that want the one-velocity model pass a FaceVelocity directly.

Accuracy and conservation

The frozen-subface transport preserves constant intrinsic phase fields and staggered one-functions. It conserves integrated liquid/gas phase quantities because every transported field uses the same complementary phase-flux pair. The higher-order staggered interpolant switches to CTU whenever either adjacent predicted phase fraction is below ctu_threshold. Smooth bulk fields can therefore retain higher-order behavior, while a field that remains discontinuous at a moving interface should be expected to converge at first order locally.

Frozen phase subfaces

At $t^n$, the transport splits every geometry-open face into liquid and gas subfaces with apertures $a_f^{\ell,n}$ and $a_f^{g,n}$. For each carrier phase $\pi'$, extrude that subface with its own velocity. For either transported phase $\pi$,

\[F_f^\pi=\sum_{\pi'\in\{\ell,g\}} |D_{f^{\pi'}}(u_{\pi'})\cap\Omega_\pi^{[k]}|_{\rm signed}, \tag{5}\]

and

\[Q_f=\Delta t\,A_f (a_f^{\ell,n}u_{\ell,f}+a_f^{g,n}u_{g,f}). \tag{6}\]

Each donating region is geometrically divided into liquid and gas, so (2) holds to roundoff even when the two carrier velocities have opposite signs. Equations (1), (3), and (4) therefore use the mixture carrier (6) in their WY dilation. The PLIC geometry is reconstructed between directional sweeps, while the face-subdivision apertures $a_f^{\pi,n}$ remain frozen for the complete step. With a solid, the subface ordering is frozen too, so the swept intervals remain those checked by the cut-donor CFL bound. Without a solid, the ordering continues to follow the reconstructed interface normal.

The approximation replaces one phase velocity by the other away from the moving interface and has a local time-integrated error $O(\Delta t^2h^{d-1})$. It can be less accurate at large density ratios. phase_flux_diagnostics reports the face-partition defect and any fraction-bound failure rather than hiding them with fraction clipping.

Fixed solid geometry is applied before the liquid/gas subdivision: $a_f^\ell+a_f^g=a_f^o$. Both partial donating regions are clipped to the solid-open cell. At an AMR connection, equation (5) is evaluated on each owned fine face segment. The same integrated value is shared by its two incident cells, so summing the segments is the reflux operation; a second coarse-face flux must not be constructed.

When projection is refreshed

The frozen-subface transport does not add an auxiliary projection. It assumes that the supplied physical phase velocities already satisfy

\[\nabla_h\!\cdot(a_f^\ell u_f^\ell+a_f^g u_f^g)=0\]

for apertures reconstructed at the current time level. A stationary analytic velocity sampled on the grid generally fails this discrete constraint when the apertures change. A passive prescribed-velocity test must therefore reimpose the mixture constraint after each aperture update. In a complete two-velocity flow solve this is the responsibility of the physical pressure projection. After a regrid, step!(flow, dt) and simulate! also project the transferred phase velocities on the new apertures before the next transport step; this extra projection does not replace the transferred physical pressure. Standalone adapt! does not perform this projection. advect! always refreshes the frozen apertures itself; it does not assume that a preceding CFL query did so.

Two-velocity WY CFL

The boundedness condition uses incoming swept volumes rather than a maximum velocity component. For frozen subfaces the safe local condition is

\[\frac{\Delta t}{V_c^o}\sum_{f\in\partial c} \sum_{\pi\in\{\ell,g\}} \max(-\sigma_{cf}A_fa_f^\pi u_f^\pi,0)\le C_{WY}, \qquad C_{WY}\le\tfrac12. \tag{7}\]

The implementation also checks the corresponding outgoing sum when the input velocity has not yet met the mixture divergence constraint. This prevents a cell from being drained while keeping the intended condition unchanged for a discretely divergence-free field. Every active phase velocity additionally satisfies $\Delta t|u_f^\pi|/h_{\rm donor}\le C_{WY}$; this protects a fine face segment whose adjacent donor is coarser. Counterflowing phase subfaces are counted separately in (7), since cancellation in their net mixture flux does not make either donating region disappear.

For a cut donor, the volume behind an individual segment or phase subface can be much smaller than the whole open cell. If its volume rate is $q$ and its open strip volume is $V_{\rm strip}$, the bound requires $\Delta t\,q\le 2C_{WY}V_{\rm strip}$. For opposing outgoing subfaces it splits their common open support and bounds each flux by the open part of its own strip on its side of the split. With $C_{WY}\le 1/2$, these constraints make the open donating regions disjoint, including at hanging faces.

The two face traces of a cut cell are clipped independently, so a phase that does not reach a face can be left with an aperture of a few machine epsilons. That residue is snapped to exactly zero, since it would describe a subface of zero width with no open strip; a face whose own open extent is at that round-off level carries no resolvable flux and is left out of the strip constraints. A genuine flux whose donor strip has no open area, or one below the sweep's area tolerance, cannot be represented by any donating region: the bound raises an ArgumentError naming the face, its donor cell and the strip area instead of returning an unusable step.

Physical pressure projection

The complete inviscid model passes the same velocity tuple to NavierStokesSystem. After compatible interface and momentum advection, it assembles the nonsymmetric $(\phi, \xi)$ CMDGFM system described in Incompressible Navier–Stokes solver. CMDGFM subtracts the old physical pressure gradient in the predictor and adds the solved increment $\phi$ after correcting both phases. It enforces the mixture-divergence target and normal-velocity continuity while retaining tangential slip. The rotating-disc integration study reinitializes the analytical discontinuous velocity before each projection so its measured error belongs to the pressure model rather than accumulated velocity transport.

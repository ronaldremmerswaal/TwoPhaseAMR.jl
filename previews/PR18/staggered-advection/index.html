Staggered advection

Discrete construction

FaceField{Axis} contains one scalar per owned face segment normal to Axis. A staggered control volume surrounds each such face. Its boundary fluxes are not sampled or reconstructed independently: they are factored algebraically from the already-owned primal face-normal fluxes. For a uniform Cartesian grid this factorization reduces to the ordinary area-weighted average, for example,

\[F^u_{i,j+1/2} = \frac{\Delta x}{2} \left(v_{i-1/2,j+1/2}+v_{i+1/2,j+1/2}\right).\]

This follows the mass/momentum-compatible construction described by Remmerswaal (2023, Chapter 6). Every dual face is stored once and contributes equal-and-opposite integrated fluxes to its two neighbouring staggered volumes, so each component of linear momentum is conserved to roundoff on a periodic mesh.

Nonconforming AMR construction

At a coarse–fine interface the coarse geometrical side is partitioned into fine owned face segments; there is no additional coarse velocity unknown. For a segment $f$ adjacent to cell $c$ and normal to component direction $a$, define

\[\alpha_{cf} = \frac{|f|}{|\partial c|_{\mathrm{side},a}}.\]

Thus $\alpha=1$ on an unsplit side and the segment fractions on every cell side sum to one. The dual volume and volume-weighted cell-to-face interpolant are

\[W_f = \frac12\sum_{c\sim f}\alpha_{cf}V_c, \qquad (I_C d)_f = W_f^{-1}\frac12 \sum_{c\sim f}\alpha_{cf}V_c d_c.\]

Let $B$ be the oriented cell–face incidence matrix and $m=A u$ the integrated primal flux. Workspace construction makes a local conservative factorization of

\[R = \frac12 P_\alpha^T B,\]

where $(P_\alpha)_{cf}=\alpha_{cf}$. Normal fluxes are routed by local stars centred on the transported face. Transverse fluxes use a product-weight transport plan between the matching sides of their two cells. This may create a wider stencil at a hanging face, but its row and column marginals are exact. The resulting dual incidence and flux interpolant satisfy

\[D_s I_F m = W^{-1}Rm = I_C D u\]

to roundoff. The construction reduces exactly to the existing two-flux MAC schedule on any uniform level. It is built only when the mesh changes, so the hot kernel traverses compact connection and coefficient arrays without tree queries or allocations.

For an oriented dual-face flux F, neighbouring states q⁻ and q⁺, normal speed a, spacing h, and time step Δt, the upwind and centred face states are

\[q_\mathrm{up} = \begin{cases}q^- & F \ge 0,\\q^+ & F < 0,\end{cases} \qquad q_\mathrm{cen} = \frac{q^-+q^+}{2},\]

For one coordinate direction, the Lax–Wendroff face state is

\[q_\mathrm{LW} = \frac{q^-+q^+}{2} - \frac{\Delta t\,a}{2h}(q^+-q^-),\]

where Δt is the current directional substep.

The AMR factorization can contribute x- and y-oriented primal fluxes to the same pair of staggered unknowns. Schedule construction now groups a connection by (minus, plus, coordinate direction), rather than only (minus, plus). This leaves the total factored flux and $D_s I_F=I_C D$ unchanged, but makes an actual coordinate sweep possible. On a hanging-face connection the local Courant number is $|\Delta t F_g|/\min(W_-,W_+)$.

The complete LW update has two full sweeps. Their order alternates between successive time steps:

\[q^{(1)} = L_x(\Delta t)q^n,\quad q^{n+1} = L_y(\Delta t)q^{(1)}, \qquad\text{then}\qquad q^{(1)} = L_y(\Delta t)q^n,\quad q^{n+1} = L_x(\Delta t)q^{(1)}.\]

Every sweep consumes the field produced by the preceding sweep. Within a velocity sweep, both component tendencies are evaluated from one input vector and are published together; this is a coordinate split, not a split between velocity components. Alternating the order cancels the leading Lie-splitting error over a pair of equal steps and retains the second-order Taylor–Green regression without a third Strang substep.

For variable transporting velocity, classical constant-coefficient LW misses the $q\,\partial_a u_a$ part of the directional predictor. Each face state therefore also contains

\[-\frac{\Delta t}{4} \left(q^- (D_a u_a)^- + q^+ (D_a u_a)^+\right).\]

The x and y directional divergences cancel for a discretely divergence-free velocity only after both sweeps; including them separately is necessary for second-order split transport. The Navier–Stokes step freezes the transporting mass flux at the old velocity over the two sweeps, matching the previous explicit treatment.

For positive dt, convection! returns the effective rate of the workspace's next two-sweep update without advancing its order flag, so advected - dt * destination equals the next result of advect!. At dt == 0, it evaluates the limiting spatial operator directly. The interface scheme remains an explicit API choice:

convection!(rate, q, velocity, dt, workspace)
advect!(q, velocity, dt, workspace;
        scheme=LW(), amr_interface_scheme=LW())

The bulk scheme defaults to LW(). When amr_interface_scheme is omitted, default_amr_interface_scheme(scheme) applies the following policy:

  • Upwind() uses Upwind everywhere;
  • Centred() and LW() use MC() at non-regular AMR connections; and
  • Fromm and the limited three-point schemes use themselves at the interface.

Either keyword accepts the corresponding lower-case symbols. Interface-only Upwind is rejected; use scheme=Upwind() when first-order upwinding is wanted throughout. For example, the decaying-turbulence showcase's effective default is

scheme=LW(), amr_interface_scheme=MC()

so that only non-regular AMR connections use the nonlinear reconstruction. An explicit compatible interface keyword has no numerical effect on a uniform mesh.

Three-point interface fluxes

For an oriented connection, let $q_{UU}$, $q_U$, and $q_D$ denote the second-upstream, upwind, and downwind values. Workspace construction follows the connection graph into the upstream half-space and selects the candidate closest to the geometrically reflected edge. The smoothness ratio includes the projected spacing ratio,

\[r = \frac{|x_D-x_U|}{|x_U-x_{UU}|} \frac{q_U-q_{UU}}{q_D-q_U},\]

so a linear profile has $r=1$ across a two-to-one change in spacing. The high-resolution state blends the conservative upwind and LW connection fluxes,

\[F_\phi = F_\mathrm{up} + \phi(r) \left(F_\mathrm{LW}-F_\mathrm{up}\right).\]

The implemented standard three-point functions are

\[\begin{aligned} \phi_\mathrm{Fromm}(r) &= \tfrac12(1+r),\\ \phi_\mathrm{minmod}(r) &= \max(0,\min(1,r)),\\ \phi_\mathrm{MC}(r) &= \max(0,\min(2r,\tfrac12(1+r),2)),\\ \phi_\mathrm{vanLeer}(r) &= \frac{r+|r|}{1+|r|},\\ \phi_\mathrm{superbee}(r) &= \max(0,\min(2r,1),\min(r,2)). \end{aligned}\]

These are the classical flux-limiter forms catalogued by Sweby (1984); the spacing correction follows the nonuniform-grid consistency analysis of Zeng (2016).

Fromm is the unrestricted average of the Lax–Wendroff and Beam–Warming corrections; it is a larger-stencil control, not a TVD limiter. Minmod, MC, van Leer, and superbee set the correction to zero at a local extremum. A small subset of factored hanging-face edges joins degrees of freedom at the same transport coordinate and therefore has no meaningful upstream half-space. Those edges explicitly fall back to the upwind state rather than constructing a fictitious slope. Reconstruction metadata is precomputed in the workspace; the flux loop adds one indexed read and does not allocate.

These classical limiter formulas do not establish a global TVD theorem for the multidimensional branched AMR graph. They do, however, address the specific interface mechanism without sacrificing conservation: the selected state still multiplies one connection flux which is added to one dual volume and subtracted from the other.

The upwind state is monotone under the returned multidimensional CFL bound. Using Upwind only on refinement-interface connections is not recommended: the isolated first-order closure creates a larger consistency error than a globally upwind discretization. Use one of the limited three-point schemes at refinement interfaces, and select a limited bulk scheme as well when monotonicity is more important than the lower dissipation of Lax–Wendroff.

The public scheme values are Upwind(), Centred(), LaxWendroff(), Fromm(), Minmod(), MonotonizedCentral(), VanLeer(), and Superbee(); Centered(), LW(), and MC() are aliases. For LW and the three-point schemes, convection! evaluates the effective split-step rate and advect! applies the split conservative update. A StaggeredVelocityAdvectionWorkspace updates both velocity components from the same state within each coordinate sweep.

Skew-symmetry invariant

Let D_c be the cell divergence and I_F the algebraic flux interpolation. The dual divergence satisfies

\[D_f I_F = I_C D_c.\]

When both scheme and amr_interface_scheme are centred, the integrated convection matrix satisfies the stronger identity

\[W C(u) + C(u)^T W = \operatorname{diag}(W I_C D_c u), \qquad 2\operatorname{diag}(C(u))=I_C D_c u.\]

The primary divergence is therefore recovered directly from the diagonal of convection, including at hanging faces. Summation by parts on a periodic mesh gives

\[\langle p,C(u)q\rangle_V = -\langle C(u)p,q\rangle_V \quad\text{when}\quad D_c u=0.\]

This identity gives exact semi-discrete momentum conservation and weighted skew-symmetry for a divergence-free carrier. It is a spatial property; a forward Euler step with the centred operator does not by itself preserve kinetic energy in time.

Current scope

The dual schedule supports uniform and 2:1-balanced mixed-level meshes with periodic or physical boundaries. A non-periodic side is impermeable: its normal velocity and advective momentum flux are zero, while its truncated dual volume is assembled explicitly. Viscous tangential wall data is handled separately by NoSlip() or FreeSlip(). Refinement subcycling and local time stepping remain future synchronization concerns; dynamic regridding currently uses one global time step and rebuilds the advection workspace after each accepted mesh change.

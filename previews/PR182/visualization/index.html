Recording and visualization

The numerical core records simulations without a graphics dependency:

  1. a VisualizationFieldRegistry names the fields to record;
  2. a Recorder, passed to simulate! as its observer, copies owned frames of those fields into a SnapshotStore;
  3. rasterize samples a stored Frame on a pixel grid, and a ViewCamera describes isotropic views.

Recording can be used headlessly, with the optional browser-based Studio, or with a Makie backend for static figures and animations. Consumers receive frames through bounded queues without making the solver wait for them; capturing frames still has a cost. The retention budget applies to previews, not lossless keyframes.

Recording

Registering fields

using TwoPhaseAMR

mesh = BlockTreeMesh(block_size=(16, 16))
velocity = FaceVelocity(mesh, (x, y, t, axis) ->
    axis == 1 ? sinpi(2x)*cospi(2y) : -cospi(2x)*sinpi(2y))
fields = VisualizationFieldRegistry()
register_default_fields!(fields, :velocity, velocity)

register_default_fields! accepts alternating names and sources. Cell fields and individual directional face fields produce one entry. A FaceVelocity or an (x_faces, y_faces) tuple produces four entries; for a source named :velocity, these are :velocity_x, :velocity_y, :velocity_magnitude, and :velocity_vorticity. Components and vorticity use the diverging :balance colormap, while scalar and magnitude fields use :viridis. Labels are derived from the names; units remain empty because the solver may be dimensional or nondimensional. register_default_fields!(fields, flow) registers the standard fields of a FlowSimulation, including its interface. Use register_field! when names, labels, units, colormaps, or color ranges need explicit control.

The registry accepts CellField, FaceField{1}, FaceField{2}, and a directional component of a FaceVelocity as scalar views. A two-component face field can be supplied either as a FaceVelocity or as an (x_faces, y_faces) tuple. Both representations support the cell-centred CentredMagnitude() and Vorticity() views:

x_faces = FaceField{1}(mesh, (x, y) -> 0.5 - y)
y_faces = FaceField{2}(mesh, (x, y) -> x - 0.5)

register_field!(fields, :x_component, x_faces; label="x component")
register_field!(fields, :magnitude, (x_faces, y_faces), CentredMagnitude())
register_field!(fields, :curl, (x_faces, y_faces), Vorticity())

Discontinuous phase quantities are registered as one field that holds both intrinsic values: PhaseScalar, PhaseFaceScalar, and intrinsic two-velocity fields. For a two-velocity field, pass the liquid/gas tuple together with its interface:

phase_fields = VisualizationFieldRegistry()
temperature = PhaseScalar(interface; liquid=300.0, gas=280.0)
register_field!(phase_fields, :temperature, temperature)
register_default_fields!(phase_fields, :velocity, (liquid_velocity, gas_velocity), interface)

Magnitude and vorticity of a phase velocity are computed within each phase, not from an already switched velocity. Registering a phase field also registers its interface, whose PLIC curve is drawn as an overlay; an embedded GeometryField is registered with its interface or with register_geometry!.

Recording a run

flow = FlowSimulation(mesh, SinglePhase(density=1.0, dynamic_viscosity=1e-3);
    velocity=(x, y, t, axis) -> axis == 1 ? sinpi(2y) : 0.0,
    diagnostics=(KineticEnergy(),))
fields = VisualizationFieldRegistry()
register_default_fields!(fields, flow)
recorder = Recorder(fields; diagnostics=flow, keyframes=EquispacedTimes(21))
result = simulate!(flow, 1.0; observer=recorder)

store = snapshot_store(recorder)
keyframes(store)              # FrameInfo of the never-dropped frames
frame = store[last(keyframes(store))]
frame[:velocity_vorticity]    # owned values, block-major cell order
newest_frame(store)           # most recently stored frame, or nothing when empty
frame_at(store, 0.5)          # stored frame nearest to t = 0.5

After completion or a requested stop, the same flow and recorder can continue to a later absolute time:

result = simulate!(flow, 2.0; observer=recorder)

The recorder appends frames and diagnostics to the same store, retaining the existing history. Step, periodic-time and wall-clock schedules retain their phase across continuations; unconsumed AtTimes targets remain absolute. EquispacedTimes distributes its count over each new call's interval.

A frame owns copies of every registered field, in native precision for keyframes, and stays valid after later mesh adaptation. Cell values are block-major, then j, then i (visualization_values order); face values list the interior faces of one direction, then its boundary faces. Phase fields return their liquid and gas vectors. frame.topology is a TopologySnapshot: the leaf-block table and staggered face tiles, shared by frames while the mesh topology and registered solid geometry are unchanged.

Keyframes are lossless and scheduled by keyframes: EquispacedTimes (a count including the initial and final time, except that a count of one requests only the final time), EveryTimeInterval (including both end times), explicit AtTimes, a step schedule (EveryNSteps, EveryStep), or nothing. By default the completed state nearest to each requested time is stored (the earlier one on ties); the requested times are listed in FrameInfo.requested_times. With exact_keyframes=true, the recorder advances an independent copy of the state from the previous step boundary to each requested time, without changing the simulation's steps or state. Exact keyframes require a fixed mesh and are rejected when the solver adapts its mesh. When fields are registered, the initial and final states are always stored.

Previews show progress between keyframes. They are throttled by preview (EveryWallInterval, EveryNSteps, EveryStep, EveryTimeInterval, or nothing for headless scripts). The recorder also throttles their capture to target max_capture_overhead (default 5 %) of the wall time between previews. They are stored in preview_precision (default Float32).

Retention limits preview memory. Retention(; memory=:auto, preview=:logarithmic, recent=64) keeps the newest recent previews at full density and thins older ones so that density halves each time their age doubles. memory=:auto is min(2 GiB, 20 % of physical memory). Over budget, previews are thinned evenly; keyframes are never dropped, and if they alone exceed the budget they are kept with one warning. The recorder logs a per-frame memory estimate when it first records. store_usage reports the frame counts, bytes, and dropped or skipped previews.

Diagnostics. With diagnostics=flow (or a DiagnosticCollection), the simulation-owned samples are mirrored at every step boundary, with vector values split into components; diagnostic_history(store, name) returns DiagnosticColumns. Each completed step is logged by step_log (time, step, time step, solver wall time, projection iterations and residuals, topology changes, and capture time).

Control. Controls are thread-safe and act at step boundaries. For example, with a fresh flow and recorder, request an initial pause before starting the solver on another task:

pause!(recorder)
task = Threads.@spawn simulate!(flow, 10.0; observer=recorder)
wait_paused(recorder)                     # wait until the initial state is paused
request_keyframe!(recorder)               # store the next step as :manual
resume!(recorder)
result = fetch(task)

While the run is active, request_stop!(recorder) ends it at the next step boundary with result.stopped == true. A breakpoint such as pause_when!(recorder, :kinetic_energy, e -> e < 0.1) pauses when a new sample first satisfies its predicate; resume the run with resume!(recorder). run_state(recorder) reports the lifecycle and run_events(snapshot_store(recorder)) lists pauses, breakpoints, stops, and failures. Errors after the initial state, for example in a breakpoint predicate, are logged and recorded as :failure events; the simulation continues. Configuration errors, such as a registry on another mesh, throw before the first step. subscribe(store; capacity) returns a bounded queue of the newly stored frames for consumers on other tasks: a consumer receives every frame it keeps up with, even previews that retention later thins, and a full queue drops (and counts) its oldest frame instead of waiting.

Custom observers subtype AbstractObserver and implement observe!, optionally stop_requested. A custom time loop can drive a recorder directly; the loop supplies the current time, step, and step size dt:

observe!(recorder, :initial, SimulationState(velocity, nothing, nothing, 0.0, 0),
    StepInfo(; dt=0.0, final_time=1.0))
# after every step:
observe!(recorder, :step, SimulationState(velocity, nothing, nothing, time, step),
    StepInfo(; dt, final_time=1.0))
observe!(recorder, :completed, SimulationState(velocity, nothing, nothing, time, step),
    StepInfo(; dt=0.0, final_time=1.0))

Studio

Studio is an optional subpackage with its own Julia environment and a committed browser bundle; normal use does not require Node.js. Its environment supplies TwoPhaseAMR, Studio, and CairoMakie. From the repository root, instantiate it once and start Julia in that environment:

julia --project=lib/TwoPhaseAMRStudio -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'
julia --project=lib/TwoPhaseAMRStudio -t 1,1

The second, interactive thread lets HTTP requests be serviced while compute threads are busy. Create a flow and recorder, then start Studio before running the simulation:

using TwoPhaseAMR, TwoPhaseAMRStudio

mesh = BlockTreeMesh(block_size=(16, 16))
flow = FlowSimulation(mesh, SinglePhase(density=1.0, dynamic_viscosity=1e-3);
    velocity=(x, y, t, axis) -> axis == 1 ? sinpi(2y) : 0.0,
    dt=0.01, diagnostics=(KineticEnergy(),))
fields = VisualizationFieldRegistry()
register_default_fields!(fields, flow)
recorder = Recorder(fields; diagnostics=flow, keyframes=EquispacedTimes(21))

driver = StudioDriver(flow, recorder)
app = studio(recorder; name="My run", open=:none)
url(app)                              # open this URL in a browser
result = simulate!(driver, 1.0)       # records through the bound recorder
# Inspect the recording and optionally continue the run in the browser:
close(app)

name labels the run without changing the recorder or solver. The default open=:browser opens a browser automatically; open=:none leaves opening the URL to the user, and open=:app requests a Chromium app window. The server continues to serve recorded history after the solver finishes until closed.

Construct the StudioDriver before starting Studio and use it for the initial simulate! call as shown. It owns the flow and recorder for subsequent Studio continuations; simulation keywords passed to StudioDriver are reused on each advance. A recorder started without a driver is still viewable and controllable while it runs, but Studio cannot continue it after it stops.

For a custom time loop, StudioDriver(advance!, recorder; time=t0) explicitly registers the continuation operation. advance!(target, recorder) must emit the recorder's initial, step, and completed observer events and return a result with time and stopped fields. This keeps a bare callback distinct from a run that has explicitly opted into continuation.

The dockable workspace opens field panels and diagnostics grouped by physical dimension, kind, and scale. Two-phase runs start with volume fraction and velocity magnitude; single-phase runs start with vorticity and pressure when those fields are registered. Use + Panel in the status bar to open a field, diagnostic, Run, or Frames panel, including a type that was closed. Drag a panel tab to move or dock it; Shift-drag it to float. The Layout menu restores the automatic arrangement, and clicking the run name opens its summary.

Field panels have a searchable field picker, cell/PLIC display, overlays, and a camera that keeps cells isotropic while using the available panel area. The Link view toggle shares pan and zoom among linked field panels on the same domain; linking is on by default. Toggle it off to navigate a panel independently; the button can also be focused and toggled with Space or Enter. Unless a field declares a fixed colour range, its default is a robust per-frame 99.5th-percentile range (symmetric for signed fields); global, full current-frame, and fixed ranges remain available. Colourbar endpoints use three significant figures; hover and click inspect the underlying cell or face values at higher precision. The pin control keeps a panel at its current frame while the shared timeline moves; unpin it to follow again. If retention later removes a pinned preview, its loaded field stays available, but another field that was not loaded for that frame may no longer be fetched. Live panels keep the last drawn frame and its colour range while newer data loads; they continue advancing even if previews arrive faster than the client can fetch them.

Drag to pan, use the wheel to zoom around the pointer, or Shift-drag a box to zoom to it. With a field plot focused, F fits the domain, 1 makes the finest cell eight pixels wide, C cycles colour-range policies, I toggles the interface, G block edges and E cell edges. Esc cancels box zoom. Two-phase panels also offer an interface-fit camera preset when interface geometry is available.

Diagnostic components are plotted together with a timeline cursor; time-axis tick precision adapts to the tick spacing. Hover to read visible series and click a chart to move the playhead. Click or use the keyboard on a legend entry to hide or show a component; hidden components are excluded from autoscaling. Compact diagnostic panels scroll as needed to keep legend controls usable. Each panel remembers its own visibility and linear or logarithmic y scale while scrubbing and updating live. Logarithmic plots omit zero and negative samples, leaving gaps. Symlog is not offered because diagnostic metadata does not provide a physically meaningful transition threshold.

Follow mode tracks the newest frame; stepping, scrubbing and playback enter a per-browser history mode without pausing recording. Preview frames appear as thin ticks, dots mark keyframes, R marks a change of stored topology, and separate markers show the playhead and live head. Hover over the timeline for the nearest frame number, time, and kind. Pause, resume, stop and request-keyframe controls apply at shared solver step boundaries and are disabled when the run is finished. A resumable run also offers Continue after completion or a requested stop: enter a later target time and start it. The suggested target is the actual end or stop time plus the original interval length. If the original interval had zero length, enter a later target manually. The same recording, diagnostics, panels, and timeline extend as it advances. The examples' --studio runs are resumable. A bare simulation callback without an explicit driver cannot be continued; this does not prevent using a closure to open Studio for an already driver-backed example.

The Export dialog is the only recording entry point. It renders a field panel and optional diagnostic groups using the server-side animation API and offers two sources; a recording uses exactly one source:

  • Stored frames samples stored frames on a uniform simulation-time grid. Frames selects the candidates (keyframes only or all stored frames) and the optional time window restricts them; without a window the whole recording is used. FPS (default 20) and Duration set the output frame count round(duration × fps) + 1, which includes both ends of the window. Duration starts at clamp(candidate frames / fps, 2, 20) seconds and stays editable; the dialog shows the resolved value. Each output frame shows the candidate frame nearest to its target time (ties go to the earlier frame), so a sparse recording repeats frames and a dense one skips them. The dialog shows the resulting output-frame count, distinct stored frames, uniform Δt, and simulation time per video second, and warns when a sparse recording repeats many frames. Stored jobs can be cancelled cooperatively.
  • Live stream records the frames the run produces from now until stopped, including previews that retention later thins. Simulation time per video second and FPS set the uniform output spacing Δt = sim_per_second / fps; output frame k shows the arrived frame nearest to t_start + k · Δt, using one frame of look-ahead, so video time also advances linearly in simulation time. Simulation time per video second starts at one tenth of the run's remaining simulation time (or of the stored frame span when the end is unknown) and stays editable. A sparse stream repeats the last frame and a dense one skips frames. The dialog shows Δt and the simulation time per video second; duration and frame count are known only when the recording stops.

Choose a camera, colour range, diagnostic arrangement (right or below), resolution, output file, and (for stored frames) a theme. Theme is stored-only: the live figure is created lazily on the encoder task, outside the caller's theme block. Width and height must be positive even pixel counts; the frame rate is a positive integer. The dialog reports progress and offers a download when a file completes. When diagnostics sit to the right in a fixed-size export, the requested camera view is fitted isotropically in the available field area; extra domain area may be visible if the output panel has a different aspect. Preview frame renders the stored frame the time line shows first for a stored source (or the selected panel's current frame for a live source) with the chosen settings. Preview and animation rendering cannot run concurrently. Exports honor the chosen diagnostic panel's hidden components and y scale. Live encoding uses a bounded queue and reports dropped frames. With no interactive thread and one compute thread, the live queue remains bounded and encoding is deferred until the solver is idle; frames lost while the queue is full are included in the dropped count. Recording state is server-owned and shared between connected browsers, independent of timeline navigation in each browser.

Client extensions can register dockable panel types through window.twoPhaseAMRStudio.registerPanel; the supplied client exposes getField for binary fields and getData for topology, phase planes, diagnostics, and the step log. Each request is matched to its response by ID.

Studio follows the browser's light/dark preference by default. The Theme menu selects System, Light, or Dark and persists the choice per browser. Client panels should use the same CSS variables instead of fixed colours; they change together when the theme changes:

PurposeCSS variables
Shell and dialogs--surface, --surface-raised, --text, --muted, --border, --focus, --shadow, --dialog-backdrop, --hover, --drop-overlay, --error, --status-running, --status-pending
Timeline--timeline-keyframe, --timeline-regrid, --timeline-playhead, --timeline-live-head, --timeline-preview
Field panels and colourbars--field-background, --field-foreground, --colorbar-text

Diagnostics--diagnostic-background, --diagnostic-text, --diagnostic-axis, --diagnostic-grid, --diagnostic-series-1, --diagnostic-series-2, --diagnostic-series-3

Interface segments instead use a black outline and white core, so they remain legible over any colormap in either theme.

The server listens on 127.0.0.1 and chooses an available port by default. Its URL contains a fresh random 128-bit token required for HTTP assets and WebSocket connections: treat the entire URL as a secret. It grants access to run data, solver controls, and recording output, so do not publish it in logs or issue reports. Binding another address is an explicit host choice. For remote use, keep the default and forward the port over SSH (ssh -N -L local_port:127.0.0.1:server_port host), then open http://127.0.0.1:local_port/?token=... with the same token. Closing Studio does not stop the simulation.

Automatic browser launch uses an owner-only temporary redirect file rather than placing the tokenized URL in process arguments; the file is removed when the Studio server closes.

Studio protocol v1

Studio uses a single authenticated WebSocket at /ws?token=…. JSON messages have a type. The server sends hello (protocol version, run and frame metadata), frame, frames, state, and error. Clients send control with pause, resume, stop, keyframe, or continue; get with frames, field, topology, planes, diagnostics, or steplog; and recording with start, stop, export, cancel, or preview. A field request specifies the stored frame ID, field name, request ID, encoding, and, for two-phase values, liquid or gas. It returns a whole-field binary message; it does not imply a change to the viewer's playhead.

The initial hello has protocol: 1, a runs array, a frames array, and a recording object. Run metadata includes an ID, name, domain, block size, initial/final times, fields, and diagnostics. Diagnostic metadata includes label, units, component names, kind, physical dimension and scale. The client groups series by (kind, dimension, scale), with separate groups for unknown dimensions unless matching units are supplied. Frame metadata contains id, time, step, kind, topology, and field statistics. state messages carry run_state, current time, t_final, step, dt, store_bytes, store_budget, frame_count, steps_per_second, stopped, resumable, suggested_final_time, and the shared recording status. A resumable completed or stopped run can be advanced with {"type":"control","action":"continue","final_time":2.0}; the target must be finite and later than the current time. The rate is the reciprocal mean wall time over up to 20 recent completed steps, or null before a step is available. t_final is null before a target is available and otherwise retains the current or most recently requested target, including after completion or stop. stopped distinguishes a requested stop from natural completion; run_state remains completed in both cases. A frame announces a committed frame; request frames to reconcile promotions and previews removed by retention.

For example, {"type":"control","action":"pause"} requests a cooperative pause and {"type":"get","what":"field","frame":4,"field":"pressure", "encoding":"f32","request":7} requests one complete field. The request ID in its binary reply is 7. A two-phase field additionally specifies "phase":"liquid" or "phase":"gas". Clients must check the version and message length before interpreting a reply.

Recording is a server-owned session, for example:

{"type":"recording","action":"start","source":"live","path":"run.mp4","field":"pressure","diagnostics":[]}
{"type":"recording","action":"stop"}

A stored export is one-shot: {"type":"recording","action":"export","source":"stored",...}; cancel it with {"type":"recording","action":"cancel"}. Stored options include frames (keyframes or all), window, fps, and duration; live options include fps, sim_per_second, and the queue capacity. The status reports active, source, field, path, status, encoded, dropped, total, progress, url, times (the simulation time of every live output frame, once completed), and any error. Stored progress counts output frames submitted to the encoder and can reach 1 before file finalization. A cancellation request transitions through cancelling to cancelled; completion may win the final commit race. Only completed exposes the token-authenticated download URL. A schedule action reports the time-linear plan without starting a job. Navigating the timeline sends none of these commands.

Both stored and live commands may include diagnostic_options, an array of {"names":["kinetic_energy"],"scale":"log","hidden":[{"name":"kinetic_energy","component":1}]} objects. names identifies a complete selected diagnostic group, and components are one-based. The scale is linear or log; logarithmic plots omit non-positive values. A stored preview command accepts the export settings plus a request ID and stored frame ID, but no output path is required. It replies with recording_preview, the same request ID, and a token-authenticated relative PNG URL, without changing the animation job.

The optional mode (cell or plic) is a validated display hint; it does not transform the numeric payload. A phase field is requested separately for each intrinsic phase. get requests for planes provide majority masks and PLIC data, and topology provides block geometry, face-tile mappings and solids, so the client can perform either display.

Binary messages have a 32-byte little-endian header followed by packed values:

Byte offsetTypeMeaning
04 bytesASCII TPAS magic
4uint16protocol version (1)
6uint16kind (1 = field)
8uint32client request ID
12uint8data type (1 = float32, 2 = uint16)

Float32 payloads are direct, including nonfinite values. A uint16 payload decodes as offset + scale * value; 0xffff represents missing data and decodes to NaN. All nonfinite inputs use this sentinel in the quantized encoding. The complete field is transferred in the ordering of its Frame; the renderer can build on this without changing the recording.

Static and export

Phase display

Whether a phase field is shown by majority cells or sharply at the PLIC plane is a drawing choice, not a recording one. With phase_rendering=:cell (the default), each cell shows the value of its majority phase: liquid when its liquid volume fraction is at least 50 %, gas otherwise, with no blending. For embedded geometry, the fraction is relative to the open fluid volume and fully solid cells have no value. Face phase fields keep their staggered dual cells, and each part of a dual cell takes the majority phase of the cell it lies in. With phase_rendering=:plic, mixed cells are split at their PLIC plane and the solid part of cut cells is empty; staggered fields use the plane of the cell containing the point.

Rasterizing frames and cameras

rasterize(frame, field, (xmin, xmax, ymin, ymax), (w, h); phase_rendering) returns a w × h Matrix{Float32} of values at pixel centres (NaN outside the domain or without a value). Cell fields are piecewise constant; a face value fills its staggered dual cell, the half cells on both sides of its face, and each of the two fine faces of a coarse–fine interface fills its half of the coarse cell. Periodic seams need no special treatment. locate returns the cell containing a point; locate_face finds the staggered face associated with a point and axis and returns a FaceRef, or nothing outside the domain or where no face exists:

cell = locate(frame, (0.25, 0.5))
face = locate_face(frame, (0.25, 0.5), 1)  # x-directed face

face.boundary selects the boundary rather than interior face schedule, and face.index is an index in that global schedule, not an index into a frame's per-axis field-value vector.

A ViewCamera is a centre and one isotropic scale (world units per pixel), so a view always fills its panel and cells stay square. fit_camera shows a whole domain, zoom keeps the point under the cursor fixed, zoom_to_box shows a box completely, and visible_rect returns the rectangle to rasterize.

Static figures and animations

Load CairoMakie for figures, GIFs, and videos, including in headless scripts. It is a separate dependency from the numerical core; the Studio environment above and the repository's examples environment already include it. In a different active environment, install CairoMakie there before importing it. Two functions cover the common cases:

using TwoPhaseAMR, CairoMakie
store = snapshot_store(recorder)
export_figure("vorticity.png", store; field=:velocity_vorticity,
    at=[0.0, 0.5, 1.0], blocks=true)
export_animation("vorticity.gif", store; field=:velocity_vorticity,
    diagnostics=[:kinetic_energy], fps=20, duration=2.0)

export_figure draws one panel per requested time (the stored frame nearest to it; by default the latest frame), and also accepts a Frame or a registry, whose current state is captured. export_animation builds the figure once and swaps the frames through an Observable. Panels and frames share one view, colour range, and colour bar:

  • the view is camera=:fit (the domain) or a rectangle (xmin, xmax, ymin, ymax), for example a zoom window; field panels take the aspect of the view, so the view fills them exactly and cells stay square;
  • the colour range is colorrange if given, else the registered range, else the range of the field over all shown frames, symmetric about zero for vorticity and diverging colormaps such as :balance.

With diagnostics (names or :all), each diagnostic plot has a time cursor at the shown frame. Choose arrangement=:right or :below; the default :auto uses the right for a single field and below for several. title is automatic (t = …), nothing, a string, or a function of the frame.

interface, geometry, and blocks select the overlays, and interfacecolor (default :black) and blockcolor (default (:gray40, 0.8)) colour the interface and block-outline lines. Exports follow the active Makie theme, and the scale bar takes the axis text colour on a contrasting backdrop. A dark variant, for example of a README animation that follows the reader's colour scheme, combines a dark theme with light text, a diverging colormap whose neutral is dark, and light lines:

with_theme(theme_dark(); textcolor=:gray85) do
    export_animation("vorticity_dark.gif", store; field=:velocity_vorticity,
        colormap=:berlin, blocks=true, interfacecolor=:white,
        blockcolor=(:white, 0.2))
end

Stored or live animations

An animation shows either stored or live frames, never a mix. Both sources play time linearly in simulation time; neither maps video frames one-to-one to stored frames. The default source=:stored samples the store after (or during) the run. frames=:keyframes (default) or :all selects the candidates and window=(t0, t1) restricts them; without a window the whole recording is used. fps (default 20) and duration set the output count to round(fps × duration) + 1, capped at 100 000 frames; without duration it is clamp(candidate count / fps, 2, 20) seconds. Give nframes instead to set the count directly. Output times are uniform over the window and each frame shows the candidate nearest to its target time, with ties going to the earlier frame. The automatic title is the simulation time of the displayed frame. Stored exports accept a progress(i,n) callback called after each output frame is submitted and a zero-argument cancel() predicate checked between frames and around finalization. A cancelled export returns nothing, removes its temporary output, and preserves any existing destination; a successful export returns its path. Cancellation is cooperative.

source=:live records the frames of a recorder as the run produces them, previews and keyframes alike, including previews that retention later thins. Its uniform output spacing is Δt = sim_per_second / fps (by default one tenth of the run's remaining simulation time, or of the stored frame span, per second of video): output frame k shows the arrived frame nearest to t_start + k · Δt, where t_start is the simulation time at recording start (the last completed step, seeded with the newest stored frame). A frame that arrives decides every output time up to its own time, so only one frame of look-ahead is needed; sparse streams repeat frames and dense streams skip them. On close, the last arrived frame is held through the simulation time at stop, which is frozen when the recording stops (before the queue drains) so steps taken while it drains are not covered; recording.stop_time reports it. For example, continue the flow to a later time with a fresh recorder:

recorder = Recorder(fields; diagnostics=flow, preview=EveryNSteps(5))
recording = export_animation("live.mp4", recorder; field=:velocity_vorticity,
    source=:live, diagnostics=[:kinetic_energy], fps=20, sim_per_second=1.0)
simulate!(flow, 2.0; observer=recorder)
close(recording)    # flushes the time line and writes live.mp4
recording.encoded, recording.dropped, recording.ids, recording.times

Live encoding does not make the solver wait for the encoder. It receives frames through a subscribe queue of capacity frames (default 32) on a thread other than the solver's. If the encoder falls behind, frames arriving at a full queue are dropped and counted, and close reports the counts. Frame capture and encoding still consume computational resources. The recording is independent of any viewer: reading the store, viewing old frames, or scrubbing history from other tasks does not change it. Without a second thread (Julia started with -t 1 and no interactive thread), frames are queued during the run and encoded after it. Without a registered or given colour range, a live recording uses the range of the frames recorded so far, and its diagnostics grow with the run.

Building blocks

For custom layouts, compose the building blocks:

fig = Figure(size=(1000, 420))
ax = fieldaxis(fig[1, 1]; camera=(0.0, 1.0, 0.1, 0.4))
frame = Observable(store[first(keyframes(store))])
plot = amrfield!(ax, frame, :velocity_vorticity; blocks=true)
Colorbar(fig[1, 2], plot)
diagnosticsgrid!(fig[1, 3], store; names=[:kinetic_energy],
    cursor=lift(f -> f.time, frame))
save("panel.png", fig)

fieldaxis creates an axis whose limits always fill its panel with one isotropic scale: a box zoom on a wide panel shows more of the domain instead of empty space. camera is :fit, a rectangle, a domain, or a ViewCamera (centre and world units per pixel). Field axes hide ticks and draw a scale bar by default (decorations=:minimal); :full shows coordinates. amrfield! draws a field of a frame, an Observable of frames, or a registry: it rasterizes the visible part of the domain at the panel's resolution whenever the frame, the limits, or the panel size change, and draws PLIC interfaces, embedded solids, and optionally block outlines as vector overlays. Rasterized fields keep vector output small and scale to large meshes.

diagnosticsgrid! draws the diagnostics of a store, recorder, FlowSimulation, or DiagnosticCollection in one axis per group of diagnostic_groups: diagnostics with the same kind, physical dimension, and scale share an axis, vector diagnostics are split into components, and defects use a logarithmic axis. references adds dashed reference curves from functions of time or other diagnostics, transform=:relative plots changes relative to the first sample, and until (a time or an Observable of times) shows only the samples up to that time, reading a live store again whenever it changes.

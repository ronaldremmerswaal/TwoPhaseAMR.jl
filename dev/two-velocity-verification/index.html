Two-velocity verification

The two-velocity verification suite, benchmark/two_velocity/, compares the two-velocity pressure projections on problems with exact or reference answers and tracks the progress on the small phase values of the mimetic projection. It measures and reports; the contracts of the package stay in test/.

Running the suite

From the repository root, once per checkout:

julia --project=benchmark -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.develop(path="."); Pkg.instantiate()'

then

julia --project=benchmark -t 1 benchmark/two_velocity/run.jl --tier=1 --compare
julia --project=benchmark -t 1 benchmark/two_velocity/run.jl --tier=2,3 --methods=mimetic,cmdgfm --compare

The run writes results.toml and results.md (all values, with observed orders) to benchmark/two_velocity/output/. --compare judges every metric against benchmark/two_velocity/baseline.toml: worse when it moved in the wrong direction by more than max(rtol |baseline|, atol), better when it moved the other way by as much. The relative tolerance is 1e-3 in tier 1 (single projections, deterministic up to the projection tolerance), 2e-2 in tier 2 and 0.25 in tier 3 (the impact is singular); exact cases carry an absolute tolerance at their solver level. Orders, counts and step numbers are reported only. The run fails (exit status 1) when a metric is worse, a measured metric of a case that ran is missing, or a case failed (an exception or a projection that did not converge). --record writes the run's tiers and methods into the baseline and --update-docs regenerates the tables below from it; re-record only for an intentional change and explain it in the pull request. TWO_VELOCITY_PERTURB=1 loosens the projection tolerance to 1e-3 to show the failure path. Further options: --methods, --resolutions, --impact, --output (see the header of run.jl and benchmark/README.md).

Tier 1: single projections

One projection with Δt = 1 per case and method (tolerance 1e-8 on Δt max|div u_mix|), on the package geometry (PLIC apertures and staggered fractions) of a uniform n × n mesh of the unit square, periodic in x, walls at y = 0 and y = 1, liquid below the graph y = η(x) or inside the circle. The interface lies off the grid lines (0.37h above, circle centres shifted by (0.21h, 0.37h)). These are the laboratory tests T1–T7 of the mimetic-projection audit, run with every two-velocity method. Norms follow the audit: the rms over the mixed faces is weighted by the relative mass K = m_l m_g/(m_l + m_g) (m_π the phase mass of the staggered volume).

  • T1 continuous shear. u = ½ tanh((y − ½)/0.05) e_x in both phases is single valued and solenoidal: the projection must keep it and create no phase-velocity jump. Flat, cosine (a = 0.01) and triangle (slopes 0.06 and 0.3) interfaces, DR 1 and 1000.
  • T2 vortex sheet (liquid −½, gas +½ in x) and T3 gravity (both phases −1 in y, g Δt = 1) over the wavy interface y = ½ + 0.37h + ε cos 2πx, ε = 0.005, against the exact first-order per-phase fields (harmonic pressures, extended analytically beyond the interface): the jump error divided by U ε k (g Δt ε k), split into faces with both values wetted and faces with a value without aperture, and each value against its own phase's exact field. T3 at DR 1 is exact rest.
  • T4 sheet over tilted and curved interfaces (triangle slopes 0.3 and 1, circle R = 0.25, DR 1): the projected jump must be the tangential part J − n(n·J) of the sheet jump J; ±0.1 around the kinks are excluded.
  • T5 single-valued gradient predictor u* = −Gφ, φ = cos 2πx cos πy (DR 1): exact answer rest.
  • T6 exact two-phase gradient ∇p_π/ρ_π of harmonic pressures that are continuous with a continuous normal flux at a flat interface: exact answer rest.
  • T7 manufactured Poisson problem below, with walls (predictor ∇p_π/ρ_π, exact answer rest and p; DR 1 and 1000) and periodic in both directions with the divergence-free rotation of the former gallery example 12 (DR 1000).

T7: manufactured pressure with a gradient jump

This is the former gallery example 12. Let r² = (x − ½)² + (y − ½)², liquid inside the circle r < 0.3. The pressure is continuous at the interface but has a phase-dependent gradient:

\[p_*(x,y)=\sin(2\pi x)+\cos(2\pi y) +40\,(r^2-0.3^2)(x-y)\,\mathbf 1_{r<0.3}.\]

Equal x/y amplitudes make the normal and tangential surface-rms parts of the scaled jump ξ = [∇p/ρ] nearly equal on the circle (the suite checks their ratio, 0.8–1.25). In the periodic case the predictor is the divergence-free liquid rotation u = 2 cos(2πr²) (y − ½, −(x − ½)) (gas at rest) plus ∇p_*/ρ, so one projection with Δt = 1 should return the rotation and p_*. Periodic pressure has an arbitrary constant: the pressure errors subtract its volume-weighted mean. The phase-gradient errors are errors of the physical gradients realised by the projection, ρ_π (u*_π − u⁺_π)/Δt (rms weighted by the phase fraction of the dual volume; the maximum excludes nearly absent phases), and xi is the error of their scaled jump on the mixed faces. At n = 32 the published rows give the numbers the example stated (pressure rms/maximum 1.04/8.99, phase gradient 90.5/5305).

Reading the tier-1 table

Exact by construction ([E]), at round-off or at the projection tolerance:

  • MM keeps the shear of T1 exactly: a single-valued solenoidal field is a fixed point of a projection orthogonal in the metric 𝓜.
  • CMDGFM keeps the T1 shear over the flat interface (CMDGFM-L imposes the increment of the own jump on an exactly tangential face, CMDGFM-C gives such a face no row, so it keeps its jump) and removes the single-valued gradient of T5 to round-off.
  • T3 at DR 1 is at rest to the projection tolerance for every method.

Not convergent, or first order only, by construction (method limitations, not regressions); the brackets name the marks of the table:

  • [C1] CMDGFM-C infers each phase from one donor face, the two-point difference ±2hU' of a resolved shear (issue #171): T1 converges at first order, close to 20h for this shear (DR 1 cosine 0.53 / 0.30 / 0.16).
  • [C2] CMDGFM-C leaves the mixed faces without a regular row at their predictor jump (ConstantJumpInference). In T6 these are the cut faces parallel to the flat interface, and they carry all of the remaining jump: the ratio stays at 0.61 at DR 1000 for every n. The plateaus of T4 and T7 come partly from such faces and partly from the one-donor rows.
  • [L] CMDGFM-L falls back to the one-donor rule where the crossing-speed limiter sees the shear cross a steep interface (T1 triangle s = 0.3, first order, 0.60 / 0.29 / 0.171), and its β = 0 rows on faces with |n_t| > |n_a| keep the donors' jump on the slope-1 triangle of T4. Both are pinned as @test_broken in test/two_phase/two_velocity/projection/cmdgfm_jump_inference.jl (issue #171).
  • [M] MM leaves an O(h) jump in T5–T7: the sampled gradient of a potential is not a gradient of the mimetic metric in cut cells.
  • [G] T2 and T3 rise from n = 64 to n = 128 (T3 at DR 1000 for every method). This is a change of geometry, not of the solve: ε = 0.64h at n = 128, so the crests rise 0.37h + 0.64h = 1.01h above a grid line and the troughs 0.27h below the one beneath, leaving slivers of one phase in the neighbouring cell rows (at n = 32, 64 the wave stays within one row). A few sliver faces carry most of the K-weighted error. T3: CMDGFM-L two crest faces of liquid fraction 0.016 and 0.002 (error 4.2–4.5, weight K 0.93 and 0.67), MM two crest faces with error 1.78 (68 % of Σ K e²); T2 (CMDGFM-C): faces of liquid fraction 0.002 on the crossed grid lines. The T3 metrics agree to eight digits between projection tolerances 1e-7 and 1e-8 and to four digits down to 1e-11; T3 at DR 1000 uses 1e-7, because the round-off floor of Δt max|div u_mix| under the hydrostatic pressure ρ_l g y reaches 3e-9 (MM) to 5e-8 (CMDGFM) at n = 128.
  • [?] Observed, cause not established here: CMDGFM-L's T7 ratios with walls and the scaled jump xi and the largest phase-gradient error of the periodic T7 case for both CMDGFM rules do not decrease with n. CMDGFM-L's T4 circle error converges at order 0.4 only.

Tier 2: short flows

Each method, and the one-velocity model as the reference, runs:

  • hydrostatic rest: a flat pool at y = ½ + h/3 under g = 1, DR 1000, both label orders, 50 steps of Δt = 1e-3 (exact rest);
  • static drop: inviscid drop R = 0.2, σ = 1 (CGFM), free-slip box, n = 32, t = 0.4 with simulate!, DR 1 and 1000, both label orders: late raw and resolved spurious currents (resolved: mixed faces with phase aperture > 0.05) and the energy behaviour (largest kinetic energy, and that of the last third over the first third);
  • capillary wave against Prosperetti's solution: the standing wave of the integration test (λ = 1, a₀ = λ/100, La 3000), a quarter period, n = 16, 32, 64, DR 1 and 1000;
  • Kelvin–Helmholtz linear growth (two-velocity methods only: one velocity field cannot carry the sheet's velocity jump, its error is 7.7): the inviscid sheet of the integration test (DR 1000, We 0.5, 32 cells per wavelength) against linear theory, as an ensemble: the interface on a grid line with the amplitude scaled by 1, 1 ± 0.0025 and 1 ± 0.005 (the noise of the error), shifted by h/3, a four times larger amplitude, and the flow moving at U = 2 (Galilean);
  • translation: a sheet translated normally at speed 1 with tangential streams ∓½ (an exact solution), DR 1000, a resolved layer and a film of 1.5 cells, until t = 2h;
  • label symmetry: a static drop and a capillary wave stepped eight times with the phase labels exchanged (level set, densities and viscosities): the values must exchange (issue #200).

Tier 3: drop impact

A drop of radius 0.08 falls at V = 2 onto a pool of depth 0.3 in the unit box (ρ = (1000, 1), μ = (0.32, 0.0064), σ = 6.4 CGFM, g = 9.81, interphase drag, free slip) until t = 0.12, with simulate! and its step controller (CFL 0.45); members 1–5 shift drop and pool by fractions of a cell of the n = 32 mesh. Inviscid impact is singular, so the maxima are compared with a loose tolerance. Raw maxima take every present value; resolved maxima the values with phase fraction at least ½ and a positive aperture. The gas values are classified by their own aperture a: slaved (a = 0), small (0 < a ≤ 0.05, which contains the bottleneck apertures a ≈ √(ρ_g/ρ_l) of the MM spikes) and regular. The anatomy columns give the aperture, fraction and time of the value of the raw gas peak, the mean number of slaved and small gas values per step, and the steps in which such a value exceeds 5V.

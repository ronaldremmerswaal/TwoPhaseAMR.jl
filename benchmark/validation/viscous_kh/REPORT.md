# Viscous Kelvin–Helmholtz instability under mesh refinement: one- vs two-velocity model

Setup, equations, reference solver and measures are described in [README.md](README.md);
all numbers below are from `results/tables.md` (regenerated by `analyze.jl`).

## 1. Question

The two-velocity model represents an inviscid vortex sheet sharply and is designed to
reduce to the one-velocity model when the shear layer is resolved. Does it model an
*unresolved* viscous shear layer ($\delta\ll h$) better than the one-velocity model,
and how do both converge once the layer is resolved ($\delta\gg h$)?

## 2. What the thesis says

The thesis motivates the two-velocity model by the cost of resolving the interfacial
shear layer ($\delta_{SL}\approx 4.91\sqrt{\mu_g\tau/(\rho_gU_\tau)}$, at least four
times thinner than the most unstable KH wavelength, ch. 7 introduction) and replaces
the unresolved layer by a tangential velocity jump. The viscous step uses the
equivalent one-fluid velocity "to obtain the one-velocity solution under refinement";
the jump was observed to vanish under refinement for a third-order Stokes wave
(Re = 4·10⁴, resolved layer). The conclusions explicitly leave open "how well the
subgrid viscous stresses are actually modelled" and recommend the viscous KH problem
with Orr–Sommerfeld reference solutions (Boomkamp 1997; Bagué et al. 2010) — this study.

## 3. Reference and its verification

Two-fluid Orr–Sommerfeld problem for the diffusing composite error-function layer
(Yecko et al. 2002; Boeck & Zaleski 2005; Bagué et al. 2010), Chebyshev collocation per
layer (Boomkamp et al. 1997). Because the layer thickens during the runs (quasi-steady
growth rates drift by 2 % (thin) to 40 % (thick)), the reference is the **linear
initial-value problem** started from the $t=0$ eigenmode — the simulations start from
the same eigenmode, so $\hat\eta(t)$ is compared directly. No body force is used to
maintain the base flow (it could not be represented when $\delta\ll h$).

Study references (`results/reference_summary.txt`): time-step error < 10⁻⁷, collocation

drive is amplified in the growth rate; the IVP growth fitted over [T/3, T] is 1.750
(r1_thin) and 1.036 (r10_thin).

**Unresolved regime (δ/h ≲ ½).** The one-velocity model does not carry a vortex
sheet: the jump is spread over one cell row, and at ρ ratio 1 with We = 2 this
discrete layer is *stable* — the perturbation oscillates and decays (λ/h = 16–64,
amplitude error ≈ 100 %). At ρ ratio 10 it is the opposite: the one-velocity model
grows much too fast (|η̂(T)| up to 6× the reference). The two-velocity model keeps a
sharp jump and grows at a rate between the viscous OS rate and the vortex-sheet rate:
growth error 2–14 % (ρ = 1) and 6–28 % (ρ = 10), i.e. it is the only one of the two
that predicts the instability qualitatively and, mostly, quantitatively on meshes
that do not resolve the layer. Its error is bounded below by the physical difference
between a sheet and the thin viscous layer (here ≈ 25 % in growth rate, see §3),
which no sharp model without a subgrid stress can remove.

**Resolved regime (δ/h ≳ 1).** The one-velocity model converges (ρ = 1: growth error
10 % → 3 %, |η̂(T)| error 1.5 %, 1.4 % at δ/h = 1, 2; the profile error decreases at
order ≈ 1.4). The two-velocity model is *less* accurate here (growth error 45 %, 17 %;
ρ = 10: 24 % at δ/h = 1) because it carries a spurious tangential jump on the cut faces
(0.70ΔU at δ₀/h = 1, 0.39ΔU at 2; visible in `profile_r1_thin.png` as the gap at
y = 0), which converges only at first order in h (§5). The profiles away from the
interface converge for both models.

**Cost.** Per step the two-velocity model costs 1.1–1.5× the one-velocity model
(e.g. 195 s vs 154 s at λ/h = 256, 3072 steps, 11 776 cells); steps are set by the
capillary limit dt ∝ h^{3/2} for both. Per wall-clock second (`efficiency.png`) the
two-velocity model is far more accurate on unresolved meshes (ρ = 1, λ/h = 64: 8.8 %
amplitude error in 8.7 s vs 87 % for the one-velocity model in 5.5 s), the
one-velocity model is more accurate once δ/h ≥ 1 (1.5 % in 24 s at λ/h = 128 vs 29 %
in 195 s for the two-velocity model at λ/h = 256).

## 5. Defect found: spurious tangential jump from a continuous velocity

`jump_reproducer.jl` (uniform mesh, ρ and μ equal, σ = 0, interface y = 0.01 cos 2πx,
*both* phases initialised with the same resolved profile u = ½tanh(y/δ), δ = 0.05):

| δ/h | step 1 | step 10 | step 40 | step 160 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.6 | 0.528 | 0.496 | 0.472 | 0.290 |
| 3.2 | 0.302 | 0.285 | 0.272 | 0.235 |
| 6.4 | 0.158 | 0.152 | 0.140 | 0.123 |

max|u_g − u_l| appears in one step and is O(h|U′|) (first order). Mechanism (code
reading): a newly cut face is seeded by `_extend_phase_face_values!`
(`src/two_phase/two_velocity/velocity_fields.jl:76-105`) from same-phase neighbours,
i.e. from the row across the interface; the CTU partial-flux update for minority
fractions (`src/two_phase/phase_face_transport.jl:193, 356-360`) divides by the small
predicted phase fraction and keeps the minority velocity at the neighbour-row value;
the viscous step adds one common increment to both phases (`two_velocity/stepping.jl:
119-153`) and CMDGFM only removes normal jumps, so nothing relaxes u_g − u_l. In a
resolved layer the model therefore does not reduce to the one-velocity model faster
than O(h), which explains its poorer resolved-regime accuracy.

## 6. CTU threshold (exploratory)

`ctu_threshold/` tests whether the jump comes from the first-order CTU (upwind) path
used whenever a dual face has a predicted phase fraction below `ctu_threshold`
(default 0.5): thresholds {0.5, 0.1, 0.01, 10⁻¹²} × bulk schemes {Upwind,
LaxWendroff, MC} on the reproducer (jump after 1 and 160 steps, observed order, bound
and failure checks), and the r1_thin two-velocity KH runs at λ/h = 64, 128, 256 with MC
at θ = 0.5 and 10⁻¹². The threshold is not reachable from the public options; the
scripts rebuild the WY workspace with the public constructor
`WYAdvectionWorkspace(interface; ctu_threshold)` and store it in the mutable flow
workspace (`set_ctu_threshold!` in `kh_run.jl`). The study meshes have no hanging
faces near the interface (reproducer: uniform; KH: level transitions at |y| ≥ λ/32).
Results: `ctu_threshold/results.md`, `ctu_threshold/jump_vs_h.png` (produced by the
batch, see §8). **Future work (owner):** a robust scheme needs a high-order extension
of CTU — high order wherever possible as long as boundedness is guaranteed.

## 7. Conclusions

* **Unresolved shear layer (δ ≪ h):** the two-velocity model is clearly better. The
  one-velocity model's one-cell numerical layer changes the physics qualitatively
  (stabilised at ρ ratio 1, strongly over-amplified at ρ ratio 10); the two-velocity
  model reproduces the growth to 2–28 % and the instability is present on every mesh.
  Its remaining error is the sheet-vs-layer difference, which a subgrid tangential
  stress would have to supply.
* **Resolved shear layer (δ ≳ h):** the one-velocity model converges (≈ 3 % growth,
  1.4 % amplitude at δ/h = 2 for ρ = 1) and is more accurate per cell and per second.
  The two-velocity model converges more slowly because of the spurious O(h|U′|)
  cut-face jump (§5); the thesis' expectation that it "reduces to the one-velocity
  model" holds only asymptotically, at first order.
* Crossover: around δ/h ≈ ½–1 for both density ratios.

## 8. Status and limitations

* Results above: thin cases at λ/h = 16–256 (r10_thin: 32 pending). Queued in the
  detached batch `results/logs/chain.sh` (≈ 4 h, commits automatically when done):
  λ/h = 512 for both thin cases with field output (→ `figures/fields_r1_thin.png`), the
  thick cases (Re_δ = 100, δ₀ = λ/32, λ/h = 16–256, δ₀/h up to 8), the uniform-mesh
  and half-amplitude checks, and the CTU study. Tables/figures regenerated by the batch
  were not inspected by me; numbers in §4 may change slightly at the margins but the
  rows shown are final for those resolutions.
* Time integration of TwoPhaseAMR is first order and dt ∝ h^{3/2} (capillary), so
  O(h^{1.5}) is the best expected order.
* Phase speed at ρ ratio 1 is zero by symmetry (reproduced to < 10⁻⁵ except by the
  decaying one-velocity runs at λ/h = 16, 32).
* Linear regime: the reference amplitude at T is 2.6·10⁻³λ (kA ≈ 0.017); the
  one-velocity runs at ρ ratio 10 reach kA ≈ 0.09 and may contain nonlinear effects.
  The half-amplitude check (batch) quantifies the residual nonlinearity.
* Reference uncertainty: ≤ 4·10⁻⁴ (r10_thin), well below all simulation errors.
* One mode, one Weber number (We = 2) and two Reynolds numbers; the sensitivity of the
  sheet-vs-layer difference to We is large (§3) and not explored.

\chapter{Interface representation, reconstruction and curvature}\label{ch:interface}
\chapterstatus[e805528147163a1c2fad4bbc5a5bf9d9106db836]{reference}
The interface between liquid and gas is represented by cell-averaged volume
fractions; its geometry is reconstructed from them, cell by cell, as one
straight line (PLIC) together with a curvature estimate from height
functions. This chapter states how the fractions are defined and stored, how
the line, its normal and its plane constant are computed, how the curvature and
its fallbacks are chosen, how contact angles enter, and how fractions are
initialised. It follows the thesis' interface description~\thesis{\S2.3},
reconstruction~\thesis{\S3.3} and curvature~\thesis{\S4.7} and states where the
code differs. Reconstruction is an algebraic map from fractions to geometry;
time discretisation is not applicable, and \cref{sec:interface:time} states
when the map is evaluated. Transport of the fractions is the subject of
\cref{ch:vof-transport}; the embedded solid of \cref{ch:cutcells} enters
through the open cell parts.

\section{Continuous description}\label{sec:interface:continuous}
The open part $\domain^{\open}$ of the domain (\cref{sec:setting:continuous})
is split into the liquid region $\domain^{\liq}(\timevar)$ and the gas region
$\domain^{\gas}(\timevar)=\domain^{\open}\setminus\domain^{\liq}(\timevar)$;
the interface $\interface(\timevar)$ is their common boundary. Its unit normal
$\inormal$ points into the gas and its curvature is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:curvature}
  \curv=\divergence\inormal ,
\end{equation}
so a convex liquid drop has positive curvature~\thesis{eq.~(2.6)}. Where
$\interface$ is locally a graph over the coordinate $\coordk{t}$ with height in
the direction $k\neq t$, $\coordk{k}=\hfun{\pm k}(\coordk{t})$, and the normal
has a positive ($+$) or negative ($-$) $\unitvec{k}$-component~\thesis{\S2.3.1},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:graph}
  \inormal=\frac{\pm\unitvec{k}\mp\hfun{\prime}\,\unitvec{t}}{\sqrt{1+(\hfun{\prime})^2}},
  \qquad
  \curv=\mp\frac{\hfun{\prime\prime}}{\roundpar{1+(\hfun{\prime})^2}^{3/2}}
\end{equation}
(\thesis{eqs.~(3.60) and (4.92)} in two dimensions; see
\cref{dev:interface:signs} for the signs). Where $\interface$ meets a wall, the
static contact-angle condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:contact}
  \inormal\cdot\wallnormal=\cos\cangle,\qquad 0<\cangle<\pi,
\end{equation}
holds, with $\wallnormal$ the unit wall normal pointing into the fluid and
$\cangle$ measured in the liquid~\thesis{eq.~(2.8)}. The code imposes
\cref{eq:interface:contact} only through the curvature
(\cref{sec:interface:contact}), never through the reconstructed line. A level
set $\lset$ with $\interface=\set{\coord}{\lset(\coord)=0}$ and liquid where
$\lset\le0$ is used only to initialise the fractions
(\cref{sec:interface:init}); it is not transported or reinitialised.

\section{Phase fractions}\label{sec:interface:fractions}
For every cell the code stores the liquid volume per \emph{full} cell volume
and derives the fraction of the \emph{open} part,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:fractions}
  \phasefrac{\phase}{c}=\frac{\meas{\opencell{c}\cap\domain^{\phase}}}{\meas{c}},\qquad
  \condfrac{\phase}{c}=\frac{\phasefrac{\phase}{c}}{\openfrac{c}}\quad(\openfrac{c}>0),
\end{equation}
so that $\phasefrac{\liq}{c}+\phasefrac{\gas}{c}=\openfrac{c}$ and
$\condfrac{\liq}{c}+\condfrac{\gas}{c}=1$; without a solid
$\openfrac{c}=1$ and the two coincide with the thesis'
$\phasefrac{\phase}{c}$~\thesis{eq.~(2.35)}. Covered cells ($\openfrac{c}=0$)
carry $\condfrac{\liq}{c}=0$ and $\condfrac{\gas}{c}=1$. The conditional fraction $\condfrac{\liq}{c}$
is what the reconstruction, the height functions, the constitutive laws and
the forces read; $\phasefrac{\liq}{c}$ is the transported, conserved quantity.

Every reconstruction first validates and snaps the stored fraction with the
\emph{fraction floor} $\ffloor\in(0,\half)$ (default $\sqrt{\machineeps}\approx1.5\times10^{-8}$):
a value outside $[0,\openfrac{c}]$ by more than $128\machineeps\max(1,\openfrac{c})$
is an error, otherwise
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:floor}
  \phasefrac{\liq}{c}\leftarrow\begin{cases}
    0, & \condfrac{\liq}{c}\le\ffloor,\\
    \openfrac{c}, & \condfrac{\liq}{c}\ge1-\ffloor,\\
    \operatorname{clamp}(\phasefrac{\liq}{c},0,\openfrac{c}), & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and $\condfrac{\liq}{c}$ is recomputed. Each snap changes the liquid volume by
at most $\ffloor\meas{\opencell{c}}$, the clamp by round-off only; the halo slots of both fields are then
filled with the zero-gradient rule of \cref{sec:mesh:halo}. A cell is
\emph{mixed} if $0<\condfrac{\liq}{c}<1$ after snapping. Where the code needs a
phase indicator (the frozen indicator of the transport,
\cref{ch:vof-transport}, and the jump of the conservative ghost-fluid force,
\cref{ch:forces}) it uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:indicator}
  \indicator{\liq}_c=\begin{cases}1, & \condfrac{\liq}{c}>\half,\\ 0, & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}
  \qquad \indicator{\gas}_c=1-\indicator{\liq}_c .
\end{equation}

\begin{deviation}{Fraction floor}
The thesis treats a cell as an interface cell if
$\phasefrac{\phase}{c}>\ffloor=10^{-8}$ for both phases~\thesis{\S3.3}. The
code instead overwrites the stored fraction by~\cref{eq:interface:floor} at
every reconstruction, i.e.\ after every directional transport sweep. Volume is
therefore conserved only up to $\ffloor\meas{\opencell{c}}$ per snapped cell and
reconstruction.
\end{deviation}

\begin{deviation}{Indicator at equal fractions}
The thesis assigns the tie $\phasefrac{\liq}{c}=\phasefrac{\gas}{c}$ to the
liquid~\thesis{eq.~(2.37)}; \cref{eq:interface:indicator} assigns it to the gas.
\end{deviation}

\section{PLIC reconstruction}\label{sec:interface:plic}
Each cell is mapped to the reference cell $\refcell=[-\half,\half]^2$ by
$\coord=\pos{c}+\cellscale{c}\lcoord$, $\cellscale{c}=\diag{\cellwidth{c}{1},\cellwidth{c}{2}}$.
In a mixed cell the liquid occupies one half-plane of the open
part~\thesis{eq.~(3.58)},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:plane}
  \localpart{\liq}{c}=\set{\lcoord\in\localpart{\open}{c}}{\lnormal{c}\cdot\lcoord\le\lconst{c}},
  \qquad
  \inormal_c=\frac{\cellscale{c}^{-1}\lnormal{c}}{\abs{\cellscale{c}^{-1}\lnormal{c}}_2},\quad
  \idist{c}=\frac{\lconst{c}}{\abs{\cellscale{c}^{-1}\lnormal{c}}_2},
\end{equation}
where $\localpart{\open}{c}=\refcell$ in uncut cells and the open polygon of
\cref{eq:cutcells:open} otherwise. The thesis' line
$\inormal_c\cdot(\coord-\pos{c})=\idist{c}$~\thesis{\S3.3} is the image of
$\lnormal{c}\cdot\lcoord=\lconst{c}$. The local normal $\lnormal{c}$ is
stored unnormalised (WY) or with $\abs{(\lnormal{c})_1}+\abs{(\lnormal{c})_2}=1$;
\cref{eq:interface:plane} is invariant under its scaling.

In uncut cells the plane constant follows from the closed-form area fraction of
a rectangle $\prod_k[\rlo{k},\rhi{k}]\subseteq\refcell$ below the
line~\cite{scardovelli2000}. With the projected widths
$\pwidth{k}=\abs{(\lnormal{c})_k}(\rhi{k}-\rlo{k})$, $\pwidth{<}=\min_k\pwidth{k}$,
$\pwidth{>}=\max_k\pwidth{k}$ and the plane constant measured from the lowest corner,
$\zdist=\lconst{c}-\sum_k\min\roundpar{(\lnormal{c})_k\rlo{k},(\lnormal{c})_k\rhi{k}}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:area}
  \planearea=\begin{cases}
    0, & \zdist\le0,\\
    \cornerarea(\zdist), & 0<\zdist\le\half(\pwidth{1}+\pwidth{2}),\\
    1-\cornerarea(\pwidth{1}+\pwidth{2}-\zdist), & \half(\pwidth{1}+\pwidth{2})<\zdist<\pwidth{1}+\pwidth{2},\\
    1, & \zdist\ge\pwidth{1}+\pwidth{2},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation*}
  \cornerarea(\zdist)=\frac{\zdist^2}{2\pwidth{<}\pwidth{>}}\ \ (\zdist<\pwidth{<}),\qquad
  \cornerarea(\zdist)=\frac{\zdist-\pwidth{<}/2}{\pwidth{>}}\ \ (\zdist\ge\pwidth{<}),
\end{equation*}
is the fraction of the rectangle with $\lnormal{c}\cdot\lcoord\le\lconst{c}$;
for $\pwidth{<}\le\machineeps \pwidth{>}$ the code uses
$\operatorname{clamp}(\zdist/\pwidth{>},0,1)$, and the third regime is evaluated
by the first through the reflection $\zdist\mapsto\pwidth{1}+\pwidth{2}-\zdist$
(the inclusion--exclusion form of the distribution function loses
$\machineeps\,\pwidth{>}/\pwidth{<}$). The plane constant is the root of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:intercept}
  \planearea(\lnormal{c},\lconst{c};\refcell)=\condfrac{\liq}{c}\quad(\text{uncut}),\qquad
  \meas{\localpart{\liq}{c}}=\condfrac{\liq}{c}\,\meas{\localpart{\open}{c}}\quad(\text{cut}),
\end{equation}
found by 54 bisection steps on $[-r,r]$, $r=\half\sum_k\abs{(\lnormal{c})_k}$
(uncut), or on the range of $\lnormal{c}\cdot\lcoord$ over the vertices of
$\localpart{\open}{c}$ (cut), where the area of the clipped polygon is computed
by Sutherland--Hodgman clipping~\cite{sutherland1974} and the shoelace formula.
The PLIC segment $\interface_c$ is the part of the line inside
$\localpart{\open}{c}$; its midpoint in $\refcell$ (the average of the distinct
points where the line meets the sides of $\refcell$) is $\icentroid{c}$.
\Cref{fig:interface:plic} shows the reconstruction on a circle.

\emph{Checks} (\code{figures/generate.jl}). Over \chkPlaneSamples\ samples of
normals and fractions the bisection root of~\cref{eq:interface:intercept}
differs from the closed-form inverse of Scardovelli and Zaleski by at most
$\chkPlaneInverse$ for $10^{-2}\le\condfrac{\liq}{c}\le1-10^{-2}$ (near pure
cells the plane constant is ill-conditioned,
$\partial\lconst{c}/\partial\condfrac{\liq}{c}\sim(\condfrac{\liq}{c})^{-1/2}$ and
$(1-\condfrac{\liq}{c})^{-1/2}$; the bisection evaluates $\planearea$ with an
absolute error $\machineeps$, so its root is accurate to
$\machineeps\,(1-\condfrac{\liq}{c})^{-1/2}\abs{\lnormal{c}}_1$ near full cells, at most
$2\times10^{-13}\abs{\lnormal{c}}_1$ for the fractions that survive the floor of
\cref{eq:interface:floor}, while the closed form stays within a few
$10^{-16}\abs{\lnormal{c}}_1$ everywhere),
and the area of the reconstructed polygon reproduces $\condfrac{\liq}{c}$ to
$\chkPlaneArea$ for all samples. In the cut cells of the fixture of
\cref{fig:interface:contact:embedded}, $\meas{\localpart{\liq}{c}}$
reproduces $\condfrac{\liq}{c}\meas{\localpart{\open}{c}}$ to
$\chkOpenInterceptResidual$.

\section{Height functions}\label{sec:interface:heights}
Normals and curvature are computed from column sums of conditional fractions.
For the cells of a leaf block the stencil value is $\condfrac{\liq}{c}$; four
layers of halo values at the block's level are filled when the topology
changes, independently of the halo of \cref{sec:mesh:halo}: a slot $\hc$ takes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:height-values}
  \condfrac{\liq}{\hc}=\sum_{b}\frac{\meas{\hc\cap b}}{\meas{\hc}}\,
    \planearea\roundpar{\lnormal{b},\lconst{b};\cellscale{b}^{-1}(\hc\cap b-\pos{b})},
\end{equation}
the liquid fraction of the donor PLIC over the overlap (the donor fraction for
pure donors), clamped to $[0,1]$. Slots beyond a wall, and with a solid all
cells and slots that overlap a cell with $\openfrac{c}<1$, are
\emph{unavailable}: columns may end at a wall or at a cut cell but never
integrate the solid as gas.

A column with principal direction $+\unitvec{k}$ ($-\unitvec{k}$) uses
$\condfrac{\liq}{}$ ($\condfrac{\gas}{}=1-\condfrac{\liq}{}$). Through the cell
$c+m\unitvec{t}$, $t\neq k$, it consists of the available cells
$c+m\unitvec{t}+n\unitvec{k}$, $n_0\le n\le n_1$, a contiguous run in
$-3\le n\le3$ that starts at the first available cell. It is valid if the
first value is one, the last is zero, the values do not increase, and no two
consecutive mixed values are equal (thesis Properties~1 with three cells on either side~\thesis{\S2.3.1}). Its height, in cells of direction $k$ and relative
to the centre of $c+m\unitvec{t}$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:height}
  \hdisc{m}=\sum_{n=n_0}^{n_1}\condfrac{\liq}{c+m\unitvec{t}+n\unitvec{k}}+n_0-\half
  \qquad(\text{direction }+\unitvec{k};\ \condfrac{\gas}{}\text{ for }-\unitvec{k}),
\end{equation}
the column-sum form of the thesis' midpoint height~\thesis{eq.~(2.40)}. The
equality tests use the tolerance $64\machineeps$.
\Cref{fig:interface:heights:lhf} shows three such columns.

\section{Normals and choice of the plane}\label{sec:interface:normals}
\emph{WY normal.} Every reconstruction starts from the normal of Weymouth and
Yue~\cite{weymouth2010}~\thesis{\S3.3.2}, computed from the $3\times3$ block of
conditional fractions (halo values of \cref{sec:mesh:halo} at block borders).
With $\wydiff{k}=\condfrac{\liq}{c-\unitvec{k}}-\condfrac{\liq}{c+\unitvec{k}}$,
the principal axis $k=1$ if $\abs{\wydiff{1}}>\abs{\wydiff{2}}$ and $k=2$
otherwise, $t\neq k$, the column sums
$\colsum{c}=\sum_{m=-1}^{1}\condfrac{\liq}{c+m\unitvec{k}}$ and the side
$\wyside=\operatorname{sgn}(\wydiff{t})\operatorname{sgn}(\condfrac{\liq}{c}-\half)$,
with $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=1$ in the first and $-1$ in the second factor,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:wy}
  \lnormal{c}^{\mathrm{WY}}=\operatorname{sgn}(\wydiff{k})\,\unitvec{k}
    -\frac{\colsum{c+\wyside\unitvec{t}}-\colsum{c}}{\wyside}\,\unitvec{t}
\end{equation}
in local coordinates, and the plane constant
follows from~\cref{eq:interface:intercept}. It reproduces straight lines
exactly. In cut cells the covered neighbours enter with
$\condfrac{\liq}{}=0$.

\emph{LHF normal.} For each of the four signed directions $\pm\unitvec{k}$
with valid heights $\hdisc{-1},\hdisc{0},\hdisc{1}$ through $c-\unitvec{t}$,
$c$, $c+\unitvec{t}$, central differences give
$\hfun{\prime}=\cellwidth{c}{k}(\hdisc{1}-\hdisc{-1})/(2\cellwidth{c}{t})$ and
$\hfun{\prime\prime}=\cellwidth{c}{k}(\hdisc{1}-2\hdisc{0}+\hdisc{-1})/\cellwidth{c}{t}^2$
and, by~\cref{eq:interface:graph}, a candidate normal $\inormal^{(i)}$ and
curvature $\curv^{(i)}$~\thesis{\S3.3.1}. A candidate whose normal makes the
angle $\hfangle{i}=\arccos\abs{\inormal^{(i)}\cdot\unitvec{k}}$ with its
principal direction gets the quality and weight
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:lhf}
  \hfquality{i}=\smoothstep\roundpar{\frac{\pi/4+\hftol-\hfangle{i}}{2\hftol}},\quad
  \hftol=\frac{\pi}{20},\qquad
  \fitweight{i}=\hfquality{i}\prod_{j\neq i}(1-\hfquality{j}),
\end{equation}
over the candidates with $\hfquality{i}>0$, and the LHF result is
$\inormal\propto\sum_i\fitweight{i}\inormal^{(i)}$,
$\curv=\sum_i\fitweight{i}\curv^{(i)}/\sum_i\fitweight{i}$~\thesis{eq.~(3.61)}.
Thus $\hfquality{i}=1$ for $\hfangle{i}\le\pi/5$, $\hfquality{i}=0$ for
$\hfangle{i}\ge3\pi/10$, and one perfect candidate excludes the others.

\emph{Wall normal.} In a cell adjacent to a non-periodic domain wall whose
current line meets the wall side of the cell, the centred stencil lacks a column.
With the wall normal along axis $k$, inward sign $\inward$, $t\neq k$ and the
principal direction $s\unitvec{t}$, $s=\operatorname{sgn}((\inormal_c)_t)$, the
heights $\hdisc{0}$ through $c$ and $\hdisc{1}$ through $c+\inward\unitvec{k}$
give the one-sided slope and normal
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:wall-normal}
  \hfun{\prime}=\inward\,\frac{\cellwidth{c}{t}(\hdisc{1}-\hdisc{0})}{\cellwidth{c}{k}},\qquad
  \inormal^{(w)}=\frac{s\,\unitvec{t}-s\,\hfun{\prime}\,\unitvec{k}}{\sqrt{1+(\hfun{\prime})^2}},
\end{equation}
which contains no contact angle. In a corner cell the candidates with the
largest quality $\max(\hfquality{},\sqrt{\machineeps})$ are averaged.

\emph{Choice of the plane.} The keyword \code{reconstruction_method} selects
which normal defines the line used by transport (\cref{tab:interface:methods}).
For a mixed cell the code first takes a candidate: the wall normal (code 5) if
it applies; otherwise, unless the cell is an embedded triple cell
($0<\solidfrac{c}<1$), the LHF normal (code 1); otherwise the GHF fit of
\cref{sec:interface:curvature} (code 2); otherwise none (code 3). If the method
adopts the candidate, $\lnormal{c}=\cellscale{c}\inormal/\abs{\cellscale{c}\inormal}_1$
and $\lconst{c}$ is recomputed; otherwise the cell keeps its previous line,
initially the WY line. Because the height values in level-transition halos
depend on the neighbours' lines~\cref{eq:interface:height-values}, the
candidate pass is repeated three times on frozen copies of the lines (once for
\code{:wy}); cells are independent within a pass.

\emph{Limitation in embedded triple cells.} Because the LHF is skipped there and
the GHF line is adopted only by \code{:lhf_ghf}, triple cells always carry the
WY line under \code{:lhf} and \code{:wy}, and that line reads covered neighbours
as gas. On the sloped-wall fixture of \cref{fig:interface:contact:embedded}
with $16\times8$, $32\times16$ and $64\times32$ cells, the largest deviation
$\abs{\inormal_c-\inormal}_2$ of the triple-cell normals from the exact normal is
$\chkTripleNormalLhfA$, $\chkTripleNormalLhfB$, $\chkTripleNormalLhfC$ under
\code{:lhf} (nearly reversed normals) and $\chkTripleNormalGhfA$,
$\chkTripleNormalGhfB$, $\chkTripleNormalGhfC$ under \code{:lhf_ghf}; the
largest curvature errors are $\chkTripleCurvLhfA$, $\chkTripleCurvLhfB$,
$\chkTripleCurvLhfC$ (negative curvature at the clipping
bound~\cref{eq:interface:clip}) and $\chkTripleCurvGhfA$, $\chkTripleCurvGhfB$,
$\chkTripleCurvGhfC$ for the exact $1/4.2$. The embedded-wall tests of the
package use \code{:lhf_ghf}.

\emph{Checks} (\code{figures/generate.jl}). On the circle fixture of
\cref{fig:interface:plic} the normals and curvatures of the \chkLhfCells\ LHF
cells, evaluated independently from~\cref{eq:interface:height,eq:interface:lhf},
differ from the code's by at most $\chkLhfNormalDiff$ and $\chkLhfCurvDiff$, and
the WY normals~\cref{eq:interface:wy} of all mixed cells by $\chkWyNormalDiff$.

\begin{deviation}{Reconstruction family}
The thesis falls back to ELVIRA~\cite{pilliod2004} where no LHF normal
exists~\thesis{\S3.3.1}, reconstructs triple points with LVIRA or
MOF~\thesis{\S3.3.4} and also studies MOF and parabolic reconstructions
(PLVIRA, PROST, PMOF; \thesis{\S3.3.3 and Ch.~5}). The code implements the WY,
LHF, one-sided wall and GHF normals of this section only; it advects no
centroid, and in triple cells it uses the WY or GHF line as stated above.
\end{deviation}

\begin{deviation}{Height-function quality}
With $z=\inormal^{(i)}\cdot\unitvec{k}/\cos(\pi/4+\hftol)$ the thesis prints
the quality as $3z^3-2z^2$ for $0<z<1$ and one for $z\ge1$~\thesis{eq.~(3.62)}, which is neither monotone on that interval nor equal
to one for $\hfangle{i}<\pi/4-\hftol$ as the text requires. The code uses the
smoothstep in the angle~\cref{eq:interface:lhf}, which has the stated limits;
this correction is approved by the author.
\end{deviation}

\section{Curvature}\label{sec:interface:curvature}
\emph{LHF curvature.} Where the LHF normal of \cref{sec:interface:normals}
exists, its weighted curvature is used~\thesis{eq.~(4.92)}.

\emph{GHF fit.} Otherwise the generalised height function of
Popinet~\cite{popinet2009} with the weights of the thesis~\thesis{\S4.7.1} is
fitted. Let $\inormal=\inormal_c$ be the current normal of the cell,
$\itangent=(-(\inormal)_2,(\inormal)_1)$, $\icentroid{c}$ the midpoint of its
segment and $h=\min_k\cellwidth{c}{k}$. Every valid height of every signed
direction $\pm\unitvec{k}$ through a cell $c'$ of the $3\times3$ block around
$c$ gives the point $\ghfpoint{j}=\pos{c'}+\cellwidth{c}{k}\hdisc{0}\unitvec{k}$,
$\hdisc{0}$ that column's height~\cref{eq:interface:height}, with weight $\fitweight{j}=\max(0,\pm\inormal\cdot\unitvec{k})^2$; points
with zero weight or closer than $h/2$ to an earlier point are discarded (order:
rows, columns, axis, sign). In the frame
$\tcoord=\itangent\cdot(\ghfpoint{j}-\icentroid{c})/h$,
$\ncoord=\inormal\cdot(\ghfpoint{j}-\icentroid{c})/h$ the parabola minimises
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:ghf}
  \sum_j\fitweight{j}\roundpar{\parcoef{2}\tcoord^2+\parcoef{1}\tcoord+\parcoef{0}-\ncoord}^2
\end{equation}
\thesis{eq.~(4.93)}, with the coordinates of $\ghfpoint{j}$ and, in a triple
cell, the contact row~\cref{eq:interface:contact-row} added with weight $10^3$,
provided there are at least three rows and the smallest
singular value of the matrix exceeds $10^{-10}$ times the largest. It yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:ghf-result}
  \inormal^{\mathrm{GHF}}=\frac{\inormal-\parcoef{1}\itangent}{\sqrt{1+\parcoef{1}^2}},\qquad
  \curv^{\mathrm{GHF}}=-\frac{2\parcoef{2}}{h\roundpar{1+\parcoef{1}^2}^{3/2}},
\end{equation}
evaluated at $\icentroid{c}$. In a triple cell both are negated if the
components of $\inormal^{\mathrm{GHF}}$ and $\inormal$ along the wall tangent
have opposite signs. If the height points do not determine the fit, the thesis
uses the segment midpoints of the neighbouring mixed cells with unit weights;
the code tries the $3\times3$, $5\times5$ and $7\times7$ blocks in turn, with
the previous-pass lines of the block's cells and WY lines of halo cells,
\cref{fig:interface:heights:ghf} shows a height-point fit.

\emph{Choice and bounds.} The curvature of a mixed cell is, in this order: the
domain-wall contact curvature of \cref{sec:interface:contact}; in a triple cell
with an embedded contact angle, the constrained fit; the candidate's curvature
(LHF or GHF); for a wall-normal cell without one, the unconstrained GHF fit.
The result is clipped to the mesh,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:clip}
  \curv_c\leftarrow\operatorname{clamp}\roundpar{\curv_c,\,-\frac{1}{2\max_k\cellwidth{c}{k}},\,\frac{1}{2\max_k\cellwidth{c}{k}}},
\end{equation}
and is undefined (\code{NaN}) where no estimate exists and in pure cells; the
force of \cref{ch:forces} raises an error on a face that needs it.

\emph{Checks} (\code{figures/generate.jl}). On the circle fixture (exact
curvature \chkCircleKappa) the LHF curvatures deviate by at most $\chkLhfExact$ and
the GHF curvatures of the \chkWyCells\ remaining mixed cells by $\chkGhfExact$;
an independent evaluation of~\cref{eq:interface:ghf,eq:interface:ghf-result},
including the centroid fallback used in \chkGhfFallbacks\ of them, agrees with
the code to $\chkGhfCurvDiff$.

\begin{deviation}{GHF centroid fallback}
The thesis' fallback fits the interface centroids of the $3\times3$ neighbourhood
only~\thesis{\S4.7.1}; the code enlarges the block to $5\times5$ and $7\times7$
before giving up, so that under-resolved arcs beside walls still receive a
curvature.
\end{deviation}

\section{Contact angles}\label{sec:interface:contact}
The contact angle enters only the curvature of the cells at the wall; the
lines of \cref{sec:interface:normals} do not depend on it. The defaults are
$\cangle=\pi/2$ on every non-periodic domain wall and on an embedded solid;
\code{contact_angle} takes one angle or a tuple (left, right, bottom, top)
whose entries may be \code{nothing}, and \code{embedded_contact_angle} the solid's.

\emph{Domain walls.} For a mixed cell next to a wall with prescribed $\cangle$
whose line meets the wall side (wall normal along axis $k$, inward sign
$\inward$, $t\neq k$), two branches follow the thesis~\thesis{\S4.7.2},
\cref{fig:interface:contact}. If $\abs{\cangle-\pi/2}\le\pi/4$, the heights
along $s\unitvec{t}$, $s=\operatorname{sgn}((\inormal_c)_t)$, through the wall cell
and its inward neighbour, $\hphys{m}=\cellwidth{c}{t}\hdisc{m}$ for $m=0,1$, are
extended by a ghost value that imposes~\cref{eq:interface:contact} on the
height function, whose derivative along $\inward\unitvec{k}$ at the wall is
$-\cos\cangle/(s\sin\cangle)$~\cite{afkhami2008},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:contact-right}
  \hphys{-1}=\hphys{0}+\cellwidth{c}{k}\frac{\cos\cangle}{s\sin\cangle},\qquad
  \curv_c=-s\,\frac{(\hphys{1}-2\hphys{0}+\hphys{-1})/\cellwidth{c}{k}^2}
    {\roundpar{1+\roundpar{(\hphys{1}-\hphys{-1})/(2\cellwidth{c}{k})}^2}^{3/2}} .
\end{equation}
If $\abs{\cangle-\pi/2}>\pi/4$, the phase of the wedge at the wall,
$s=\operatorname{sgn}(\cos\cangle)$ (liquid for acute, gas for obtuse angles),
is measured by heights from the wall: $\hphys{m}=\cellwidth{c}{k}\hdisc{m}$,
$m=1,2$, in the next two columns away from the contact point, each the sum over
the four cells from the wall (non-increasing values, last one empty). With
$\coordk{t}$ measured along the wall from the first of these columns, away from
the contact point, the parabola
$\hfun{}=\parcoef{2}\coordk{t}^2+\parcoef{1}\coordk{t}+\hphys{1}$ through both
heights that vanishes with slope $\abs{\tan\cangle}$ at an unknown point is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:contact-acute}
  \begin{gathered}
  \parcoef{2}=\frac{(\hphys{2}-\hphys{1})^2-\cellwidth{c}{t}^2\tan^2\cangle}
    {\cellwidth{c}{t}^2\roundpar{\hphys{1}+\hphys{2}+\sqrt{4\hphys{1}\hphys{2}+\cellwidth{c}{t}^2\tan^2\cangle}}},\qquad
  \parcoef{1}=\frac{\hphys{2}-\hphys{1}}{\cellwidth{c}{t}}-\parcoef{2}\cellwidth{c}{t},\\
  \curv_c=\frac{-2s\,\parcoef{2}}{\roundpar{1+(\parcoef{1}-2\parcoef{2}\cellwidth{c}{t})^2}^{3/2}},
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
required $\hphys{2}>\hphys{1}>0$. The parabolas through both heights with the
slope $\abs{\tan\cangle}$ at an unknown zero have
$\parcoef{2}=(\hphys{1}+\hphys{2}\pm\sqrt{D})/\cellwidth{c}{t}^2$,
$D=4\hphys{1}\hphys{2}+\cellwidth{c}{t}^2\tan^2\cangle$~\thesis{\S4.7.2};
\cref{eq:interface:contact-acute} is the minus sign, written without the
subtraction of nearly equal numbers. It is the thesis' root with
$B\tan\cangle>0$~\thesis{eq.~(4.94)}, where the slope of the wedge phase is
$\abs{\tan\cangle}$, i.e.\ the root with $\parcoef{1}>0$: its zero lies at
$\coordk{t}=-2\hphys{1}/(\parcoef{1}+\abs{\tan\cangle})<0$, before the first
column, whereas the other root has it beyond. The slope in the curvature is
evaluated at the wall cell, $\coordk{t}=-\cellwidth{c}{t}$
(\cref{dev:interface:contact-acute}). The first wall (left, right, bottom, top)
whose branch has valid heights determines the curvature.

\emph{Embedded walls.} In a triple cell the curvature is the GHF
fit~\cref{eq:interface:ghf} with one more row~\thesis{eq.~(4.97)}. The triple point
$\triplept$ is the intersection of the interface and solid lines of the cell,
if it lies in the cell; with the solid normal into the fluid,
$\wallnormal=\cellscale{c}^{-1}\snormal{c}/\abs{\cellscale{c}^{-1}\snormal{c}}_2$
(\cref{ch:cutcells}), and $\tcoord^*=\itangent\cdot(\triplept-\icentroid{c})/h$,
the linearised condition~\cref{eq:interface:contact} on the fitted normal is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:contact-row}
  -2\tcoord^*(\itangent\cdot\wallnormal)\,\parcoef{2}-(\itangent\cdot\wallnormal)\,\parcoef{1}
    =\cos\cangle-\inormal\cdot\wallnormal,
\end{equation}
added to~\cref{eq:interface:ghf} as a row multiplied by $10^3$ (weight $10^6$ in the
sum, the height rows being multiplied by $\sqrt{\fitweight{j}}$). It is the unnormalised
fitted normal $\inormal-(\parcoef{1}+2\parcoef{2}\tcoord^*)\itangent$ inserted
in~\cref{eq:interface:contact}, i.e.\ the exact condition with the factor
$\sqrt{1+(\parcoef{1}+2\parcoef{2}\tcoord^*)^2}$ on its right-hand side replaced by one:
the fitted unit normal at $\triplept$ makes the angle $\cangle'$ with
$\wallnormal$ where $\cos\cangle'=\cos\cangle/\sqrt{1+(\parcoef{1}+2\parcoef{2}\tcoord^*)^2}$, an error
$\approx\frac12(\parcoef{1}+2\parcoef{2}\tcoord^*)^2\cot\cangle$ of the angle. If the two lines
do not meet in the cell the fit is computed without this row; if the fit fails, the
candidate's curvature is kept.
\Cref{fig:interface:contact:embedded} shows a triple cell.

\begin{deviation}{Signs of the height-function formulas}\label{dev:interface:signs}
With $\inormal$ pointing into the gas, $\curv=\divergence\inormal$~\thesis{eq.~(2.6)}
and $\wallnormal$ pointing into the fluid with $\cangle$ measured in the
liquid, \cref{eq:interface:graph} carries $\mp$ for $\hfun{\pm k}$, the
right-angle branch has $\mathrm{d}\hfun{+2}/\mathrm{d}\coordk{1}=-1/\tan\cangle$
at a wall with $\wallnormal=\unitvec{1}$, and the acute branch has
$\curv=-2\parcoef{2}/(1+(\hfun{\prime})^2)^{3/2}$. The thesis
prints~\thesis{eq.~(4.92)} $\curv=\pm(\dots)/W^3$, whereas the
preceding line, $\curv=\divergence\inormal$ with $\inormal=\pm(-H_x,-H_y,1)/W$, gives
$\mp$; in \S4.7.2 it prints $\mathrm{d}H^{+y}/\mathrm{d}x=1/\tan\cangle$ right after
$\cos\cangle=-H_x/\sqrt{1+H_x^2}$, which gives $-1/\tan\cangle$; and it prints
$\curv=2A/\sqrt{1+B^2}^{\,3}$ for its acute example, whose positive principal direction
gives $-2A/(1+B^2)^{3/2}$ (the printed sign is correct for the negative direction,
$s=-1$, obtuse angles). Each of the three results contradicts a premise printed
in the same paragraph (the three results are those obtained with $\inormal$ pointing
into the liquid, the premises those with $\inormal$ into the gas), so they
are typographical errors (pending the author's confirmation). The code follows the
definitions above: its curvature converges to $1/R>0$ for sessile drops at
$20^\circ$, $55^\circ$, $70^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $125^\circ$ and $160^\circ$, and the
ghost value~\cref{eq:interface:contact-right} makes the discrete wall slope of the
height function exactly $-\cos\cangle/(s\sin\cangle)$.
\end{deviation}

\begin{deviation}{Acute and obtuse contact curvature}\label{dev:interface:contact-acute}
The thesis evaluates the curvature of the contact parabola with the slope at the
first height column, $\curv=2\parcoef{2}/(1+\parcoef{1}^2)^{3/2}$ in the notation
used here (sign as printed, see \cref{dev:interface:signs})~\thesis{\S4.7.2};
the code evaluates the slope at the column of the wall cell, one cell closer to the
contact point~\cref{eq:interface:contact-acute}. The two values differ by the
relative amount $6\cellwidth{c}{t}\parcoef{2}\parcoef{1}/(1+\parcoef{1}^2)$, of
order $\cellwidth{c}{t}\abs{\curv}$. Both are first-order accurate: on
sessile drops with $R/h=96$ at $\cangle=10^\circ,20^\circ,30^\circ,150^\circ,160^\circ,170^\circ$ the largest
curvature errors over the contact cells are $5.6\times10^{-4}$ to $3.6\times10^{-3}$ (code) and
$1.1\times10^{-3}$ to $2.2\times10^{-3}$ (thesis) for $1/R=0.167$, neither
uniformly smaller.
\end{deviation}

\section{Phase apertures and interface height}\label{sec:interface:apertures}
The phase apertures of a face are the averages of the one-sided traces of the
two adjacent reconstructions~\thesis{eq.~(2.41)}. With $\localface{f}{c}$ the
local image of the segment $f$ in the coordinates of $c$, the liquid trace is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:liquid-trace}
  \phasetrace{\liq}{c}{f}=\frac{\meas{\localface{f}{c}\cap\localpart{\liq}{c}}}{\meas{\localface{f}{c}}}
\end{equation}
(the solid half-plane is applied first, \cref{sec:cutcells:apertures}; pure
cells give $0$ or the open trace $\traceap{c}{f}$). On an interior face,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:phase-aperture}
  \phaseaperture{\liq}{f}=\begin{cases}
    0, & \text{no open cell of }\cellset{f}\text{ has }\condfrac{\liq}{c}>0,\\
    \aperture{f}, & \text{no open cell of }\cellset{f}\text{ has }\condfrac{\liq}{c}<1,\\
    \operatorname{clamp}\roundpar{\half\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\phasetrace{\liq}{c}{f},\,0,\,\aperture{f}}, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad \phaseaperture{\gas}{f}=\aperture{f}-\phaseaperture{\liq}{f},
\end{equation}
and on a boundary face $\phaseaperture{\liq}{f}=\operatorname{clamp}(\phasetrace{\liq}{\cbnd{f}}{f},0,\aperture{f})$.
The pure-phase cases keep an absent phase exactly closed. The apertures belong
to the reconstructed state: they are computed once per reconstruction and read
by the CFL bound, the two-velocity transport and the projection.

The public \apifun{interface_height} returns, for a column in direction $k$ at
transverse position $\coordk{t}$ (wrapped if periodic), the height obtained by
integrating the reconstructed phase indicator along the column,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interface:height-api}
  \hfun{}(\coordk{t})=\begin{cases}
    \domorigin{k}+\sum_{c}\cellwidth{c}{k}\,\linefrac{c}, & \text{liquid at the low end},\\
    \domorigin{k}+\domextent{k}-\sum_{c}\cellwidth{c}{k}\,\linefrac{c}, & \text{liquid at the high end},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\linefrac{c}$ is the liquid fraction of the line $\coordk{t}=\text{const}$
in cell $c$ according to its PLIC. The two ends must be in opposite phases and
no cell of the column may be cut by a solid; otherwise it throws.

\emph{Checks} (\code{figures/generate.jl}). On the \chkPhaseFaces\ faces with
both phases of the sloped-wall fixture, an independent evaluation
of~\cref{eq:interface:liquid-trace,eq:interface:phase-aperture} agrees with the
code to $\chkPhaseApertureDiff$; $\phaseaperture{\liq}{f}+\phaseaperture{\gas}{f}=\aperture{f}$
holds to $\chkPhasePartition$.

\section{Initialisation}\label{sec:interface:init}
Fractions are initialised from a prescribed liquid region $\initdomain$; the
fields are then reconstructed. Three descriptions are supported.

\emph{Half-plane}
$\initdomain=\set{\coord}{\normal\cdot\coord\le d}$ (\apifun{initialize!}
with a normal and a constant): in uncut cells
$\phasefrac{\liq}{c}=\planearea(\cellscale{c}\normal,\,d-\normal\cdot\pos{c};\refcell)$
exactly by~\cref{eq:interface:area}; in cut cells
$\phasefrac{\liq}{c}=\meas{\set{\lcoord\in\localpart{\open}{c}}{\cellscale{c}\normal\cdot\lcoord\le d-\normal\cdot\pos{c}}}$.

\emph{Polygon} (\apitype{PolygonIndicator}, a simple polygon in either
orientation): $\phasefrac{\liq}{c}=\meas{\initdomain\cap c}/\meas{c}$ by
Sutherland--Hodgman clipping against the four sides of $c$ and, with a solid,
against the solid half-plane in local coordinates, clamped to
$[0,\openfrac{c}]$. No quadrature tolerance is involved.

\emph{Level set} ($\initdomain=\set{\coord}{\lset(\coord)\le0}$, any finite
real function): per cell the area is computed recursively to the absolute
tolerance $\inittol\meas{c}$ ($\inittol=10^{-10}$, depths at most
\code{maxdepth} $=16$). For a rectangle $\qbox$,
\begin{enumerate}
\item if a Lipschitz bound $\lipschitz$ is given and
  $\abs{\lset(\pos{\qbox})}\ge\lipschitz\operatorname{diam}(\qbox)/2$, $\qbox$
  is pure;
\item $\lset$ is sampled at the corners, edge midpoints and centre; if all nine
  samples have one strict sign (and no $\lipschitz$ is given, depth at least
  \code{min_depth}), $\qbox$ is pure;
\item if the samples change sign or vanish and $\lset$ is strictly monotone in
  direction $k$ on the three sample lines, with one-sided finite differences
  (step $10^{-5}$ of the width) of the same sign at all nine points, the area is
  the integral over $\coordk{t}$ of the length of
  $\set{\coordk{k}}{\lset(\coord)\le0}$ on the line through $\coordk{t}$, found by
  bisection; the integral is split where $\lset$ changes sign on the two
  transverse sides and evaluated by adaptive 8-point Gauss--Legendre quadrature
  with interval halving;
\item otherwise $\qbox$ is split into four quarters, each with a quarter of
  its tolerance; at depth \code{maxdepth} initialisation fails.
\end{enumerate}
Fractions within $64\machineeps$ of zero or one are set to zero or
one. Failures leave the field unchanged. Without $\lipschitz$, features that
fall between the samples of item~2 are not detected; \code{min_depth} forces a
finer search. The code also contains the rule ``accept $\qbox$ if
$\meas{\qbox}$ does not exceed its tolerance'' before item~4, but since both are
quartered at every level it is never reached. Consequently, with $\lipschitz$
given, a level set that touches a side of $\qbox$ tangentially at a point that
is never sampled is neither certified pure nor split into monotone pieces, and
initialisation fails for any \code{maxdepth}: a circle of radius $0.27$ centred
at $(0.52,0.47)$ in the unit square with $16\times16$ cells and
\code{lipschitz=1} touches the grid line $\coordk{1}=0.25$ and throws, while
the same circle without \code{lipschitz} initialises with a volume error of
$10^{-16}$.

\emph{With a solid}, the level set is first initialised and reconstructed on a
field without solid (same \code{reconstruction_method}); then
$\phasefrac{\liq}{c}=\meas{\localpart{\open}{c}\cap\set{\lcoord}{\lnormal{c}\cdot\lcoord\le\lconst{c}}}$
with that field's line, the exact intersection of two PLIC reconstructions
rather than of the exact regions.

\section{Time discretisation and update schedule}\label{sec:interface:time}
Time discretisation is not applicable: the reconstruction is a map from the
current fractions and solid to lines, curvatures and apertures. It is
evaluated by \apifun{reconstruct!} after initialisation, after every
directional sweep of the transport (\cref{ch:vof-transport}), after a regrid
(the transfer of \cref{ch:amr-criteria-transfer} prolongs fractions by
clipping the parent's lines and then reconstructs) and after the solid's
geometry changes. A call is a no-op if the fractions, the leaf set and the
solid's revision are unchanged.

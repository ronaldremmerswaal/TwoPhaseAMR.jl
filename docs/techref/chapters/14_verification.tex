\chapter{Verification}\label{ch:verification}
\chapterstatus[c46cf60f29a3ee5a0771ec516942ab3987ee28c7]{reference}
The preceding chapters state what the code computes. This chapter states how
that is checked. It defines the diagnostics through which tests, examples and
users observe a flow (\cref{sec:verif:diagnostics}), describes the test suite
and the tiers in which continuous integration runs it
(\cref{sec:verif:suite}), the regression oracle that detects any change of
the results of the examples (\cref{sec:verif:oracle}) and the derivation
audits that check the statements of this reference against the code
(\cref{sec:verif:audits}). It then lists the verification cases with what
each asserts: manufactured solutions and observed orders
(\cref{sec:verif:orders}), conservation and other invariants
(\cref{sec:verif:invariants}), benchmark flows with exact, theoretical or
published references (\cref{sec:verif:benchmarks}) and the examples gallery
(\cref{sec:verif:gallery}), and it closes with the known gaps
(\cref{sec:verif:gaps}). The thesis validates the VOF
transport~\thesis{\S3.5}, the surface-tension models~\thesis{\S4.8}, the
two-fluid transport~\thesis{\S6.5} and the two-fluid flow
solver~\thesis{\S7.7}; several cases below repeat those studies. Validation
against experiments is not part of the suite.

\section{Diagnostics}\label{sec:verif:diagnostics}
A diagnostic maps the state at the end of a step to a number or a pair of
numbers. The kinetic energy and the linear momentum are sums over the
interior faces $\facesint$, weighted with the open staggered volumes
$\opendualvol{f}$ ($=\dualvol{f}$ without a solid), the staggered mixture
density $\stagdens_f$ and the clamped staggered liquid fraction
$\clampfrac{f}$ of~\cref{eq:forces:density}: with one velocity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verif:energy}
  \kinenergy=\tfrac12\sum_{f\in\facesint}\opendualvol{f}\,\stagdens_f\,\velo_f^2,\qquad
  \linmom{k}=\sum_{f\in\facesint\cap\facesaxis{k}}\opendualvol{f}\,\stagdens_f\,\velo_f ,
\end{equation}
with $\stagdens_f$ replaced by the density $\dens$ in one phase, and with two
velocities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verif:energy-two}
  \begin{gathered}
  \kinenergy=\tfrac12\sum_{f\in\facesint}\opendualvol{f}\roundpar{\dens^{\liq}\clampfrac{f}(\velo^{\liq}_f)^2+\dens^{\gas}(1-\clampfrac{f})(\velo^{\gas}_f)^2},\\
  \linmom{k}=\sum_{f\in\facesint\cap\facesaxis{k}}\opendualvol{f}\roundpar{\dens^{\liq}\clampfrac{f}\velo^{\liq}_f+\dens^{\gas}(1-\clampfrac{f})\velo^{\gas}_f},
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where a product with a vanishing weight ($\opendualvol{f}$, $\clampfrac{f}$
or $1-\clampfrac{f}$) is zero, also where the phase velocity is undefined.
Boundary faces carry prescribed values, not unknowns,
and do not enter. The phase volumes
$\phasevol{\liq}=\sum_c\meas{c}\phasefrac{\liq}{c}$ and
$\phasevol{\gas}=\meas{\opencell{\domain}}-\phasevol{\liq}$, with the open
volume $\meas{\opencell{\domain}}=\sum_c\meas{c}\openfrac{c}$
($\meas{\domain}$ without a solid), give the phase masses
$\dens^{\phase}\phasevol{\phase}$. The potential and surface energies are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verif:energies}
  \begin{gathered}
  \potenergy=-\gravacc\cdot\roundpar{\dens^{\liq}\firstmom{\liq}+\dens^{\gas}\roundpar{\firstmom{\open}-\firstmom{\liq}}},\qquad
  \surfenergy=\surftens\meas{\interface},\\
  \meas{\interface}=\sum_{c:\ 0<\condfrac{\liq}{c}<1}\meas{\interface_c}
    +\sum_{f\in\facesint\ \text{separating}}\meas{f}\,\aperture{f},
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $\firstmom{\open}$ and $\firstmom{\liq}$ are the first moments
$\int\coord\dd\coord$ of the open region and of the reconstructed liquid
region: in a cell with $0<\condfrac{\liq}{c}<1$ the part of $\opencell{c}$ on
the liquid side of its PLIC line (\cref{ch:interface}), and $\opencell{c}$
itself if $\condfrac{\liq}{c}\ge1$. A mixed cell thus contributes the moment
of its phase polygons, not its mass at its centre; the potential is zero at
the origin. $\interface_c$ is the PLIC segment of a mixed cell, clipped to
the open part, and a face is separating if one neighbour has
$\condfrac{\liq}{c}\le\ffloor$ and the other $\condfrac{\liq}{c}\ge1-\ffloor$:
an interface on a grid line has no PLIC segment and is measured by the face.
The measure $\meas{\interface}$ is the length of the reconstruction; on a
circle it converges with an observed order of about $0.7$, so $\surfenergy$
is a diagnostic of changes, not a second-order approximation of the exact
surface energy.
The total energy is $\kinenergy+\potenergy+\surfenergy$; in one phase
$\potenergy=\surfenergy=0$.
The divergence norm is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verif:divergence-norm}
  \divnorm=\Big(\sum_{c:\ \openfrac{c}>0}\meas{\opencell{c}}\,(\mathfrak{D}^{\star}\velo)_c^2\Big)^{1/2},\qquad
  \mathfrak{D}^{\star}\velo=\begin{cases}
    \divh\velo, & \text{one velocity, no solid},\\
    \divopen\velo, & \text{one velocity, solid},\\
    \divmix(\velo^{\liq},\velo^{\gas}), & \text{two velocities},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with the divergences of \cref{eq:setting:divergence,eq:setting:open-divergence,eq:projection:mixture-divergence}:
the constraint of the projection (\cref{ch:projection}), not a
density-weighted divergence. The boundary faces enter the divergence with
their prescribed values (and, with two velocities, the boundary phase
apertures), unlike the energies. The largest velocity is $\max\abs{\velo_f}$ over
the interior and boundary faces, with two velocities over the faces on which
a phase is active; the remaining diagnostics are the time-step bound
$\dtflow(\flowcfl)$ of~\cref{eq:stab:flow-bound} and the numbers of leaf
blocks and cells.

\section{Test suite and continuous integration}\label{sec:verif:suite}
Every Julia file under \code{test/} is registered in \code{test/groups.jl}:
in one or more test groups (an integration group lists its prerequisites),
among the helpers, or among the exclusions (the entry points, the separately
provisioned Makie suite and the MimeticFV comparison). The group
\code{registry} fails when a file is unregistered or a registered file is
missing, and when a default group is not in exactly one unit shard. The
default suite (\code{Pkg.test()}) is \code{registry} and the eleven groups
that are neither integration groups nor one of the separate groups
\code{performance} and \code{threading_equivalence}, which have their own
jobs. Arguments select groups by name, \code{integration} selects the thirteen
integration groups and \code{shard:A}, \code{shard:B}, \code{shard:C} the unit
shards. The unit groups check contracts, exact identities and short
manufactured-solution studies; the integration groups run refinement studies
and benchmark flows; \code{performance} holds allocation contracts;
\code{threading_equivalence} repeats five short flows (Taylor--Green vortices on
an adaptively refined mesh, two-phase CMDGFM on an embedded sloped wall, a
capillary wave with interface refinement, the two-velocity Stokes-3 wave, which
must regrid during the run, and the cylinder) in fresh processes with one, two
and four Julia threads and the threading grain at one, so that every loop
takes its threaded branch. It requires bitwise equal SHA-256 hashes of every
state field (velocity components per phase, pressure, liquid fraction) and of
every recorded diagnostic history, equal step, mesh-update and leaf-block
counts, and warm steps of the Taylor--Green and capillary cases within a
recorded allocation budget, so the oracle of \cref{sec:verif:oracle} holds for
every thread count.

\section{Regression oracle}\label{sec:verif:oracle}
The regression oracle detects every change of the results of ten short runs
of the examples (\cref{tab:verif:oracle}); it does not judge whether a change
is an improvement. For each case it records the number of steps, the final
time, the last value of every diagnostic history of the flow (the volume
error for the transport-only vortex), and for each velocity component (per
phase with two velocities, on the owned interior faces of its direction), the
pressure and the liquid fraction (on the cell interiors) four statistics: the
SHA-256 hash of the raw values, the Euclidean norm and the largest magnitude
of the finite values, and the numbers of values and of finite values.
Inactive two-velocity faces hold \code{NaN}: they enter the hash and the
counts, not the norms. \nolinkurl{benchmark/oracle/check.jl} compares the
steps and the counts exactly and the final time and the diagnostics with
relative and absolute tolerance $10^{-10}$; it compares the fields by their
hashes, that is bitwise, when the Julia version and the thread count equal
those of the record (Julia 1.12.7, one thread), and otherwise by their norms
and largest magnitudes with the same tolerance. The mode and the tolerance
can be set, and \code{ORACLE_PERTURB=1} changes one velocity of
the Taylor--Green case by one unit in the last place to exercise the failure
path. \nolinkurl{benchmark/oracle/perf.jl} records first-run latency and the time
and allocations of ten warm steps (\code{perf_baseline.toml}); these are
machine-local reference points, not contracts.

\section{Derivation audits}\label{sec:verif:audits}
A derivation audit checks one claim of this reference (an equation, an
identity, a bound or a measured statement) independently of the chapter that
states it: symbolically, and numerically against the package, mostly through
an independent re-implementation of the discrete formula evaluated on random
2:1-balanced, periodic, walled and cut meshes. Each audit is archived in a
folder of \code{proof_review/}, mostly named after the label of the claim,
with \code{audit.md} (the steps, the errors found, the sharpness of the claim
and its history) and \code{scripts/}, whose \code{run_all.jl} reruns every
check and exits with an error if one fails; \code{proof_review/README.md} has
one row per audited claim with the audited lines, the date, the status and
the outcome. In this version of the reference $67$ audit folders cover
Chapters 3 to 14. A finding is either applied to the chapter or, if the code is at
fault, filed as an issue and stated in a deviation box or in the text, as
\issue{63} and \issue{70} from the audits of \cref{ch:momentum-transport} and
\issue{78} and \issue{79} from those of \cref{ch:viscosity}.

Each reference chapter states the commit against which it was audited (the
optional argument of \verb|\chapterstatus|, printed below its title);
\code{coverage.toml} repeats the commit, and \code{check_links.py} fails if
the two differ. The same script checks that every \verb|\srcsym| names a
symbol defined in its file, every \verb|\testsym| an existing \code{@testset}
title, and every claim of \code{coverage.toml} its source symbols and test
titles. Its re-audit report, which does not fail, lists for each reference
chapter the files it refers to (through \verb|\srcsym|, the sources of its
claims and the source files classified for it in \code{coverage.toml}) that
changed between the audited commit and \code{HEAD}. A listed chapter is
re-read against the changes and re-pinned, with a new audit where a stated
equation changed. Numbers quoted in the chapters are computed by the scripts
of \code{docs/techref/figures/}, whose assertions fail when the
implementation no longer matches the statements; they need Julia and are run
by hand (\code{generate.jl}, or \code{generate.jl amr} and
\code{generate.jl stability} for one chapter), while the PDF build reads only
their committed output.

\section{Known gaps}\label{sec:verif:gaps}
\emph{Open defects.} Of the issues labelled \emph{bug} and open at the audited
commit, these concern accuracy or the completion of a step: first-order
velocity next to inclined embedded no-slip walls (\issue{110}), pinned by a
test at the observed orders; first-order shear across an aligned material viscosity jump
with the geometric-mean vertex viscosity (\issue{113}), not tested; a
CMDGFM projection with the ILU
preconditioner that stops unconverged with iterations left (\issue{126});
conjugate gradients with non-symmetric multigrid cycles (\issue{128}). The open
bugs \issue{117} and \issue{118} (refinement criteria) and \issue{127} (an
allocation contract) do
not affect accuracy. Since
the audited commit, PR~\#125 has fixed \issue{117} and \issue{118}
(\cref{sec:amr:band,sec:amr:curvature}), PR~\#130 \issue{126} and
\issue{127}, \issue{121}, the one-phase \code{cfl} range, is fixed by the
step check of~\cref{eq:stab:check}, which now uses the top of the accepted
range (\cref{sec:stab:bounds}); \issue{49}, a level-set initialisation
that could fail for valid input, is fixed by the certified acceptance
of~\cref{sec:interface:init}; and \issue{36} is fixed: a periodic direction
with two cells is resolved by the face orientation, whose normal component
fixes the sign of the seam displacement, while the multigrid coarsest level
still keeps four cells per periodic axis (\cref{sec:solvers:hierarchy});
\issue{70} is fixed by the outflow limiter of
\cref{dev:momentum:ctu-hanging}, \issue{71} by the open-strip phase schedule
of~\cref{sec:momentum:two-velocity}, and \issue{72} no longer reproduces: the
cut-donor bound keeps the dual Courant number at or below its limit next to a
solid (\cref{sec:momentum:cfl}). The
others are open. The open investigation is the fallback for orphaned edges at
open walls (\issue{99}).

\emph{Order in time.} No test asserts an order in time
(\cref{sec:stab:accuracy}). The temporal series at a fixed mesh, with the
step halved twice, assert that the error does not increase (one-velocity
capillary wave) or does not grow by more than a factor $1.5$, $2$ or $2.1$
(two-velocity capillary and Kelvin--Helmholtz waves). The one-velocity
capillary wave at density ratio one gave $0.0126$, $0.0046$ and $0.0030$,
the frozen two-velocity wave $0.00655$, $0.00782$ and $0.01137$ and the
Kelvin--Helmholtz wave of the two-velocity model.

\emph{References.} The examples' numbers are not asserted and can lag the
code (\cref{sec:verif:gallery}). The cylinder regression pins a transient,
the developed-wake comparison is manual, and the gallery's cylinder flow is
at another Reynolds number than the test and the manual study. No test
compares the sloped sessile droplet, the decaying turbulence, the Stokes wave
or the LGPI case with a reference, and none asserts a contact angle or the
size of the parasitic currents of a sessile drop. The Ghia comparison runs on
one mesh.

\emph{Tiers.} The integration groups run weekly and the heavy groups
monthly, the latter without failing the run, so that a regression only they
detect reaches \code{main} first; the oracle, the cylinder study and the
MimeticFV comparison run by hand (\cref{sec:verif:suite}).

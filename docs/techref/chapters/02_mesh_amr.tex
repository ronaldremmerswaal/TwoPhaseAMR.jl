\chapter{Block-forest AMR mesh}\label{ch:mesh-amr}
\chapterstatus{reference}
Every operator of the package acts on one composite mesh: the leaf cells of a
2:1-graded forest of quadtrees whose nodes are blocks of a fixed number of
Cartesian cells. This chapter defines the forest, the owned cells and face
segments that carry the unknowns, the halo slots that blocks keep for local
stencils, and when the mesh changes. The thesis uses the same kind of mesh
(block based, graded, refined by bisection of a uniform base
mesh~\thesis{\S2.5}) and refers to Van der Plas~\cite{vanderplas2017} for its
implementation and for the operators near refinement
interfaces~\thesis{\S2.2}; the definitions below are those of this package. The
mesh has no continuous counterpart and no time discretisation: it is constant
during a time step and changes only between steps (\cref{sec:mesh:regrid}).

\section{Domain}\label{sec:mesh:domain}
The domain is a rectangle,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:domain}
  \domain=\prod_{k=1}^{2}\roundpar{\domorigin{k},\,\domorigin{k}+\domextent{k}},
\end{equation}
and each direction $k$ is either periodic, with the lines
$\coordk{k}=\domorigin{k}$ and $\coordk{k}=\domorigin{k}+\domextent{k}$ identified, or
bounded by two walls that form the physical boundary
$\physbnd\subset\partial\domain$. The package is two-dimensional throughout.
An embedded solid does not change the mesh; its open measures are defined in
\cref{sec:setting:open}.
\begin{implnote}
\apitype{BlockTreeMesh} keywords \code{origin} $=(\domorigin{1},\domorigin{2})$,
\code{extent} $=(\domextent{1},\domextent{2})$ (positive) and \code{periodic}
(default \code{(true, true)}).
\end{implnote}

\section{Forest, blocks and levels}\label{sec:mesh:forest}
Per direction $k$ the mesh is fixed by the number of level-zero cells
$\basecells{k}$, the number of cells per block $\blocksize{k}$ (even and at
least two; $\basecells{k}$ is a multiple of $\blocksize{k}$) and the
refinement ceiling $\level\ge0$. The cell width of level $\lev$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:widths}
  \meshwidth{\lev}{k}=2^{-\lev}\basewidth{k},\qquad
  \basewidth{k}=\domextent{k}/\basecells{k},\qquad 0\le\lev\le\level .
\end{equation}
The forest $\forest$ consists of $\rootcount{1}\rootcount{2}$ quadtrees,
$\rootcount{k}=\basecells{k}/\blocksize{k}$, whose nodes are blocks. A block
$\blk$ is addressed by its key $\blockkey{\blk}=(\lev,i,j)$, with
$0\le i<2^{\lev}\rootcount{1}$ and $0\le j<2^{\lev}\rootcount{2}$, and covers
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:block-box}
  \blk=\prod_{k=1}^{2}\squarepar{\domorigin{k}+i_k\blocksize{k}\meshwidth{\lev}{k},\;
     \domorigin{k}+(i_k+1)\blocksize{k}\meshwidth{\lev}{k}},\qquad (i_1,i_2)=(i,j).
\end{equation}
The roots are the level-zero blocks $(0,i,j)$; they can be refined but never
removed. A refined block $(\lev,i,j)$ has the four children
$(\lev+1,2i+\iota,2j+\jmath)$, $\iota,\jmath\in\{0,1\}$: refinement bisects
both directions. The leaves $\leaves\subset\forest$, the blocks that are not
refined, tile $\domain$. A leaf $\blk$ is divided into
$\blocksize{1}\times\blocksize{2}$ congruent cells $c=(\blk,i,j)$,
$1\le i\le\blocksize{1}$, $1\le j\le\blocksize{2}$, counted from its lower
left corner, so that $\cellwidth{c}{k}=\meshwidth{\blocklevel{c}}{k}$ and
$\meas{c}=\cellwidth{c}{1}\cellwidth{c}{2}$. The cells are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:cells}
  \cells=\bigcup_{\lev=0}^{\level}\cellslev{\lev},\qquad
  \cellslev{\lev}=\set{(\blk,i,j)}{\blk\in\leaves,\ \blocklevel{\blk}=\lev},
\end{equation}
a disjoint union that tiles $\domain$; $\pos{c}$ is the centroid of $c$.

Leaves that touch differ by at most one level,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:grading}
  \abs{\blocklevel{\blk}-\blocklevel{\blk'}}\le1\quad
  \text{for all }\blk,\blk'\in\leaves\text{ with }\overline{\blk}\cap\overline{\blk'}\neq\emptyset,
\end{equation}
where the closures may meet in a corner only and across a periodic seam. The
code enforces~\cref{eq:mesh:grading} by refinement-only balancing: while a
leaf touches a leaf that is more than one level finer, the coarser one is
split. Balancing runs after the forest is built from the user's \code{refine}
function and after every regrid. \Cref{fig:mesh:forest} shows a forest in which
balancing refined a root that \code{refine} did not mark. Contact is decided on
the integer lattice of the block keys, scaled to the finer of the two levels and
wrapped across periodic seams, so the balance does not depend on the rounding of
block coordinates; a comparison of floating-point coordinates could miss blocks
that touch when the domain extents are not exactly representable, and violated
\cref{eq:mesh:grading} there until \issue{46} was fixed (PR~\#92). A periodic
direction with exactly two level-0 cells is supported: the displacement does not
inherit the sign tie of the half-period centre offset, because it takes its
normal component from the mean cell width, positive by the face orientation, and
only its tangential component from the wrap on that lattice
(\issue{36}, \issue{163};
\testsym{test/mesh/topology.jl}{periodic direction resolved by exactly two cells}).

The thesis writes $\minwidth=2^{-\level}\basemesh$ for the width of the finest
cells, with $\level$ the number of refinement levels~\thesis{\S2.5}. Here
$\level$ is only a ceiling: the finest level present, $\actlevel$, may be lower,
and widths are per direction. The thesis notation is recovered for
$\basewidth{1}=\basewidth{2}=\basemesh$ and $\actlevel=\level$.

The leaves are stored in an array sorted by the key components $(\lev,j,i)$.
A cell reference \apitype{CellRef}\code{(b, i, j)} uses the position \code{b} of its
block in this array; \code{b} changes whenever the leaf set changes, whereas
$\blockkey{\blk}$ is a stable address.

\section{Faces, hanging segments and stored unknowns}\label{sec:mesh:faces}
Faces are the pieces of cell sides shared by exactly one cell on either side.
On a grid line normal to $\unitvec{k}$, every nonempty intersection of a side
of a cell below the line with a side of a cell above it is an interior face
$f\in\facesint$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:face}
  \begin{aligned}
    &\cminus{f}:\ \text{the cell below},\quad \cplus{f}:\ \text{the cell above},\quad
    \fnormal{f}=\unitvec{k},\\
    &\meas{f}:\ \text{the length of the intersection},\quad \pos{f}:\ \text{its midpoint}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In a periodic direction the lines $\coordk{k}=\domorigin{k}$ and
$\coordk{k}=\domorigin{k}+\domextent{k}$ are one line: its faces (the seam) are
interior faces with $\cminus{f}$ the last and $\cplus{f}$ the first cell in
direction $k$, and $\pos{f}$ lies on $\coordk{k}=\domorigin{k}$. A cell side on a wall
is one boundary face $f\in\facesbnd$, with the single cell $\cbnd{f}$,
$\fnormal{f}=\unitvec{k}$ and outward side $\fside{f}=+1$ on
$\coordk{k}=\domorigin{k}+\domextent{k}$ and $\fside{f}=-1$ on $\coordk{k}=\domorigin{k}$.
Boundary faces are never split. All faces are $\faces=\facesint\cup\facesbnd$.

Blocks are aligned with the lattice of their level and touching leaves differ
by at most one level~\cref{eq:mesh:grading}, so every cell side is one face or
two. If $\cside{f}$ is the side of a coarse cell next to two cells one level
finer,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:hanging}
  \hanging{\cside{f}}=\{f_1,f_2\}\subset\facesint,\qquad
  f_1\cup f_2=\cside{f},\qquad \meas{f_1}=\meas{f_2}=\tfrac12\meas{\cside{f}},
\end{equation}
and the common endpoint of $f_1$ and $f_2$ is a \emph{hanging vertex}. The
side $\cside{f}$ only groups its segments: nothing is stored on it. A cell
thus has between four and eight faces $\faceset{c}$. As in
the thesis~\thesis{\S2.2.1}, the orientation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:orientation}
  \orient{c}{f}=\begin{cases}+1, & c=\cminus{f},\\ -1, & c=\cplus{f},\\
    \fside{f}, & f\in\facesbnd,\end{cases}
  \qquad
  \sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\orient{c}{f}=0\quad\text{for }f\in\facesint,
\end{equation}
makes $\orient{c}{f}\fnormal{f}$ the outward normal of $c$ on $f$.

Cell quantities are stored once per owned cell and face-normal quantities
once per owned face (\cref{fig:mesh:hanging}). The stored face value is the
coordinate component $\velo_f$ of~\cref{eq:setting:face-average}, also on
boundary faces, where the outward component is $\fside{f}\velo_f$. A quantity
of the coarse side, such as the flux through $\cside{f}$, is the sum over
$\hanging{\cside{f}}$. Because the operators of \cref{ch:discrete-setting}
evaluate each segment flux once and add it to both adjacent cells with
opposite orientation, and because all levels advance with the same time step,
these operators need no flux correction between levels (``refluxing'' in
level-subcycled AMR~\cite{berger1989}); none is implemented.

\section{Halo slots}\label{sec:mesh:halo}
Around its owned cells each leaf block stores $\halowidth\ge1$ layers of halo
slots (default $\halowidth=1$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:halo-slots}
  \halocells{\blk}=\set{(\blk,i,j)}{1-\halowidth\le i\le\blocksize{1}+\halowidth,\
    1-\halowidth\le j\le\blocksize{2}+\halowidth}\setminus\cells,
\end{equation}
which continue the block's own lattice: a slot $\hc$ has the width of the
block's cells and centre $\pos{\hc}$. Slots are not unknowns.
\apifun{fill_halo!} sets every slot whose centre lies in $\domain$ (after
periodic wrapping) from owned cells,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:halo-fill}
  \avar_{\hc}=\sum_{b\in\donors{\hc}}\haloweight{\hc}{b}\avar_b,\qquad
  \haloweight{\hc}{b}=\tfrac14\#\set{r}{\halosample{\hc}{r}\in b},\qquad
  \halosample{\hc}{r}=\pos{\hc}+\roundpar{\pm\tfrac14\meshwidth{\lev}{1},\,\pm\tfrac14\meshwidth{\lev}{2}},
\end{equation}
where $\lev=\blocklevel{\blk}$, the four sample points are wrapped in periodic
directions and $\donors{\hc}$ are the leaf cells that contain them. If
$\halowidth\le\blocksize{k}/2$, every slot lies in a leaf that touches $\blk$,
so by~\cref{eq:mesh:grading} only three cases occur: a slot of a same-level
neighbour copies that cell ($\haloweight{\hc}{b}=1$); a slot inside a coarser
cell copies the coarse value (piecewise-constant injection,
$\haloweight{\hc}{b}=1$); a slot covering four finer cells takes their mean
($\haloweight{\hc}{b}=\frac14$), which is their area average. The fixture of
\cref{fig:mesh:forest} has \chkHaloSame, \chkHaloCoarser\ and \chkHaloFiner\
slots of these kinds, \chkHaloPhysical\ physical slots and no other kind.

A slot whose centre lies beyond a wall is a physical slot. Its donor $b$ is
the leaf cell that contains $\clampdom{\pos{\hc}}$, the slot centre wrapped in
periodic directions and clamped to $\bar\domain$, and its value is set by the
boundary rule passed to \apifun{fill_halo!},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mesh:halo-physical}
  \avar_{\hc}=\avar_b\quad(\code{ZeroGradient}),\qquad
  \avar_{\hc}=\avar^{\physbnd}\roundpar{\pos{\hc},\timevar}\quad(\code{Dirichlet}),
\end{equation}
with $\avar^{\physbnd}$ the user function; a Dirichlet value is thus imposed at
the slot centre, half a cell beyond the wall, not on the boundary face. Whether
this is the intended semantics is under investigation
(\issue{43}).

The rule~\cref{eq:mesh:halo-fill} reproduces constants and is exact for the
average of finer cells, but its coarse-to-fine values are piecewise constant,
i.e.\ first-order accurate. The divergence, gradient and elliptic operators of
\cref{ch:discrete-setting} read owned cells through the face lists and never
use halo slots. Halos are read by, among others, the regrid transfer, the
interpolation-error refinement indicator, interface reconstruction and VOF
transport; their chapters state how.

\section{Regrid and schedule rebuild}\label{sec:mesh:regrid}
The forest changes only in \apifun{adapt!}, which receives one label per leaf
(refine, keep or coarsen) from the refinement indicators of
\cref{ch:amr-criteria-transfer}. Every leaf labelled \emph{refine} with
$\blocklevel{\blk}<\level$ is split. Four sibling leaves are merged into their
parent if all four are labelled \emph{coarsen}, none of them was created in
the last \code{coarsen_delay} steps, and the parent's level is at least
\code{minimum_level}. Refinement-only balancing then
restores~\cref{eq:mesh:grading}; it splits a merged parent again where needed.

If the leaf set changed, every derived structure is rebuilt from the forest:
the leaf array and block positions, the face lists $\facesint$ and $\facesbnd$
with their order, the dual volumes of \cref{sec:setting:measures} and the halo
schedule. Registered fields are transferred to the new cells and faces by the
maps of \cref{ch:amr-criteria-transfer}, and topology-dependent operators
(\cref{sec:setting:time}) are rebuilt. In \apifun{step!}\code{(flow, dt)}
the forest is adapted after the physical step, at most every
\code{update_interval} steps; time discretisation is not applicable to the
mesh itself.

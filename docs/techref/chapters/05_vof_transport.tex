\chapter{Volume-of-fluid and phase-scalar transport}\label{ch:vof-transport}
\chapterstatus[a2ac51b9109e59011e71253b0279367490676f3d]{reference}
The liquid fraction of \cref{sec:interface:fractions} is transported by a
directionally split geometric volume-of-fluid method of Weymouth and
Yue~\cite{weymouth2010} (WY)~\thesis{\S3.2.3}: one sweep per coordinate
direction, each moving time-integrated phase volumes through the owned faces,
with a dilation term weighted by a phase indicator that is frozen for the time
step, and a reconstruction of the interface between the sweeps. Intrinsic
phase scalars are carried by the same phase volumes. For the two-velocity
model the phase volumes come from one of two carriers: frozen phase subfaces,
each extruded with its own phase velocity, or a projected liquid velocity
with an auxiliary gas carrier. This chapter states the continuous balance,
the discrete balance and the geometric fluxes, the update of a sweep, the
bounds, snapping and time-step restriction, the phase scalars, both carriers,
and the time-integrated balances with the exact conditions under which they
conserve volume. The transport is a nonlinear map of the reconstruction
(\cref{ch:interface}) and is not derived from a method-of-lines
semi-discretisation; the face integrals are approximated in space and time at
once. The thesis transports mass and momentum with dimensionally unsplit
donating regions~\thesis{\S\S6.3--6.4}; the deviations are stated where they
arise. Staggered phase scalars and phase momentum, which are advanced in the
same sweeps, are the subject of \cref{ch:momentum-transport}.

\section{Continuous model}\label{sec:vof:continuous}
The open domain $\domain^{\open}$ (\cref{sec:setting:continuous}) is split into
the liquid and gas regions $\domain^{\phase}(\timevar)$, whose common boundary
$\interface(\timevar)$ moves with the normal velocity $\Velo^{\phase}\cdot\inormal$;
there is no phase change, so this normal velocity is continuous across
$\interface$~\thesis{eq.~(2.2)}. The one-velocity model has
$\Velo^{\liq}=\Velo^{\gas}=\Velo$; the two-velocity model has a velocity per
phase. With $\phasepart{\phase}{c}(\timevar)=\opencell{c}\cap\domain^{\phase}(\timevar)$
and $\phasepart{\phase}{f}(\timevar)=\opencell{f}\cap\domain^{\phase}(\timevar)$
the open parts of a cell and of a face occupied by phase $\phase$, the volume
of each phase in a fixed cell obeys, over one time step,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:cont-volume}
  \meas{\phasepart{\phase}{c}(\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}})}-\meas{\phasepart{\phase}{c}(\timevar^{\timelevel{n}})}
  +\int_{\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}}^{\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}}}\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}
    \integral{\phasepart{\phase}{f}(\timevar)}{\Velo^{\phase}\cdot\orient{c}{f}\fnormal{f}}{S}\dd\timevar=0
\end{equation}
\thesis{eqs.~(3.2) and (6.14)}; the solid boundary carries no flux
($\Velo\cdot\normal=0$, \cref{ch:cutcells}). A cell-averaged intrinsic phase
quantity $\avar^{\phase}$ (a phase density, a tracer concentration) is carried
by its phase,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:cont-scalar}
  \integral{\phasepart{\phase}{c}(\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}})}{\avar^{\phase}}{V}
  -\integral{\phasepart{\phase}{c}(\timevar^{\timelevel{n}})}{\avar^{\phase}}{V}
  +\int_{\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}}^{\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}}}\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}
    \integral{\phasepart{\phase}{f}(\timevar)}{\avar^{\phase}\,\Velo^{\phase}\cdot\orient{c}{f}\fnormal{f}}{S}\dd\timevar=0,
\end{equation}
which is the thesis' conservation law~\thesis{eq.~(6.2)} with the constant
$\dens^{\phase}$ divided out;
\cref{eq:vof:cont-volume} is the case $\avar^{\phase}=1$. Adding
\cref{eq:vof:cont-volume} for both phases and letting the step tend to zero
gives the mixture constraint
$\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\sum_{\phase}\integral{\phasepart{\phase}{f}}{\Velo^{\phase}\cdot\orient{c}{f}\fnormal{f}}{S}=0$~\thesis{eqs.~(2.5b) and (6.15)}.
On impermeable or periodic boundaries, summing \cref{eq:vof:cont-volume}
over all cells shows that the volume of each phase is constant.

\section{Discrete balance and phase fluxes}\label{sec:vof:balance}
The stored unknown is the liquid fraction per full cell $\phasefrac{\liq}{c}$;
the transport works with the conditional fraction
$\condfrac{\liq}{c}=\phasefrac{\liq}{c}/\openfrac{c}$ and the phase volume
$\meas{\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\phase}{c}=\meas{c}\phasefrac{\phase}{c}$,
$\condfrac{\gas}{c}=1-\condfrac{\liq}{c}$ (\cref{eq:interface:fractions}).
Writing the space--time integral over $f$ in~\cref{eq:vof:cont-volume} as
the time-integrated phase volume $\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\phase}_f$ that
crosses $f$ in the direction $\fnormal{f}$, the balance of every open cell is
the exact finite-volume statement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:balance}
  \meas{\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\phase,\timelevel{n+1}}{c}
  =\meas{\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\phase,\timelevel{n}}{c}
  -\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\orient{c}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\phase}_f ,
\end{equation}
and the scheme consists entirely in the geometric approximation of these
face volumes from the reconstruction (\cref{sec:vof:donating}) and in the
directional splitting (\cref{sec:vof:sweeps}). Every owned segment is
evaluated once and enters its two cells with opposite orientation, so the face
terms cancel in the sum over cells exactly, also at hanging segments and
periodic seams (\cref{eq:setting:gauss}); physical boundary faces carry no
volume (\cref{sec:vof:cfl}). A time discretisation separate from the spatial
one does not exist: the flux is a space--time integral evaluated at once, and
no method-of-lines form of the scheme is implemented.

\section{Directional sweeps}\label{sec:vof:sweeps}
A time step consists of two sweeps $j=1,2$ along the axes
$\sweepaxis{1}\neq\sweepaxis{2}$, $(\sweepaxis{1},\sweepaxis{2})\in\{(1,2),(2,1)\}$.
Before the first sweep the fractions are reconstructed (\cref{ch:interface})
and the indicator~\cref{eq:interface:indicator} is frozen,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:indicator}
  \indicator{\liq}_c=\begin{cases}1, & \condfrac{\liq,\timelevel{n}}{c}>\half,\\ 0, & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}
  \qquad \indicator{\gas}_c=1-\indicator{\liq}_c ,
\end{equation}
for both sweeps. With one velocity, the carrier volume of a face is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:carrier}
  \dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f=\dt\meas{f}\aperture{f}\velo_f ,
\end{equation}
the same in both sweeps ($\aperture{f}=1$ without a solid); the two-velocity
carriers replace it in \cref{sec:vof:two-velocity}. Sweep $j$ moves the faces
$f\in\facesaxis{\sweepaxis{j}}$ with the liquid volumes
$\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}_f$ cut from the reconstruction
after sweep $j-1$ (\cref{sec:vof:donating}) and updates every open cell,
$\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{0}}{c}=\condfrac{\liq,\timelevel{n}}{c}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:sweep}
  \meas{\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}
  =\meas{\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j-1}}{c}
  -\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{\sweepaxis{j}}}\orient{c}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}
    \roundpar{\volumeflux^{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}_f-\indicator{\liq}_c\volumeflux^{\open}_f}
  +\meas{\opencell{c}}\snapchange{j}{c},
\end{equation}
where $\snapchange{j}{c}$ is the change by clamping and snapping
(\cref{sec:vof:floor}); covered cells keep $\condfrac{\liq}{c}=0$. Then
$\phasefrac{\liq}{c}=\openfrac{c}\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$ and the
interface is reconstructed. This is the thesis'
sweep~\thesis{eq.~(3.46)} with the open volumes of the cut cells, the owned
segments of the composite mesh and the time-integrated volumes in place of
$\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux_f$: the term
$\indicator{\liq}_c\sum_{f}\orient{c}{f}\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f$ is the
dilation of the one-dimensional carrier, which is not divergence free.
Because the liquid and gas volumes of a face partition its carrier volume,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:partition}
  \dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}_f+\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}_f
  =\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f
\end{equation}
(exactly, by the construction of \cref{eq:vof:phase-flux}), the gas fraction
$1-\condfrac{\liq}{c}$ obeys~\cref{eq:vof:sweep} with $\liq$ and $\gas$
exchanged: in a cell with $\indicator{\liq}_c=0$ the liquid is moved
conservatively, in a cell with $\indicator{\liq}_c=1$ the gas, which is the
thesis' geometric reading of the dilation term~\thesis{eqs.~(3.48)--(3.49)}.
Summing the two sweeps gives, with
$\volumeflux^{\liq}_f=\volumeflux^{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}_f$ for
$f\in\facesaxis{\sweepaxis{j}}$ and $\meas{c}(\divh\volumeflux^{\open})_c=\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\orient{c}{f}\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f$
(\cref{eq:setting:divergence}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:step}
  \meas{\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\liq,\timelevel{n+1}}{c}
  =\meas{\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\liq,\timelevel{n}}{c}
  -\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\orient{c}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\liq}_f
  +\indicator{\liq}_c\,\dt\meas{c}(\divh\volumeflux^{\open})_c
  +\meas{\opencell{c}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\snapchange{j}{c},
\end{equation}
the discrete balance~\cref{eq:vof:balance} plus the dilation of the full
carrier and the snapped volume~\thesis{\S3.2.3}. With
$\meas{c}(\divh\volumeflux^{\open})_c=\meas{\opencell{c}}(\divopen\velo)_c$ for one
velocity, the dilation vanishes in every cell in which the transport velocity
is discretely divergence free (\cref{sec:vof:conservation}).
\Cref{fig:vof:sweeps} shows a step; there the dilation of a sweep reaches
$\chkVofSweepDilation$ of a cell volume and cancels over the two sweeps.

In a flow simulation the transport velocity is the projected velocity of the
previous step with the boundary values enforced, and the order alternates,
$(1,2)$ in the first step, $(2,1)$ in the second, and so on; \apifun{advect!}
takes the order as a keyword (default $(1,2)$). Before each sweep the halos of
the phase scalars are filled (zero gradient at walls, \cref{sec:mesh:halo});
the fractions need no halo for the fluxes, which read only the two cells of a
face.

\section{Donating regions and phase volumes}\label{sec:vof:donating}
Let $f\in\facesaxis{k}$ be swept with carrier velocity $\velo_f\neq0$. Its
donor is the upwind cell,
$\donor{f}=\cminus{f}$ if $\velo_f\ge0$ and $\donor{f}=\cplus{f}$ otherwise,
so that $\orient{\donor{f}}{f}=\operatorname{sgn}(\velo_f)$. In the local
coordinates $\lcoord$ of the donor (\cref{sec:interface:plic}), $f$ occupies
the side $(\lcoord)_k=\half\orient{\donor{f}}{f}$ over its tangential extent
$\rlo{t}\le(\lcoord)_t\le\rhi{t}$, $t\neq k$ (a half side if the donor is the
coarse cell at a level transition, \cref{eq:mesh:hanging}). The donating
rectangle of depth $\hat d$ is the strip of the donor along that side,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:rectangle}
  \drect{f}(\hat d)=\set{\lcoord}{\half-\hat d\le\orient{\donor{f}}{f}(\lcoord)_k\le\half,\
    \rlo{t}\le(\lcoord)_t\le\rhi{t}},\qquad 0\le\hat d\le1,
\end{equation}
and its depth is fixed by the carrier volume,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:depth}
  \drdepth{f}=\begin{cases}
    \dt\abs{\velo_f}/\cellwidth{\donor{f}}{k}, & \text{no solid},\\
    \aperture{f}\dt\abs{\velo_f}/\cellwidth{\donor{f}}{k}, & \text{solid present, }\solidfrac{\donor{f}}=0,\\
    \text{root of }\meas{\drect{f}(\hat d)\cap\localpart{\open}{\donor{f}}}\meas{\donor{f}}
      =\abs{\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f}, & 0<\solidfrac{\donor{f}}<1 .
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In the first two cases the area of $\drect{f}(\drdepth{f})$ times
$\meas{\donor{f}}$ is the carrier volume (the second is clamped to
$\hat d\le1$); in a pure-gas or pure-liquid donor the rectangle is not
needed. In a mixed cut donor the depth is found by 54 bisection steps on
$[0,1]$, the open area being evaluated with the solid half-plane
(\cref{eq:cutcells:solid}); if the carrier volume exceeds the open area of
the whole strip $\drect{f}(1)$, both in units of $\meas{\donor{f}}$, by more
than $256\machineeps$ times the larger of one and that area, the transport
stops with an error. The donating region is the open part of the rectangle
in physical coordinates,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:region}
  \dr_f=\set{\pos{\donor{f}}+\cellscale{\donor{f}}\lcoord}{\lcoord\in\drect{f}(\drdepth{f})\cap\localpart{\open}{\donor{f}}},
\end{equation}
and the liquid fraction of the swept volume is that of the donor's
reconstruction in it,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:swept-fraction}
  \sweptfrac{f}=\begin{cases}
    \condfrac{\liq}{\donor{f}}, & \condfrac{\liq}{\donor{f}}\in\{0,1\},\\
    \planearea\roundpar{\lnormal{\donor{f}},\lconst{\donor{f}};\drect{f}(\drdepth{f})}, & \solidfrac{\donor{f}}=0,\\
    \operatorname{clamp}\roundpar{\meas{\drect{f}(\drdepth{f})\cap\localpart{\liq}{\donor{f}}}\meas{\donor{f}}\big/\abs{\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f},\,0,\,1}, & 0<\solidfrac{\donor{f}}<1,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with the rectangle-area function~\cref{eq:interface:area} in uncut donors and
Sutherland--Hodgman clipping of the rectangle by the solid and the liquid
half-planes in cut donors ($\localpart{\liq}{c}$ of~\cref{eq:interface:plane}).
The phase volumes of the face are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:phase-flux}
  \dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}_f=\sweptfrac{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f,
  \qquad
  \dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}_f=\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f-\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}_f ,
\end{equation}
both zero if $\velo_f=0$. In an uncut donor
$\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\liq}_f$ is the liquid volume of the reconstruction
inside $\dr_f$, the thesis' flux~\thesis{eq.~(3.45)}. On the composite mesh
each owned segment has its own rectangle: a coarse donor whose side carries two
segments has two rectangles, each over half of the side, with the depths of
the two segment velocities, whereas a fine donor sweeps its whole side
(\cref{fig:vof:donating:coarse-fine}). The tangential offset of the segment is
wrapped across periodic seams.

\emph{Limitation at level transitions.} The bisection requires that the open
part of the strip $\drect{f}(1)$ hold the carrier volume. For uncut donors and
for cut donors whose sides normal to $\unitvec{k}$ are single faces this
follows from the outgoing bound of \cref{eq:vof:cfl} (the open part of the
strip is then $\meas{\opencell{\donor{f}}}$ or, for an uncut donor, the
rectangle itself). For a coarse cut donor the strip behind one segment is
half of the cell and its open part can be much smaller than the aperture of
the segment suggests. In the fixture of \code{strip_limitation_check}
(\code{figures/vof.jl}), a coarse cell with open fraction
$\chkVofStripOpen$ next to two finer cells, the segment with
$\aperture{f}=\chkVofStripAperture$ must sweep $\chkVofStripTarget$ of the
coarse volume at $\dt=\dtwy=\chkVofStripDt$, but its half-strip has only
$\chkVofStripArea$ open, so \apifun{advect!} stops with the error at the time
step that \apifun{stable_timestep} returns (the donor is mixed: in the one-velocity path pure donors need no rectangle, whereas the frozen carrier
evaluates the strip over each phase subface also in pure donors and can refuse a step on a single-level cut
mesh as well); the largest accepted step is $\dtwy$ times the ratio of the open area of the strip to the carrier volume of the
segment ($0.947$ in this fixture; see \issue{56}).

\emph{Checks} (\code{figures/vof.jl}, \code{one_velocity_flux_check}). An
independent evaluation of~\cref{eq:vof:rectangle,eq:vof:depth,eq:vof:region,eq:vof:swept-fraction,eq:vof:phase-flux}
by polygon clipping with its own bisection, on four fixtures at $\dt=\dtwy$,
reproduces \code{liquid_flux} on \chkVofFluxUniformFaces\ faces between cells
of one level without solid (\chkVofFluxUniformMixed\ with a mixed donor) to
$\chkVofFluxUniformDiff\meas{\donor{f}}$, on \chkVofFluxCoarseFaces\ faces at
level transitions (\chkVofFluxCoarseMixed\ mixed donors) to
$\chkVofFluxCoarseDiff\meas{\donor{f}}$ and on \chkVofFluxCutFaces\ faces with
$\aperture{f}<1$ or a cut donor (\chkVofFluxCutMixed\ mixed cut donors) to
$\chkVofFluxCutDiff\meas{\donor{f}}$; the partition~\cref{eq:vof:partition}
holds to $\chkVofPartition$.

\begin{deviation}{Donating regions in cut cells and at level transitions}
The thesis' split donating region is the extrusion of the face by
$\dt\abs{\velo_f}$ on a conforming mesh~\thesis{eq.~(3.45)}. The code
extrudes each owned segment in units of its donor's width and, with a solid,
chooses the depth so that the open part of the rectangle has the carrier
volume~\cref{eq:vof:depth}: in cut donors by inverting the clipped area, in
uncut donors by shortening the rectangle by $\aperture{f}$. This is the
thesis' enforcement of donating-region volumes~\thesis{eq.~(6.38)} applied to
the solid; it makes the phase volumes a bounded partition of the carrier
volume~\cref{eq:vof:partition}.
\end{deviation}

\section{Bounds, clamping and snapping}\label{sec:vof:floor}
After the update of a sweep every open cell is checked and the fraction is
clamped and snapped with the fraction floor $\ffloor$
(\cref{eq:interface:floor}). With $\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$ the
right-hand side of~\cref{eq:vof:sweep} without $\snapchange{j}{c}$, the code
raises an error if the liquid volume
$\meas{\opencell{c}}\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$ leaves
$[0,\meas{\opencell{c}}]$ by more than $128\machineeps\meas{c}$
(\srcsym{src/vof/fluxes.jl}{_wy_fraction_in_bounds}): the round-off of the
clipped and summed volumes is measured in units of the full cell, not
amplified by a small open fraction (since \issue{55}), while genuine
violations, such as those of \issue{56}, still raise. Otherwise it clamps
$\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$ to $[0,1]$ and snaps it, which together give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:snap}
  \condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}=\begin{cases}
    0, & \preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}\le\ffloor,\\
    1, & \preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}\ge1-\ffloor,\\
    \preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad \snapchange{j}{c}=\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}-\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c} .
\end{equation}
The reconstruction that follows applies~\cref{eq:interface:floor} again,
which leaves these values unchanged up to rounding. Each snap changes the
liquid volume by at most $\ffloor\meas{\opencell{c}}$, each clamp by at most
$128\machineeps\meas{c}$, and both enter the balance
\cref{eq:vof:step} (\cref{dev:interface:floor}).

\emph{Boundedness.}\phantomsection\label{prop:vof:boundedness} The run-time check is the only guarantee in general. The
argument of the thesis' boundedness lemma~\thesis{Lemma~3.3} carries over when,
in the sweep, the rectangles of the faces through which a cell donates are
pairwise disjoint: the outflow of the phase that the cell moves
conservatively (liquid if $\indicator{\liq}_c=0$, gas otherwise) is then at
most the volume of that phase in the reconstruction, so that phase fraction
stays non-negative, and its inflow over both sweeps is at most
$\cflin{c}\le\wycflval\le\half$ of the cell (\cref{eq:vof:cfl}), so it stays
at most $\half+\cflin{c}\le1$; up to rounding and to the at most $\ffloor$ added
by a snap after the first sweep, $0\le C\le1$ before clamping. The
rectangles are disjoint in uncut donors, where $\drdepth{f}\le\wycflval\le\half$
by the extrusion bound, so rectangles of opposite sides lie in opposite halves
and those of the two segments of one side have disjoint tangential extents.
They are also disjoint in a cut donor whose two sides normal to $\unitvec{k}$ are single faces
when the rectangles span the whole side (one-velocity and projected carriers): two overlapping
full-width rectangles would cover the cell, so their open volumes would sum to at least
$\meas{\opencell{c}}$, whereas the outgoing bound limits them to
$\wycflval\meas{\opencell{c}}<\meas{\opencell{c}}$. Neither argument applies to a cut coarse
donor with two segments on a side, nor to the frozen carrier in cut donors, whose subface
rectangles span only part of the side, and there the bound can fail: if the donor's own plane
closes a side (trace $0$) while the uncut neighbour behind it is open (trace $1$), the face has
$\aperture{f}=\half$, the rectangle of such a segment can reach almost through the cell, overlap the
rectangle of the opposite face in the open moved phase and make its outflow exceed its volume with

(\issue{56} collects explicit
configurations at the stable step, for one velocity and for the frozen carrier).

far corner, $\cflout{c}=\half$ gives $\drdepth{f}=1-\epsilon+\epsilon/\sqrt2\to1$. The bound
$\cflin{c}\le\half$ cannot be relaxed: a cell with $\condfrac{\liq,\timelevel{n}}{c}=\half$ that receives

\section{Time-step restriction}\label{sec:vof:cfl}
For a carrier with face rates $\meas{f}\aperture{f}\velo_f$ the code bounds,
in every open cell and on every face,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:cfl}
  \begin{gathered}
  \cflin{c}=\frac{\dt}{\meas{\opencell{c}}}\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\posflux{-\orient{c}{f}\meas{f}\aperture{f}\velo_f},\qquad
  \cflout{c}=\frac{\dt}{\meas{\opencell{c}}}\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\posflux{\orient{c}{f}\meas{f}\aperture{f}\velo_f},\\
  \cflext{f}=\frac{\dt\abs{\velo_f}}{\cellwidth{\donor{f}}{\faxis{f}}},
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
summed over the faces of both axes, by $\wycflval$. \apifun{stable_timestep}
returns the largest such step,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:dtwy}
  \dtwy=\wycflval\Big/\max\curlypar{\max_{c}\frac{\cflin{c}}{\dt},\,\max_{c}\frac{\cflout{c}}{\dt},\,\max_{f}\frac{\cflext{f}}{\dt}},
  \qquad 0<\wycflval\le\half\ (\text{default }\half),
\end{equation}
and $\infty$ for a vanishing velocity; covered cells do not enter. It also
requires a finite velocity with $\velo_f=0$ on every physical boundary face
(``impermeable or periodic boundaries''); inflow and outflow boundaries are
not supported by the VOF transport. \apifun{advect!} checks
$\dt\le(1+10^{-6}+32\machineeps)\dtwy$ with $\wycflval=\half$ unless
\code{check_timestep=false}. The extrusion bound keeps every uncut rectangle
in the half of its donor next to the face: for a segment on a coarse donor,
whose length is half the side, the thesis' bound on $\dt\meas{f}\abs{\velo_f}$
would allow a depth of a whole cell.

In a flow simulation, \apifun{step!} checks
$\dt\le(1+10^{-6}+32\machineeps)\min\curlypar{\dtwy,\dtcap,\dtgrav}$ (\cref{eq:stab:check}; the $10^{-6}$ admits the stretched last step of \issue{98}) with
$\wycflval=\half$ for the velocity it transports (the frozen two-velocity
carrier skips this check when \apifun{simulate!} has just evaluated the bound
for the same state), where, with
$h=\min_{c,k}\cellwidth{c}{k}$ (\cref{ch:stability-accuracy}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:dt-forces}
  \dtcap=\sqrt{\frac{(\dens^{\liq}+\dens^{\gas})h^3}{4\pi\surftens}}\ \ (\surftens>0),\qquad
  \dtgrav=\sqrt{\frac{(\dens^{\liq}+\dens^{\gas})h}{2\abs{\dens^{\liq}-\dens^{\gas}}\abs{\gravacc}}}
\end{equation}
($\abs{\gravacc}>0$, $\dens^{\liq}\neq\dens^{\gas}$),
and $\infty$ otherwise. Without a prescribed step, \apifun{simulate!}
evaluates these bounds with $\wycflval=0.45$ (keyword \code{cfl}) for the
boundary-enforced velocity at the start of every step and takes
$\dt^{\timelevel{n}}=\min\curlypar{\dt^{\timelevel{n-1}},\dtwy,\dtcap,\dtgrav}$: the step
never grows; an infinite first bound gives a step of $1/128$ of the requested
interval, and the last step is shortened to end at the final time.

\begin{sloppypar}
\emph{Checks} (\code{cfl_check}). An independent evaluation
of~\cref{eq:vof:cfl,eq:vof:dtwy} equals \apifun{stable_timestep} to
$\chkVofCflDiff$ (relative) on the uniform, composite and cut fixtures,
\apifun{advect!} accepts $\dtwy$ and rejects $(1+2\times10^{-6})\dtwy$, and the
deepest uncut rectangle at $\dtwy$ has $\drdepth{f}=\chkVofCflDepth$. On the
cut fixture with the plain streamfunction velocity of
\cref{sec:vof:conservation} the outgoing bound of the smallest cut cell
($\openfrac{c}=\chkVofCutSmallest$) gives $\dtwy=\chkVofCutDt$, whereas the
extrusion bound alone would allow $\chkVofCutExtrusionDt$: small cut cells
reduce the step, since no cells are merged.
\end{sloppypar}

\section{Phase scalars}\label{sec:vof:scalars}
A \apitype{PhaseScalar} stores intrinsic values $\avar^{\liq}_c$ and
$\avar^{\gas}_c$; the conservative amounts
$\meas{\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\phase}{c}\avar^{\phase}_c$ exist only during an
update, and \apifun{integrate}\code{(q, phase)} returns their sum. The values
weighting the dilation are frozen at the start of the step,
$\indicator{\phase}_c\avar^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}_c$. Sweep $j$ updates the amount
with the phase volumes of the fraction update,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:scalar}
  \meas{\opencell{c}}(\condfrac{\phase}{c}\avar^{\phase}_c)^{\splitlevel{j}}
  =\meas{\opencell{c}}(\condfrac{\phase}{c}\avar^{\phase}_c)^{\splitlevel{j-1}}
  -\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{\sweepaxis{j}}}\orient{c}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}
    \roundpar{\volumeflux^{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}_f(\fluxinterp\avar^{\phase})_f
      -\indicator{\phase}_c\avar^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}_c\volumeflux^{\open}_f}
\end{equation}
($\condfrac{\gas}{}=1-\condfrac{\liq}{}$ and $\volumeflux^{\gas}$
of~\cref{eq:vof:phase-flux}). The code stores it in fluctuation form: with the
snapped fraction $\condfrac{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$,
\begin{multline}\label{eq:vof:fluctuation}
  \avar^{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}_c=\avar^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_c
  +\frac{1}{\meas{\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{c}}
  \sum_{f\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{\sweepaxis{j}}}\orient{c}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}
  \Big(\volumeflux^{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}_f\roundpar{\avar^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_c-(\fluxinterp\avar^{\phase})_f}\\
    +\indicator{\phase}_c\volumeflux^{\open}_f\roundpar{\avar^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}_c-\avar^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_c}\Big)
\end{multline}
if $\condfrac{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{c}>\ffloor$, and
$\avar^{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}_c=\avar^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_c$
otherwise. Without snapping~\cref{eq:vof:fluctuation} equals~\cref{eq:vof:scalar}: it is an algebraic
rearrangement. Let $\delta\condfrac{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{c}\defeq
\condfrac{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{c}-\preclamp{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$ be the change of the
phase fraction by clamping and snapping; it is $\snapchange{j}{c}$ for the liquid, $-\snapchange{j}{c}$
for the gas (the complement of the liquid), and for the auxiliary gas carrier
of \cref{sec:vof:projected} the change by the clamp and the snap to zero. If the snapped fraction
exceeds $\ffloor$, the amount after the sweep exceeds~\cref{eq:vof:scalar} by
$\meas{\opencell{c}}\,\delta\condfrac{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{c}\,\avar^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_c$,
so a snap to a positive fraction leaves the snapped volume with the value
$\avar^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_c$. A snap to zero removes the amount that~\cref{eq:vof:scalar}
assigns to the cell; since all faces of such a cell use upwind states, this is of order
$\ffloor\meas{\opencell{c}}\max\abs{\avar}$. A constant $\avar^{\phase}$ gives a zero increment, so
constants are preserved exactly (bitwise), also across level transitions, in cut cells and when
fractions snap.

The face value is the upwind value near the interface and the selected scheme
in the bulk. Let $c'$ be the cell of $f$ upwind of the phase volume
$\volumeflux^{\phase}_f$ ($\cminus{f}$ if it is non-negative), and
$\avar^{\mathrm{u}}$, $\avar^{\mathrm{d}}$ and $\avar^{\mathrm{uu}}$ the values
of $c'$, of the other cell of $f$ and of the slot
$c'-\orient{c'}{f}\unitvec{k}$ beyond $c'$ (a halo slot at block borders). The
face is \emph{near the interface} if a cell $c\in\cellset{f}$ has
$\min\{\condfrac{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}{c},\preclamp{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{c}\}<1-64\machineeps$; then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:face-state}
  (\fluxinterp\avar^{\phase})_f=\begin{cases}
    \avar^{\mathrm{u}}, & \text{near the interface or \apitype{Upwind}},\\
    \half(\avar^{\mathrm{u}}+\avar^{\mathrm{d}}), & \text{\apitype{Centred}},\\
    \avar^{\mathrm{LW}}=\avar^{\mathrm{u}}+\half(1-\mathcfl_f)(\avar^{\mathrm{d}}-\avar^{\mathrm{u}}), & \text{\apitype{LaxWendroff}},\\
    \avar^{\mathrm{u}}+\limiter{\mathrm{M}}(\limratio{f})(\avar^{\mathrm{LW}}-\avar^{\mathrm{u}}), & \text{three-point scheme M},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\preclamp{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$ is the fraction after the sweep before
clamping~\cref{eq:vof:snap} (for the auxiliary gas carrier of
\cref{sec:vof:projected}, $\auxgas{}{c}$ before and after the sweep),
$\mathcfl_f=\abs{\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\phase}_f}/\min_{c\in\cellset{f}}\meas{\opencell{c}}$
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:limiter}
  \limratio{f}=\frac{\cellwidth{\cminus{f}}{k}+\cellwidth{\cplus{f}}{k}}{2\cellwidth{c'}{k}}\,
    \frac{\avar^{\mathrm{u}}-\avar^{\mathrm{uu}}}{\avar^{\mathrm{d}}-\avar^{\mathrm{u}}},
\end{equation}
with $\limratio{f}=1$ if both differences are at most
$16\machineeps\max(1,\abs{\avar^{\mathrm{uu}}},\abs{\avar^{\mathrm{u}}},\abs{\avar^{\mathrm{d}}})$
and $\limratio{f}=0$ if only the downstream one is. The limiters are those of
\cref{tab:vof:limiters}~\cite{sweby1984}. Faces between levels use the scheme
\code{amr_interface_scheme}, by default \code{MonotonizedCentral} for
\code{LaxWendroff} and \code{Centred} and the scheme itself otherwise; an
upwind interface scheme requires \code{Upwind}. \apifun{advect!} defaults to
\code{LaxWendroff}; a flow simulation passes its momentum scheme
(\cref{ch:momentum-transport}). Staggered phase scalars and phase momentum
are advanced in the same sweeps with interpolants of these phase volumes
(\cref{ch:momentum-transport}).

\section{Two-velocity carriers}\label{sec:vof:two-velocity}
\begin{sloppypar}
With intrinsic phase velocities $\velo^{\liq}_f$, $\velo^{\gas}_f$, absent
(\code{NaN}) where a phase has no staggered volume, the keyword
\code{interface_transport} of \apitype{TwoVelocityModel} and of
\apitype{WYAdvectionWorkspace} selects the carrier of the sweeps, either
\apitype{FrozenInterfaceFluxes} (the default) or
\apitype{ProjectedLiquidVelocity}; both use the update~\cref{eq:vof:sweep} for
the physical liquid fraction. The projected carrier
(\cref{sec:vof:projected}) is a legacy option, kept for comparison: the frozen
interface fluxes with the CMDGFM projection of \cref{ch:projection} are the
main two-velocity method.
\end{sloppypar}

\subsection{Frozen interface fluxes}\label{sec:vof:frozen}
The phase apertures $\phaseaperture{\liq}{f}$, $\phaseaperture{\gas}{f}$ of the
reconstruction at the start of the step (\cref{eq:interface:phase-aperture})
are frozen for both sweeps. In a flow simulation they are those of the
preceding physical projection, or of the projection after an intervening
regrid. Each face is divided into an open interval and two phase
subfaces. In the local tangential coordinate of the donor, with
$w=\rhi{t}-\rlo{t}$, the open interval lies at the end towards which the
averaged tangential solid normal points, and the liquid subface at the end
towards which the averaged tangential interface normal does not point,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:subface}
  \begin{gathered}
  \opencell{f}=\begin{cases}[\rhi{t}-\aperture{f}w,\rhi{t}], & (\snormal{\cminus{f}}+\snormal{\cplus{f}})_t>0,\\
    [\rlo{t},\rlo{t}+\aperture{f}w], & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}
  \qquad
  \phasepart{\phase}{f}\subseteq\opencell{f},\\
  \meas{\phasepart{\phase}{f}}=\min\curlypar{\phaseaperture{\phase}{f},\aperture{f}}w,\\
  \phasepart{\liq}{f}\text{ at the lower end of }\opencell{f}\text{ iff }(\lnormal{\cminus{f}}+\lnormal{\cplus{f}})_t\ge0,
  \qquad\phasepart{\gas}{f}\text{ at the other end},
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $\phasepart{\phase}{f}=\opencell{f}$ if
$\phaseaperture{\phase}{f}\ge\aperture{f}$. The stored local normals of both
cells enter (in pure cells those of the WY stencil); the solid's are those
of~\cref{eq:cutcells:solid}; the widths are frozen, the positions follow the
current reconstruction. Every phase with $\phaseaperture{\phase}{f}>0$ and
$\velo^{\phase}_f\ne0$ sweeps the rectangle~\cref{eq:vof:rectangle} over
$\phasepart{\phase}{f}$, upwind of $\velo^{\phase}_f$, whose open part has the
volume $\dt\meas{f}\phaseaperture{\phase}{f}\abs{\velo^{\phase}_f}$ (bisection
whenever a solid is present, also in pure donors, with the error of
\cref{sec:vof:donating} if the strip over the subface is too small; otherwise
$\drdepth{f}=\dt\abs{\velo^{\phase}_f}/\cellwidth{\donor{f}}{k}$); its liquid
fraction $\sweptfrac{f,\phase}$ follows from~\cref{eq:vof:swept-fraction},
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:frozen-flux}
  \dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f=\sum_{\phase}\dt\meas{f}\phaseaperture{\phase}{f}\velo^{\phase}_f,\qquad
  \dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\liq}_f=\sum_{\phase}\sweptfrac{f,\phase}\,\dt\meas{f}\phaseaperture{\phase}{f}\velo^{\phase}_f,
\end{equation}
with $\volumeflux^{\gas}_f$ from~\cref{eq:vof:partition}. The liquid volume of
a face thus includes liquid swept through the gas subface and vice versa
(\cref{fig:vof:frozen}). The fractions then follow~\cref{eq:vof:sweep} with
this carrier, and the gas is the complement. \phantomsection\label{prop:vof:frozen-dilation}The dilation of a step in a cell $c$ with $\indicator{\liq}_c=1$ is
$\dt\sum_{f}\orient{c}{f}\meas{f}(\phaseaperture{\liq}{f}\velo^{\liq}_f+\phaseaperture{\gas}{f}\velo^{\gas}_f)$,
$\dt$ times the discrete mixture divergence of $c$ on the frozen apertures~\thesis{eq.~(6.19)}: it
vanishes exactly when the constraint holds in $c$; in a cell with $\indicator{\liq}_c=0$ there is
no dilation whatever the divergence. Of the two sweeps only the sum vanishes. The two-velocity
projections impose the constraint, up to their tolerance, on the phase apertures of the
reconstruction at the time of the projection (\cref{ch:projection}). In a flow
simulation, if AMR changes the topology after that projection, the transferred
velocities are projected again on the new apertures before the next transport
step; the transferred physical pressure is kept. Standalone \apifun{adapt!} does
not project, so a passive transport caller must impose the constraint. The time-step
bound is~\cref{eq:vof:cfl} with the rate of each face replaced by the rates of
its two subfaces,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:frozen-cfl}
  \cflin{c}=\frac{\dt}{\meas{\opencell{c}}}\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\sum_{\phase}\posflux{-\orient{c}{f}\meas{f}\phaseaperture{\phase}{f}\velo^{\phase}_f},\qquad
  \cflext{f}=\max_{\phase:\,\phaseaperture{\phase}{f}>0}\frac{\dt\abs{\velo^{\phase}_f}}{\cellwidth{\donor{f}}{\faxis{f}}},
\end{equation}
$\cflout{c}$ likewise; it validates only active phase velocities and requires
$\velo^{\phase}_f=0$ on the boundary faces of cells that contain the phase.
Without a solid and with $\velo^{\liq}_f=\velo^{\gas}_f$ on every face the two subface rectangles tile
the one-velocity rectangle: their widths add up to the segment because
$\phaseaperture{\liq}{f}+\phaseaperture{\gas}{f}=\aperture{f}=1$ (to rounding), both have the depth
$\dt\abs{\velo_f}/\cellwidth{\donor{f}}{k}$, and in the second sweep the widths are still the frozen ones
while the positions follow the current reconstruction. The carrier, the phase volumes and the
time-step bound then coincide with those of \cref{sec:vof:donating} up to rounding. With a solid
the subfaces lie in the open interval of the segment and each has the depth that holds its own volume
in its own open strip, whereas the one-velocity rectangle spans the whole segment with one depth, so
the fluxes differ.

\subsection{Projected liquid velocity and auxiliary gas carrier}\label{sec:vof:projected}
Once per step the liquid velocity is extended and projected. A face is
\emph{known} for the liquid if its staggered conditional fraction is positive,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:staggered-fraction}
  \stagfrac{\liq}{f}=\frac{\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\liq}{c}}
    {\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}}
  \quad\roundpar{=\frac{\cellwidth{\cminus{f}}{k}\condfrac{\liq}{\cminus{f}}+\cellwidth{\cplus{f}}{k}\condfrac{\liq}{\cplus{f}}}{\cellwidth{\cminus{f}}{k}+\cellwidth{\cplus{f}}{k}}\text{ without solid}},
\end{equation}
zero if the open staggered volume vanishes~\cref{eq:cutcells:dual}
(\thesis{eq.~(2.36)}), and for the gas if $\stagfrac{\liq}{f}<1$. In
$\extlayers$ passes (default 3) every unknown face of an axis that is
connected to known faces of that axis by the staggered transport schedule
(\cref{ch:momentum-transport}) receives the mean of their values and becomes
known; the faces still unknown get zero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:extension}
  \extvelo{\phase}_f=\begin{cases}
    \velo^{\phase}_f, & f\text{ known},\\
    \frac{1}{\#N(f)}\sum_{g\in N(f)}\extvelo{\phase}_g, & N(f)\neq\emptyset\text{ in one of the passes},\\
    0, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $N(f)$ the known neighbours at the start of the pass, and boundary values
zero. With the face coefficient $\facecoef_f=(\aperture{f})^2$ where
$\stagfrac{\liq}{f}>0$ and $\facecoef_f=0$ elsewhere, and the cells with
$\condfrac{\liq}{c}>0$ active, the projected liquid velocity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:projection}
  \projvelo^{\liq}=\extvelo{\liq}-\gradopen_{\facecoef}\projpres,\qquad
  -\roundpar{\divopen\gradopen_{\facecoef}\projpres}_c=-(\divopen\extvelo{\liq})_c\ \ (\condfrac{\liq}{c}>0),\qquad
  \projpres_c=0\ \ (\condfrac{\liq}{c}=0),
\end{equation}
with the cut-cell operators of \cref{sec:setting:elliptic} ($\divh$ and
$\gradw{\facecoef}$ without solid). The elliptic solve
(\cref{ch:solvers}) stops when
$\max_{c:\,\condfrac{\liq}{c}>0}\abs{(\divopen\projvelo^{\liq})_c}\le\max(\projtol,\cdot)$,
the largest strong residual, with $\projtol=\sqrt{\machineeps}$ by default,
a round-off floor and at most 200 cycles; failure is an error, and without
active cells $\projvelo^{\liq}=\extvelo{\liq}$. The faces with
$\facecoef_f=0$ keep their extended value.

The sweeps then run with two carriers. The liquid uses \cref{sec:vof:donating}
with $\velo_f=\projvelo^{\liq}_f$: carrier
$\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f=\dt\meas{f}\aperture{f}\projvelo^{\liq}_f$,
liquid volume from the rectangle of $\projvelo^{\liq}_f$, fraction
update~\cref{eq:vof:sweep}. The gas volume is cut independently from the
rectangle of $\extvelo{\gas}_f$,
$\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\gas}_f=(1-\sweptfrac{f}[\extvelo{\gas}])\,\dt\meas{f}\aperture{f}\extvelo{\gas}_f$,
so~\cref{eq:vof:partition} does not hold, and it moves an auxiliary carrier
that is reset to the physical complement at the start of every sweep and has
no dilation term,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:aux-gas}
  \auxgas{}{c}\leftarrow1-\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j-1}}{c},\qquad
  \meas{\opencell{c}}\auxgas{,\splitlevel{j}}{c}=\meas{\opencell{c}}\auxgas{}{c}
  -\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{\sweepaxis{j}}}\orient{c}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}_f ,
\end{equation}
with an error if its volume $\meas{\opencell{c}}\auxgas{,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$ is
below $-128\machineeps\meas{c}$ (\srcsym{src/vof/fluxes.jl}{_wy_negative_fraction_out_of_bounds};
it has no upper bound); it is then clamped at zero and set to zero at or
below $\ffloor$. The gas scalars use~\cref{eq:vof:scalar,eq:vof:fluctuation}
with $\condfrac{\gas}{c}$ replaced by $\auxgas{}{c}$ and
$\indicator{\gas}_c$ by zero. At each reset the gas amount changes by
$\meas{\opencell{c}}(1-\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}-\auxgas{,\splitlevel{j}}{c})\avar^{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}_c$;
\apifun{phase_flux_diagnostics} reports the accumulated discrepancies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:reset}
  \gasreset=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{c}\meas{\opencell{c}}\roundpar{\auxgas{,\splitlevel{j}}{c}-1+\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}},\qquad
  \abs{\gasreset}=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{c}\meas{\opencell{c}}\abs{\auxgas{,\splitlevel{j}}{c}-1+\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}},
\end{equation}
together with the initial and final divergence and the cycles of the
projection. The time step is bounded by
$\min\{\dtwy(\projvelo^{\liq}),\dtwy(\extvelo{\gas})\}$; this check is always
made.

\emph{Checks} (\code{projected_step_check}). The liquid fractions after a step
equal those of a one-velocity step with carrier $\projvelo^{\liq}$ to
$\chkVofProjectedReduction$; an independent evaluation
of~\cref{eq:vof:aux-gas,eq:vof:reset} reproduces $\abs{\gasreset}$ to
$\chkVofResetDiff$ (relative); on the cut fixture with tolerance $10^{-12}$
the projection leaves
$\max\abs{\divopen\projvelo^{\liq}}=\chkVofProjectedDivergence$ and the liquid
volume changes by $\chkVofProjectedChange$, within the bound
$\dt\chkVofProjectedDivergence\sum_{\indicator{\liq}_c=1}\meas{\opencell{c}}=\chkVofProjectedBound$
of \cref{sec:vof:conservation}.

\begin{deviation}{Projected-liquid carrier}
The thesis proposes this carrier: a Poisson problem projects the liquid
velocity on the faces with $\stagfrac{\liq}{f}>0$ with $\projpres=0$ in
liquid-free cells, missing velocities are extrapolated as
constants~\thesis{\S6.3.2}, and the gas is transported with its own donating
regions into an auxiliary mass that is consistent but not bounded above,
while the gas volume is the complement~\thesis{\S6.3.3, eq.~(6.23)}. The code
differs in that it uses WY sweeps with a dilation term instead of the
unsplit method~\thesis{eq.~(6.20)}, extends by $\extlayers$ averaging passes
and zero beyond, solves the projection to a tolerance, and resets the
auxiliary gas carrier to the complement before every sweep instead of carrying
it through the step; the reset is not conservative for the gas scalars and
is reported by~\cref{eq:vof:reset}.
\end{deviation}

\section{Time-integrated balances}\label{sec:vof:conservation}
Summing~\cref{eq:vof:step} over all cells, the liquid volume
$\phasevol{\liq}=\sum_c\meas{\opencell{c}}\condfrac{\liq}{c}=\sum_c\meas{c}\phasefrac{\liq}{c}$
of a step changes by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:volume-balance}
  \phasevol{\liq,\timelevel{n+1}}-\phasevol{\liq,\timelevel{n}}
  =\dt\sum_{c}\indicator{\liq}_c\meas{c}(\divh\volumeflux^{\open})_c
  +\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_c\meas{\opencell{c}}\snapchange{j}{c},
\end{equation}
because the liquid volumes of the interior faces cancel and the boundary faces
carry none; the gas volume, the complement, changes by the opposite amount.
This identity holds for all three carriers, with $\volumeflux^{\open}$ the
respective carrier, up to rounding. Each phase volume is therefore conserved
to rounding if, besides impermeable or periodic boundaries,
\begin{itemize}
\item the carrier is discretely divergence free in every cell with
  $\indicator{\liq}_c=1$: $(\divopen\velo)_c=0$ for one velocity,
  $\sum_f\orient{c}{f}\meas{f}(\phaseaperture{\liq}{f}\velo^{\liq}_f+\phaseaperture{\gas}{f}\velo^{\gas}_f)=0$
  on the frozen apertures, $(\divopen\projvelo^{\liq})_c=0$ for the projected
  carrier; and
\item no fraction is clamped or snapped ($\snapchange{j}{c}=0$).
\end{itemize}
A velocity produced by a projection is divergence free only to its solver
tolerance, so in a simulation the volume changes per step by the dilation of
that residual. For the projected carrier every cell with $\indicator{\liq}_c=1$
is active, so by the stopping rule of \cref{sec:vof:projected} the dilation is
bounded,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:projected-dilation-bound}
  \begin{aligned}
  \abs{\dt\sum_c\indicator{\liq}_c\meas{c}(\divh\volumeflux^{\open})_c}
  &\le\dt\,\max_{c:\,\condfrac{\liq}{c}>0}\abs{(\divopen\projvelo^{\liq})_c}\sum_{\indicator{\liq}_c=1}\meas{\opencell{c}}\\
  &\le\dt\,\max(\projtol,\roundfloor)\sum_{\indicator{\liq}_c=1}\meas{\opencell{c}},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\roundfloor$ the round-off floor of the solver (at least
$128\machineeps$). For $\projtol$ above the floor, in particular for the
default, the bound is $\dt\projtol\sum_{\indicator{\liq}_c=1}\meas{\opencell{c}}$. For a phase
scalar, summing~\cref{eq:vof:scalar} over the cells and the two sweeps gives,
if no fraction is clamped or snapped,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vof:scalar-balance}
  \sum_c\meas{\opencell{c}}(\condfrac{\phase}{c}\avar^{\phase}_c)^{\timelevel{n+1}}
  -\sum_c\meas{\opencell{c}}(\condfrac{\phase}{c}\avar^{\phase}_c)^{\timelevel{n}}
  =\dt\sum_c\indicator{\phase}_c\avar^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}_c\meas{c}(\divh\volumeflux^{\open})_c ,
\end{equation}
so the frozen dilation values make the dilation of a scalar vanish together
with that of the volume. A snap to a positive fraction adds
$\meas{\opencell{c}}\,\delta\condfrac{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{c}\,\avar^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_c$
with $\delta\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}=\snapchange{j}{c}$ and
$\delta\condfrac{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}{c}=-\snapchange{j}{c}$, a snap to zero removes the
cell's amount, and the projected carrier adds, for the gas, the resets of the
auxiliary carrier (\cref{sec:vof:projected}),
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_c\meas{\opencell{c}}\roundpar{1-\condfrac{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}-\auxgas{,\splitlevel{j}}{c}}\avar^{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}_c
\end{equation*}
(the second term of the sum is the reset implied by reading the amount
through the physical complement at the end of the step). The intrinsic values are
not bounded by these identities; near-empty cells are excluded by the floor
in~\cref{eq:vof:fluctuation}.

\emph{Checks} (\code{step_balance_check} for one velocity,
\code{frozen_step_check} and \code{projected_step_check} for the two
carriers of two velocities). An independent sweep
sequence (fluxes, update~\cref{eq:vof:sweep}, clamp and snap, then the code's
reconstruction) reproduces the fractions of \apifun{advect!} after a full step
to $\chkVofReplicaDiff$ for all carriers and fixtures, and the
identity~\cref{eq:vof:volume-balance} holds to $\chkVofBalanceResidual$ in
volume. With face velocities from a streamfunction, which are exactly
divergence free on the composite mesh (on the cut fixture the open volumes are
differences of a streamfunction that vanishes on solid vertices,
$\max\abs{\divopen\velo}=\chkVofOpenDivergence$), no fraction is snapped and the
liquid volume changes relatively by $\chkVofStepUniform$ (uniform mesh),
$\chkVofStepAmr$ (composite) and $\chkVofStepCut$ (cut cells). With the plain
streamfunction velocity on the cut fixture, which is not divergence free in
the open cells, the volume changes by $\chkVofCutDilation$, equal to the
dilation term of~\cref{eq:vof:volume-balance}.

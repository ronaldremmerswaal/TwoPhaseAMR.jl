\chapter{Method overview}\label{ch:overview}
\chapterstatus[619ea56a09f25f9c0d214d416a1350b4d48edf23]{reference}
TwoPhaseAMR.jl solves the incompressible Navier--Stokes equations of one
fluid, or of two immiscible fluids separated by a sharp interface with
surface tension, in a two-dimensional rectangle that may contain a fixed
embedded solid, on a block-structured quadtree mesh that adapts between time
steps. This chapter is the entry point of the reference. It states what the
reference is and how its statements are tied to the code
(\cref{sec:overview:scope}), the governing equations
(\cref{sec:overview:models}), the discretisation chapter by chapter
(\cref{sec:overview:glance}), the stages of one time step and of a run
(\cref{sec:overview:step}), the selectable methods with their status and the
public types of every chapter (\cref{sec:overview:options}), and the known
limitations with the issues that track them
(\cref{sec:overview:limitations}). It introduces no discretisation of its
own: every formula it quotes is stated, with its implementation and its
tests, in the chapter it points to, and the time step
of \cref{sec:overview:step} is the composition of the fully discrete updates
of those chapters.

\section{Scope of this reference}\label{sec:overview:scope}
The reference states what the code computes; the code is normative. Every
equation chapter describes the implementation at the commit against which it
was audited, printed under its title, and where the implementation differs from
the thesis of Remmerswaal~\cite{remmerswaal2023} it states the
implementation and records the difference in a box titled \emph{Deviation
from the thesis}; \cref{app:deviations} lists all of them. They range from
typographical errors of the thesis, confirmed by an audit
(\cref{dev:interface:signs,dev:projection:interface-work}), through methods
that replace those of the thesis
(\cref{dev:forces:st-stage,dev:projection:incremental}) to parts of the
thesis that are not implemented (\cref{dev:overview:scope,dev:forces:st-models}).
A known defect of the code is not described as intended behaviour: it is
stated as a defect, with the issue that tracks it
(\cref{sec:overview:limitations}).

The models, the notation and most methods are those of the thesis. Its
Chapter~2 introduces the two-phase Navier--Stokes equations, the discrete
setting, the interface description, cut cells and the refinement criteria.
Part~I (Chapters~3 to~5) treats the geometric volume-of-fluid method, surface
tension and interface reconstruction with one velocity; Part~II treats the
transport of mass and momentum (Chapter~6), the pressure projections,
diffusion and gravity of the one- and two-velocity formulations (Chapter~7)
and their application to liquid impacts (Chapter~8). Derivations are cited
from the thesis and the original literature and are not repeated. Where the
package goes beyond the thesis, for the block forest and its transfer maps
(\cref{ch:mesh-amr,ch:amr-criteria-transfer}), the cut-cell construction
(\cref{ch:cutcells}), the linear solvers (\cref{ch:solvers}) and the
selection of the time step (\cref{ch:stability-accuracy}), the construction
is the package's and is stated in full.

\begin{deviation}{Dimension and compressibility}\label{dev:overview:scope}
The thesis formulates the two-phase equations in $d\in\{2,3\}$
dimensions~\thesis{\S2.1}, reports three-dimensional simulations of breaking
wave impacts~\thesis{\S8.4} and outlines the generalisation to compressible
flow in remarks~\thesis{Remarks~6.1 and~7.2}. The package is two-dimensional
and incompressible throughout.
\end{deviation}

\section{Governing equations}\label{sec:overview:models}
\emph{Domain and phases.} The domain $\domain$ is a rectangle whose
directions are periodic or bounded by walls (\cref{sec:mesh:domain}). A
fixed solid $\domain^{\solid}=\set{\coord\in\domain}{\lset^{\solid}(\coord)\le0}$
may be embedded, and the fluid occupies
$\domain^{\open}=\domain\setminus\domain^{\solid}$
(\cref{sec:cutcells:continuous}). With two phases, $\domain^{\open}$ is split
into the liquid and gas regions $\domain^{\liq}(\timevar)$ and
$\domain^{\gas}(\timevar)$, whose common boundary is the interface
$\interface(\timevar)$, with unit normal $\inormal$ pointing into the gas and
curvature $\curv=\divergence\inormal$ (\cref{sec:interface:continuous});
$\jump{\cdot}$ is the gas minus the liquid value. There is no phase change.

\emph{Two-velocity formulation.} Each phase is an incompressible Newtonian
fluid with its own velocity and pressure, constant density $\dens^{\phase}$
and viscosity $\visc^{\phase}$~\thesis{\S2.1.1}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overview:two-velocity}
  \begin{gathered}
  \divergence\Velo^{\phase}=0,\qquad
  \dens^{\phase}\roundpar{\partial_\timevar\Velo^{\phase}+\Velo^{\phase}\cdot\gradient\Velo^{\phase}}
  =\divergence\stresstensor^{\phase}+\dens^{\phase}\gravacc+\dens^{\phase}\bodyacc
  \quad\text{in }\domain^{\phase}(\timevar),\\
  \jump{\Velo\cdot\inormal}=0,\qquad
  \inormal\cdot\jump{\stresstensor}=\surftens\curv\inormal
  \quad\text{on }\interface(\timevar),
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with $\stresstensor^{\phase}=-\pres^{\phase}\idmat+\visc^{\phase}\roundpar{\gradient\Velo^{\phase}+(\gradient\Velo^{\phase})^{\transpose}}$,
the gravitational acceleration $\gravacc$ and a prescribed body acceleration
$\bodyacc(\coord,\timevar)$, which the code adds to the thesis' model
(\cref{eq:forces:cont-momentum,eq:forces:stress-balance}). The interface
moves with the normal velocity, which is continuous~\thesis{eq.~(2.2)}; the
tangential velocity may jump.

\emph{One-velocity formulation.} If the velocity is continuous,
$\jump{\Velo}=0$, the phase balances add up to one momentum balance for one
velocity $\Velo$ with the piecewise constant density
$\dens=\dens^{\liq}\indicator{\liq}+\dens^{\gas}(1-\indicator{\liq})$ and
viscosity $\visc$, $\indicator{\liq}(\coord,\timevar)$ the liquid
indicator~\thesis{\S2.1.2, eq.~(2.9)}; surface tension is then the singular
force $\surftens\curv\gradient\indicator{\liq}=-\surftens\curv\inormal\idelta$
(\cref{sec:forces:continuous}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overview:one-velocity}
  \divergence\Velo=0,\qquad
  \dens\roundpar{\partial_\timevar\Velo+\Velo\cdot\gradient\Velo}
  =\divergence\stresstensor+\surftens\curv\gradient\indicator{\liq}
   +\dens\gravacc+\dens\bodyacc,
\end{equation}
with $\stresstensor=-\pres\idmat+\visc\roundpar{\gradient\Velo+(\gradient\Velo)^{\transpose}}$;
the phase regions move with $\Velo$, so that the volume of each phase in a
fixed cell obeys~\cref{eq:vof:cont-volume}. A single phase obeys the same
equations with constant $\dens$ and $\visc$, without interface and without
gravity (\apitype{SinglePhase} has no gravity parameter); the body
acceleration can be prescribed for every model.

\emph{Boundary conditions.} A wall side is a stationary or moving no-slip
wall, a free-slip wall, a prescribed inflow, or an outflow or zero-gradient
side (\cref{sec:viscosity:continuous,sec:projection:stage}); periodic
directions need none. On the solid boundary $\Velo\cdot\normal=0$, and the
tangential condition enters only the viscous stress (\cref{ch:cutcells}).
Where the interface meets a wall or the solid, the static contact angle
$\inormal\cdot\wallnormal=\cos\cangle$~\cref{eq:interface:contact} enters
only the curvature (\cref{sec:interface:contact}). The VOF transport, and
hence every two-phase flow, requires impermeable or periodic boundaries
(\cref{sec:vof:cfl}).

\emph{Constraints in time.} In the split time step the pressure enforces
incompressibility: every step ends with a projection onto a discrete
constraint, the open divergence for one velocity and, for two velocities,
the aperture-weighted mixture divergence together with a condition on the
normal velocity jump at the interface (\cref{sec:projection:continuous}).

\section{The discretisation at a glance}\label{sec:overview:glance}
\emph{Mesh} (\cref{ch:mesh-amr}). All fields live on the leaf cells of a
2:1-graded forest of quadtrees whose nodes are blocks of a fixed number of
Cartesian cells (by default $16\times16$). Cell values are stored once per
owned cell and face-normal values once per owned face, so that the side of a
coarse cell next to two finer cells carries two face segments; halo slots
around every block serve local stencils. All levels advance with the same
time step and every face flux is added to both of its cells, so no
refluxing is needed. The forest changes only between time steps.

\emph{Discrete setting} (\cref{ch:discrete-setting}). The unknowns are those
of a staggered (MAC) scheme on the composite mesh: cell averages of the
pressure and of the fractions, face averages of the normal velocity. The
divergence is the exact flux balance of a cell; the gradient
$\gradh=\rieszmat\jumpmat$ is its negative adjoint, with a sparse symmetric
positive definite inverse face metric $\rieszmat$ that is diagonal on
conforming patches and couples faces at level transitions; the weighted
gradient and the elliptic operator of the projections and of the forces are
built from both. A solid enters through the open measures of the cells and
faces that it cuts.

\emph{Interface} (\cref{ch:interface}). The interface is represented by the
liquid volume fraction of every cell. It is reconstructed cell by cell as
one straight line (PLIC) with a plane constant that matches the fraction;
by default its normal is that of the local height functions where they exist,
one-sided at walls, and otherwise the Weymouth--Yue normal
(\cref{tab:interface:methods}). The curvature comes from the height
functions, with generalised height functions as fallback, and the static
contact angle enters it at walls and at the solid. Fractions are initialised
from a half-plane, a polygon or a level set.

\emph{Volume-of-fluid transport} (\cref{ch:vof-transport}). The fraction is
transported by the directionally split geometric method of Weymouth and
Yue~\cite{weymouth2010}: two sweeps per step in alternating order, each
moving the liquid volumes cut from the current reconstruction through the
owned faces, with a dilation term weighted by a phase indicator frozen for
the step, and with clamping and snapping to the bounds. Intrinsic phase
scalars move with the same phase volumes. With two velocities the carrier is
the frozen interface flux: each phase subface of the start-of-step
reconstruction is extruded with its own phase velocity.

\emph{Momentum transport} (\cref{ch:momentum-transport}). The velocity
components are transported over staggered control volumes whose dual-face
fluxes are fixed linear combinations of the primal face fluxes; at level
transitions and in cut cells the combination keeps the dual divergence equal
to an interpolation of the primal one. Face states are upwind, centred,
Lax--Wendroff (the default) or limited three-point states. A single phase
convects its velocity; the one-velocity model transports the
density-weighted momentum and the two-velocity model the intrinsic phase
velocities and staggered phase scalars, both inside the VOF sweeps with the
interpolated phase volumes as fluxes~\thesis{\S6.4}.

\emph{Embedded solid} (\cref{ch:cutcells}). A fixed solid is embedded
without changing the mesh: every cell carries its solid fraction and, if it
is cut, one half-plane that approximates the solid, from which the open
polygons, the face apertures and the open staggered volumes follow; the
velocity on faces that the solid covers is zero. Small cut cells are not
merged.

\emph{Forces} (\cref{ch:forces}). Surface tension (the conservative
ghost-fluid model CGFM, the default, or CSF) and gravity (the mimetic gravity
model of the thesis) are integrated face jumps passed through the weighted
Riesz map of the pressure gradient, so that a force that is the jump of a
cell function is exactly a pressure gradient and the projection removes it
(balanced force). Both are explicit increments of the predictor after the
viscous step; the face curvature is interpolated from the cell curvatures. A
prescribed body acceleration is added at the midpoint of the step, before
the viscous step.

\emph{Viscosity} (\cref{ch:viscosity}). The viscous step is the one implicit
substep, an implicit midpoint rule. A single phase uses a componentwise
staggered Laplacian of the kinematic viscosity with geometric transition
contacts and, with a solid, rows for the embedded walls; two phases use the
variable stress $\divergence(2\visc\strainrate)$ with the geometric mean
of the phase viscosities, and the two-velocity model diffuses the
mass-weighted mixture velocity and gives both phases its increment.

\emph{Projection} (\cref{ch:projection}). Every step ends with one pressure
projection: a Poisson projection for a shared velocity (one phase, or two
phases with one velocity), and for the phase velocities the conservative
multidimensional ghost-fluid method (CMDGFM) of the thesis. Both are
incremental: the step subtracts the gradient of the previous pressure and the
projection solves for the increment, so that a shortened step removes only
its share of the divergence left by the previous step. After a regrid the
phase velocities are projected once more.

\emph{Linear solvers} (\cref{ch:solvers}). The projections and the viscous
step are sparse linear systems, solved by Krylov
methods with a stopping rule and a round-off floor: CG for the Poisson
projection and the two-phase viscous step, CG or
BiCGSTAB after a symmetry test for the one-phase viscous step, and BiCGSTAB
for the CMDGFM. They are preconditioned by
V-cycles of a tree geometric multigrid built from the forest
(\cref{tab:solvers:systems}).

\emph{Refinement and transfer} (\cref{ch:amr-criteria-transfer}). After a
step, refinement indicators (interface and solid bands, curvature, vorticity
and interpolation error) label every leaf block \emph{refine}, \emph{keep} or
\emph{coarsen}. \apifun{adapt!} edits and balances the forest and transfers
every registered field: cell fields by conservative restriction and limited
linear prolongation, fractions and phase scalars through the reconstruction,
face velocities along grid lines with corrections that keep the divergence
of a shared velocity cell by cell on meshes without a solid.

\emph{Time step} (\cref{ch:stability-accuracy}). The explicit stages bound
the step: the transport (the staggered bound in one phase, the WY bound in
two) and, with two phases, the capillary and gravity-wave bounds.
\apifun{simulate!} chooses steps from these bounds at CFL number $0.45$,
never increases the step within a run and adjusts the last step to end at
the final time; \apifun{step!} rejects a step above the bounds at CFL number
$\half$. No order in time is claimed for the complete step.

\section{One time step}\label{sec:overview:step}
A step advances the liquid fraction and the phase scalars, the velocity or
the two phase velocities, and the pressure from $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$ to
$\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}}=\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}+\dt$ by a fixed sequence of
stages, the split step of~\cref{eq:forces:stages}. Its stages are those of
the thesis' steps T1 to T5~\thesis{\S7.2} with the changes of
\cref{tab:overview:thesis}. The velocity after the transport, the viscous
step and the forces is $\velo^{\stage{1}}$, $\velo^{\stage{2}}$ and
$\velo^{\stage{3}}$, after the old-pressure predictor $\velo^{\star}$. The
step takes a reference step $\dtref\ge\dt$ ($\dtref=\dt$ for a direct call):
with the incremental projections, a shorter step keeps the share
$1-\dt/\dtref$ of the divergence that it inherits
(\cref{sec:projection:pressure-update}).

\subsection{One velocity}\label{sec:overview:step-one}
\apifun{step!} with a \apitype{FaceVelocity} runs the same stages for one
phase and for two phases with one velocity; the two-phase stages extend the
single-phase ones by dispatch.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Start.} The velocity gets its boundary values at
  $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$: prescribed faces from the boundary conditions,
  open faces keeping their projected values, faces covered by the solid zero
  (\cref{sec:projection:stage}). This is $\velo^{\timelevel{n}}$. For
  $\dt<\dtref$ its open divergence is kept for the
  projection~\cref{eq:projection:reference-rhs}.
\item \emph{Transport.} The step is checked against the bound of the
  transport and, with two phases, the capillary and gravity
  bounds~\cref{eq:stab:check}. One phase: $\velo^{\timelevel{n}}$ is
  convected with itself as frozen carrier by the staggered
  scheme~\cref{eq:momentum:split}, in two directional sweeps of alternating
  order for the default Lax--Wendroff states, with the boundary values at
  $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$. Two phases: the WY
  sweeps~\cref{eq:vof:sweep} with the carrier $\velo^{\timelevel{n}}$
  advance the fraction to $\condfrac{\liq,\timelevel{n+1}}{}$ and the phase
  scalars and, in the same sweeps, the density-weighted momentum
  (\cref{sec:momentum:one-velocity}), in alternating order. The result is
  $\velo^{\stage{1}}$.
\item \emph{Body acceleration.}
  $\velo^{\stage{1}}\leftarrow\velo^{\stage{1}}+\dt\,\bodyacc(\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}+\half\dt)$
  on the interior faces~\cref{eq:forces:body} (nothing is added without a body
  acceleration); then, in every step, the boundary values at
  $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}+\half\dt$, open faces extrapolating the change
  since $\velo^{\timelevel{n}}$.
\item \emph{Viscous step.} The implicit midpoint
  step~\cref{eq:viscosity:single-midpoint} (one phase)
  or~\cref{eq:viscosity:phase-midpoint} (two phases, with the viscosity and
  the density of $\condfrac{\liq,\timelevel{n+1}}{}$; omitted if both
  viscosities vanish) gives $\velo^{\stage{2}}$.
\item \emph{Phase forces} (two phases). The pressure coefficient
  $\facecoef$ is rebuilt from $\condfrac{\liq,\timelevel{n+1}}{}$
  (\cref{eq:forces:coefficient}) and, for unequal densities, its
  preconditioner is refreshed. Then
  $\velo^{\stage{3}}=\velo^{\stage{2}}+\dt\forcemap\fjump{\surftens}+\dt\forcemap\fjump{\gravacc}$,
  surface tension first, each term only if $\surftens>0$ or
  $\gravacc\neq\mathbf{0}$ (\cref{sec:forces:time}). With one phase
  $\velo^{\stage{3}}=\velo^{\stage{2}}$.
\item \emph{End-of-step boundary values} at $\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}}$,
  after the forces; covered faces zero.
\item \emph{Old-pressure predictor.} The gradient of $\pres^{\timelevel{n}}$
  in the pressure map of the projection, times $\dt$, is subtracted, giving
  $\velo^{\star}$~\cref{eq:projection:pressure-predictor}. This map is the
  weighted gradient of the refreshed coefficient, $\forcemap\jumpmat$ on
  the interior faces; it vanishes on boundary faces with a prescribed flux
  and corrects open ones (\cref{sec:projection:pressure-update}).
\item \emph{Projection.} The Poisson
  projection~\cref{eq:projection:one-system} of $\velo^{\star}$, with the
  retained share of the inherited
  divergence~\cref{eq:projection:reference-rhs}, gives the increment
  $\presinc$. The velocity is corrected by $\dt$ times the gradient of
  $\presinc$ in the same map, open boundary faces included, and
  $\pres^{\timelevel{n+1}}=\pres^{\timelevel{n}}+\presinc$
  (\cref{eq:projection:pressure-increment}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Two velocities}\label{sec:overview:step-two}
\apifun{step!} with a \apitype{TwoPhaseVelocity} advances the intrinsic phase
velocities $\velo^{\liq}$ and $\velo^{\gas}$, which are absent (\code{NaN})
where their phase has no staggered volume.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Start.} Both phases get their boundary values at
  $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$ (\cref{sec:projection:stage}), covered faces are
  set to zero and absent values marked with the current interface. With the
  CMDGFM and $\dt<\dtref$ the mixture flux of $\velo^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}$
  on the phase apertures of the start-of-step reconstruction is saved for the
  projection (\cref{sec:projection:pressure-update}).
\item \emph{Transport.} The step is checked against the bounds unless
  \apifun{simulate!} has just evaluated them for this state (automatic step,
  frozen interface fluxes); that evaluation also reconstructs the interface and
  freezes the phase apertures of the step start (\cref{sec:vof:frozen}). The
  WY sweeps with the frozen carrier~\cref{eq:vof:frozen-flux} advance the
  fraction, the phase scalars, the
  staggered phase scalars and the phase velocities
  (\cref{sec:momentum:two-velocity}), in alternating order, to
  $\velo^{\phase,\stage{1}}$.
\item \emph{Body acceleration} on both phases where they are
  present~\cref{eq:forces:body}; then, in every step, the boundary values at
  $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}+\half\dt$.
\item \emph{Viscous step.} The mass-weighted mixture velocity is advanced
  by~\cref{eq:viscosity:phase-midpoint} and its increment is added to both
  phases~\cref{eq:viscosity:two-velocity}, giving $\velo^{\phase,\stage{2}}$
  (omitted if both viscosities vanish).
\item \emph{End-of-step boundary values} of both phases at
  $\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}}$, here before the forces.
\item \emph{Pressure map.} For the CMDGFM without a solid the transported
  interface is reconstructed and the one-velocity coefficient rebuilt, so
  that $\forcemaptv=\forcemap$; with a solid the CMDGFM uses the operator
  that it caches, with the coefficient $\twocoef$ of the transported
  fractions (\cref{sec:forces:two-velocity}).
\item \emph{Phase forces.} The common increments
  $\dt\forcemaptv\fjump{\surftens}+\dt\forcemaptv\fjump{\gravacc}$ are added
  to both phases where they are present~\cref{eq:forces:two-velocity},
  giving $\velo^{\phase,\stage{3}}$.
\item \emph{Projection.} CMDGFM: the common old-pressure gradient is
  subtracted,
  $\velo^{\phase,\star}=\velo^{\phase,\stage{3}}-\dt\,\forcemaptv\jumpmat\pres^{\timelevel{n}}$~\cref{eq:projection:pressure-predictor},
  the system~\cref{eq:projection:cmdgfm-system} is solved for the increment
  $\presinc$ and the gradient jump $\gradjump$, with the saved flux for a
  shortened step, and
  $\pres^{\timelevel{n+1}}=\pres^{\timelevel{n}}+\presinc$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{A run}\label{sec:overview:run}
\apifun{simulate!} advances to the final time $\tend$ by repeated steps; a
\apitype{FlowSimulation} supplies its fields, system, workspace, time and
diagnostic collection and accumulates its counters over successive calls. A
call proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Set-up.} With refinement indicators an AMR workspace registers
  the fields to transfer (\cref{sec:amr:when}); dynamic adaptation excludes
  observers that advance copies of the state over several steps (exact
  keyframes). The diagnostics are recorded and the observer receives the
  event \code{:initial}; an exception there ends the call before the first
  step, without a \code{:failed} event. The observer is asked whether to stop
  (an exception of the poll is logged and counts as no).
\item \emph{Step size.} Without a prescribed step (\code{dt=nothing}) the
  boundary values at $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$ are imposed and the step is
  the proposal of~\cref{eq:stab:selection} from the flow bound at CFL number
  $0.45$; otherwise it is the prescribed step. The last step is stretched or
  shortened to end at $\tend$, and the reference step $\dtref$ is formed
  with it.
\item \emph{Step.} One \apifun{step!} as in
  \cref{sec:overview:step-one,sec:overview:step-two}. A linear solve that has
  not converged (viscous step, projection, and the projection after a
  regrid) stops the run with an error; a step that fails a run-time check is
  not retried (\cref{sec:stab:gaps}).
\item \emph{Regrid.} With indicators, \apifun{adapt!} is called with the
  number of completed steps and the time at the end of the step, and acts at
  most every \code{update_interval} steps (\cref{sec:amr:marking}). If the
  leaf set changed, the topology-dependent operators and workspaces are
  rebuilt, the divergence that the transfer leaves is associated with the
  proposal $\dtprop$, and with two velocities the transferred velocities are
  projected~\cref{eq:projection:regrid}.
\item \emph{Events.} The diagnostics due at this step are recorded and the
  observer receives \code{:step} with the step size, the projection
  statistics, whether the topology changed and the wall time of the step; an
  exception of the observer is logged and the run continues, an interrupt
  propagates. The observer is asked whether to stop; if so, the run ends at
  this step boundary.
\item \emph{End.} After the last step or a requested stop the diagnostics are
  recorded and the observer receives \code{:completed}; the last step and,
  after an automatic step, the proposal are retained for a call that
  continues the run (\cref{sec:stab:selection}). If the run throws, the
  observer receives \code{:failed} with the exception (an error of the
  observer there is dropped), which is then rethrown.
\end{enumerate}
A standalone \apifun{step!} ends the retained history, and so does
\apifun{project!}\code{(flow)}, which projects an initial velocity without
changing the physical pressure (\cref{sec:projection:pressure-update}).

\section{Known limitations}\label{sec:overview:limitations}
Besides the scope of \cref{dev:overview:scope}, the implementation has these
limits by design. Two-phase flows need impermeable or periodic boundaries
(\cref{sec:vof:cfl}); the contact angle is static and enters only the
curvature (\cref{sec:interface:contact}); there is no phase change. No order
in time is claimed for the split step, and the orders in space that the
tests assert, with their known first-order spots, are those of
\cref{tab:stab:orders}. Small cut cells are not merged and reduce the step
(\cref{sec:vof:cfl}), and a step that fails a run-time check is not retried
(\cref{sec:stab:gaps}).
\Cref{tab:overview:limitations} groups the defects and open questions that
the chapters state and that are tracked by issues open at the audited commit;
\cref{sec:verif:gaps} adds the gaps of the verification itself.

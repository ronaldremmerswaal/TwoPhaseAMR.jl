\chapter{Stability and accuracy}\label{ch:stability-accuracy}
\chapterstatus[2ca00c4601c72e97ce3bb2b3fd0cff58e3c7e3e9]{reference}
The explicit parts of a time step limit its size: the geometric transport of
the fractions and of the momentum
(\cref{ch:vof-transport,ch:momentum-transport}) and the surface-tension and
gravity increments (\cref{ch:forces}). The viscous step is implicit
(\cref{ch:viscosity}) and the projection has no time step of its own
(\cref{ch:projection}). This chapter states the bounds that the code
evaluates and how a flow simulation combines them
(\cref{sec:stab:bounds}), how \apifun{simulate!} turns them into a sequence
of steps and how \apifun{step!} checks a given step
(\cref{sec:stab:selection}), the bounds, step sequences and empirical
stability margins of representative flows (\cref{sec:stab:measured}), the
configurations in which a step within the bounds still fails
(\cref{sec:stab:gaps}), and the order of accuracy of the building blocks
together with the orders that the tests assert (\cref{sec:stab:accuracy}).
The thesis states the transport bound~\thesis{eqs.~(3.50)--(3.51)}, the
capillary bound~\thesis{eq.~(4.1)} and the gravity
bound~\thesis{eq.~(7.4)}, and its refinement studies refine the step through
these bounds~\thesis{\S7.7}; how the bounds become a sequence of steps is not
stated there. The step selection has no discretisation of its own: it maps
the state at the start of a step to the size of that step.

\section{Time-step bounds}\label{sec:stab:bounds}
Every bound is the largest step for which a set of rates satisfies an
inequality (\cref{tab:stab:bounds}). The advective bounds limit the volume
that a carrier moves through the faces of a control volume relative to its
open volume; the capillary and gravity bounds limit the step relative to the
period of the shortest interface wave that the mesh represents. A bound
without rates (a vanishing velocity, $\surftens=0$, $\gravacc=\mathbf{0}$ or
$\dens^{\liq}=\dens^{\gas}$) is $\infty$.

\emph{Flow bound.} \apifun{stable_timestep}\code{(velocity, solver, workspace; cfl=0.45)}
and \apifun{stable_timestep}\code{(flow; cfl=0.45)} return
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stab:flow-bound}
  \begin{gathered}
  \dtflow(\flowcfl)=\min\curlypar{\dtadv(\flowcfl),\ \dtcap,\ \dtgrav},\\
  \dtadv(\flowcfl)=\begin{cases}
    \dtstag\ \text{at }\stagcfl=\flowcfl, & \text{one phase},\\
    \dtwy\ \text{of the velocity at }\wycflval=\flowcfl, & \text{two phases, one velocity},\\
    \dtwy\ \text{of~\cref{eq:vof:frozen-cfl}}, & \text{two velocities},\\
  \end{cases}
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
for the velocity passed to them, with $\dtcap$ and $\dtgrav$ only in two
phases; the last row is the transport of
\cref{sec:vof:frozen}. Both force bounds use
$\minwidth=\min_{c,k}\cellwidth{c}{k}$, the smallest width of all leaf cells
in either direction, not the width at the interface; on a mesh refined
elsewhere, at a solid for instance, they are stricter than the interface
needs. In two phases $\flowcfl$ must lie in $(0,\half]$ and in one phase in
$(0,1]$; outside that range \apifun{stable_timestep} and \apifun{simulate!}
throw an error that names the range, also when a prescribed \code{dt} makes
it unused. The ranges are those of the bounds the code evaluates, not
measured limits of the transports (\cref{sec:stab:measured} finds the WY bound
sufficient rather than necessary): the WY sweeps of one carrier limit the face
extrusion to half the donor width, and at $\stagcfl\le1$ every dual-face
Courant number of the staggered bound stays within one, so the
Lax--Wendroff face state is a convex combination of its upwind neighbours
(\cref{sec:momentum:cfl}; the first-order upwind update stays convex up to
$\stagcfl\le2$). \apifun{step!}
checks every step against $\dtflow$ at the top of the range of the model
(\cref{eq:stab:check}), so every step that \apifun{stable_timestep} returns
is accepted and the keyword alone sets the margin. Before \issue{121} was
fixed, the one-phase check used $\stagcfl=\half$, and \apifun{simulate!} with
$\flowcfl>\half$ stopped at its first advection-limited step. Only $\dtadv$
scales with $\flowcfl$. Since
$\dtcap\propto\minwidth^{3/2}$ and $\dtgrav\propto\minwidth^{1/2}$ while the
advective bounds are proportional to $\minwidth$ for a given velocity, the
capillary bound becomes the active one and the gravity bound an inactive one
under mesh refinement; the thesis notes the latter for its gravity
bound~\thesis{eq.~(7.4)}. For a translation with speed $\transspeed$ on a
uniform mesh, where $\dtadv=\flowcfl\minwidth/\transspeed$, the capillary
bound is the smaller one below the width
$4\pi{\flowcfl}^{2}\surftens/((\dens^{\liq}+\dens^{\gas})\transspeed^{2})$ and
the gravity bound only above
$(\dens^{\liq}+\dens^{\gas})\transspeed^{2}/(2{\flowcfl}^{2}\abs{\dens^{\liq}-\dens^{\gas}}\abs{\gravacc})$.

\emph{Transport bounds on composite meshes and in cut cells.} The advective
bounds are those of \cref{sec:vof:cfl,sec:momentum:cfl}. The WY bound limits
the inflow and the outflow of every open cell, the extrusion of every
face relative to its donor width, and the available open portions of cut
donor strips and opposing strip pairs~\cref{eq:vof:cfl,eq:vof:geometric-cfl}.
The staggered bound sums the absolute rates of every dual
control volume, including the boundary dual faces where a level transition
meets a wall~\cref{eq:momentum:cfl}. Both divide by open volumes, and no
cells are merged, so small cut cells reduce the step
(\cref{sec:stab:measured}). The two-phase momentum transport has no bound of
its own (\cref{sec:momentum:cfl}).

\emph{Capillary bound.} $\dtcap$ is the bound of Brackbill, Kothe and
Zemach~\cite{brackbill1992} in the form of the thesis~\thesis{eq.~(4.1)}.
With the phase speed $\capspeed=(\pi\surftens/((\dens^{\liq}+\dens^{\gas})\minwidth))^{1/2}$
of the capillary wave of wavelength $2\minwidth$, $\dtcap=\minwidth/(2\capspeed)$,
which is $2^{-1/2}$ times the period of the capillary wave of wavelength
$\minwidth$. Denner and van Wachem attribute the bound to the temporal
sampling of capillary waves, irrespective of an explicit or implicit surface
tension, propose the static bound $\minwidth/(\sqrt2\,\capspeed)$, a factor
$\sqrt2$ less restrictive, and add the Doppler shift of a flow with speed
$\transspeed$ along the interface,
$\minwidth/(\sqrt2\,\capspeed+\transspeed)$~\cite[eqs.~(43)--(44)]{denner2015}.
The code adds no speed to $\capspeed$: \cref{eq:stab:flow-bound} takes the
minimum of $\dtcap$ and the advective bound. For a flat interface translated
along itself with speed $\transspeed$ on a uniform mesh without solid, where
$\dtwy=\wycflval\minwidth/\transspeed$, the selected step
$\min\{\wycflval\minwidth/\transspeed,\ \minwidth/(2\capspeed)\}$ exceeds
the dynamic bound $\minwidth/(\sqrt2\,\capspeed+\transspeed)$ by at most the
factor $\wycflval+2^{-1/2}$ ($1.16$ at $\wycflval=0.45$), attained where the
two bounds of the code are equal ($\transspeed=2\wycflval\capspeed$).

\emph{Gravity bound.} The thesis bounds the step by the period of a gravity
wave of wavelength $\minwidth/(2\pi)$, a wavelength found necessary in
numerical experiments, and remarks that the bound is not
restrictive~\thesis{eq.~(7.4)}; the code multiplies it by $2^{-1/2}$
(\cref{dev:stab:gravity}).

\emph{No viscous bound.} The viscous step is implicit
(\cref{sec:viscosity:time}), and the flow bound is the convective one also
for a large viscosity. In two phases the implicit midpoint step does not
increase the mass-weighted kinetic energy for any $\dt$, up to the
residual~\cref{eq:viscosity:energy}. The single-phase geometric stiffness is
also positive semidefinite: backward Euler is contractive for every $\dt$,
as is midpoint when $\velo^{\timelevel{n}}=\velo^{\stage{1}}$. The actual
single-phase split step evaluates its explicit half at
$\velo^{\timelevel{n}}$ (\cref{dev:viscosity:time-level}), so, by
\cref{eq:viscosity:split-bound}, its energy grows by at most
$\big((\velo^{\stage{1}}-\velo^{\timelevel{n}})^{\transpose}\dualvolmat(\velo^{\stage{1}}-\velo^{\timelevel{n}})\big)^{1/2}$,
the dual-volume distance between the transported and the old velocity, plus
the work of moving walls; the old transition-band energy growth is removed
(\issue{80}). $\dtvisc$, the explicit
estimate of \apifun{stable_timestep}\code{(operator; cfl=0.9)} for a
\apitype{StaggeredDiffusionOperator}, is used by no flow step. The body force
is integrated with the midpoint rule and has no bound~\cref{eq:forces:body}.

\begin{deviation}{Gravity time-step factor}\label{dev:stab:gravity}
The thesis' gravity bound is the period of a gravity wave of wavelength
$\minwidth/(2\pi)$, $\big((\dens^{\liq}+\dens^{\gas})\minwidth/(\abs{\dens^{\liq}-\dens^{\gas}}\abs{\gravacc})\big)^{1/2}$~\thesis{eq.~(7.4)}.
The code multiplies it by the safety factor $2^{-1/2}$ that the thesis
applies to the capillary bound~\thesis{eq.~(4.1)}, so that
$\dtgrav$~\cref{eq:vof:dt-forces} is stricter by $\sqrt2$.
\end{deviation}

\section{Step selection and the step check}\label{sec:stab:selection}
Without a prescribed step (\code{dt=nothing}, the default of
\apifun{simulate!} and of a \apitype{FlowSimulation} built without
\code{dt}), \apifun{simulate!} advances from $\timevar^{\timelevel{0}}$ to
$\tend$ as follows. At the start of step $n$ it imposes the boundary values
at $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$, evaluates
$\dtflow^{\timelevel{n}}=\dtflow(\flowcfl)$ of~\cref{eq:stab:flow-bound} with
$\flowcfl=0.45$ (keyword \code{cfl}) and sets
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stab:selection}
  \begin{gathered}
  {\dtprop}^{\timelevel{n}}=\min\curlypar{{\dtprop}^{\timelevel{n-1}},\ \dtflow^{\timelevel{n}}},\qquad
  {\dtref}^{\timelevel{n}}=\max\curlypar{{\dtprop}^{\timelevel{n}},\ {\dtdef}^{\timelevel{n}},\ \dt^{\timelevel{n}}},\\
  \dt^{\timelevel{n}}=\begin{cases}
    \tend-\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}, & \tend-\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}-{\dtprop}^{\timelevel{n}}<10^{-6}\,{\dtprop}^{\timelevel{n}},\\
    {\dtprop}^{\timelevel{n}}, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}\\
  \timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}}=\begin{cases}
    \tend, & \dt^{\timelevel{n}}=\tend-\timevar^{\timelevel{n}},\\
    \min\curlypar{\tend,\ \timevar^{\timelevel{n}}+\dt^{\timelevel{n}}}, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with ${\dtprop}^{\timelevel{-1}}$ the proposal retained from a previous call
(below) or $\infty$. If ${\dtprop}^{\timelevel{0}}$ is still infinite, the
step is capped at $(\tend-\timevar^{\timelevel{0}})/128$, so that a flow at
rest without forces takes at least $128$ steps. Steps are taken while
$\tend-\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}>16\machineeps\max\{1,\abs{\tend},\abs{\timevar^{\timelevel{0}}}\}$
(a threshold fixed at the start of the call),
and the call ends at $\tend$. The reference step $\dtref$ of the incremental
projections (\cref{sec:projection:pressure-update}) uses $\dtdef$, the length
of the previous physical step, after a regrid the proposal of the step before
it, and $0$ at the first step of a run; its third argument matters only for
a stretched last step, which must not exceed its reference
(\cref{eq:projection:reference-rhs}). With a prescribed step (keyword
\code{dt}) the proposal is that step in every step, no bound is evaluated,
and the rest of~\cref{eq:stab:selection} applies.

The following hold by construction. The proposal never increases: a
transient velocity peak, a solenoidal correction of an impulsive start or a
regrid to finer cells lowers the step for the rest of the run, and a decaying
flow or a coarsening never raises it again. Every step of a call but the last
equals its proposal. The last step of a call that follows a step of the same
call lies between $10^{-6}$ and $1+10^{-6}$ times its proposal: a remainder
below $10^{-6}$ of the proposal is absorbed into the step before it
(\issue{98}: before, a step of $5\times10^{-14}$ remained), and a remainder
above it is a step of its own. Such a shortened step keeps, through
$\dtref\ge\dtprop$, the share $1-\dt/\dtref$ of the divergence that the previous
projection left instead of dividing it by the short step
(\cref{eq:projection:reference-defect}, \issue{90}).

\emph{Retained proposals.} A call continues the previous one if its
velocity, pressure and solver are the same objects, its start time equals
the end time of the previous call, and the mesh topology has not changed
since; it then starts from the retained proposal (not from a shortened or
stretched last step) and with $\dtdef$ of the previous step (\issue{91}).
After a call with a prescribed step no proposal is retained, but $\dtdef$
is; a call that takes no step keeps a finite proposal. A full
\apifun{step!}\code{(flow, dt)} resets the proposal and retains the defect
reference of the completed step or transfer. A field-level \apifun{step!},
\apifun{project!}\code{(flow)} or a replaced workspace ends both histories.
A run split into calls whose ends lie on its step sequence therefore takes the
same steps as the uninterrupted run, whereas an end
between two steps shortens one step and changes the trajectory. The cap of
an infinite first proposal depends on the requested interval, so the first
step of a flow at rest also depends on how the run is split.

\emph{The step check.} \apifun{step!} accepts a step if, for the velocity
at the start of the step with its boundary values at
$\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stab:check}
  \dt\le\roundpar{1+10^{-6}+32\machineeps}\dtflow(\cflmax),\qquad
  \cflmax=\begin{cases}1,&\text{one phase},\\\half,&\text{two phases},\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and otherwise stops with an error that names the active bound (advective,
capillary or gravity). The check uses the top $\cflmax$ of the accepted
\code{cfl} range, so that every step \apifun{stable_timestep} returns is
accepted and the keyword alone sets the margin: a step that \apifun{simulate!}
selects with $\flowcfl=0.45$ has the margin $\cflmax/0.45$ against the check
if it is advection-limited ($2.22$ in one phase, $1.11$ in two), and only
the tolerance if it is capillary- or gravity-limited; the relative $10^{-6}$
admits the stretched
last step. With frozen interface fluxes, \apifun{step!} skips the check for a
step that \apifun{simulate!} has just selected for the same state (that
evaluation also freezes the apertures of the time-step start,
\cref{sec:vof:frozen}). The WY \apifun{advect!} makes the same check with
$\dtwy$ at $\wycflval=\half$ unless \code{check_timestep=false}; the
staggered and cell-centred \apifun{advect!} make none. The relative
$10^{-6}$ came with the stretched last step (\issue{98}); a step at
$(1+10^{-6})\dtwy$ is therefore accepted, and the rejection probe of the
check in \cref{sec:vof:cfl} lies at $(1+2\times10^{-6})\dtwy$ (\issue{115}).

The capillary wave starts at rest, so its first step is capillary-limited
with an infinite advective bound; after the steps the advective bound is
$\chkStabWaveAdvRatio$ times larger than $\dtcap$. In the cylinder flow the
cut cells reduce the bound of the unit inflow from $\chkStabCylPlain$ on the
same mesh without the solid to $\chkStabCylAdv$, a factor
$\chkStabCylReduction$ (the smallest open fraction of a cut cell is
$\chkStabCylOpen$); the first step projects the impulsive start and raises
the bound to $\chkStabCylBoundOne$, and it reaches $\chkStabCylBoundEnd$,
but the proposal keeps the first step, $\chkStabCylGap$ times below the bound
for the rest of the run. The Kelvin--Helmholtz wave is advection-limited and
grows; its steps follow the falling bound. In the combined wave all three
bounds are finite and the capillary one is active.

\emph{Empirical stability margin} (\code{stability_margin_check}). From the
initial state, a run takes a fixed number of steps of size
$\stepmult\,\dt^{\timelevel{0}}$, with $\dt^{\timelevel{0}}$ the step selected
at the start, through the prepared two-velocity path of
\apifun{simulate!}, in which \apifun{step!} does not check the step; the
largest stable multiple $\stepmult^{*}$ is the largest tested multiple below
the first unstable one. The capillary wave, run with two velocities, takes
$\chkStabWaveMarginSteps$ steps and is stable if its largest velocity stays
below the velocity amplitude of the linear standing wave (initial amplitude
times angular frequency): it is stable up to
$\stepmult^{*}=\chkStabWaveMargin$ (largest velocity $\chkStabWaveStableSpeed$
times that amplitude), while at $\stepmult=\chkStabWaveUnstable$ the
velocity exceeds it after $\chkStabWaveBlowup$ steps and reaches
$\chkStabWaveUnstableSpeed$ times it during the $\chkStabWaveMarginSteps$-step
run. The criterion is a blow-up test over $\chkStabWaveMarginSteps$ steps:
bisection with it places the limit in $[2.594,2.602]$, and over $160$ steps
the stable runs also reach the amplitude, which the linear wave itself
attains after about $70$ steps, so that the criterion no longer
discriminates there (derivation audit,
\code{proof_review/ch-stability-accuracy/}). The capillary bound, with its
factor $2^{-1/2}$, is thus about $2.6$ times below the limit observed within
$\chkStabWaveMarginSteps$ steps, which exceeds the static bound
$\minwidth/(\sqrt2\,\capspeed)$ of Denner and van Wachem. The
Kelvin--Helmholtz wave takes $\chkStabKhMarginSteps$ steps and is stable if
they complete: it does up to $\stepmult^{*}=\chkStabKhMargin$
($\wycflval=\chkStabKhMarginCfl$ relative to the initial bound), while at
$\stepmult=\chkStabKhUnstable$ ($\wycflval=\chkStabKhUnstableCfl$) the WY
transport stops in step $\chkStabKhFailStep$ with a conditional fraction
above one; bisection places the limit in $[1.425,1.434]$. Since the bound
falls during the run, the last step of the run at $\stepmult^{*}$ is
$1.29$ times $\dtflow(\half)$ of its own state, an effective
$\wycflval\approx0.65$ (derivation audit). The bound $\wycflval\le\half$ is
sufficient for the boundedness of the transport on this mesh, not necessary.
The single-phase step check admits no step above $\dtflow(1)$, so the margin of
the cylinder flow is not measured here beyond $1/0.45\approx2.22$.

\section{Known gaps between the bounds and stability}\label{sec:stab:gaps}
The bounds are sufficient for the properties that the thesis proves on
meshes without solid. The cut-donor geometric constraints extend the WY
donating-region argument to the cut cells and frozen subfaces described in
\cref{prop:vof:boundedness}. The run-time checks remain active if a caller
supplies a larger step or disables the time-step check; \apifun{simulate!}
does not retry a refused step.

\begin{deviation}{Open strip of a cut coarse donor}\label{dev:stab:strip}
At a level transition a segment's open strip can be smaller than suggested by
the whole donor volume or the shared aperture. The former bound could request
more than that strip held at its returned step (\issue{52}). The present
bound includes the strip's open area (\cref{eq:vof:geometric-cfl}), while
retaining the whole-cell incoming and outgoing constraints.
\end{deviation}

\begin{deviation}{Boundedness of cut donors}\label{dev:stab:boundedness}
The thesis proves $0\le\condfrac{\liq}{c}\le1$ using disjoint donating
regions~\thesis{Lemma~3.3, eqs.~(3.50)--(3.51)}. In a cut donor a rectangle
can extend past the cell midpoint, so the previous bound did not ensure
disjointness for hanging sides or frozen phase subfaces (\issue{56}).
The current bound separates the open portions of opposing outgoing strips
within their common support and thereby restores the geometric argument
(\cref{prop:vof:boundedness}).
\end{deviation}

\begin{deviation}{Frozen subface flux in cut donors}\label{dev:stab:frozen-flux}
The bound uses each frozen phase subface's own tangential interval and
donor open volume, rather than the whole-cell volume. Its ordering and
aperture are frozen together for both sweeps. A nonzero shared-aperture
flux with no open volume behind its subface admits no usable step, which the
bound reports as an error naming the face and donor cell; otherwise the strip
condition accounts for the volume the sweep cuts
(\issue{56}, \cref{sec:vof:frozen}).
\end{deviation}

\emph{Momentum Courant numbers next to a solid.} The two-phase momentum
transport is bounded only through the WY bound, which bounds its dual
Courant numbers by $\wycflval$ on $2{:}1$-balanced meshes without solid and,
through the cut-donor strip bound, also with a solid
(\cref{sec:momentum:cfl}); the measured ratio to $\wycflval$ over random cut
meshes with a level transition is one to round-off, so the selected Lax--Wendroff
or limited face state there is a convex combination of its two neighbours as
well (\issue{72}).

\emph{Energy of the split step.} The full split step is not claimed to be
energy-stable: the centred and Lax--Wendroff convection add energy in time
(\cref{eq:momentum:euler-energy}), the CMDGFM has no energy identity
(\cref{sec:projection:cmdgfm}), and the single-phase viscous step has
different explicit and implicit arguments (\cref{sec:stab:bounds}). The
bounds therefore limit the step by the time scales of transport and
interface waves, not by an energy estimate.

\section{Accuracy}\label{sec:stab:accuracy}
\Cref{tab:stab:orders} collects the formal orders that the chapters state for
the building blocks and the orders that the tests assert. An order is the
exponent of the mesh width in the error under refinement; the tests measure
it from the errors on two or three meshes, as the logarithm of the error
ratio divided by that of the width ratio, and assert a lower bound. Where the
step is refined with the mesh (all flow tests), the measured order is that of
the space--time error.

\emph{Space.} The orders of \cref{tab:stab:orders} are those of operator
applications or single solves on manufactured fields, at fixed geometry.
The known first-order spots are local: the closures of inclined embedded
walls (\issue{110}; the test pins the observed $0.6$ and $0.8$ and a
non-growing error, \cref{eq:viscosity:cut}), the variable-viscosity stress
at level transitions without solids (\issue{112}: $0.985$ and $1.002$ in
$L^2$ on fixed composite trees, second order on uniform meshes), the
geometric-mean vertex viscosity of the thesis~\thesis{\S7.5.1} at a
material jump parallel to an interface-aligned shear (\issue{113}: $L^2$
orders $1.48$, $0.78$, $0.82$, $0.90$, $0.95$ for $n=16$ to $512$ against
$1.96$ to $1.98$ for equal viscosities; no test). An orphaned transverse
edge next to a level transition is linear-exact at every wall type
(\issue{99}): a \apitype{NoSlip}, \apitype{FreeSlip} or \apitype{Inflow} side
closes it against the prescribed wall velocity -- exact for wall data constant
along the wall, first order for data that varies along it -- and an
\apitype{Outflow} or \apitype{ZeroGradientVelocity} side differences a
level-matched pair of unknowns instead of the offset pair. Next to triple points and at the
contact line the reconstruction is first order (\cref{ch:interface}).

\emph{Time.} The step is a sequence of stages (\cref{eq:forces:stages}):
the transport, the body force at the midpoint, the implicit viscous step,
the forward-Euler forces and the incremental projection. No order in time is
claimed for the complete step, and no test asserts one: the temporal series
at fixed mesh assert only that the error does not grow by more than a
factor $1.5$ to $2.1$ when the step is halved twice. For the frozen
two-velocity capillary wave at density ratio one the errors were
$0.006525$, $0.007361$ and $0.010889$ for the steps $1$, $\half$ and
$\tfrac14$: the spatial error dominates and grows with the number of steps.
In a refinement study the step follows the active bound: $\dt\propto\minwidth$
when advection-limited, $\dt\propto\minwidth^{3/2}$ when capillary-limited and
$\dt\propto\minwidth^{1/2}$ when gravity-limited, so that an error of first
order in $\dt$ limits the order in $\minwidth$ to $1$, $3/2$ or $1/2$ when it
dominates: a ceiling, not a prediction (at density ratio one the capillary
wave converges with $1.8$). The Kelvin--Helmholtz test runs on meshes where the advective
bound is active and expects the first-order split time error.

\emph{Flows.} The flow tests assert the space--time orders: the
Taylor--Green vortex $1.8$ (step proportional to $\minwidth$); a manufactured
wave equation on composite meshes $1.5$ in $L^2$ ($1.0$ for the velocity in
the maximum norm); Prosperetti's standing capillary waves $1.8$ at density
ratio one and $1.5$ at $1000$, the gravity wave $0.4$; the two-velocity
Kelvin--Helmholtz dispersion $0.8$, the Rayleigh--Taylor dispersion
$1.7$ ($1.3$ at density ratio $3$) and the combined wave $0.79$; the vortex
reversal $1.5$; and the sessile droplet $1.2$.

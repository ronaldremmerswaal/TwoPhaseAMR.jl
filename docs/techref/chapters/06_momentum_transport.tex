\chapter{Momentum and staggered transport}\label{ch:momentum-transport}
\chapterstatus[be4763176ad4ecac72b0858e81ba7452b0f4bf8d]{reference}
The velocity components live on the faces, so their convection is a
finite-volume transport over the staggered control volumes of
\cref{sec:setting:measures}. The code builds, once per mesh, a schedule of
\emph{dual faces} that join two staggered control volumes, and obtains the
volume flux through every dual face as a fixed linear combination of the
fluxes through the owned primal faces~\thesis{\S6.4.1}: on a uniform mesh
the average of two of them, at level transitions and in cut cells a
factorisation that keeps the dual divergence equal to an interpolation of the
primal one. Face states on the dual faces are upwind, centred,
Lax--Wendroff or limited three-point states; the update is explicit, in
two directional sweeps for the second-order states. The same schedule carries
three kinds of transport: the convection of the single-phase velocity, the
density-weighted momentum of the one-velocity two-phase model, and the
intrinsic phase velocities and staggered phase scalars of the two-velocity
model, the latter two inside the volume-of-fluid sweeps of
\cref{ch:vof-transport} and with the interpolated phase volumes as their
fluxes. This chapter states the continuous balance, the dual schedule and its
divergence identity, the face states, the three updates with their time
levels, the time-step bound and the exact conditions under which momentum,
the mass--momentum consistency and, for the centred operator, kinetic energy
are conserved; the last section gives the cell-centred upwind transport of a
passive scalar. Derivations are in the thesis~\thesis{\S\S6.2, 6.4 and
App.~6.C}.

\section{Continuous model}\label{sec:momentum:continuous}
For one phase of constant density, the convective part of the balance of the
velocity component $\avarstag=\Velo\cdot\unitvec{k}$ over the staggered
control volume $\dualcv{f}$ of a face $f\in\facesaxis{k}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:cont-single}
  \integral{\dualcv{f}}{\avarstag(\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}})}{V}
  -\integral{\dualcv{f}}{\avarstag(\timevar^{\timelevel{n}})}{V}
  +\int_{\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}}^{\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}}}
    \integral{\partial\dualcv{f}}{\avarstag\,\Velo\cdot\normal}{S}\dd\timevar=0;
\end{equation}
pressure, viscosity and forces are separate stages of the time step
(\cref{ch:overview}). In two phases each phase carries its own amount
over the part $\dualcv{f}^{\phase}(\timevar)=\dualcv{f}\cap\domain^{\open}\cap\domain^{\phase}(\timevar)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:cont}
  \integral{\dualcv{f}^{\phase}(\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}})}{\dens^{\phase}\avarstag^{\phase}}{V}
  -\integral{\dualcv{f}^{\phase}(\timevar^{\timelevel{n}})}{\dens^{\phase}\avarstag^{\phase}}{V}
  +\int_{\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}}^{\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}}}
    \integral{\partial\dualcv{f}^{\phase}(\timevar)\setminus\interface(\timevar)}
      {\dens^{\phase}\avarstag^{\phase}\,\Velo^{\phase}\cdot\normal}{S}\dd\timevar=0
\end{equation}
\thesis{eq.~(6.39)}, the staggered counterpart of~\cref{eq:vof:cont-scalar}:
the interface carries no flux because there is no phase change. The
one-velocity model transports the mixture momentum, the sum
of~\cref{eq:momentum:cont} over both phases with
$\Velo^{\liq}=\Velo^{\gas}=\Velo$ and $\avarstag^{\phase}=\Velo\cdot\unitvec{k}$;
the two-velocity model transports each phase velocity,
$\avarstag^{\phase}=\Velo^{\phase}\cdot\unitvec{k}$; a staggered phase scalar
is an intrinsic quantity with $\dens^{\phase}$ divided out. With
$\avarstag^{\phase}=1$,~\cref{eq:momentum:cont} is the balance of the phase
mass of $\dualcv{f}$. If $\divergence\Velo=0$ and the boundary is periodic or
impermeable, $\integral{\domain}{\avarstag\,\Velo\cdot\gradient\avarstag}{V}=0$,
so~\cref{eq:momentum:cont-single} conserves $\integral{\domain}{\avarstag}{V}$
and $\integral{\domain}{\tfrac12\avarstag^2}{V}$, the kinetic energy of the
component.

\section{Dual control volumes and primal--dual fluxes}\label{sec:momentum:dual}
\emph{Unknowns and volumes.} The unknowns of component $k$ are the values
$\avarstag_f$ on the interior faces $f\in\facesaxis{k}\cap\facesint$. The
phase transport of a bounded domain or of a mesh with a solid adds the
boundary faces $f\in\facesaxis{k}\cap\facesbnd$ as unknowns with a half
control volume in their cell. Every unknown has the transport volume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:volumes}
  \stagvol{f}=\half\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}
  =\begin{cases}
    \dualvol{f}, & \text{no solid},\\
    \opendualvol{f}, & \text{solid present},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad f\in\facesint,
\end{equation}
with the strip $\stripset{c}{f}$ of \cref{eq:setting:open-dual}, the
single term of $\cbnd{f}$ for a boundary unknown, and $\stagvol{f}=1$ where
this measure vanishes (no flux reaches such a closed unknown). The
volume-weighted interpolation of a cell function to the faces is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:interpolation}
  (\interpolantsbp\avar)_f=\frac{1}{2\stagvol{f}}\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}
    \meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}\,\avar_c ,
\end{equation}
$\avar_{\cbnd{f}}$ on a boundary unknown; without solid it is the
thesis' interpolant $\interpolantsbp$~\thesis{eq.~(2.30)} with the
extrusion volumes~\cref{eq:setting:dual-volume}, and the staggered fractions
$\stagfrac{\phase}{f}=(\interpolantsbp\condfrac{\phase}{})_f$ of
\cref{eq:vof:staggered-fraction} use it.

\emph{Sides.} For a cell $c$ and the transported component $k$, the faces
of $\facesaxis{k}$ in $\faceset{c}$ lie on the low or the high side of $c$,
and each covers a fraction of its side ($t\neq k$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:sides}
  \sidefaces{s}{c}=\set{f\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{k}}{\orient{c}{f}=s},\qquad
  \sidefrac{c}{f}=\frac{\meas{f}}{\cellwidth{c}{t}},\qquad
  \sum_{f\in\sidefaces{s}{c}}\sidefrac{c}{f}\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation}
with $\sidefrac{c}{f}=\half$ for a segment of a hanging coarse side
(\cref{eq:mesh:hanging}) and the sum zero for a wall side whose boundary
face is not an unknown.

\emph{Dual faces.} A dual face $g\in\dualfaces$ joins two unknowns, its tail
$\dminus{g}$ and head $\dplus{g}$, with $\dorient{f}{g}=+1$ for
$f=\dminus{g}$ and $-1$ for $f=\dplus{g}$, and carries the primal fluxes of
one axis $\faxis{g}$ with weights $\dweight{g}{f}$; there is one dual face
per pair of unknowns and axis, and reversing its orientation changes the sign
of its weights. With $(f',f)$ the dual face with tail $f'$ and head $f$, of
axis $k$ in (N) and $t\neq k$ in (T), the weights are the sums of the
contributions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:weights}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\text{(N)}\quad \dweight{(f',f)}{f}\mathrel{+}=\tfrac12\,\orient{c}{f}\,\sidefrac{c}{f'},
    &&f\in\facesaxis{k},\ c\in\cellset{f},\ f'\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{k},\ f'\neq f,\\
  &\text{(T)}\quad \dweight{(f',f'')}{f}\mathrel{+}=\tfrac12\,\sidefrac{\cminus{f}}{f'}\,\sidefrac{\cplus{f}}{f''},
    &&f\in\facesaxis{t},\ f'\in\sidefaces{s}{\cminus{f}},\ f''\in\sidefaces{s}{\cplus{f}},\ s=\pm1.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Rule (N) routes
the flux of a face normal to $\unitvec{k}$ from the other faces of its two
cells into the face itself; rule (T) couples the faces on the same side of
the two cells of a transverse face, a transport plan whose marginals are the
fractions $\sidefrac{c}{f}/2$. The integrated volume flux of a dual face is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:dual-rate}
  \dualrate{g}=\sum_{f\in\faces}\dweight{g}{f}\,\meas{f}\,\volumeflux_f,
  \quad \volumeflux_f=\aperture{f}\velo_f,\qquad
  \dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}=\sum_{f\in\faces}\dweight{g}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\phase}_f,
\end{equation}
the first for the single-phase convection and the second for the phases,
with the time-integrated phase volumes of \cref{ch:vof-transport}, which
carry their apertures already, and $\aperture{f}=1$
without solid. On a conforming patch every $\sidefrac{c}{f}$ is one and a
dual face carries two primal fluxes with weight $\half$: the dual face
through the centre of the cell between $f'$ and $f$ and the one through the
common vertex of $f$ and $f'$ along $\unitvec{t}$ have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:uniform-weights}
  \dualrate{g}=\half\roundpar{\meas{f'}\volumeflux_{f'}+\meas{f}\volumeflux_{f}},
  \qquad
  \dualrate{g}=\half\roundpar{\meas{h}\volumeflux_{h}+\meas{h'}\volumeflux_{h'}},
\end{equation}
$h,h'\in\facesaxis{t}$ the two faces with that vertex as end point, the
thesis' area-weighted interpolant $\interpolantflux$~\thesis{eq.~(6.10)}
(\cref{fig:momentum:dual:uniform}). On a uniform fully periodic mesh the code
stores exactly these two-flux faces; on every other mesh it
builds~\cref{eq:momentum:weights}, which reduces to them away from level
transitions. At a hanging side the fine segment $f_1$ of
\cref{fig:momentum:dual:coarse-fine} has \chkMomCoarseLinks\ dual faces,
among them $\dualrate{g}=\tfrac14\meas{f_2}\volumeflux_{f_2}-\tfrac14\meas{f_1}\volumeflux_{f_1}$
to the other segment of the same side and couplings with weight
$\frac18$ to the segments of the coarse sides above and below. With a solid
the uncut weights multiply the open fluxes $\meas{f}\aperture{f}\velo_f$
and the volumes are the open ones (\cref{fig:momentum:dual:cut}), the
averaging of volume fluxes of Dröge and Verstappen~\cite{droge2005} that the
thesis follows~\thesis{\S6.4.1}.

\emph{Divergence identity.} The staggered divergence of the dual faces
normal to $\unitvec{t}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:dual-divergence}
  \stagvol{f}\,(\divstag_{t}\dualrate{})_f=\sum_{g\in\dualfaceset{f},\ \faxis{g}=t}\dorient{f}{g}\,\dualrate{g},
  \qquad \divstag=\divstag_1+\divstag_2,
\end{equation}
the thesis' staggered divergence~\thesis{eq.~(6.5)}. For every unknown $f$
whose cells have unknowns on both sides
($\sum_{f'\in\sidefaces{s}{c}}\sidefrac{c}{f'}=1$ for $s=\pm1$ and
$c\in\cellset{f}$), the weights~\cref{eq:momentum:weights} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:identity}
  \stagvol{f}\,(\divstag_{t}\dualrate{})_f
  =\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\frac{\sidefrac{c}{f}}{2}
    \sum_{f'\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{t}}\orient{c}{f'}\,\meas{f'}\volumeflux_{f'},
  \qquad t=1,2,
\end{equation}
since a primal flux enters the dual faces of its cells with weights that sum
to $\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\orient{c}{f'}$ per unknown $f$ and cancel in the
dual faces between two unknowns of the same cell. In a cell that is uncut
or whose side is a single face, $\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\meas{\opencell{c}}=\half\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}$,
so the right-hand side is $\stagvol{f}$ times the
interpolation~\cref{eq:momentum:interpolation} of the directional primal
divergence: the thesis' operator connection
$\divstag\interpolantflux=\interpolantsbp\divh$~\thesis{Lemma~6.1,
eq.~(6.13)}, on the composite mesh and per sweep direction. In a cut cell
whose side carries two segments the open part of the half strip is in general
not half the open cell, and the two sides of~\cref{eq:momentum:identity} are
then not related by $\interpolantsbp$; \cref{sec:momentum:one-velocity}
quantifies the effect.

\emph{Non-regular dual faces.} A dual face is \emph{regular} unless it is in
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:regular}
  \dualnonreg=\set{g\in\dualfaces}{\#\set{f}{\dweight{g}{f}\neq0}\neq2
    \ \text{or}\ \abs{\dweight{g}{f}}\neq\half\ \text{for some }f
    \ \text{or}\ \dualvol{\dminus{g}}\neq\dualvol{\dplus{g}}},
\end{equation}
decided once with the uncut weights and volumes to $64\machineeps$
(relative); every dual face of \cref{fig:momentum:dual:coarse-fine} is
non-regular. In the phase transport of a bounded domain or of a mesh with a
solid every dual face is treated as non-regular
(\cref{sec:momentum:one-velocity}).

\emph{Checks} (\code{figures/momentum.jl}, \code{convection_flux_check}). An
independent construction of~\cref{eq:momentum:weights} reproduces the weights
of the code's schedules exactly on the uniform, composite and disc fixtures
(the cut weights after multiplication by the apertures),
and~\cref{eq:momentum:identity} holds to $\chkMomIdentity$ for a velocity that
is not solenoidal.

\begin{deviation}{Primal--dual fluxes on composite meshes and in cut cells}
The thesis defines the dual faces and their area-weighted interpolant for
conforming meshes, with $\meas{g}=\half\sum_{f}\meas{f}$ over the two primal
faces of the same normal~\thesis{eqs.~(6.5)--(6.13)}. The code generalises
the interpolant to hanging sides by the factorisation~\cref{eq:momentum:weights},
whose dual faces need not have a geometric face, may join unknowns on one
line, and are kept separate per axis so that a directional sweep uses only
fluxes of its own direction; with a solid it multiplies the weights by the
apertures and uses the open volumes~\cref{eq:momentum:volumes}.
\end{deviation}

\section{Face states}\label{sec:momentum:states}
Let $g$ be a dual face with rate $\dualrate{g}$ (time-integrated volume
$\dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}$ in the phase transport), upwind unknown
$\stencildof{u}{g}=\dminus{g}$ if the rate is non-negative and $\dplus{g}$
otherwise, downwind unknown $\stencildof{d}{g}$ the other one, and Courant
number $\mathcfl_g=\dt\abs{\dualrate{g}}/\min(\stagvol{\dminus{g}},\stagvol{\dplus{g}})$.
The face state of a staggered quantity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:states}
  \begin{gathered}
  (\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)_g=\begin{cases}
    \avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}, & \apitype{Upwind},\\
    \half\roundpar{\avarstag_{\dminus{g}}+\avarstag_{\dplus{g}}}, & \apitype{Centred},\\
    \avarstag^{\mathrm{LW}}_g, & \apitype{LaxWendroff},\\
    \text{\cref{eq:momentum:three-point}}, & \text{three-point scheme M},
  \end{cases}\\
  \avarstag^{\mathrm{LW}}_g=\half(1+\mathcfl_g)\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}+\half(1-\mathcfl_g)\avarstag_{\stencildof{d}{g}}
      -\tfrac14\dt\sum_{f\in\{\dminus{g},\dplus{g}\}}\avarstag_f(\divstag_{\sweepaxis{j}}\dualrate{})_f,
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where the last term of the Lax--Wendroff state, the directional dual
divergence~\cref{eq:momentum:dual-divergence} of the transporting flux in
the current sweep $j$, belongs to the single-phase transport only: the
variable-coefficient predictor $\avarstag-\half\dt\,\partial_k(\velo_k\avarstag)$
splits into an advective part, the first two terms, and a compressive part,
whose directional parts cancel only after both sweeps for a solenoidal
velocity. The phase transport uses the first two terms. On the uniform
schedule $\mathcfl_g=\dt\abs{\dualrate{g}}/\stagvol{f}$ is the classical
$\dt\abs{\velo}/h$ of the dual face. The three-point schemes \apitype{Fromm},
\apitype{Minmod}, \apitype{MonotonizedCentral}, \apitype{VanLeer} and
\apitype{Superbee} limit the Lax--Wendroff correction,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:three-point}
  (\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)_g=\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}
    +\limiter{\mathrm{M}}(\limratio{g})\roundpar{\avarstag^{\mathrm{LW}}_g-\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}},
\end{equation}
with the limiters of \cref{tab:vof:limiters} (\cref{fig:momentum:limiter:sweby})
and the smoothness ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:ratio}
  \limratio{g}=\frac{\axdist{d}{g}}{\axdist{uu}{g}}\,
    \frac{\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}-\avarstag_{\stencildof{uu}{g}}}{\avarstag_{\stencildof{d}{g}}-\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}},
  \qquad
  \axdist{d}{g}=\abs{(\pos{\stencildof{d}{g}}-\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}})_{t}},\quad
  \axdist{uu}{g}=\abs{(\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}}-\pos{\stencildof{uu}{g}})_{t}},
\end{equation}
$t=\faxis{g}$, $\pos{f}$ the face centroids and periodic displacements to
the nearest image; the tolerance rule of~\cref{eq:vof:limiter} sets
$\limratio{g}=1$ or $0$ for vanishing differences. The second upwind
unknown is chosen once, when the schedule is built: among the unknowns joined
to $\stencildof{u}{g}$ by another dual face of axis $t$ and lying beyond
$\stencildof{u}{g}$ along $\unitvec{t}$, it is the one nearest to the
reflection of $\stencildof{d}{g}$ through $\stencildof{u}{g}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:upstream}
  \stencildof{uu}{g}=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{f}\abs{\pos{f}-\roundpar{2\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}}-\pos{\stencildof{d}{g}}}}_2,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{sgn}\roundpar{(\pos{\stencildof{d}{g}}-\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}})_t}\,(\pos{f}-\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}})_t<-\tau,
\end{equation}
ties broken by the lower index, $\tau=64\machineeps\max(1,\domextent{t})$.
If no unknown qualifies, or if $\abs{(\pos{\stencildof{d}{g}}-\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}})_t}\le\tau$
(two segments of one side), the state is upwind; so is a limited state at a
local extremum ($\limratio{g}\le0$). On a uniform mesh this is the classical
second upwind neighbour with $\axdist{d}{g}=\axdist{uu}{g}$; at a level
transition the spacing ratio keeps $\limratio{g}=1$ for a linear profile
(\cref{fig:momentum:limiter:stencil}), the non-uniform-grid correction of
Zeng~\cite{zeng2016}.

\emph{Scheme at level transitions.} Regular dual faces use the keyword
\code{scheme} (default \apitype{LaxWendroff}), non-regular ones
$g\in\dualnonreg$ the keyword \code{amr_interface_scheme}, whose default
\apifun{default_amr_interface_scheme} keeps \apitype{Upwind} and a
three-point scheme and replaces \apitype{Centred} and \apitype{LaxWendroff}
by \apitype{MonotonizedCentral}; an upwind interface scheme with another bulk
scheme is rejected. On a uniform periodic mesh $\dualnonreg$ is empty. The
reason for the default is measurable: on a composite mesh with a unit value at
the most-coupled interface unknown and zeros elsewhere, the spatial operator
of Lax--Wendroff (and of Fromm) at the interface makes a zero unknown grow,
i.e.\ creates a new extremum for every positive step, whereas the four
limiters do not (test below).

\begin{deviation}{Directional face states}
The thesis interpolates to the centroid of an unsplit donating region of the
dual face, along a line through the downwind node with upwind values
$\tanginterp_1\avarstag$, $\tanginterp_2\avarstag$ from (bi)linear
interpolation, and limits with $\Psi(\iota)$ of a spacing-weighted slope
ratio~\thesis{eqs.~(6.47)--(6.48)}. The code uses the one-dimensional states
of every dual face in directional sweeps: the Lax--Wendroff
state~\cref{eq:momentum:states} with Courant number per dual face and, in the
single-phase transport, the compressive term; the upstream unknown of the
dual graph~\cref{eq:momentum:upstream}; and a separate scheme on non-regular
dual faces, for which the thesis has no counterpart.
\end{deviation}

\section{Single-phase convection}\label{sec:momentum:single}
\emph{Spatial operator.} With the rates $\dualrate{g}$ of an advecting velocity
$\velo$, the staggered convection operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:operator}
  \stagvol{f}\,(\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag})_f
  =\sum_{g\in\dualfaceset{f}}\dorient{f}{g}\,\dualrate{g}\,(\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)_g
  +\sum_{b\in\dualbnd{f}}\fside{b}\,\dualrate{b}\,\avarstag_b,
\end{equation}
the thesis' $\advectionstag{\massflux}{\avarstag}=\divstag\roundpar{(\interpolantflux\massflux)(\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)}$~\thesis{eq.~(6.44)}
with a constant density. The boundary dual faces $\dualbnd{f}$ exist only
when a boundary condition is passed (the Navier--Stokes step does); they
close the control volumes next to a wall, whose boundary faces are not
unknowns of the single-phase schedule. For an unknown $f$ in the wall cell
$c=\cbnd{b}$ of a boundary face $b\in\facesaxis{k}$ and on the opposite
side, and for every unknown $f$ of a cell with a boundary face
$b\in\facesaxis{t}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:boundary}
  \begin{aligned}
  &b\in\facesaxis{k}:\quad \dualrate{b}=\half\meas{f}\roundpar{\velo_b+\velo_f},\quad
    \avarstag_b=\begin{cases}
      \avarstag^{\physbnd}_b\ \text{if }\fside{b}\dualrate{b}<0,\ \text{else }\avarstag_f, & \apitype{Upwind},\\
      \half\roundpar{\avarstag_f+\avarstag^{\physbnd}_b}, & \text{other schemes},\end{cases}\\
  &b\in\facesaxis{t}:\quad \dualrate{b}=\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\meas{b}\,\velo_b,\quad
    \avarstag_b=\begin{cases}
      \Velo^{\physbnd}(\pos{b},\timevar)\cdot\unitvec{k}, & \apitype{NoSlip},\ \apitype{Inflow},\\
      \avarstag_f, & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\avarstag^{\physbnd}_b$ the stored boundary value of the transported
component and $\velo_b$ the normal velocity of the boundary face;
$\fside{b}$ orients the dual face outward. With walls $\velo_b=0$, so only
the first kind carries a flux, $\half\meas{f}\velo_f$ through the centre of
the wall cell.

\emph{Time discretisation.} \apifun{advect!} advances with forward Euler. For
\apitype{LaxWendroff} and the three-point schemes (and, on a composite mesh,
whenever \code{amr_interface_scheme} is one of them) it makes two directional
sweeps along the axes $\sweepaxis{1}\neq\sweepaxis{2}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:split}
  \avarstag^{\splitlevel{j}}=\avarstag^{\splitlevel{j-1}}
    -\dt\,\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag^{\splitlevel{j-1}}}\big|_{\sweepaxis{j}},
  \quad j=1,2,\qquad \avarstag^{\splitlevel{0}}=\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}},\quad
  \avarstag^{\timelevel{n+1}}=\avarstag^{\splitlevel{2}},
\end{equation}
where $\advectionstag{\velo}{\cdot}|_{\sweepaxis{j}}$ keeps the dual faces
with $\faxis{g}=\sweepaxis{j}$ and the boundary dual faces of walls normal
to $\unitvec{\sweepaxis{j}}$, and the Lax--Wendroff term uses
$\divstag_{\sweepaxis{j}}$; the order $(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$ alternates between
successive calls. \apitype{Upwind} and \apitype{Centred} (with an upwind or
centred interface scheme) make one unsplit step
$\avarstag^{\timelevel{n+1}}=\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}-\dt\,\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}}$.
For a \apitype{FaceVelocity} both components are evaluated from the same
input in each sweep and published together, a coordinate split and not a
split between components. The advecting velocity is fixed during the step
unless the field advects itself (\code{advect!(velocity, dt, workspace)}),
in which case each sweep uses the published intermediate. The single-phase
Navier--Stokes step transports $\velo^{\timelevel{n}}$ with itself frozen as
carrier, after enforcing its boundary values at $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$,
and passes its boundary condition and $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$ to the
boundary dual faces. \apifun{convection!} returns
$(\avarstag-\avarstag^{\timelevel{n+1}})/\dt$ for a split scheme without
advancing the order, and $\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag}$ with all dual
faces and $\mathcfl_g=0$ for $\dt=0$.

\emph{Limitations at walls.} The identity~\cref{eq:momentum:identity} needs
unknowns on both sides of a cell; at a wall the boundary dual
faces~\cref{eq:momentum:boundary} supply the missing terms, so that for a
discretely solenoidal $\velo$ with walls the constant is again a steady state
($\abs{\advectionstag{\velo}{1}}\le\chkMomWallsIdentity$ relative on a composite
mesh with walls). Two configurations are not covered. (i)~Where a level
transition meets a wall normal to $\unitvec{k}$, a fine cell whose
low side is interior lies below a coarse wall cell; rule (T) finds no
partner on the wall side of the coarse cell, and the flux of their common
transverse face does not reach the unknown on that side of the fine cell. On
a composite mesh whose refined root touches a wall, $\abs{\advectionstag{\velo}{1}}$
reaches $\chkMomAmrWallsLocal$ of the unknown's rate
$\sum_{g}\abs{\dualrate{g}}/\stagvol{f}$ at the face centred at
$\chkMomAmrWallsAt$ (\chkMomAmrWallsCount\ unknowns). (ii)~The boundary dual
faces use the uncut measures $\meas{f}$ and $\meas{b}$ also with a solid,
whereas the interior ones carry $\aperture{f}$; in a cut cell next to a wall
$\abs{\advectionstag{\velo}{1}}$ reaches $\chkMomCutWallsLocal$ of the local rate
(\chkMomCutWallsCount\ unknowns of the sloped-wall fixture of
\cref{ch:vof-transport}, \code{boundary_identity_check}). In both cases a
constant velocity is not a steady state of the convection; momentum is still
exchanged pairwise.

\section{Conservation and kinetic energy}\label{sec:momentum:conservation}
\emph{Momentum.} Every dual face enters its two control volumes with opposite
orientation, so summing~\cref{eq:momentum:split} over the unknowns,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:momentum-balance}
  \sum_{f}\stagvol{f}\avarstag^{\splitlevel{j}}_f
  =\sum_{f}\stagvol{f}\avarstag^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f
  -\dt\sum_{f}\sum_{b\in\dualbnd{f},\ b\in\facesaxis{\sweepaxis{j}}}\fside{b}\,\dualrate{b}\,\avarstag_b,
\end{equation}
for every scheme, interface scheme and mesh: on a periodic mesh the momentum
$\sum_f\stagvol{f}\avarstag_f$ of each component is conserved up to rounding,
also at level transitions and in cut cells (the unsplit step likewise).
\code{integrate(field, workspace)} returns it.

\emph{Constants.} A constant $\avarstag$ gives
$\advectionstag{\velo}{1}=\divstag\dualrate{}$, which by~\cref{eq:momentum:identity}
vanishes for a velocity that is discretely solenoidal,
$\divopen\velo=0$ (\cref{eq:setting:open-divergence}), on periodic meshes and
at walls except in the two configurations of \cref{sec:momentum:single}. The
Lax--Wendroff state of a constant is the constant plus the compressive term,
which cancels over the two sweeps only for a solenoidal velocity.

\emph{Kinetic energy.} For the all-centred operator,
\code{scheme=amr_interface_scheme=Centred()}, the state is the mean of the two
unknowns and~\cref{eq:momentum:operator} gives, on a periodic mesh (and with
walls where the boundary values $\avarstag^{\physbnd}_b$ vanish),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:skew}
  \ipstag{\psi}{\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag}}+\ipstag{\avarstag}{\advectionstag{\velo}{\psi}}
  =\sum_f\stagvol{f}\,\avarstag_f\psi_f\,(\divstag\dualrate{})_f,
  \qquad 2\,\frac{\partial(\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag})_f}{\partial\avarstag_f}=(\divstag\dualrate{})_f :
\end{equation}
the operator is skew-adjoint in $\ipstag{\cdot}{\cdot}$ up to half its dual
divergence on the diagonal. For a discretely solenoidal velocity the
semi-discrete system $\dd\avarstag/\dd\timevar=-\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag}$
therefore conserves $\tfrac12\ipstag{\avarstag}{\avarstag}$, on uniform,
composite and cut meshes; this is the conservation of quadratic invariants of
the thesis~\thesis{App.~6.C, eqs.~(6.78)--(6.81)}, where the Lax--Wendroff
state reduces to the centred one as $\dt\to0$, and the symmetry-preserving
property of Verstappen and Veldman~\cite{verstappen2003}. The default
\code{Centred()} uses \code{MonotonizedCentral} on non-regular dual faces and
is not skew on a composite mesh. The fully discrete forward-Euler step with
the centred operator adds energy,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:euler-energy}
  \tfrac12\ipstag{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n+1}}}{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n+1}}}
  -\tfrac12\ipstag{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}}{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}}
  =\tfrac12\dt^2\,\ipstag{\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}}}{\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}}}\ \ge0
  \qquad(\divstag\dualrate{}=0),
\end{equation}
so centred transport is not energy-stable in time; no energy statement is
claimed for the split Lax--Wendroff or limited updates, which dissipate
energy in the checks below.

\emph{Checks} (\code{convection_conservation_check}). On the uniform, composite
and disc fixtures with velocities from a streamfunction (on the disc fixture
one that vanishes on solid vertices, so $\divopen\velo=0$): one step of
\code{Upwind}, \code{LaxWendroff}, \code{MonotonizedCentral} and
\code{Superbee} changes the momentum by at most $\chkMomMomentum$ (relative);
the all-centred operator assembled from \apifun{convection!} at $\dt=0$
satisfies~\cref{eq:momentum:skew} with $\max\abs{\stagvol{}\advectionstag{}{}+(\stagvol{}\advectionstag{}{})^{\transpose}}\le\chkMomSkew$
relative to its largest entry and twice its diagonal equals the dual
divergence to $\chkMomDiagonal$; one centred forward-Euler step
satisfies~\cref{eq:momentum:euler-energy} to $\chkMomEuler$ of the growth term
(the cut fixture, whose small step makes the growth tiny); one default
Lax--Wendroff step decreases the energy by $\chkMomLwEnergyUniform$ (uniform)
and $\chkMomLwEnergyAmr$ (composite) of its value.

\section{Time-step restriction}\label{sec:momentum:cfl}
For the single-phase convection the code bounds the sum of the absolute rates
of every control volume,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:cfl}
  \dtstag=\stagcfl\Big/\max_{k=1,2}\ \max_{f}\ \frac{1}{\stagvol{f}}\sum_{g\in\dualfaceset{f}}\abs{\dualrate{g}},
  \qquad 0<\stagcfl\le1,
\end{equation}
over the dual faces of both axes (not the boundary dual faces), with
$\dtstag=\infty$ for a vanishing velocity. \apifun{stable_timestep}\code{(velocity, workspace; cfl=0.9)}
returns it; \apifun{step!} requires $\dt\le(1+32\machineeps)\dtstag$ with
$\stagcfl=\half$, and \apifun{simulate!} chooses steps with $\stagcfl=0.45$.
Small open volumes reduce the bound, as no cells are merged. Under the bound
the unsplit upwind update is a convex combination of old values when the
velocity is solenoidal (test below). No stability or TVD statement is
claimed for the split second-order updates on composite meshes: the classical
limiter regions of \cref{fig:momentum:limiter:sweby} are one-dimensional.

The two-phase transport of \cref{sec:momentum:one-velocity,sec:momentum:two-velocity}
has no bound of its own: the step is bounded by the volume-of-fluid bound,
capillary and gravity limits of \cref{sec:vof:cfl}. On a uniform mesh
without solid this bounds the dual Courant numbers: a dual face through a
cell centre carries half of an incoming and half of an outgoing phase volume
of that cell, each at most $\wycflval\meas{c}$, and a transverse dual face
half of a face volume of each of two cells, so $\mathcfl_g\le\wycflval\le\half$
with $\stagvol{f}=\meas{c}$.

\section{Two phases, one velocity}\label{sec:momentum:one-velocity}
The one-velocity model transports the momentum $\stagvol{f}\stagdens_f\velo_f$
of every staggered control volume inside the sweeps of
\cref{sec:vof:sweeps}, with the phase volumes of the fraction update
interpolated to the dual faces~\cref{eq:momentum:dual-rate}, so that the
staggered mass follows the cell mass algebraically~\thesis{\S6.4.1}. The
staggered density is the mixture density of the interpolated
fractions~\cref{eq:momentum:interpolation},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:density}
  \stagdens_f=\dens^{\liq}\stagfrac{\liq}{f}+\dens^{\gas}\stagfrac{\gas}{f},
  \qquad \stagfrac{\gas}{f}=1-\stagfrac{\liq}{f}.
\end{equation}
In sweep $j$ along $\sweepaxis{j}$ the carrier dilation per open volume of a
cell, the quantity frozen in~\cref{eq:vof:sweep}, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:sweep-dilation}
  \sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{c}=\frac{1}{\meas{\opencell{c}}}
    \sum_{f\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{\sweepaxis{j}}}\orient{c}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f
  \quad(\openfrac{c}>0),
\end{equation}
and zero in covered cells, and the staggered fractions predicted for the end
of the sweep interpolate the clamped fractions before snapping
($\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$ of~\cref{eq:vof:snap}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:predicted}
  \stagpred{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{f}=\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\operatorname{clamp}(\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{},0,1)}_f,
  \qquad \stagpred{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}{f}=1-\stagpred{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{f},
  \qquad \stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}_f=\textstyle\sum_\phase\dens^{\phase}\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{f}.
\end{equation}
Each component is updated in fluctuation form, as stored by the code:
\begin{multline}\label{eq:momentum:one-velocity}
  \velo^{\splitlevel{j}}_f=\velo^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f+\frac{1}{\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}_f}\Bigg(
    \frac{1}{\stagvol{f}}\sum_{\phase}\dens^{\phase}\sum_{g\in\dualfaceset{f},\ \faxis{g}=\sweepaxis{j}}\dorient{f}{g}\,
      \dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}\roundpar{\velo^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f-(\fluxinterpstag\velo^{\splitlevel{j-1}})^{\phase}_g}\\
    +\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\Big[\textstyle\sum_\phase\dens^{\phase}\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}\Big]}_f
      \roundpar{\velo^{\timelevel{n}}_f-\velo^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f}\Bigg),
\end{multline}
with the frozen indicator~\cref{eq:vof:indicator} and the start values
$\velo^{\timelevel{n}}$ in the dilation term, the analogue
of~\cref{eq:vof:fluctuation}; the face state of phase $\phase$ is that of the
modified flux interpolant below. A constant $\velo$ gives a zero increment
whatever the fractions, so constants are preserved exactly. Multiplying
by $\stagvol{f}\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}_f$ shows the conservative form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:conservative}
  \stagvol{f}\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}_f\velo^{\splitlevel{j}}_f
  =\stagvol{f}\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f\velo^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f
  -\sum_{\phase}\dens^{\phase}\sum_{g}\dorient{f}{g}\,\dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}\,(\fluxinterpstag\velo)^{\phase}_g
  +\stagvol{f}\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\Big[\textstyle\sum_\phase\dens^{\phase}\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}\Big]}_f\velo^{\timelevel{n}}_f
\end{equation}
provided the staggered mass obeys the interpolated cell mass update,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:mass-identity}
  \stagvol{f}\roundpar{\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}_f-\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f}
  =-\sum_{\phase}\dens^{\phase}\sum_{g}\dorient{f}{g}\,\dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}
  +\stagvol{f}\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\Big[\textstyle\sum_\phase\dens^{\phase}\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}\Big]}_f,
\end{equation}
the thesis' mass--momentum consistency $\advectionstag{\massflux}{1}=\interpolantsbp(\advection{\massflux}{1})$~\thesis{eq.~(6.45)}
with the dilation of the split update. By~\cref{eq:momentum:identity} and the
fraction update~\cref{eq:vof:sweep},~\cref{eq:momentum:mass-identity} holds
exactly, up to rounding, when (a)~no fraction is clamped in the sweep and
(b)~every cell $c\in\cellset{f}$ is uncut or has the face $f$ as the single
face of its side, so that $\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}=\sidefrac{c}{f}\meas{\opencell{c}}$.
Where (b) fails, in a cut coarse cell whose side carries two segments, the
staggered mass and momentum drift from the cell mass by the difference of the
two weightings. A snap of a fraction after the sweep~\cref{eq:vof:snap}
changes $\stagdens$ but not $\velo$, so it changes the momentum by
$\stagvol{f}\,\delta\stagdens_f\velo_f$, as for the phase scalars of
\cref{sec:vof:scalars}. Summing~\cref{eq:momentum:conservative} over the
unknowns and the two sweeps, the momentum
$\sum_f\stagvol{f}\stagdens_f\velo_f$ of each component changes by
$\sum_j\sum_f\stagvol{f}(\interpolantsbp[\sum_\phase\dens^\phase\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}])_f\velo^{\timelevel{n}}_f$,
which vanishes for a carrier that is solenoidal in every cell, since the
frozen values make the two dilations of a cell cancel; it is therefore
conserved up to rounding under (a), (b), no snapping, and periodic or
impermeable boundaries. This is the thesis' exact conservation of the sum of
the phase momenta~\thesis{eq.~(6.53)}: the update divides the sum of the
phase momenta by the sum of the phase masses.

\emph{Modified flux interpolant.} The state of phase $\phase$ on the dual
face $g$ is the bulk state~\cref{eq:momentum:states} of the selected scheme,
without the compressive term and with $\mathcfl_g=\abs{\dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}}/\min\stagvol{}$,
unless an adjacent predicted fraction is small, in which case the flux is
upwinded term by term:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:ctu}
  \dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}(\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)^{\phase}_g=\begin{cases}
    \displaystyle\sum_{f}\dweight{g}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\phase}_f\;
      \avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g,f}}, &
      \min\roundpar{\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{\dminus{g}},\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{\dplus{g}}}<\minfracval,\\
    \dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}\,(\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)_g, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\stencildof{u}{g,f}=\dminus{g}$ if the partial volume
$\dweight{g}{f}\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\phase}_f$ is non-negative and
$\dplus{g}$ otherwise, and $\minfracval$ is \code{ctu_threshold} (default
$\half$, in $(0,1]$). Keeping the partial interpolated volumes separate lets
fluid in transit through a nearly empty control volume, entering through one
primal face and leaving through another, bypass its value: the
corner-transport-upwind (CTU) flux of the thesis~\thesis{eqs.~(6.49)--(6.51)},
whose switch bounds the update of a draining control volume~\thesis{Lemmas~6.2
and 6.3}.

\emph{Boundaries and solids.} In a bounded domain and with a solid the phase
schedule~\cref{eq:momentum:volumes} includes the boundary unknowns (whose
primal volumes are zero, \cref{sec:vof:cfl}), so the identity holds there
without boundary dual faces; the transported boundary values are overwritten
when the Navier--Stokes step enforces its boundary condition. This schedule
marks every dual face as non-regular and stores no upstream unknowns: the
bulk \code{scheme} is not used, and a three-point interface scheme
(\code{MonotonizedCentral} by default for \code{LaxWendroff} and
\code{Centred}) reduces to the upwind state. With the default options the
two-phase staggered transport of a bounded domain or of a mesh with a solid
is therefore first-order upwind in the bulk and CTU near the interface.

\emph{Checks} (\code{one_velocity_momentum_check},
\code{bounded_phase_scheme_check}, \code{constant_velocity_check}). An
independent replica of~\cref{eq:momentum:density,eq:momentum:sweep-dilation,eq:momentum:predicted,eq:momentum:one-velocity,eq:momentum:ctu},
with its own schedule and states and the code's primal phase volumes,
reproduces the velocity after \apifun{advect!} to $\chkMomOneReplica$
(relative) on the uniform, composite, cut and walled fixtures (drop and
streamfunction velocity of \cref{ch:vof-transport}; on the cut fixture a gas
bubble crosses a level transition and the disc of \cref{fig:momentum:dual}
lies in the liquid). Without cut cells, with
\code{LaxWendroff} and with \code{MonotonizedCentral} in the order $(2,1)$,
no fraction is clamped or snapped, the mass identity~\cref{eq:momentum:mass-identity}
holds to $\chkMomOneMass$ in every unknown and sweep, and the momentum changes
by at most $\chkMomOneMomentum$ (relative). On the cut fixture the identity
holds to the same accuracy wherever (b) holds, misses by up to
$\chkMomOneCutMass\,\dens^{\liq}$ per unit volume in \chkMomOneCutDofs\
unknown-sweeps next to cut coarse cells with two segments on a side, and the
momentum changes by $\chkMomOneCutMomentum$. A constant transported velocity
is reproduced exactly on all fixtures. On a bounded uniform mesh
\code{LaxWendroff}, \code{MonotonizedCentral} and \code{Centred} give the
upwind result bitwise, whereas on the same mesh with periodic boundaries they
differ from it by up to $\chkMomBoundedPeriodicDiff$.

\begin{deviation}{Modified flux interpolant in directional sweeps}
The thesis switches to the CTU interpolant when the staggered fraction at
the end of the unsplit step falls below $\minfracval=\half$, with partial
volume fluxes of the unsplit donating regions over the $2\times3^{d-1}$
neighbouring faces~\thesis{eqs.~(6.49)--(6.51)}. The code applies the switch
per directional sweep with the predicted fractions~\cref{eq:momentum:predicted}
and upwinds the partial interpolated volumes of the
weights~\cref{eq:momentum:weights} between the two unknowns of the dual face
only; the bulk state is the directional one of \cref{sec:momentum:states},
and a dilation term with frozen values completes the split update.
\end{deviation}

\section{Two velocities and staggered phase scalars}\label{sec:momentum:two-velocity}
The two-velocity model stores intrinsic phase velocities, and a
\apitype{PhaseFaceScalar} stores intrinsic liquid and gas values on the
faces of one axis; both are transported by the same update, per phase, with
the phase volumes of the carrier of \cref{sec:vof:two-velocity}. Before the
sweeps, a value is \emph{absent} where its phase has no open staggered
volume ($\stagvol{f}$ of~\cref{eq:momentum:volumes} zero, or
$\operatorname{clamp}(\stagfrac{\liq}{f},0,1)=0$ for the liquid, $=1$ for
the gas); absent values are extended in two passes over the dual faces, each
taking the mean of the present values of the unknowns joined to it, and set
to zero beyond,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:extension}
  \extstag{\phase}_f=\begin{cases}
    \avarstag^{\phase}_f, & \text{present},\\
    \frac{1}{\#N(f)}\sum_{f'\in N(f)}\extstag{\phase}_{f'}, & N(f)\neq\emptyset\text{ in one of the two passes},\\
    0, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
$N(f)$ the unknowns with a value that share a dual face with $f$ at the start
of the pass. These working values are also the frozen start values
$\avarstag^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}$ of the dilation term. In sweep $j$, with the
predicted fraction $\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{f}$
of~\cref{eq:momentum:predicted},
\begin{multline}\label{eq:momentum:two-velocity}
  \avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}_f=\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_f
  +\frac{1}{\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{f}}\Bigg(
    \frac{1}{\stagvol{f}}\sum_{g\in\dualfaceset{f},\ \faxis{g}=\sweepaxis{j}}\dorient{f}{g}\,
      \dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}\roundpar{\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_f-(\fluxinterpstag\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}})_g}\\
    +\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\big[\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}\big]}_f
      \roundpar{\avarstag^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}_f-\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_f}\Bigg)
  \quad\text{if }\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{f}>\ffloor,
\end{multline}
and $\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}_f=\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_f$
otherwise, with the modified interpolant~\cref{eq:momentum:ctu}. There is no
density: for constant $\dens^{\phase}$ it cancels, and the conserved amount
of phase $\phase$ is $\dens^{\phase}\sum_f\stagvol{f}\stagfrac{\phase}{f}\avarstag^{\phase}_f$
(\apifun{integrate}\code{(q, phase)} for a phase scalar). The two carriers
differ as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item \apitype{FrozenInterfaceFluxes}: the phase volumes of~\cref{eq:vof:frozen-flux}
  partition the carrier, $\stagpred{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}{}=1-\stagpred{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{}$,
  and both phases follow~\cref{eq:momentum:two-velocity} with their indicator.
\item \apitype{ProjectedLiquidVelocity}: the liquid volumes come from the
  projected carrier $\projvelo^{\liq}$, the transported liquid values from the
  unprojected liquid velocity. The gas uses the auxiliary carrier
  of~\cref{eq:vof:aux-gas}: its predicted fraction is
  $\stagpred{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}{f}=(\interpolantsbp\max(\auxgas{}{}-\Delta_{\gas},0))_f$,
  $\Delta_{\gas}$ the auxiliary-carrier update of the sweep, its fraction is
  reset to the complement at the start of every sweep, and its dilation term
  is zero. The reset changes the gas momentum; the code accumulates, for
  each component $k$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:gas-reset}
    \gasmomreset{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_f\dens^{\gas}\stagvol{f}\,\avarstag^{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}_f
      \roundpar{1-\stagpred{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{f}-\stagpred{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}{f}},
  \end{equation}
  reported by \apifun{phase_flux_diagnostics} with the absolute values
  likewise.
\end{itemize}
After the step the velocities of phases without open staggered volume in the
final state are stored as \code{NaN}~\cref{eq:vof:extension}. As
for~\cref{eq:momentum:conservative}, the update conserves the phase amount up
to the frozen dilation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:two-velocity-balance}
  \sum_f\stagvol{f}\stagfrac{\phase}{f}\avarstag^{\phase}_f\Big|^{\timelevel{n+1}}_{\timelevel{n}}
  =\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_f\stagvol{f}\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\big[\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}\big]}_f\avarstag^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}_f
  \ \ \roundpar{+\,\gasmomreset{}/\dens^{\gas}\text{ for the auxiliary gas}},
\end{equation}
under conditions (a) and (b) of \cref{sec:momentum:one-velocity}, without
snapping and with every updated unknown above the floor; the dilation sum
vanishes for a carrier that is solenoidal in every cell (the mixture
constraint on the frozen apertures, or the projected liquid carrier).

\emph{Checks} (\code{two_velocity_momentum_check}). An independent replica
of~\cref{eq:momentum:extension,eq:momentum:two-velocity,eq:momentum:ctu,eq:momentum:gas-reset}
reproduces both phase velocities after \apifun{advect!} to $\chkMomTwoReplica$
(relative) for both carriers on the uniform, composite and walled fixtures
and the \code{NaN} pattern exactly; the balance~\cref{eq:momentum:two-velocity-balance}
holds to $\chkMomTwoBalance$ (relative), where the second streamfunction
used as gas velocity makes the frozen carrier non-solenoidal in mixed cells
and the dilation sum non-zero; for the projected carrier the liquid amount
changes by at most $\chkMomTwoProjectedLiquid$ and the reported reset equals
the replica's to $\chkMomTwoReset$ (relative).

\begin{deviation}{Two-velocity momentum}
The thesis transports the liquid momentum with the liquid mass fluxes of the
projected-liquid method and the gas momentum with the gas mass fluxes of its
auxiliary carrier, divided by the auxiliary gas mass and reset to the
complement after every time step~\thesis{eqs.~(6.54)--(6.55)}. The code
resets after every directional sweep and reports the
discrepancy~\cref{eq:momentum:gas-reset}, transports the unprojected liquid
velocity with the projected-carrier volumes, and also offers the frozen
carrier (\cref{sec:vof:frozen}), with which both phase amounts are
balanced by~\cref{eq:momentum:two-velocity-balance}.
\end{deviation}

\section{Cell-centred scalar transport}\label{sec:momentum:cell-scalar}
A passive \apitype{CellField} $\avar$ is transported by the conservative law
$\partial_\timevar\avar+\divergence(\avar\Velo)=0$ with the explicit upwind
finite-volume update
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:cell-scalar}
  \begin{gathered}
  \meas{\opencell{c}}\avar^{\timelevel{n+1}}_c=\meas{\opencell{c}}\avar^{\timelevel{n}}_c
  -\dt\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\orient{c}{f}\,\meas{f}\aperture{f}\velo_f\,\avar^{\mathrm{u}}_f,\\
  \avar^{\mathrm{u}}_f=\begin{cases}
    \avar_{\cminus{f}}\text{ if }\velo_f\ge0,\ \avar_{\cplus{f}}\text{ otherwise}, & f\in\facesint,\\
    \avar_{\cbnd{f}}\text{ if }\fside{f}\velo_f\ge0,\ \avar^{\physbnd}_f\text{ otherwise}, & f\in\facesbnd,
  \end{cases}
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where the inflow value $\avar^{\physbnd}_f$ is the cell value
(\code{ZeroGradient}) or the prescribed function at $(\pos{f},\timevar)$
(\code{Dirichlet}); without solid $\aperture{f}=1$ and
$\meas{\opencell{c}}=\meas{c}$, and covered cells are not updated. Every owned
segment is evaluated once, so the update is conservative on the composite
mesh (\cref{sec:setting:sample}); a constant is preserved where
$\divopen\velo=0$. The bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:cell-cfl}
  \dtcell=\stagcfl\Big/\max_c\frac{1}{\meas{c}}\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\meas{f}\abs{\velo_f},
  \qquad 0<\stagcfl\le1\ (\text{default }0.9),
\end{equation}
uses the full cell volumes and no apertures; for a solenoidal velocity it
makes the uncut update a convex combination of upwind values, but with a
solid it does not bound the open-volume update of small cut cells. The
transport is not used by the flow solvers, whose cell quantities are the
phase fractions and phase scalars of \cref{ch:vof-transport}.

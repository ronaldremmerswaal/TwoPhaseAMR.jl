\chapter{Momentum and staggered transport}\label{ch:momentum-transport}
\chapterstatus[afcce97778b5b41eb1f7335e464c157c8572d2f5]{reference}
The velocity components live on the faces, so their convection is a
finite-volume transport over the staggered control volumes of
\cref{sec:setting:measures}. The code builds, once per mesh, a schedule of
\emph{dual faces} that join two staggered control volumes, and obtains the
volume flux through every dual face as a fixed linear combination of the
fluxes through the owned primal faces~\thesis{\S6.4.1}: on a uniform mesh
the average of two of them, at level transitions and in cut cells a
factorisation that keeps the dual divergence equal to an interpolation of the
primal one. Face states on the dual faces are upwind, centred,
Lax--Wendroff or limited three-point states; the update is explicit, in
two directional sweeps for the second-order states. The same schedule carries
three kinds of transport: the convection of the single-phase velocity, the
density-weighted momentum of the one-velocity two-phase model, and the
intrinsic phase velocities and staggered phase scalars of the two-velocity
model, the latter two inside the volume-of-fluid sweeps of
\cref{ch:vof-transport} and with the interpolated phase volumes as their
fluxes. This chapter states the continuous balance, the dual schedule and its
divergence identity, the face states, the three updates with their time
levels, the time-step bound and the exact conditions under which momentum,
the mass--momentum consistency and, for the centred operator, kinetic energy
are conserved, and it collects which face state each dual face uses; the last
section gives the cell-centred upwind transport of a passive scalar.
Derivations are in the thesis~\thesis{\S\S6.2, 6.4 and App.~6.C}.

\section{Continuous model}\label{sec:momentum:continuous}
For one phase of constant density, the convective part of the balance of the
velocity component $\avarstag=\Velo\cdot\unitvec{k}$ over the staggered
control volume $\dualcv{f}$ of a face $f\in\facesaxis{k}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:cont-single}
  \integral{\dualcv{f}}{\avarstag(\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}})}{V}
  -\integral{\dualcv{f}}{\avarstag(\timevar^{\timelevel{n}})}{V}
  +\int_{\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}}^{\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}}}
    \integral{\partial\dualcv{f}}{\avarstag\,\Velo\cdot\normal}{S}\dd\timevar=0;
\end{equation}
pressure, viscosity and forces are separate stages of the time step
(\cref{ch:overview}). In two phases each phase carries its own amount
over the part $\dualcv{f}^{\phase}(\timevar)=\dualcv{f}\cap\domain^{\open}\cap\domain^{\phase}(\timevar)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:cont}
  \integral{\dualcv{f}^{\phase}(\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}})}{\dens^{\phase}\avarstag^{\phase}}{V}
  -\integral{\dualcv{f}^{\phase}(\timevar^{\timelevel{n}})}{\dens^{\phase}\avarstag^{\phase}}{V}
  +\int_{\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}}^{\timevar^{\timelevel{n+1}}}
    \integral{\partial\dualcv{f}^{\phase}(\timevar)\setminus\interface(\timevar)}
      {\dens^{\phase}\avarstag^{\phase}\,\Velo^{\phase}\cdot\normal}{S}\dd\timevar=0
\end{equation}
\thesis{eq.~(6.39)}, the staggered counterpart of~\cref{eq:vof:cont-scalar}:
the interface carries no flux because there is no phase change. The
one-velocity model transports the mixture momentum, the sum
of~\cref{eq:momentum:cont} over both phases with
$\Velo^{\liq}=\Velo^{\gas}=\Velo$ and $\avarstag^{\phase}=\Velo\cdot\unitvec{k}$;
the two-velocity model transports each phase velocity,
$\avarstag^{\phase}=\Velo^{\phase}\cdot\unitvec{k}$; a staggered phase scalar
is an intrinsic quantity with $\dens^{\phase}$ divided out. With
$\avarstag^{\phase}=1$,~\cref{eq:momentum:cont} is the balance of the phase
mass of $\dualcv{f}$. If $\divergence\Velo=0$ and the boundary is periodic or
impermeable, $\integral{\domain}{\avarstag\,\Velo\cdot\gradient\avarstag}{V}=0$,
so~\cref{eq:momentum:cont-single} conserves $\integral{\domain}{\avarstag}{V}$
and $\integral{\domain}{\tfrac12\avarstag^2}{V}$, the kinetic energy of the
component.

\section{Dual control volumes and primal--dual fluxes}\label{sec:momentum:dual}
\emph{Unknowns and volumes.} The unknowns of component $k$ are the values
$\avarstag_f$ on the interior faces $f\in\facesaxis{k}\cap\facesint$. The
phase transport of a bounded domain or of a mesh with a solid adds the
boundary faces $f\in\facesaxis{k}\cap\facesbnd$ as unknowns with a half
control volume in their cell. Every unknown has the transport volume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:volumes}
  \stagvol{f}=\half\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}
  =\begin{cases}
    \dualvol{f}, & \text{no solid},\\
    \opendualvol{f}, & \text{solid present},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad f\in\facesint,
\end{equation}
with the strip $\stripset{c}{f}$ of \cref{eq:setting:open-dual}, the
single term of $\cbnd{f}$ for a boundary unknown, and $\stagvol{f}=1$ where
this measure vanishes (no flux reaches such a closed unknown). The
volume-weighted interpolation of a cell function to the faces is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:interpolation}
  (\interpolantsbp\avar)_f=\frac{1}{2\stagvol{f}}\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}
    \meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}\,\avar_c ,
\end{equation}
$\avar_{\cbnd{f}}$ on a boundary unknown; without solid it is the
thesis' interpolant $\interpolantsbp$~\thesis{eq.~(2.30)} with the
extrusion volumes~\cref{eq:setting:dual-volume}, and the staggered fractions
$\stagfrac{\phase}{f}=(\interpolantsbp\condfrac{\phase}{})_f$ of
\cref{eq:vof:staggered-fraction} use it.

\emph{Sides.} For a cell $c$ and the transported component $k$, the faces
of $\facesaxis{k}$ in $\faceset{c}$ lie on the low or the high side of $c$,
and each covers a fraction of its side ($t\neq k$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:sides}
  \sidefaces{s}{c}=\set{f\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{k}}{\orient{c}{f}=s},\qquad
  \sidefrac{c}{f}=\frac{\meas{f}}{\cellwidth{c}{t}},\qquad
  \sum_{f\in\sidefaces{s}{c}}\sidefrac{c}{f}\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation}
with $\sidefrac{c}{f}=\half$ for a segment of a hanging coarse side
(\cref{eq:mesh:hanging}) and the sum zero for a wall side whose boundary
face is not an unknown.

\emph{Dual faces.} A dual face $g\in\dualfaces$ joins two unknowns, its tail
$\dminus{g}$ and head $\dplus{g}$, with $\dorient{f}{g}=+1$ for
$f=\dminus{g}$ and $-1$ for $f=\dplus{g}$, and carries the primal fluxes of
one axis $\faxis{g}$ with weights $\dweight{g}{f}$; there is one dual face
per pair of unknowns and axis, and reversing its orientation changes the sign
of its weights. With $(f',f)$ the dual face with tail $f'$ and head $f$, of
axis $k$ in (N) and $t\neq k$ in (T), the weights are the sums of the
contributions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:weights}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\text{(N)}\quad \dweight{(f',f)}{f}\mathrel{+}=\tfrac12\,\orient{c}{f}\,\sidefrac{c}{f'}
    &&\text{for }f\in\facesaxis{k},\ c\in\cellset{f},\\
  &&&\phantom{\text{for }}f'\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{k},\ f'\neq f,\\
  &\text{(T)}\quad \dweight{(f',f'')}{f}\mathrel{+}=\tfrac12\,\sidefrac{\cminus{f}}{f'}\,\sidefrac{\cplus{f}}{f''}
    &&\text{for }f\in\facesaxis{t},\ s=\pm1,\\
  &&&\phantom{\text{for }}f'\in\sidefaces{s}{\cminus{f}},\ f''\in\sidefaces{s}{\cplus{f}}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Rule (N) routes
the flux of a face normal to $\unitvec{k}$ from the other faces of its two
cells into the face itself; rule (T) couples the faces on the same side of
the two cells of a transverse face, a transport plan whose marginals are the
fractions $\sidefrac{c}{f}/2$. The integrated volume flux of a dual face is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:dual-rate}
  \dualrate{g}=\sum_{f\in\faces}\dweight{g}{f}\,\meas{f}\,\volumeflux_f,
  \quad \volumeflux_f=\aperture{f}\velo_f,\qquad
  \dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}=\sum_{f\in\faces}\dweight{g}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\phase}_f,
\end{equation}
the first for the single-phase convection and the second for the phases,
with the time-integrated phase volumes of \cref{ch:vof-transport}, which
carry their apertures already, and $\aperture{f}=1$
without solid. On a conforming patch every $\sidefrac{c}{f}$ is one and a
dual face carries two primal fluxes with weight $\half$: the dual face
through the centre of the cell between $f'$ and $f$ and the one through the
common vertex of $f$ and $f'$ along $\unitvec{t}$ have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:uniform-weights}
  \dualrate{g}=\half\roundpar{\meas{f'}\volumeflux_{f'}+\meas{f}\volumeflux_{f}},
  \qquad
  \dualrate{g}=\half\roundpar{\meas{h}\volumeflux_{h}+\meas{h'}\volumeflux_{h'}},
\end{equation}
$h,h'\in\facesaxis{t}$ the two faces with that vertex as end point, the
thesis' area-weighted interpolant $\interpolantflux$~\thesis{eq.~(6.10)}
(\cref{fig:momentum:dual:uniform}). On a uniform fully periodic mesh the code
stores exactly these two-flux faces; on every other mesh it
builds~\cref{eq:momentum:weights}, which reduces to them away from level
transitions. At a hanging side the fine segment $f_1$ of
\cref{fig:momentum:dual:coarse-fine} has \chkMomCoarseLinks\ dual faces,
among them $\dualrate{g}=\tfrac14\meas{f_2}\volumeflux_{f_2}-\tfrac14\meas{f_1}\volumeflux_{f_1}$
to the other segment of the same side and couplings with weight
$\frac18$ to the segments of the coarse sides above and below. With a solid
the uncut weights multiply the open fluxes $\meas{f}\aperture{f}\velo_f$
and the volumes are the open ones (\cref{fig:momentum:dual:cut}), the
averaging of volume fluxes of Dröge and Verstappen~\cite{droge2005} that the
thesis follows~\thesis{\S6.4.1}; only the one-velocity momentum replaces the
fractions $\sidefrac{c}{f}$ of a cut cell by the open-strip
fractions~\cref{eq:momentum:open-fractions}. Walls add the boundary dual faces
of \cref{sec:momentum:single}.

\emph{Divergence identity.} The staggered divergence of the dual faces
normal to $\unitvec{t}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:dual-divergence}
  \stagvol{f}\,(\divstag_{t}\dualrate{})_f=\sum_{g\in\dualfaceset{f},\ \faxis{g}=t}\dorient{f}{g}\,\dualrate{g},
  \qquad \divstag=\divstag_1+\divstag_2,
\end{equation}
the thesis' staggered divergence~\thesis{eq.~(6.5)}. For every unknown
$f\in\facesaxis{k}$ whose cells have unknowns on both sides
($\sum_{f'\in\sidefaces{s}{c}}\sidefrac{c}{f'}=1$ for $s=\pm1$ and
$c\in\cellset{f}$) and no flux through a boundary face, the
weights~\cref{eq:momentum:weights} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:identity}
  \stagvol{f}\,(\divstag_{t}\dualrate{})_f
  =\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\frac{\sidefrac{c}{f}}{2}
    \sum_{f'\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{t}}\orient{c}{f'}\,\meas{f'}\volumeflux_{f'},
  \qquad t=1,2.
\end{equation}
For $t=k$ the flux of a face $f'\neq f$ of $c$ contributes
$\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\orient{c}{f'}\meas{f'}\volumeflux_{f'}$ to the left side
through the dual face joining $f$ and $f'$, as on the right, while the flux of
$f$ itself contributes $-\half\orient{c}{f}(\sidesum{c}-\sidefrac{c}{f})\meas{f}\volumeflux_f$,
with $\sidesum{c}=\sum_{s=\pm1}\sum_{f'\in\sidefaces{s}{c}}\sidefrac{c}{f'}$; the
difference to the right side is $-\half\sidesum{c}\orient{c}{f}\meas{f}\volumeflux_f$,
which cancels between the two cells of $f$ for $\sidesum{c}=2$. For $t\neq k$
the flux of a face $h\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{t}$ reaches $f$ through the
dual faces between $f$ and the unknowns $f''$ on the same side $s$ of the
neighbour $c'$ across $h$, with weights summing to
$\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\orient{c}{h}\sum_{f''\in\sidefaces{s}{c'}}\sidefrac{c'}{f''}$,
which is the right side when that sum is one. On a $2{:}1$-balanced mesh the
hypothesis implies this: a side of $c'$ without unknown is a wall, so either
$f$ lies on that wall or $c'$ is coarser than $c$ and the other cell of $f$
reaches the wall. At a wall the two sides differ by exactly the missing terms:
a wall cell ($\sidesum{c}=1$) with the boundary face $b\in\facesaxis{k}$ lacks
$\half\fside{b}\meas{f}(\volumeflux_b+\volumeflux_f)$, a boundary face
$b\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{t}$ lacks $\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\fside{b}\meas{b}\volumeflux_b$,
and an unknown whose neighbour $c'$ has a wall on the side $s$, which happens
where a level transition meets a wall normal to $\unitvec{k}$, lacks
$\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\orient{c}{h}\meas{h}\volumeflux_h$; the boundary dual faces
of \cref{sec:momentum:single} supply exactly these terms (\cref{eq:momentum:closed-identity}).
In a cell that is uncut or in which $f$ is the single face of its side,
$\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\meas{\opencell{c}}=\half\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}$,
so the right-hand side is $\stagvol{f}$ times the
interpolation~\cref{eq:momentum:interpolation} of the directional primal
divergence: the thesis' operator connection
$\divstag\interpolantflux=\interpolantsbp\divh$~\thesis{Lemma~6.1,
eq.~(6.13)}, on the composite mesh and per sweep direction. In a cut cell
whose side carries two segments the open part of the half strip is in general
not half the open cell: the right side then differs from
$\stagvol{f}(\interpolantsbp\divopen_t\velo)_f$ by
$\half\sum_{c}\roundpar{\sidefrac{c}{f}\meas{\opencell{c}}-\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}}(\divopen_t\velo)_c$,
$\divopen_t$ the part of $\divopen$ through the faces of $\facesaxis{t}$, which
vanishes for $\divopen_t\velo=0$; the one-velocity momentum avoids the
difference with the fractions~\cref{eq:momentum:open-fractions}.

\emph{Non-regular dual faces.} A dual face is \emph{regular} unless it is in
\begin{multline}\label{eq:momentum:regular}
  \dualnonreg=\bigl\{\,g\in\dualfaces\bigm|\#\set{f}{\dweight{g}{f}\neq0}\neq2
    \ \text{or}\ \abs{\dweight{g}{f}}\neq\half\ \text{for some }f\\
    \text{or}\ \dualvol{\dminus{g}}\neq\dualvol{\dplus{g}}
    \ \text{or}\ \curlypar{\dminus{g},\dplus{g}}\cap\facesbnd\neq\emptyset\,\bigr\},
\end{multline}
decided once, when the schedule is built, with the uncut weights and volumes
to $64\machineeps$ (relative), so that a dual face between cut cells is
regular when its uncut counterpart is. The last condition concerns the
boundary unknowns of the phase schedule of a bounded domain or of a mesh with
a solid, whose uncut volume is half their cell. Every dual face of
\cref{fig:momentum:dual:coarse-fine} is non-regular;
\cref{sec:momentum:flux-rules} collects which face state each kind of dual
face uses.

\emph{Checks} (\code{figures/momentum.jl}, \code{convection_flux_check}). An
independent construction of~\cref{eq:momentum:weights} reproduces the weights
of the code's schedules exactly on the uniform, composite and disc fixtures
(the cut weights after multiplication by the apertures),
and~\cref{eq:momentum:identity} holds to $\chkMomIdentity$ for a velocity that
is not solenoidal.

\begin{deviation}{Primal--dual fluxes on composite meshes and in cut cells}
The thesis defines the dual faces and their area-weighted interpolant for
conforming meshes, with $\meas{g}=\half\sum_{f}\meas{f}$ over the two primal
faces of the same normal~\thesis{eqs.~(6.5)--(6.13)}. The code generalises
the interpolant to hanging sides by the factorisation~\cref{eq:momentum:weights},
whose dual faces need not have a geometric face, may join unknowns on one
line, and are kept separate per axis so that a directional sweep uses only
fluxes of its own direction; with a solid it multiplies the weights by the
apertures and uses the open volumes~\cref{eq:momentum:volumes}, and for the
one-velocity momentum it replaces the fraction of a segment of a hanging side
of a cut cell by its open-strip fraction~\cref{eq:momentum:open-fractions}.
At walls the single-phase schedule adds the boundary dual
faces~\cref{eq:momentum:boundary}.
\end{deviation}

\section{Face states}\label{sec:momentum:states}
Let $g$ be a dual face with rate $\dualrate{g}$ (time-integrated volume
$\dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}$ in the phase transport), upwind unknown
$\stencildof{u}{g}=\dminus{g}$ if the rate is non-negative and $\dplus{g}$
otherwise, downwind unknown $\stencildof{d}{g}$ the other one, and Courant
number $\mathcfl_g=\dt\abs{\dualrate{g}}/\min(\stagvol{\dminus{g}},\stagvol{\dplus{g}})$.
The face state of a staggered quantity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:states}
  \begin{gathered}
  (\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)_g=\begin{cases}
    \avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}, & \apitype{Upwind},\\
    \half\roundpar{\avarstag_{\dminus{g}}+\avarstag_{\dplus{g}}}, & \apitype{Centred},\\
    \avarstag^{\mathrm{LW}}_g, & \apitype{LaxWendroff},\\
    \text{\cref{eq:momentum:three-point}}, & \text{three-point scheme M},
  \end{cases}\\
  \avarstag^{\mathrm{LW}}_g=\half(1+\mathcfl_g)\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}+\half(1-\mathcfl_g)\avarstag_{\stencildof{d}{g}}
      -\tfrac14\dt\sum_{f\in\{\dminus{g},\dplus{g}\}}\avarstag_f(\divstag_{\sweepaxis{j}}\dualrate{})_f,
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where the last term of the Lax--Wendroff state, the directional dual
divergence~\cref{eq:momentum:dual-divergence} of the transporting flux in
the current sweep $j$, belongs to the single-phase transport only; when a
boundary condition is passed, the boundary dual faces of the sweep are added
to it, which makes it the closed divergence $\divstagclosed_{\sweepaxis{j}}$
of~\cref{eq:momentum:closed-identity}. The
variable-coefficient predictor $\avarstag-\half\dt\,\partial_k(\velo_k\avarstag)$
splits into an advective part, the first two terms, and a compressive part,
whose directional parts cancel only after both sweeps for a solenoidal
velocity. The phase transport uses the first two terms. On the uniform
schedule $\mathcfl_g=\dt\abs{\dualrate{g}}/\stagvol{f}$ is the classical
$\dt\abs{\velo}/h$ of the dual face. The three-point schemes \apitype{Fromm},
\apitype{Minmod}, \apitype{MonotonizedCentral}, \apitype{VanLeer} and
\apitype{Superbee} limit the Lax--Wendroff correction,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:three-point}
  (\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)_g=\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}
    +\limiter{\mathrm{M}}(\limratio{g})\roundpar{\avarstag^{\mathrm{LW}}_g-\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}},
\end{equation}
with the limiters of \cref{tab:vof:limiters} (\cref{fig:momentum:limiter:sweby})
and the smoothness ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:ratio}
  \limratio{g}=\frac{\axdist{d}{g}}{\axdist{uu}{g}}\,
    \frac{\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}-\avarstag_{\stencildof{uu}{g}}}{\avarstag_{\stencildof{d}{g}}-\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}},
  \qquad
  \axdist{d}{g}=\abs{(\pos{\stencildof{d}{g}}-\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}})_{t}},\quad
  \axdist{uu}{g}=\abs{(\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}}-\pos{\stencildof{uu}{g}})_{t}},
\end{equation}
$t=\faxis{g}$, $\pos{f}$ the face centroids and periodic displacements to
the nearest image; the tolerance rule of~\cref{eq:vof:limiter} sets
$\limratio{g}=1$ or $0$ for vanishing differences. The second upwind
unknown is chosen once, when the schedule is built: among the unknowns joined
to $\stencildof{u}{g}$ by another dual face of axis $t$ and lying beyond
$\stencildof{u}{g}$ along $\unitvec{t}$, it is the one nearest to the
reflection of $\stencildof{d}{g}$ through $\stencildof{u}{g}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:upstream}
  \stencildof{uu}{g}=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{f}\abs{\pos{f}-\roundpar{2\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}}-\pos{\stencildof{d}{g}}}}_2,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{sgn}\roundpar{(\pos{\stencildof{d}{g}}-\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}})_t}\,(\pos{f}-\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}})_t<-\tau,
\end{equation}
ties broken by the lower index, $\tau=64\machineeps\max(1,\domextent{t})$.
If no unknown qualifies (as beyond an unknown next to a wall of the
single-phase schedule, whose wall face is not an unknown), or if
$\abs{(\pos{\stencildof{d}{g}}-\pos{\stencildof{u}{g}})_t}\le\tau$
(two segments of one side), the state is upwind; so is a limited state at a
local extremum ($\limratio{g}\le0$). On a uniform mesh this is the classical
second upwind neighbour with $\axdist{d}{g}=\axdist{uu}{g}$; at a level
transition the spacing ratio keeps $\limratio{g}=1$ for a linear profile
(\cref{fig:momentum:limiter:stencil}), the non-uniform-grid correction of
Zeng~\cite{zeng2016}.

\emph{Scheme selection.} The keyword \code{scheme} (default
\apitype{LaxWendroff}) selects the state of the regular dual faces, the
keyword \code{amr_interface_scheme} that of the non-regular ones
$g\in\dualnonreg$ and the states at walls; \cref{sec:momentum:flux-rules}
collects the rules.
The default interface scheme,
\apifun{default_amr_interface_scheme}\code{(scheme)}, is the bulk scheme if
that is \apitype{Upwind} or one of the three-point schemes; for the bulk
schemes \apitype{Centred} and \apitype{LaxWendroff} it is
\apitype{MonotonizedCentral}. An upwind interface scheme with another bulk
scheme is rejected. On a uniform fully periodic mesh $\dualnonreg$ is empty
and the keyword has no effect. The reason
for the default is measurable: on a composite mesh with a unit value at
the most-coupled interface unknown and zeros elsewhere, the spatial operator
of Lax--Wendroff (and of Fromm) at the interface makes a zero unknown grow,
i.e.\ creates a new extremum for every positive step, whereas the four
limiters do not (test below).

\begin{deviation}{Directional face states}
The thesis interpolates to the centroid of an unsplit donating region of the
dual face, along a line through the downwind node with upwind values
$\tanginterp_1\avarstag$, $\tanginterp_2\avarstag$ from (bi)linear
interpolation, and limits with $\Psi(\iota)$ of a spacing-weighted slope

\section{Single-phase convection}\label{sec:momentum:single}
\emph{Spatial operator.} With the rates $\dualrate{g}$ of an advecting velocity
$\velo$, the staggered convection operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:operator}
  \stagvol{f}\,(\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag})_f
  =\sum_{g\in\dualfaceset{f}}\dorient{f}{g}\,\dualrate{g}\,(\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)_g
  +\sum_{b\in\dualbnd{f}}\dorient{f}{b}\,\dualrate{b}\,\avarstag_b,
\end{equation}
the thesis' $\advectionstag{\massflux}{\avarstag}=\divstag\roundpar{(\interpolantflux\massflux)(\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)}$~\thesis{eq.~(6.44)}
with a constant density. The boundary dual faces $\dualbnd{f}$ close the
control volumes next to a wall, whose boundary faces are not unknowns of the
single-phase schedule. They are built with the schedule of every mesh with
walls and enter the operator when a boundary condition is passed (the
Navier--Stokes step does). There are three kinds: (B1)~for the boundary face
$b\in\facesaxis{k}$ of the wall cell $c=\cbnd{b}$ and an unknown $f$ on the
opposite side of $c$; (B2)~for a boundary face $b\in\facesaxis{t}$, $t\neq k$,
of a cell $c\in\cellset{f}$; (B3)~for a face $h\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{t}$
of a cell $c\in\cellset{f}$ whose other cell $c'$ has, on the side of $f$ in
$c$, the boundary face $b\in\facesaxis{k}$: the partner that rule~(T) misses
where a level transition meets a wall normal to $\unitvec{k}$. Their rates,
orientations and states are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:boundary}
  \begin{gathered}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\text{(B1)}\quad \dualrate{b}=\half\meas{f}\roundpar{\volumeflux_b+\volumeflux_f},
    &&\dorient{f}{b}=\fside{b},\\
  &\text{(B2)}\quad \dualrate{b}=\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\meas{b}\,\volumeflux_b,
    &&\dorient{f}{b}=\fside{b},\\
  &\text{(B3)}\quad \dualrate{b}=\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\sidefrac{c'}{b}\meas{h}\,\volumeflux_h,
    &&\dorient{f}{b}=\orient{c}{h},
  \end{aligned}\\
  \avarstag_b=\begin{cases}
    \avarstag^{\physbnd}_b\ \text{if }\dorient{f}{b}\dualrate{b}<0,\ \text{else }\avarstag_f,
      & \text{(B1), (B3) with an \apitype{Upwind} interface scheme},\\
    \half\roundpar{\avarstag_f+\avarstag^{\physbnd}_b}, & \text{(B1), (B3) otherwise},\\
    \Velo^{\physbnd}(\pos{b},\timevar)\cdot\unitvec{k}, & \text{(B2) at \apitype{NoSlip} and \apitype{Inflow} boundaries},\\
    \avarstag_f, & \text{(B2) otherwise},\end{cases}
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with $\volumeflux=\aperture{}\velo$ as in~\cref{eq:momentum:dual-rate}
($\velo_b$ the normal velocity and $\aperture{b}$ the open aperture of the
boundary face), and $\avarstag^{\physbnd}_b$ the stored boundary value of the
transported component on the face $b\in\facesaxis{k}$, which for a velocity
component at an impermeable wall is its normal velocity, zero. At walls
$\velo_b=0$: (B2) carries no flux, (B1) carries $\half\meas{f}\volumeflux_f$
through the centre of the wall cell, and (B3) the share
$\half\sidefrac{c}{f}\meas{h}\volumeflux_h$ of a transverse flux
($\sidefrac{c'}{b}=1$). The kind (B3) and the apertures were added for
\issue{60} and \issue{61}; before, a constant velocity was not a steady state
at the unknowns concerned.

\emph{Closed identity.} The boundary dual faces supply exactly the terms that
\cref{eq:momentum:identity} lacks at a wall (\cref{sec:momentum:dual}): for
every unknown $f$ and $t=1,2$ the closed divergence satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:closed-identity}
  \begin{aligned}
  \stagvol{f}\,(\divstagclosed_t\dualrate{})_f
  &\defeq\stagvol{f}\,(\divstag_t\dualrate{})_f
    +\sum_{b\in\dualbnd{f},\ \faxis{b}=t}\dorient{f}{b}\,\dualrate{b}\\
  &=\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\frac{\sidefrac{c}{f}}{2}
    \sum_{f'\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{t}}\orient{c}{f'}\,\meas{f'}\volumeflux_{f'},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\faceset{c}$ including the boundary faces of $c$ and $\faxis{b}$ the
axis of the primal flux that a boundary dual face carries ($k$ for (B1), $t$
for (B2) and (B3)). At $\dt=0$ every state of a constant whose boundary
values are that constant is the constant (at walls (B2) carries no flux), so
$\advectionstag{\velo}{1}=\divstagclosed\dualrate{}$: for a discretely
solenoidal velocity, $\divopen\velo=0$ (\cref{eq:setting:open-divergence}),
a constant is a steady state at every unknown, also where a level transition
meets a wall and in cut cells next to a wall.

\emph{Time discretisation.} \apifun{advect!} advances with forward Euler. If
the scheme is \apitype{LaxWendroff} or a three-point scheme, or, on any
schedule but the uniform periodic one, the interface scheme is one of them, it
makes two directional sweeps along the axes $\sweepaxis{1}\neq\sweepaxis{2}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:split}
  \avarstag^{\splitlevel{j}}=\avarstag^{\splitlevel{j-1}}
    -\dt\,\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag^{\splitlevel{j-1}}}\big|_{\sweepaxis{j}},
  \quad j=1,2,\qquad \avarstag^{\splitlevel{0}}=\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}},\quad
  \avarstag^{\timelevel{n+1}}=\avarstag^{\splitlevel{2}},
\end{equation}
where $\advectionstag{\velo}{\cdot}|_{\sweepaxis{j}}$ keeps the dual faces
with $\faxis{g}=\sweepaxis{j}$ and the boundary dual faces with
$\faxis{b}=\sweepaxis{j}$, and the Lax--Wendroff term uses
$\divstagclosed_{\sweepaxis{j}}$ (without a boundary condition
$\divstag_{\sweepaxis{j}}$); the order $(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$ alternates between
successive calls. \apitype{Upwind} and \apitype{Centred} (with an upwind or
centred interface scheme) make one unsplit step
$\avarstag^{\timelevel{n+1}}=\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}-\dt\,\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}}$.
For a \apitype{FaceVelocity} both components are evaluated from the same
input in each sweep and published together, a coordinate split and not a
split between components. The advecting velocity is fixed during the step
unless the field advects itself (\code{advect!(velocity, dt, workspace)}),
in which case each sweep uses the published intermediate. The single-phase
Navier--Stokes step transports $\velo^{\timelevel{n}}$ with itself frozen as
carrier, after enforcing its boundary values at $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$,
and passes its boundary condition and $\timevar^{\timelevel{n}}$ to the
boundary dual faces. \apifun{convection!} returns
$(\avarstag-\avarstag^{\timelevel{n+1}})/\dt$ for a split scheme without
advancing the order, and $\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag}$ with all dual
faces and $\mathcfl_g=0$ for $\dt=0$.

\emph{Checks} (\code{boundary_identity_check}). On a composite mesh with
walls, a composite mesh whose refined root touches a wall (with

zero on the boundary, and for a discretely solenoidal velocity
$\abs{\advectionstag{\velo}{1}}\le\chkMomWallsIdentity$ relative to the largest
rate of~\cref{eq:momentum:cfl} at every unknown.

\section{Conservation and kinetic energy}\label{sec:momentum:conservation}
\emph{Momentum.} Every dual face enters its two control volumes with opposite
orientation, so summing~\cref{eq:momentum:split} over the unknowns,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:momentum-balance}
  \sum_{f}\stagvol{f}\avarstag^{\splitlevel{j}}_f
  =\sum_{f}\stagvol{f}\avarstag^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f
  -\dt\sum_{f}\sum_{b\in\dualbnd{f},\ \faxis{b}=\sweepaxis{j}}\dorient{f}{b}\,\dualrate{b}\,\avarstag_b,
\end{equation}
for every scheme, interface scheme and mesh: on a periodic mesh the momentum
$\sum_f\stagvol{f}\avarstag_f$ of each component is conserved up to rounding,
also at level transitions and in cut cells (the unsplit step likewise); with
walls it changes by the fluxes through the boundary dual faces.
\code{integrate(field, workspace)} returns it.

\emph{Constants.} At $\dt=0$ a constant $\avarstag$ gives
$\advectionstag{\velo}{1}=\divstagclosed\dualrate{}$, which
by~\cref{eq:momentum:closed-identity} vanishes at every unknown for a velocity
that is discretely solenoidal, $\divopen\velo=0$
(\cref{eq:setting:open-divergence}), on periodic meshes, at walls and in cut
cells. The Lax--Wendroff state of a constant is the constant plus the
compressive term, which cancels over the two sweeps only for a solenoidal
velocity.

\emph{Kinetic energy.} For the all-centred operator, with \apitype{Centred}
as both \code{scheme} and \code{amr_interface_scheme}, the state is the mean
of the two unknowns and~\cref{eq:momentum:operator} gives, on a periodic mesh,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:skew}
  \ipstag{\psi}{\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag}}+\ipstag{\avarstag}{\advectionstag{\velo}{\psi}}
  =\sum_f\stagvol{f}\,\avarstag_f\psi_f\,(\divstag\dualrate{})_f,
  \qquad 2\,\frac{\partial(\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag})_f}{\partial\avarstag_f}=(\divstag\dualrate{})_f :
\end{equation}
the operator is skew-adjoint in $\ipstag{\cdot}{\cdot}$ up to half its dual
divergence on the diagonal. The arithmetic mean is the only two-point state
with this property: $\tailweight\avarstag_{\dminus{g}}+(1-\tailweight)\avarstag_{\dplus{g}}$
adds $(1-2\tailweight)\dualrate{g}$ to the off-diagonal symmetric part of
$\stagvol{}\advectionstag{}{}$ for the pair $\dminus{g},\dplus{g}$. With walls
and vanishing boundary values $\avarstag^{\physbnd}_b$ the same identities hold
with the closed divergence $\divstagclosed\dualrate{}$
of~\cref{eq:momentum:closed-identity} in place of $\divstag\dualrate{}$: the
boundary dual faces (B1) and (B3) have the state $\half\avarstag_f$ and add
$\half\dorient{f}{b}\dualrate{b}$ to the diagonal, which the interior dual faces
miss, and (B2) carries no flux at a wall (for non-vanishing boundary values
the left side gains
$\half\sum_f\sum_{b}\dorient{f}{b}\,\dualrate{b}\roundpar{\avarstag^{\physbnd}_b\psi_f+\psi^{\physbnd}_b\avarstag_f}$
over the boundary dual faces of kinds (B1) and (B3)). The energy of the
semi-discrete system $\dd\avarstag/\dd\timevar=-\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag}$
therefore changes at the rate
$-\tfrac12\sum_f\stagvol{f}\avarstag_f^2\,(\divstagclosed\dualrate{})_f$, and for
a discretely solenoidal velocity it conserves
$\tfrac12\ipstag{\avarstag}{\avarstag}$ on uniform, composite and cut meshes,
with or without walls; this is the conservation of quadratic invariants of
the thesis~\thesis{App.~6.C, eqs.~(6.78)--(6.81) and footnote~2}, where the
Lax--Wendroff state reduces to the centred one as $\dt\to0$, and the
symmetry-preserving property of Verstappen and Veldman~\cite{verstappen2003}.
The default \code{Centred()} uses \code{MonotonizedCentral} on non-regular
dual faces and is not skew on a composite mesh. The fully discrete
forward-Euler step with the centred operator adds energy,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:euler-energy}
  \tfrac12\ipstag{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n+1}}}{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n+1}}}
  -\tfrac12\ipstag{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}}{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}}
  =\tfrac12\dt^2\,\ipstag{\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}}}{\advectionstag{\velo}{\avarstag^{\timelevel{n}}}}\ \ge0
  \quad(\divstagclosed\dualrate{}=0),
\end{equation}
so centred transport is not energy-stable in time; no energy statement is
claimed for the split Lax--Wendroff or limited updates, which dissipate
energy in the checks below.

\emph{Checks} (\code{convection_conservation_check}). On the uniform, composite
and disc fixtures with velocities from a streamfunction (on the disc fixture
one that vanishes on solid vertices, so $\divopen\velo=0$): one step of
\code{Upwind}, \code{LaxWendroff}, \code{MonotonizedCentral} and
\code{Superbee} changes the momentum by at most $\chkMomMomentum$ (relative);
the all-centred operator assembled from \apifun{convection!} at $\dt=0$
satisfies~\cref{eq:momentum:skew} with $\max\abs{\stagvol{}\advectionstag{}{}+(\stagvol{}\advectionstag{}{})^{\transpose}}\le\chkMomSkew$
relative to its largest entry and twice its diagonal equals the dual
divergence to $\chkMomDiagonal$; one centred forward-Euler step
satisfies~\cref{eq:momentum:euler-energy} to $\chkMomEuler$ of the growth term
(the cut fixture, whose small step makes the growth tiny); one default
Lax--Wendroff step decreases the energy by $\chkMomLwEnergyUniform$ (uniform)
and $\chkMomLwEnergyAmr$ (composite) of its value. With walls
(\code{boundary_identity_check}, the three bounded meshes of
\cref{sec:momentum:single}) the all-centred operator with zero boundary values
satisfies the closed form of~\cref{eq:momentum:skew},
$\max\abs{\stagvol{}\advectionstag{}{}+(\stagvol{}\advectionstag{}{})^{\transpose}-\operatorname{diag}(\stagvol{}\divstagclosed\dualrate{})}\le\chkMomWallsSkew$
relative to its largest entry, for a velocity that is not solenoidal.

\section{Time-step restriction}\label{sec:momentum:cfl}
For the single-phase convection the code bounds the sum of the absolute rates
of every control volume,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:cfl}
  \dtstag=\stagcfl\Big/\max_{k=1,2}\ \max_{f}\ \frac{1}{\stagvol{f}}
    \Big(\sum_{g\in\dualfaceset{f}}\abs{\dualrate{g}}+\sum_{b\in\dualbnd{f}}\abs{\dualrate{b}}\Big),
  \qquad 0<\stagcfl\le1,
\end{equation}
over the dual faces of both axes and the boundary dual
faces~\cref{eq:momentum:boundary}, with $\dtstag=\infty$ for a vanishing
velocity.
\apifun{stable_timestep}\code{(velocity, workspace; cfl=0.9)}
returns it; \apifun{step!} requires $\dt\le(1+10^{-6}+32\machineeps)\dtstag$ (\cref{eq:stab:check}, \issue{98}) with
$\stagcfl=1$, the top of the range (one phase; two phases bound the step as
in~\cref{sec:vof:cfl}), and \apifun{simulate!} chooses steps with
$\stagcfl=0.45$. The check at $\stagcfl=1$ accepts every step that
\apifun{stable_timestep} returns for \code{cfl} in $(0,1]$ (\issue{121}): the
sum in~\cref{eq:momentum:cfl} bounds $\dt\abs{\dualrate{g}}$ by
$\stagcfl\min\curlypar{\stagvol{\dminus{g}},\stagvol{\dplus{g}}}$ for every
dual face $g$, because $g$ belongs to both adjacent control volumes and each
term of the sum is bounded by the sum. Hence $\mathcfl_g\le\stagcfl\le1$, and
the Lax--Wendroff face state of~\cref{eq:momentum:states} is a convex
combination of its two upwind neighbours.
Small open volumes reduce the bound, as no cells are merged. Under the bound
the unsplit upwind update (\apitype{Upwind} as both schemes) of a discretely
solenoidal velocity is a convex combination of old and boundary values: by
\cref{eq:momentum:closed-identity} the outflow of a control volume through its
dual and boundary dual faces equals its inflow, so the coefficient of the old
value, $1-\dt\sum\abs{\dualrate{}}/\stagvol{f}$ over the outflow faces, is at
least $1-\half\stagcfl$, and the inflow terms carry upwind neighbours or,
through (B1) and (B3), boundary values; $\stagcfl\le2$ would suffice for
this. The smallest coefficient at $\stagcfl=1$ was $\half$ on walled
composite meshes (derivation audit, \code{proof_review/sec-momentum-cfl/}).
No stability or TVD statement is claimed for the split second-order updates
on composite meshes: the classical limiter regions of
\cref{fig:momentum:limiter:sweby} are one-dimensional.

The two-phase transport of \cref{sec:momentum:one-velocity,sec:momentum:two-velocity}
has no bound of its own: the step is bounded by the volume-of-fluid bound,
capillary and gravity limits of \cref{sec:vof:cfl}. On a uniform mesh
without solid this bounds the dual Courant numbers: a dual face through a
cell centre carries half of the volume through each of two faces of that
cell, each at most $\wycflval\meas{c}$ (the volume through a face is at most
the volume entering or leaving the cell), and a transverse dual face half of a
face volume of each of two cells, so $\mathcfl_g\le\wycflval\le\half$
with $\stagvol{f}=\meas{c}$; a uniform translation attains $\wycflval$. The same
bound holds on every $2{:}1$-balanced mesh without solid, periodic or with
walls: a hanging fine control volume is smaller, but the volumes through the
faces of its small cell are capped by its own volume (checked as exact linear
programs over every local configuration of random meshes, not proved here).
With a solid it can fail, because the open volumes are small: $\mathcfl_g$
reached $3.3\,\wycflval$ in random cut meshes (\code{proof_review/sec-momentum-cfl/}).
The regular dual faces next to a solid use the selected scheme
(\cref{sec:momentum:flux-rules}), so a Lax--Wendroff or limited state may
there see a Courant number above one; no bound is claimed for it (\issue{72}).

\section{Two phases, one velocity}\label{sec:momentum:one-velocity}
The one-velocity model transports the momentum $\stagvol{f}\stagdens_f\velo_f$
of every staggered control volume inside the sweeps of
\cref{sec:vof:sweeps}, with the phase volumes of the fraction update
interpolated to the dual faces~\cref{eq:momentum:dual-rate}, so that the
staggered mass follows the cell mass algebraically~\thesis{\S6.4.1}. Its
schedule is the phase schedule of \cref{sec:momentum:dual} except that the
rules~(N) and~(T) take, for a segment of a hanging side of a cut cell, the
open-strip fraction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:open-fractions}
  \sidefracopen{c}{f}=\frac{\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}}{\meas{\opencell{c}}}
  \qquad(0<\openfrac{c}<1,\ \sidefrac{c}{f}<1)
\end{equation}
in place of $\sidefrac{c}{f}$, and $\sidefracopen{c}{f}=\sidefrac{c}{f}$
otherwise, which equals the quotient in an uncut cell and for a single face.
The strips of the segments of a side partition the cell, so these fractions
still sum to one per side, \cref{eq:momentum:identity} holds with them, and
$\half\sidefracopen{c}{f}\meas{\opencell{c}}=\half\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}$
is the weight of the interpolation~\cref{eq:momentum:interpolation} in every
open cell. The staggered density is the mixture
density of the interpolated fractions~\cref{eq:momentum:interpolation},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:density}
  \stagdens_f=\dens^{\liq}\stagfrac{\liq}{f}+\dens^{\gas}\stagfrac{\gas}{f},
  \qquad \stagfrac{\gas}{f}=1-\stagfrac{\liq}{f}.
\end{equation}
In sweep $j$ along $\sweepaxis{j}$ the carrier dilation per open volume of a
cell, the quantity frozen in~\cref{eq:vof:sweep}, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:sweep-dilation}
  \sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{c}=\frac{1}{\meas{\opencell{c}}}
    \sum_{f\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{\sweepaxis{j}}}\orient{c}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\open}_f
  \quad(\openfrac{c}>0),
\end{equation}
and zero in covered cells, and the staggered fractions predicted for the end
of the sweep interpolate the clamped fractions before snapping
($\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$ of~\cref{eq:vof:snap}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:predicted}
  \stagpred{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{f}=\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\operatorname{clamp}(\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{},0,1)}_f,
  \qquad \stagpred{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}{f}=1-\stagpred{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{f},
  \qquad \stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}_f=\textstyle\sum_\phase\dens^{\phase}\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{f}.
\end{equation}
Each component is updated in fluctuation form, as stored by the code:
\begin{multline}\label{eq:momentum:one-velocity}
  \velo^{\splitlevel{j}}_f=\velo^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f+\frac{1}{\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}_f}\Bigg(
    \frac{1}{\stagvol{f}}\sum_{\phase}\dens^{\phase}\sum_{g\in\dualfaceset{f},\ \faxis{g}=\sweepaxis{j}}\dorient{f}{g}\,
      \dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}\roundpar{\velo^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f-(\fluxinterpstag\velo^{\splitlevel{j-1}})^{\phase}_g}\\
    +\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\Big[\textstyle\sum_\phase\dens^{\phase}\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}\Big]}_f
      \roundpar{\velo^{\timelevel{n}}_f-\velo^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f}\Bigg),
\end{multline}
with the frozen indicator~\cref{eq:vof:indicator} and the start values
$\velo^{\timelevel{n}}$ in the dilation term, the analogue
of~\cref{eq:vof:fluctuation}; the face state of phase $\phase$ is that of the
modified flux interpolant below. A constant $\velo$ gives a zero increment
whatever the fractions, so constants are preserved exactly. Multiplying
by $\stagvol{f}\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}_f$ shows the conservative form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:conservative}
  \stagvol{f}\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}_f\velo^{\splitlevel{j}}_f
  =\stagvol{f}\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f\velo^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f
  -\sum_{\phase}\dens^{\phase}\sum_{g}\dorient{f}{g}\,\dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}\,(\fluxinterpstag\velo)^{\phase}_g
  +\stagvol{f}\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\Big[\textstyle\sum_\phase\dens^{\phase}\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}\Big]}_f\velo^{\timelevel{n}}_f
\end{equation}
provided the staggered mass obeys the interpolated cell mass update,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:mass-identity}
  \stagvol{f}\roundpar{\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}_f-\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j-1}}_f}
  =-\sum_{\phase}\dens^{\phase}\sum_{g}\dorient{f}{g}\,\dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}
  +\stagvol{f}\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\Big[\textstyle\sum_\phase\dens^{\phase}\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}\Big]}_f,
\end{equation}
the thesis' mass--momentum consistency $\advectionstag{\massflux}{1}=\interpolantsbp(\advection{\massflux}{1})$~\thesis{eq.~(6.45)}
with the dilation of the split update, where $\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j-1}}$ is
the density of the fractions stored at the start of the sweep (snapped,
\cref{eq:vof:snap}) and $\stagdens^{\splitlevel{j}}$ that of the predicted
fractions~\cref{eq:momentum:predicted}. By~\cref{eq:momentum:identity} for the
phase volumes and the fraction update~\cref{eq:vof:sweep}, for a schedule
built with the side fractions $\sidefrac{c}{f}$ the left side
of~\cref{eq:momentum:mass-identity} minus its right side is
\begin{multline}\label{eq:momentum:mass-defect}
  \half\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\roundpar{\sidefrac{c}{f}\meas{\opencell{c}}-\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}}\,\massdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{c}\\
  +\half\sum_{c\in\cellset{f}}\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}\,(\dens^{\liq}-\dens^{\gas})\,
    \roundpar{\operatorname{clamp}(\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c},0,1)-\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}},
\end{multline}
with $\massdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{c}=\meas{\opencell{c}}^{-1}\sum_\phase\dens^{\phase}\sum_{f'\in\faceset{c}\cap\facesaxis{\sweepaxis{j}}}\orient{c}{f'}\,\dt\meas{f'}\volumeflux^{\phase}_{f'}$
the net phase-mass outflow of $c$ in the sweep (the dilation terms cancel).
The first sum vanishes when (b)~every cell $c\in\cellset{f}$ is uncut or has
$f$ as the single face of its side, so that
$\meas{\stripset{c}{f}\cap\opencell{c}}=\sidefrac{c}{f}\meas{\opencell{c}}$. The
one-velocity schedule has $\sidefracopen{c}{f}$ in place of $\sidefrac{c}{f}$
in~\cref{eq:momentum:mass-defect}, so its first sum vanishes in every cell,
and~\cref{eq:momentum:mass-identity} holds exactly, up to rounding, when
(a)~no fraction is clamped in the sweep. The code stops if the liquid volume
$\meas{\opencell{c}}\preclamp{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{c}$ of an open cell leaves
$[0,\meas{\opencell{c}}]$ by more than $128\machineeps\meas{c}$ (since
\issue{55}), so the clamp changes the liquid volume by at most
$128\machineeps\meas{c}$ and the second sum is rounding in units of the cell
volume. Before \issue{63} the one-velocity schedule used the side fractions,
and where (b) failed, in a cut coarse cell whose side carries two segments,
the staggered mass and momentum drifted from the cell mass by the first sum;
the schedule of \cref{sec:momentum:two-velocity} still uses them. A snap of a
fraction after the sweep~\cref{eq:vof:snap} changes $\stagdens$ but not
$\velo$, so it changes the momentum by $\stagvol{f}\,\delta\stagdens_f\velo_f$,
as for the phase scalars of \cref{sec:vof:scalars}.
Summing~\cref{eq:momentum:conservative} over the unknowns and the two
sweeps, the momentum $\sum_f\stagvol{f}\stagdens_f\velo_f$ of each component
changes by
$\sum_j\sum_f\stagvol{f}(\interpolantsbp[\sum_\phase\dens^\phase\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}])_f\velo^{\timelevel{n}}_f$,
which vanishes for a carrier that is solenoidal in every cell, since the
frozen values make the two dilations of a cell cancel; it is therefore
conserved up to rounding under (a), without snapping, and with periodic or
impermeable boundaries. This is the thesis' exact conservation of the sum of
the phase momenta~\thesis{eq.~(6.53)}: the update divides the sum of the
phase momenta by the sum of the phase masses.

\emph{Modified flux interpolant.} The state of phase $\phase$ on the dual
face $g$ is the bulk state~\cref{eq:momentum:states} of the selected scheme,
without the compressive term and with $\mathcfl_g=\abs{\dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}}/\min\stagvol{}$,
unless an adjacent predicted fraction is small, in which case the flux is
upwinded term by term:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:ctu}
  \dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}(\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)^{\phase}_g=\begin{cases}
    \displaystyle\sum_{f}\dweight{g}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\phase}_f\;
      \avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g,f}}, &
      \min\roundpar{\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{\dminus{g}},\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{\dplus{g}}}<\minfracval,\\
    \dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}\,(\fluxinterpstag\avarstag)_g, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\stencildof{u}{g,f}=\dminus{g}$ if the partial volume
$\dweight{g}{f}\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\phase}_f$ is non-negative and
$\dplus{g}$ otherwise, and $\minfracval$ is \code{ctu_threshold} (default
$\half$, in $(0,1]$). Keeping the partial interpolated volumes separate
upwinds each of them with the value of its own upwind unknown; with
$\ctupos{g}$ and $\ctuneg{g}$ the sums of the positive partial volumes and of
the magnitudes of the negative ones,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:ctu-exchange}
  \sum_{f}\dweight{g}{f}\,\dt\meas{f}\volumeflux^{\phase}_f\;\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g,f}}
  =\dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}\avarstag_{\stencildof{u}{g}}
    +\min(\ctupos{g},\ctuneg{g})\roundpar{\avarstag_{\dminus{g}}-\avarstag_{\dplus{g}}},
\end{equation}
the upwind flux of the net volume plus a counter-flow exchange between the
two unknowns, which is diffusive and vanishes unless partial volumes of both
signs meet at the dual face. This is the corner-transport-upwind (CTU) flux
of the thesis~\thesis{eqs.~(6.49)--(6.51)} restricted to the two unknowns of
the dual face (the thesis attributes partial volumes to their donor cells
over $2\times3^{d-1}$ faces): the own flux of an unknown enters its control
volume at one dual face with the value of the upwind neighbour and leaves at
another with its own value, so no fluid bypasses the control volume. On a
conforming patch the volume of a phase leaving a control volume is half the
volume leaving each of its two cells, hence at most the volume of that phase
in it, and the coefficient of the old value in the
update stays non-negative: the update of a draining control volume is
bounded by the cell-wise outflow bound, as in the thesis
lemmas~\thesis{Lemmas~6.2 and 6.3}, and not by the switch. At a segment of a
hanging side this fails (\cref{dev:momentum:ctu-hanging}).

\emph{Boundaries and solids.} In a bounded domain and with a solid the phase
schedule~\cref{eq:momentum:volumes} includes the boundary unknowns (whose
primal volumes are zero, \cref{sec:vof:cfl}), so the identity holds there
without boundary dual faces; the transported boundary values are overwritten
when the Navier--Stokes step enforces its boundary condition. The dual faces
of this schedule are classified by~\cref{eq:momentum:regular} with the uncut
weights and volumes, and its three-point states find their upstream unknowns
among all unknowns, the boundary unknowns included: a regular dual face, also
one between cut cells, uses the selected scheme, a non-regular one or one
incident to a boundary unknown the interface scheme
(\cref{sec:momentum:flux-rules}). Before \issue{62} every dual face of this
schedule was non-regular and had no upstream unknown, so that the transport
of a bounded domain or of a mesh with a solid was first-order upwind away
from the interface.

\begin{deviation}{Modified flux interpolant in directional sweeps}
The thesis switches to the CTU interpolant when the staggered fraction at
the end of the unsplit step falls below $\minfracval=\half$, with partial
volume fluxes of the unsplit donating regions over the $2\times3^{d-1}$
neighbouring faces~\thesis{eqs.~(6.49)--(6.51)}. The code applies the switch
per directional sweep with the predicted fractions~\cref{eq:momentum:predicted}
and upwinds the partial interpolated volumes of the
weights~\cref{eq:momentum:weights} between the two unknowns of the dual face
only, a restriction of the thesis' CTU flux~\cref{eq:momentum:ctu-exchange};
the bulk state is the directional one of \cref{sec:momentum:states},
and a dilation term with frozen values completes the split update.
\end{deviation}

\begin{deviation}{CTU flux at hanging segments}\label{dev:momentum:ctu-hanging}
The thesis' lemmas bound the CTU update of a draining staggered control
volume~\thesis{Lemmas~6.2 and 6.3}. At a segment $f$ of a hanging side they
do not carry over to the code's form. Let the own flux of $f$, from
$\cminus{f}$ into $\cplus{f}$, be the only non-zero flux, all of the phase of
$\cminus{f}$ lie in its donating region and $\cplus{f}$ be empty: rule~(N)
passes $\half(2-\sidefrac{\cplus{f}}{f})$ of this flux out of the control
volume of $f$, which holds only $\half\sidefrac{\cminus{f}}{f}$ of it, and the
coefficient of the old value in the update of $f$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:own-coefficient}
  \owncoef{f}=-\frac{2-\sidefrac{\cminus{f}}{f}-\sidefrac{\cplus{f}}{f}}{\sidefrac{\cplus{f}}{f}},
\end{equation}
zero on a conforming patch but $-\half$ from a coarse into a fine cell and
$-1$ from a fine into a coarse cell. The update then leaves the range of the
old values: in one step a staggered phase scalar $0$ among neighbours $1$
became $1.98$, and a one-velocity momentum $1.95$ at the density ratio $1000$
(derivation audit, \code{proof_review/eq-momentum-ctu/}); upwinding the net
volume does not avoid this. Open: \issue{70}.
\end{deviation}

\section{Two velocities and staggered phase scalars}\label{sec:momentum:two-velocity}
The two-velocity model stores intrinsic phase velocities, and a
\apitype{PhaseFaceScalar} stores intrinsic liquid and gas values on the
faces of one axis; both are transported by the same update, per phase, with
the phase volumes of the carrier of \cref{sec:vof:two-velocity}, on the phase
schedule of \cref{sec:momentum:dual} with the side fractions $\sidefrac{c}{f}$
(not those of~\cref{eq:momentum:open-fractions}). Before the
sweeps, a value is \emph{absent} where its phase has no open staggered
volume ($\stagvol{f}$ of~\cref{eq:momentum:volumes} zero, or
$\operatorname{clamp}(\stagfrac{\liq}{f},0,1)=0$ for the liquid, $=1$ for
the gas); absent values are extended in two passes over the dual faces, each
taking the mean of the present values of the unknowns joined to it, and set
to zero beyond,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:extension}
  \extstag{\phase}_f=\begin{cases}
    \avarstag^{\phase}_f, & \text{present},\\
    \frac{1}{\#N(f)}\sum_{f'\in N(f)}\extstag{\phase}_{f'}, & N(f)\neq\emptyset\text{ in one of the two passes},\\
    0, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
$N(f)$ the unknowns with a value that share a dual face with $f$ at the start
of the pass. These working values are also the frozen start values
$\avarstag^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}$ of the dilation term. In sweep $j$, with the
predicted fraction $\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{f}$
of~\cref{eq:momentum:predicted},
\begin{multline}\label{eq:momentum:two-velocity}
  \avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}_f=\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_f
  +\frac{1}{\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{f}}\Bigg(
    \frac{1}{\stagvol{f}}\sum_{g\in\dualfaceset{f},\ \faxis{g}=\sweepaxis{j}}\dorient{f}{g}\,
      \dt\dualrate{g}^{\phase}\roundpar{\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_f-(\fluxinterpstag\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}})_g}\\
    +\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\big[\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}\big]}_f
      \roundpar{\avarstag^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}_f-\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_f}\Bigg)
  \quad\text{if }\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{f}>\ffloor,
\end{multline}
and $\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}_f=\avarstag^{\phase,\splitlevel{j-1}}_f$
otherwise, with the modified interpolant~\cref{eq:momentum:ctu}. There is no
density: for constant $\dens^{\phase}$ it cancels, and the conserved amount
of phase $\phase$ is $\dens^{\phase}\sum_f\stagvol{f}\stagfrac{\phase}{f}\avarstag^{\phase}_f$
(\apifun{integrate}\code{(q, phase)} for a phase scalar). The phase volumes
of~\cref{eq:vof:frozen-flux}
partition the carrier, $\stagpred{\gas,\splitlevel{j}}{}=1-\stagpred{\liq,\splitlevel{j}}{}$,
and both phases follow~\cref{eq:momentum:two-velocity} with their indicator.
After the step the velocities of phases without open staggered volume in the
final state are stored as \code{NaN}~\cref{eq:momentum:extension}. As
for~\cref{eq:momentum:conservative}, the update conserves the phase amount up
to the frozen dilation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:two-velocity-balance}
  \sum_f\stagvol{f}\stagfrac{\phase}{f}\avarstag^{\phase}_f\Big|^{\timelevel{n+1}}_{\timelevel{n}}
  =\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_f\stagvol{f}\roundpar{\interpolantsbp\big[\indicator{\phase}\sweepdiv{\splitlevel{j}}{}\big]}_f\avarstag^{\phase,\timelevel{n}}_f
\end{equation}
under condition (a) of \cref{sec:momentum:one-velocity} and, because this
schedule keeps the side fractions, condition (b) (\issue{71}), without snapping and with
every updated unknown above the floor; the dilation sum vanishes for a
carrier that is solenoidal in every cell (the mixture constraint on the
frozen apertures).

\emph{Checks} (\code{two_velocity_momentum_check}). An independent replica
of~\cref{eq:momentum:extension,eq:momentum:two-velocity,eq:momentum:ctu}
reproduces both phase velocities after \apifun{advect!} to $\chkMomTwoReplica$
(relative) on the uniform, composite and walled fixtures
and the \code{NaN} pattern exactly; the balance~\cref{eq:momentum:two-velocity-balance}
holds to $\chkMomTwoBalance$ (relative), where the second streamfunction
used as gas velocity makes the frozen carrier non-solenoidal in mixed cells
and the dilation sum non-zero.

\begin{deviation}{Two-velocity momentum}
The thesis transports the liquid momentum with the liquid mass fluxes of the
projected-liquid method and the gas momentum with the gas mass fluxes of its
auxiliary carrier, divided by the auxiliary gas mass and reset to the
complement after every time step~\thesis{eqs.~(6.54)--(6.55)}. The code uses
the frozen carrier (\cref{sec:vof:frozen}) instead, with which both phase
amounts are balanced by~\cref{eq:momentum:two-velocity-balance}; the
projected carrier of the thesis is not implemented.
\end{deviation}

\section{Which flux on which dual face}\label{sec:momentum:flux-rules}
The face state of a dual face depends on the transport, on the kind of the
dual face and, in two phases, on the staggered fractions next to it.
\Cref{tab:momentum:flux-rules} collects the rules and \cref{fig:momentum:rules}
shows them on a walled composite mesh; in detail:
\begin{sloppypar}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Regular dual faces}, $g\notin\dualnonreg$, take the state of
  \code{scheme}~\cref{eq:momentum:states}, by default \apitype{LaxWendroff}, in
  the single-phase convection
  (\srcsym{src/operators/advection/staggered_fluxes.jl}{_convection_schedule!})
  and in the two-phase transport
  (\srcsym{src/two_phase/phase_face_transport.jl}{_phase_connection_scheme});
  every dual face of the uniform periodic schedule counts as regular
  (\srcsym{src/operators/advection/staggered_schedules.jl}{_dual_state}).
  The classification~\cref{eq:momentum:regular} is made once, with the uncut
  weights and volumes
  (\srcsym{src/operators/advection/staggered_schedules.jl}{_assemble_amr_dual_schedule}),
  and the schedules with a solid keep it
  (\srcsym{src/embedded/operators.jl}{_cut_cell_dual_schedule},
  \srcsym{src/embedded/operators.jl}{_geometry_phase_schedule}): a dual face
  between cut cells is regular when its uncut counterpart is, also in the
  phase schedules.
\item \emph{Non-regular dual faces}, $g\in\dualnonreg$, take the state of
  \code{amr_interface_scheme}: the dual faces at level transitions and, in
  the phase schedule of a bounded domain or of a mesh with a solid, those
  incident to a boundary unknown
  (\srcsym{src/two_phase/phase_face_transport.jl}{_bounded_phase_schedule}).
  The default,
  \apifun{default_amr_interface_scheme}, is \apitype{Upwind} for
  \apitype{Upwind}, \apitype{MonotonizedCentral} for \apitype{Centred} and
  \apitype{LaxWendroff}, and the scheme itself for \apitype{Fromm} and the
  four limited schemes; an \apitype{Upwind} interface scheme with another
  bulk scheme is rejected
  (\srcsym{src/operators/advection/staggered_schemes.jl}{_validate_amr_interface_scheme}).
\item \emph{Three-point states without upstream unknown} are upwind: no
  unknown qualifies in~\cref{eq:momentum:upstream}, as beyond an unknown next
  to a wall normal to $\unitvec{k}$ in the single-phase schedule, which has
  no unknown on the wall, or the two unknowns lie on one line normal to
  $\unitvec{\faxis{g}}$, as two segments of one side
  (\srcsym{src/operators/advection/staggered_schedules.jl}{_three_point_stencils},
  \srcsym{src/operators/advection/staggered_fluxes.jl}{_amr_dual_state},
  \srcsym{src/two_phase/phase_face_transport.jl}{_phase_bulk_state}). The
  phase schedules search the upstream unknown among all unknowns, the
  boundary unknowns included.
\item \emph{Boundary dual faces}~\cref{eq:momentum:boundary} exist in the
  single-phase convection only and enter when a boundary condition is passed;
  their rates carry the open apertures of their primal faces
  (\srcsym{src/operators/advection/staggered_fluxes.jl}{_boundary_dual_flux}).
  (B1) and (B3) take the upwind state with the stored
  boundary value on inflow if \code{amr_interface_scheme} is \apitype{Upwind},
  and the centred state $\half(\avarstag_f+\avarstag^{\physbnd}_b)$ otherwise;
  (B2) takes the prescribed velocity at \apitype{NoSlip} and \apitype{Inflow}
  boundaries and $\avarstag_f$ otherwise
  (\srcsym{src/operators/advection/staggered_fluxes.jl}{_add_boundary_convection!}).
  (B3) joins a fine unknown to the wall face of a coarse cell where a level
  transition meets a wall normal to $\unitvec{k}$
  (\srcsym{src/operators/advection/staggered_schedules.jl}{_boundary_dual_faces}).
\item \emph{In the interface band} of the two-phase transport (the
  one-velocity momentum, the phase velocities of the two-velocity model and
  the staggered phase scalars) a phase takes, before all of the above, the
  CTU flux~\cref{eq:momentum:ctu} on a dual face where its predicted
  staggered fraction~\cref{eq:momentum:predicted}, the volume-weighted
  average over the staggered control volume of the cell fractions predicted
  for the end of the sweep, is below the threshold at one of the two
  unknowns, $\min(\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{\dminus{g}},\stagpred{\phase,\splitlevel{j}}{\dplus{g}})<\minfracval$
  (\srcsym{src/two_phase/phase_face_transport.jl}{_phase_transport}). The
  threshold is the keyword \code{ctu_threshold} of
  \apitype{WYAdvectionWorkspace}, default $\half$, in $(0,1]$
  (\srcsym{src/vof/workspace.jl}{WYAdvectionWorkspace}). The two phases are
  switched separately; a phase without volume at a dual face carries no flux
  there.
\end{enumerate}
\end{sloppypar}

\section{Cell-centred scalar transport}\label{sec:momentum:cell-scalar}
A passive \apitype{CellField} $\avar$ is transported by the conservative law
$\partial_\timevar\avar+\divergence(\avar\Velo)=0$ with the explicit upwind
finite-volume update
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:cell-scalar}
  \begin{gathered}
  \meas{\opencell{c}}\avar^{\timelevel{n+1}}_c=\meas{\opencell{c}}\avar^{\timelevel{n}}_c
  -\dt\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\orient{c}{f}\,\meas{f}\aperture{f}\velo_f\,\avar^{\mathrm{u}}_f,\\
  \avar^{\mathrm{u}}_f=\begin{cases}
    \avar_{\cminus{f}}\text{ if }\velo_f\ge0,\ \avar_{\cplus{f}}\text{ otherwise}, & f\in\facesint,\\
    \avar_{\cbnd{f}}\text{ if }\fside{f}\velo_f\ge0,\ \avar^{\physbnd}_f\text{ otherwise}, & f\in\facesbnd,
  \end{cases}
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where the inflow value $\avar^{\physbnd}_f$ is the cell value
(\code{ZeroGradient}) or the prescribed function at $(\pos{f},\timevar)$
(\code{Dirichlet}); without solid $\aperture{f}=1$ and
$\meas{\opencell{c}}=\meas{c}$, and covered cells are not updated. Every owned
segment is evaluated once, so the update is conservative on the composite
mesh (\cref{sec:setting:sample}); a constant is preserved where
$\divopen\velo=0$. The bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum:cell-cfl}
  \dtcell=\stagcfl\Big/\max_{c,\ \meas{\opencell{c}}>0}\ \frac{1}{\meas{\opencell{c}}}\sum_{f\in\faceset{c}}\meas{f}\aperture{f}\abs{\velo_f},
  \qquad 0<\stagcfl\le1\ (\text{default }0.9),
\end{equation}
sums over all faces of the cell, boundary faces included. With a solid it
uses the open apertures and volumes when the workspace is built with the
\apitype{GeometryField} or the geometry is passed to
\apifun{stable_timestep} (since \issue{64}); covered cells do not restrict
it. Under the bound the coefficient of the old value in~\cref{eq:momentum:cell-scalar}
is at least $1-\stagcfl\ge0$, so non-negative data stay non-negative; for
$\divopen\velo=0$ the coefficients sum to one, and the update is a convex
combination of upwind and inflow values, also in small cut cells. The
transport is not used by the flow solvers, whose cell quantities are the
phase fractions and phase scalars of \cref{ch:vof-transport}.

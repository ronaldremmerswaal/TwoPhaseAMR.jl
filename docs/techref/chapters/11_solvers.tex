\chapter{Operators and linear solvers}\label{ch:solvers}
\chapterstatus[c46cf60f29a3ee5a0771ec516942ab3987ee28c7]{reference}
Every implicit part of a time step is a sparse linear system: the pressure
projections (\cref{ch:projection}), the implicit viscous step
(\cref{ch:viscosity}) and the projection of the liquid carrier velocity
(\cref{sec:vof:projected}). The thesis analyses the conditioning of its
pressure matrices, whose largest-to-smallest nonzero eigenvalue ratio after
diagonal (Jacobi) scaling grows as $h^{-2}$, the multidimensional ghost-fluid
matrix being nonsymmetric with complex eigenvalues~\thesis{\S7.3.6}, but it
does not state how the systems are solved. This chapter states the matrices
that the code assembles and the properties that the solvers rely on
(\cref{sec:solvers:systems}), the Krylov driver shared by the elliptic solves
with its stopping rule and round-off floor (\cref{sec:solvers:krylov}), the
tree geometric multigrid preconditioner (\cref{sec:solvers:multigrid}), the
shifted viscous systems (\cref{sec:solvers:shifted}) and the solvers of the
projection systems (\cref{sec:solvers:projection}), and it reports measured
iteration counts, convergence factors, spectra and allocations
(\cref{sec:solvers:measured}). \Cref{tab:solvers:systems} lists the systems.
There is no time discretisation in this chapter: a solver maps a matrix, a
right-hand side and an initial guess to an approximate solution, and the time
step enters only through the matrices and tolerances of the calling chapters.

\section{Cell elliptic systems}\label{sec:solvers:systems}
\emph{Matrix.} The cell operator $\laph$ of~\cref{eq:setting:elliptic} is
stored in integrated form. Let $\volmat$ be the diagonal matrix of the
operator volumes: the cell volumes $\meas{c}$, the open volumes
$\meas{\opencell{c}}$ with a solid, or volumes supplied to the constructor
(the liquid-carrier projection supplies the open volumes of the cells that
contain liquid). The constructor requires that a cell is active exactly when
its volume is positive; the other cells form the inactive set
$\cellsinact$. The matrix of the solve is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:cell-matrix}
  \sysmat_{cc'}=\begin{cases}
    \reactcoef\,\volmat_{cc}\,\delta_{cc'}+\diffcoef\roundpar{\stiffmat+\bnddiag}_{cc'}, & c,c'\notin\cellsinact,\\[2pt]
    \delta_{cc'}, & c\in\cellsinact\ \text{or}\ c'\in\cellsinact,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  \stiffmat=\jumpmat^{\transpose}\coefmat^{1/2}\rieszopen\,\coefmat^{1/2}\jumpmat,
\end{equation}
with the face metric $\rieszopen$ ($\rieszmat$ without solid,
$\dualscale\rieszmat\dualscale$ with a solid) and the face coefficients
$\coefmat=\diag{\facecoef_f}$ of \cref{sec:setting:elliptic}. The product
$\stiffmat$ is assembled once per topology and replaced by
$\tfrac12(\stiffmat+\stiffmat^{\transpose})$, which is exactly symmetric in
floating point; only its values are refreshed when $\facecoef$ changes. The
diagonal $\bnddiag$ holds the conductances of the open boundary faces
$\facesout$, the boundary faces on \apitype{Outflow} and
\apitype{ZeroGradientVelocity} sides, where the pressure (increment) is zero:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:open-diagonal}
  \bnddiag_{cc}=\sum_{f\in\facesout,\ \cbnd{f}=c}\meas{f}\,\aperture{f}\,\frac{2\bndcoef{f}}{\cellwidth{c}{\faxis{f}}},
  \qquad c\notin\cellsinact,
\end{equation}
a two-point flux over the distance $\half\cellwidth{c}{\faxis{f}}$ from the
cell centre to the face, with the boundary aperture $\aperture{f}$ (one
without solid). The boundary coefficient is supplied per face, evaluated at
the face for a constant or a function coefficient, and otherwise extended from
the interior coefficients,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:boundary-coefficient}
  \bndcoef{f}=\frac{\sum_{g}\meas{g}\,\facecoef_g}{\sum_{g}\meas{g}},
\end{equation}
over the interior faces $g$ of axis $\faxis{f}$ on the side of $\cbnd{f}$
opposite to $f$; the constructor throws if there is no such face. The
two-phase pressure operator supplies its own boundary coefficients. The
weighted gradient of the projection uses the same conductances
(\cref{sec:projection:one}).

\emph{Inactive cells.} The rows and columns of $\cellsinact$ are those of the
identity, so inactive cells decouple whatever the coefficients of their faces.
Two conditions are distinguished by the operator, with the same matrix
treatment. With \code{inactive_condition=:decoupled} (cut-cell pressure) the
faces next to a covered cell have zero aperture and therefore
$\facecoef_f=0$~\cref{eq:setting:aperture}. With \code{:dirichlet} (the
liquid-carrier projection, whose inactive cells contain no liquid) the faces
between an active and an inactive cell keep their coefficient: their
contribution stays on the diagonal of the active row, which imposes a zero
value in the inactive cell. The active mask of a \code{:dirichlet} operator may
change on a fixed mesh; that of a \code{:decoupled} operator may not.

\emph{Properties.} For $\mathsf{x}$ with $\mathsf{x}_c=0$ on $\cellsinact$,
replacing the rows and columns of the inactive cells by the identity does not
change the quadratic form, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:energy}
  \mathsf{x}^{\transpose}\sysmat\,\mathsf{x}
  =\reactcoef\sum_{c}\volmat_{cc}\mathsf{x}_c^2
  +\diffcoef\,\norm{{\rieszopen}^{1/2}\coefmat^{1/2}\jumpmat\mathsf{x}}_2^2
  +\diffcoef\sum_{c}\bnddiag_{cc}\mathsf{x}_c^2 .
\end{equation}
Since $\rieszopen$ is symmetric positive definite (\cref{sec:setting:gradient})
and $\facecoef_f\ge0$, every term is non-negative: $\sysmat$ is symmetric
positive semidefinite, and positive definite for $\reactcoef>0$. For
$\reactcoef=0$ the null space is made of connected components. Consider the
graph whose vertices are the active cells and whose edges are the interior
faces with $\facecoef_f\neq0$. A component $\compcells$ of this graph is
\emph{anchored} if one of its cells has $\bnddiag_{cc}>0$ or if one of its
cells shares a face with $\facecoef_f\neq0$ with an inactive cell. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:null}
  \ker\sysmat=\operatorname{span}\set{\onevec_{\compcells}}{\compcells\ \text{an unanchored component}},
  \qquad
  \sysmat\mathsf{x}=\rhsvec\ \text{is solvable}\iff\onevec_{\compcells}^{\transpose}\rhsvec=0\ \text{for every unanchored}\ \compcells,
\end{equation}
where $\onevec_{\compcells}$ is the indicator of $\compcells$. Indeed, for the
symmetric positive semidefinite $\sysmat$, $\sysmat\mathsf{x}=0$ if and only
if $\mathsf{x}^{\transpose}\sysmat\mathsf{x}=0$; the inactive entries vanish
because their rows are those of the identity, and by~\cref{eq:solvers:energy}
with $\rieszopen$ definite the form vanishes exactly when $\mathsf{x}$ is
equal across every face with $\facecoef_f>0$, including the faces to inactive
cells, where the value is zero, and vanishes where $\bnddiag_{cc}>0$. The
solvability condition is $\rhsvec\perp\ker\sysmat$, since $\sysmat$ is
symmetric. The code labels each component by its smallest cell index, flags
the operator as singular if one component is unanchored, and caches the
labels; they are rebuilt when the mesh topology (its identity or generation),
the active mask or the set of faces with nonzero coefficient changes, and the
anchors are re-evaluated on every solve. The pressure on an unanchored
component is defined up to a constant; \cref{sec:solvers:krylov} fixes its
volume mean to zero.

\emph{Checks} (\code{solv_component_check}). On $32\times32$ cells of the unit
square with the solid band $\abs{y-0.46}<0.17$, which leaves two fluid
components with cut and covered cells, the operator has two unanchored
components; an \apitype{Outflow} side at the top anchors exactly one of them,
and one on the right side, which both components touch, anchors both and makes
the operator nonsingular. The indicators of the unanchored components are null
vectors to $\chkSolvNull$.

\emph{Factorised action.} The Krylov iteration does not use the assembled
matrix: it applies $\sysmat$ as the product of its factors, the jumps
$\jumpmat\mathsf{x}$ with the inactive entries set to zero, $\coefmat^{1/2}$,
$\rieszopen$, $\coefmat^{1/2}$ and $\jumpmat^{\transpose}$, plus the diagonal
$\reactcoef\volmat+\diffcoef\bnddiag$, and the identity on the inactive rows.
This action keeps the native, non-diagonal metric at level transitions and
equals the assembled matrix up to round-off. An operator built with
\code{assemble_matrix=false} stores no matrix; its multigrid hierarchy then
reads the diagonal, the absolute row sums and the transfer smoothing of the
finest level from an exact stencil of the factors
(\cref{sec:solvers:transfer}).

\section{Krylov driver and stopping rule}\label{sec:solvers:krylov}
The cell solves and both viscous solves run one driver around the Krylov
methods of Krylov.jl~\cite{montoison2023}: CG~\cite{hestenes1952},
BiCGSTAB~\cite{vandervorst1992}, GMRES~\cite{saad1986} or FGMRES. The CMDGFM
and mimetic projections call BiCGSTAB and MINRES in loops of their own
(\cref{sec:solvers:projection}).

\emph{Norms and stop level.} With $\volmat$ the diagonal weights of the solve
(the operator volumes for the cell systems, the staggered volumes
$\dualvolmat$ or the inertia $\massmat$ for the viscous ones), integrated
residuals are measured in the strong norms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:norms}
  \strongnorm{\resvec}=\max_{c:\,\volmat_{cc}>0}\frac{\abs{\resvec_c}}{\volmat_{cc}},\qquad
  \strongtwo{\resvec}=\roundpar{\frac{\sum_{c:\,\volmat_{cc}>0}\resvec_c^2/\volmat_{cc}}{\sum_{c:\,\volmat_{cc}>0}\volmat_{cc}}}^{1/2},
\end{equation}
the largest and the volume-weighted root-mean-square residual per unit volume.
With $\resvec_0=\rhsvec-\sysmat\mathsf{x}_0$ the residual of the initial
guess, a solve stops when the true residual satisfies
$\strongnorm{\resvec}\le\stoplevel$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:target}
  \stoplevel=\begin{cases}
    \max\curlypar{\abstol,\ \reltol\max(\strongnorm{\resvec_0},\machineeps),\ \roundfloor(\mathsf{x}_0)}, & \text{cell solves (\apifun{solve!})},\\[2pt]
    \max\curlypar{\soltol\,\strongnorm{\rhsvec},\ \roundfloor(\mathsf{x}_0)}, & \text{viscous solves},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where \apifun{solve!} defaults to $\reltol=10^{-10}$, $\abstol=0$ and $100$
iterations. The one-velocity projection passes $\reltol=0$ and
$\abstol=\presstol/\dt^2$, which is~\cref{eq:projection:one-tolerance}; the
liquid-carrier projection passes $\reltol=0$ and $\abstol=\projtol$; the
viscous solves stop relative to the right-hand side
(\cref{sec:viscosity:time}), so that their stop level does not depend on the
initial guess. The round-off floor is the scale of the terms that a residual
entry sums,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:floor}
  \roundfloor(\mathsf{x})=128\,\machineeps\max\curlypar{1,\ \max_{c:\,\volmat_{cc}>0}\frac{\roundpar{\abs{\sysmat}\abs{\mathsf{x}}}_c}{\volmat_{cc}},\ \strongnorm{\rhsvec}},
\end{equation}
with entrywise absolute values; for the factorised action $\abs{\sysmat}$ is
replaced by the entrywise larger
$\abs{\reactcoef}\volmat+\abs{\diffcoef}\roundpar{\bnddiag+\abs{\jumpmat}^{\transpose}\coefmat^{1/2}\abs{\rieszopen}\coefmat^{1/2}\abs{\jumpmat}}$.
The floor of the initial guess always applies. A floor evaluated at a later
iterate $\mathsf{x}$ with true residual $\resvec$ raises the stop level only
under a guard (\issue{97}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:guard}
  \stoplevel(\mathsf{x})=\begin{cases}
    \max\curlypar{\stoplevel,\ \roundfloor(\mathsf{x})}, & \stoplevel<\strongnorm{\resvec}<\strongnorm{\resvec_0}\ \text{and}\ \roundfloor(\mathsf{x})<\tfrac{1}{100}\strongnorm{\resvec_0},\\[2pt]
    \stoplevel, & \text{otherwise}:
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
the iterate must have reduced the residual, and its floor must be small
compared with the initial residual. An iterate that grows along a null
direction makes $\abs{\sysmat}\abs{\mathsf{x}}$ arbitrarily large while the
residual barely changes; without the guard such an iterate certified
convergence (\issue{97}: a pressure of about $10^{19}$ was reported as
converged). The guard always compares with the original $\stoplevel$, so
raised levels do not accumulate.

\emph{Restarted iteration.} A solve proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Copy the initial guess and the integrated right-hand side. If the
    operator is singular, project the right-hand side onto the range of
    $\sysmat$ and remove the component means of the guess (below).
  \item Compute $\resvec_0$, $\roundfloor(\mathsf{x}_0)$ and
    $\stoplevel$~\cref{eq:solvers:target}; stop if
    $\strongnorm{\resvec_0}\le\stoplevel$.
  \item Run the Krylov method from the current iterate with the remaining
    iteration budget. CG and BiCGSTAB stop when a callback finds
    $\strongnorm{\cdot}\le\stoplevel$ for their recursively updated residual;
    Krylov.jl's own tolerances are set to zero. GMRES and FGMRES expose the
    residual only at the end of an Arnoldi pass; they stop on the Euclidean
    norm,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:gmres}
      \norm{\resvec}_2\le\stoplevel\,\min\curlypar{1,\ \min_{c:\,\volmat_{cc}>0}\volmat_{cc}}
      \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\strongnorm{\resvec}\le\stoplevel,
    \end{equation}
    because $\abs{\resvec_c}\le\norm{\resvec}_2$ for every $c$ (the cap at one
    only tightens the test when every volume exceeds one). With right
    preconditioning their residual is that of the unpreconditioned system.
  \item Remove the component means, recompute the true residual
    $\rhsvec-\sysmat\mathsf{x}$ and stop if it meets the guarded
    level~\cref{eq:solvers:guard}, if it is not finite, or if the pass made no
    iteration; otherwise continue with step~3 until the budget is spent.
  \item Remove the component means, recompute the true residual and the
    guarded level, and report \code{converged} if the residual is finite and
    meets it.
\end{enumerate}
A recursively updated residual drifts from $\rhsvec-\sysmat\mathsf{x}$ by
round-off, which the strong norm amplifies in cut cells with a small volume;
the restart replaces it by the true residual without changing the
discretisation. If BiCGSTAB returns a non-finite iterate with a
preconditioner (a $0/0$ before an almost exact intermediate residual is
detected), the pass is repeated without the preconditioner. CG receives the
preconditioner as Krylov.jl's \code{M}, applied by \code{ldiv!} as
$\mathsf{z}=\precond\resvec$; BiCGSTAB, GMRES and FGMRES apply it from the right
(\code{N}). CG requires $\precond$ to be symmetric and positive definite on
the space in which the iterates live (\cref{sec:solvers:vcycle}).

\emph{Singular systems.} For each unanchored component $\compcells$ the
right-hand side is made compatible and the solution gauged,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:compatibility}
  \rhsvec\leftarrow\rhsvec-\volmat\onevec_{\compcells}\frac{\onevec_{\compcells}^{\transpose}\rhsvec}{\onevec_{\compcells}^{\transpose}\volmat\onevec_{\compcells}},
  \qquad
  \mathsf{x}\leftarrow\mathsf{x}-\onevec_{\compcells}\frac{\onevec_{\compcells}^{\transpose}\volmat\mathsf{x}}{\onevec_{\compcells}^{\transpose}\volmat\onevec_{\compcells}},
\end{equation}
which is~\cref{eq:projection:one-gauge}; the removed means and their anchors
are reported, and with \code{compatibility=:error} the solve throws if
$\abs{\onevec_{\compcells}^{\transpose}\rhsvec}>256\machineeps\max(1,\sum_{c\in\compcells}\abs{\rhsvec_c})$.
Covered cells get a zero right-hand side and a zero solution. For a consistent
symmetric positive semidefinite system, CG converges to a solution
\cite{kaasschieter1988}; the gauge removes the null-space part of the iterate
after every pass. The projections are applied independently on every
component, so that a component with a net boundary flux does not disturb the
others (\issue{95}).

\emph{Statistics and failure.} \apitype{LinearSolverStats} records the
iterations summed over the passes (\code{cycles}), the strong norms of
$\resvec_0$ and of the final residual, the final $\stoplevel$
(\code{target_linf}), \code{converged}, the removed means and anchors, the
names of the method and the preconditioner, and a residual scale:
$\max(\strongnorm{\resvec_0},\machineeps)$ for \apifun{solve!},
$\strongnorm{\rhsvec}$ for the viscous solves and $1/\dt^2$ as set by the
projections, which also overwrite \code{compatible}
(\cref{sec:projection:one}). Failure messages quote the final residual divided
by this scale. \apifun{solve!} and the projections report a missed tolerance;
the viscous steps and the liquid-carrier projection throw, and
\apifun{simulate!} throws for a projection that is not converged.

\emph{Initial guesses.} \apifun{solve!} starts from the solution field it is
given; the one-velocity and liquid-carrier projections start from a zero
increment; the viscous solves start from the destination field, the velocity
before the viscous step; the CMDGFM projection may start from the previous
increment (\cref{sec:solvers:projection}); the mimetic projection starts from
zero.

\emph{Storage.} A solver owns its Krylov workspace, its solution,
right-hand-side, residual and product vectors and the scratch of the
factorised action. After a regrid these are resized in place: Krylov.jl's
vectors keep their capacity, Krylov.jl's own warm-start flag is cleared, and
a GMRES basis longer than the new number of unknowns forces a new workspace.
\Cref{sec:solvers:measured} reports the allocations of repeated solves.

\emph{Other preconditioners.} The symbols \code{:amg} (AlgebraicMultigrid.jl,
smoothed aggregation or Ruge--St\"uben, with a pseudo-inverse coarse solve
for a singular operator), \code{:ilu} (IncompleteLU.jl's Crout
ILU~\cite{li2003}, drop tolerance $10^{-3}$), \code{:ichol} (the limited-memory
incomplete $\mathsf{LDL}^{\transpose}$ of LimitedLDLFactorizations.jl, symmetric
operators only), a factory $\sysmat\mapsto\precond$ or a pre-built object with
\code{ldiv!} replace the tree multigrid; they act on the assembled matrix.

\section{Tree geometric multigrid}\label{sec:solvers:multigrid}
The default preconditioner of the cell systems is one V-cycle of a geometric
multigrid method on a sequence of coarser block trees; the operator is
rediscretised on every tree, the cell transfer is a Jacobi-smoothed
piecewise-constant interpolation in the manner of smoothed
aggregation~\cite{vanek1996}, and the coarsest system is factorised. Geometric
multigrid for quadtree and octree AMR is also used by, e.g.,
Afivo~\cite{teunissen2018}. Here every coarse mesh is again a complete block
tree, obtained by pruning, so that every level operator is a composite-mesh
operator of \cref{ch:discrete-setting}.
The viscous hierarchies of \cref{sec:solvers:shifted} reuse the coarsening,
smoothing and coarse solve with a different transfer.

\subsection{Hierarchy}\label{sec:solvers:hierarchy}
The solver meshes $\mgmesh{0},\mgmesh{1},\dots,\mgmesh{J}$ start with the
composite mesh $\mgmesh{0}$. Above the root level, $\mgmesh{\mgindex+1}$
replaces every complete family of four leaf blocks of $\mgmesh{\mgindex}$ by
its parent (pruning \code{:all}; with \code{:deepest} it caps the deepest
level instead), and the tree constructor restores the 2:1 balance. Once all
blocks are roots, $\mgmesh{\mgindex+1}$ merges $2\times2$ root blocks, which
halves the base mesh, while both root counts are even and every periodic axis
keeps at least four cells (with two cells per period the face displacement is
ambiguous, \issue{36}). Coarsening stops when the mesh has at most
\code{coarse_size} cells (default $64$), when $J+1$ reaches \code{max_levels}
(default $32$), when no coarser mesh exists, or, with pruning \code{:all},
when a pruned mesh is not smaller. If coarsening stops early the last mesh is
factorised however large it is.

\subsection{Coarse operators and transfer}\label{sec:solvers:transfer}
\emph{Rediscretisation.} The operator of $\mgmesh{\mgindex+1}$ is the cell
operator~\cref{eq:solvers:cell-matrix} on that mesh, with its own face metric,
the same $\reactcoef$, $\diffcoef$ and open sides, and restricted coefficients,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:coarse-operator}
  \mgmat{\mgindex+1}=\sysmat\roundpar{\mgmesh{\mgindex+1};\ \facecoef^{\mgindex+1},\ \gamma^{\partial,\mgindex+1}},
  \qquad
  \facecoef^{\mgindex+1}_F=\sum_{f\subset F}\frac{\meas{f\cap F}}{\meas{F}}\,\facecoef^{\mgindex}_f,
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over the faces $f$ of $\mgmesh{\mgindex}$ that lie on the
line of the coarse face $F$: the length-weighted arithmetic mean along $F$
(boundary coefficients likewise). No Galerkin product
${\mgprolong{\mgindex}}^{\transpose}\mgmat{\mgindex}\mgprolong{\mgindex}$ is
formed. With a solid and the solver's geometry passed to the hierarchy, each
coarse mesh carries its own geometry: the solid fraction of a coarse cell is
the volume mean of the fractions of its fine cells, reconstructed by PLIC,
which defines the coarse apertures, open volumes and active cells; the
material part $\matcoef_F$ of the coefficient is the mean of
$\matcoef_f=\facecoef_f/(\aperture{f})^2$ weighted by
$\aperture{f}\,\meas{f\cap F}/\meas{F}$ over the fine faces with
$\aperture{f}>0$ (the aperture-weighted mean over all fine faces if there are
none), and $\facecoef_F=\matcoef_F(\aperture{F})^2$. Without geometry, a coarse
cell of a pressure operator with inactive cells is active if one of its fine
cells is active, and then has its full volume.

\emph{Transfer.} The tentative transfer assigns each cell of
$\mgmesh{\mgindex}$ to the coarse cell that contains its centre,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:tentative}
  \roundpar{\mgtent{\mgindex}}_{cC}=\begin{cases}1, & \pos{c}\in C,\\ 0, & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}
  \qquad c\in\mgmesh{\mgindex},\ C\in\mgmesh{\mgindex+1},
\end{equation}
which is well defined because the meshes are nested. One damped-Jacobi step
smooths it,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:smoothed-transfer}
  \mgprolong{\mgindex}=\roundpar{\idmat-\transferweight{\mgindex}\mathsf{D}_{\mgindex}^{-1}\mathsf{A}_{\mgindex}}\mgtent{\mgindex},
  \qquad
  \transferweight{\mgindex}=\frac{4}{3\gershbound{\mgindex}},
\end{equation}
with $\mathsf{A}_{\mgindex}$ the stiffness $\stiffmat+\bnddiag$ of
$\mgmat{\mgindex}$ with its inactive rows (the full $\mgmat{\mgindex}$ on a
matrix-free finest level; the two coincide for a pressure operator,
$\reactcoef=0$, $\diffcoef=1$), $\mathsf{D}_{\mgindex}$ its diagonal, rows with
a non-positive diagonal left unsmoothed, and the Gershgorin bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:gershgorin}
  \gershbound{\mgindex}=\max_{i:\,\mathsf{A}_{ii}>0}\ \sum_{i'}\frac{\abs{\mathsf{A}_{ii'}}}{\mathsf{A}_{ii}}
  \ \ge\ \max\abs{\operatorname{spec}\roundpar{\mathsf{D}_{\mgindex}^{-1}\mathsf{A}_{\mgindex}}}
\end{equation}
(with $\transferweight{\mgindex}=0$ if $\gershbound{\mgindex}=0$). The weight
$4/(3\gershbound{})$ uses the Gershgorin bound in place of the spectral radius
of $\mathsf{D}_{\mgindex}^{-1}\mathsf{A}_{\mgindex}$~\cite[Thm.~4.6]{saad2003};
for the five-point operator of a uniform mesh it gives $\transferweight{}=2/3$.
Restriction is the transpose, ${\mgprolong{\mgindex}}^{\transpose}$, applied to
integrated residuals. If the fine or the coarse level has inactive cells
(cut-cell pressure, or $\reactcoef=0$ with inactive cells), the entries that
couple an inactive fine or coarse cell are set to zero and every active fine
row is normalised,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:active-transfer}
  \roundpar{\mgprolong{\mgindex}}_{cC}\leftarrow\frac{\roundpar{\mgprolong{\mgindex}}_{cC}}{\sum_{C'\notin\cellsinact}\roundpar{\mgprolong{\mgindex}}_{cC'}},
  \qquad c,C\notin\cellsinact,
  \qquad\text{so that}\quad\mgprolong{\mgindex}\onevec^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mgindex+1}=\onevec^{\mathrm{act}}_{\mgindex}:
\end{equation}
the transfer preserves the null vector of the active domain, which is one on
the active and zero on the covered cells; a transfer that preserved the
constant background vector would send coarse corrections into covered cells.
An active fine row with a zero sum is an error. The sparsity patterns of the
transfers and coarse matrices are fixed per topology; coefficient changes only
refresh their values (\cref{sec:solvers:reuse}).

\subsection{V-cycle}\label{sec:solvers:vcycle}
\emph{Smoother.} A sweep of damped Jacobi on level $\mgindex$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:jacobi}
  \mathsf{x}\leftarrow\mathsf{x}+\jacobiweight{\mgindex}\,\mathsf{D}^{-1}\roundpar{\rhsvec-\mgmat{\mgindex}\mathsf{x}},
  \qquad
  \jacobiweight{\mgindex}=\min\curlypar{1,\ \frac{4}{3\gershbound{\mgindex}}},
\end{equation}
with $\mathsf{D}$ and $\gershbound{\mgindex}$ those of $\mgmat{\mgindex}$; a
non-positive diagonal is an error, except a zero diagonal (an isolated cell)
on a singular level. Since the eigenvalues $\lambda$ of
$\mathsf{D}^{-1}\mgmat{\mgindex}$ lie in $[0,\gershbound{\mgindex}]$,
$\jacobiweight{\mgindex}\lambda\le\tfrac43<2$: the sweep reduces the error in
the energy norm. On a uniform mesh the rows of the five-point operator give
$\gershbound{}=2$ and $\jacobiweight{}=2/3$.

\emph{Component projections.} On a level with unanchored components the cycle
applies, with $\mathsf{v}_{\compcells}=\volmat_{\mgindex}\onevec_{\compcells}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:projectors}
  \compproj{\mgindex}\mathsf{x}=\mathsf{x}-\sum_{\compcells}\onevec_{\compcells}\frac{\mathsf{v}_{\compcells}^{\transpose}\mathsf{x}}{\mathsf{v}_{\compcells}^{\transpose}\onevec_{\compcells}},
  \qquad
  \compproj{\mgindex}^{\transpose}\rhsvec=\rhsvec-\sum_{\compcells}\mathsf{v}_{\compcells}\frac{\onevec_{\compcells}^{\transpose}\rhsvec}{\mathsf{v}_{\compcells}^{\transpose}\onevec_{\compcells}},
\end{equation}
the removal of the volume means of the solution and its transpose, the
compatible projection of an integrated right-hand side, both over the
unanchored components of $\mgmesh{\mgindex}$. Coarsening may merge, split or
anchor components; every level uses its own.

\emph{Cycle.} Applied to an integrated residual $\resvec$, the preconditioner
returns $\precond\resvec=\mathsf{x}_0$ of the following recursion with
$\rhsvec_0=\resvec$, $\nu_1=\nu_2=2$ sweeps by default:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item on a singular level, $\rhsvec_{\mgindex}\leftarrow\compproj{\mgindex}^{\transpose}\rhsvec_{\mgindex}$;
    on $\mgmesh{J}$ solve directly (below) and return;
  \item $\mathsf{x}_{\mgindex}=0$; $\nu_1$ sweeps~\cref{eq:solvers:jacobi}, each
    followed by $\mathsf{x}_{\mgindex}\leftarrow\compproj{\mgindex}\mathsf{x}_{\mgindex}$
    on a singular level;
  \item $\rhsvec_{\mgindex+1}={\mgprolong{\mgindex}}^{\transpose}\roundpar{\rhsvec_{\mgindex}-\mgmat{\mgindex}\mathsf{x}_{\mgindex}}$,
    and $\mathsf{x}_{\mgindex+1}$ by the recursion on level $\mgindex+1$;
  \item $\mathsf{x}_{\mgindex}\leftarrow\compproj{\mgindex}\roundpar{\mathsf{x}_{\mgindex}+\mgprolong{\mgindex}\mathsf{x}_{\mgindex+1}}$
    ($\compproj{\mgindex}$ on a singular level only), then $\nu_2$ sweeps as in step~2.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Coarse solve.} The last level is factorised by sparse LU
(UMFPACK~\cite{davis2004}, through Julia's \code{lu}). If it has $m$
unanchored components with indicator matrix
$\mathsf{E}=[\onevec_{\compcells_1},\dots,\onevec_{\compcells_m}]$, the
factorised matrix is the bordered system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:augmented}
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mgmat{J} & \volmat_J\mathsf{E}\\
    \mathsf{E}^{\transpose}\volmat_J & 0
  \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix}\mathsf{x}_J\\ \boldsymbol{\mu}\end{bmatrix}
  =\begin{bmatrix}\rhsvec_J\\ 0\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
which is nonsingular and returns the solution with zero volume mean on every
component; for the compatible $\rhsvec_J$ of step~1 the multipliers
$\boldsymbol{\mu}$ vanish. A factorisation is refactorised in place when the
pattern of the new matrix, including stored zeros, equals the stored one: the
values are copied and only the numerical factorisation is repeated, reusing
the symbolic analysis; otherwise it is factorised anew.

\emph{Symmetry and definiteness.} CG needs a preconditioner that is symmetric
and positive definite on the space of its iterates. The V-cycle is arranged
for this: restriction is the transpose of prolongation, the same number of
sweeps of the same symmetric smoother precede and follow the coarse
correction, the projections enter in transposed pairs, and the coarse solve is
exact on the compatible space. Let $\precond_{\mgindex}$ be the cycle started
on level $\mgindex$ and $\mathcal W_{\mgindex}$ the volume-mean-free space of
the unanchored components of $\mgmesh{\mgindex}$, on which
$\langle\mathsf x,\mathsf y\rangle_{\mgindex}=\mathsf x^{\transpose}\mgmat{\mgindex}\mathsf y$
is an inner product. With
$\mathsf S=\compproj{\mgindex}(\idmat-\jacobiweight{\mgindex}\mathsf D^{-1}\mgmat{\mgindex})$
and $\nu=\nu_1=\nu_2$ the error of one cycle is
\begin{equation}
  \idmat-\precond_{\mgindex}\mgmat{\mgindex}=\mathsf S^{\nu}\,\mathsf T\,\mathsf S^{\nu},
  \qquad
  \mathsf T=\idmat-\compproj{\mgindex}\mgprolong{\mgindex}\,\precond_{\mgindex+1}\,{\mgprolong{\mgindex}}^{\transpose}\mgmat{\mgindex}.
\end{equation}
Since $\mgmat{\mgindex}\compproj{\mgindex}=\mgmat{\mgindex}$, both factors are
selfadjoint for $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mgindex}$, and
$\langle\mathsf T\mathsf x,\mathsf x\rangle_{\mgindex}=\norm{\mathsf x}_{\mgindex}^{2}-\mathsf r^{\transpose}\mgprolong{\mgindex}\precond_{\mgindex+1}{\mgprolong{\mgindex}}^{\transpose}\mathsf r\le\norm{\mathsf x}_{\mgindex}^{2}$
with $\mathsf r=\mgmat{\mgindex}\mathsf x$ whenever $\precond_{\mgindex+1}$ is
symmetric positive semidefinite, as the exact coarse solve~\cref{eq:solvers:augmented} is.
The smoother contracts, $\norm{\mathsf S\mathsf x}_{\mgindex}<\norm{\mathsf x}_{\mgindex}$ on
$\mathcal W_{\mgindex}\setminus\{0\}$, exactly when
$0<\jacobiweight{\mgindex}\lambda<2$ on the nonzero spectrum of
$\mathsf D^{-1}\mgmat{\mgindex}$, which~\cref{eq:solvers:jacobi} guarantees
($\jacobiweight{\mgindex}\lambda\le\tfrac43$). Hence
$\langle\precond_{\mgindex}\mgmat{\mgindex}\mathsf e,\mathsf e\rangle_{\mgindex}\ge\norm{\mathsf e}_{\mgindex}^{2}-\norm{\mathsf S^{\nu}\mathsf e}_{\mgindex}^{2}>0$,
and by induction from the coarsest level $\precond$ is symmetric, positive
definite on the mean-free space and zero on the $\mathsf v_{\compcells}$. The
argument uses no relation between $\mgprolong{\mgindex}$ and $\mgmat{\mgindex}$,
hence no Galerkin product; for nonsingular levels it is
Theorems~2 and~3 of~\cite{tatebe1993}. It needs $\nu_1=\nu_2$; CG with
the tree multigrid preconditioner requires equal pre- and post-sweep counts,
while nonsymmetric Krylov methods may use unequal counts (\issue{128}). The
argument fails for $\jacobiweight{\mgindex}\lambda>2$. It does not make
the cycle a contraction: $\lambda_{\max}(\precond\sysmat)$ exceeds one on an
operator with an open side ($1.45$) and on a Dirichlet-inactive operator
without geometry ($2.2$), so the cycle is a preconditioner, not a stand-alone
solver, and its convergence factor is not bounded a priori; it is measured
(\cref{sec:solvers:measured}).

\emph{Checks} (\code{solv_vcycle_check}). The dense matrix $\precond$,
assembled from the $\precond$-images of the unit vectors, on the unit square
with $16\times16$ and $32\times32$ uniform cells, on a composite mesh of
$\chkSolvVcycleCells$ cells, for the drop at density ratio $1000$ and for the
split domain of \cref{sec:solvers:systems} with its geometry: $\precond$ is
symmetric to $\chkSolvVcycleAsym$ (largest entry of
$\precond-\precond^{\transpose}$ relative to the largest entry of
$\precond$); its columns have zero volume mean on every unanchored component to
$\chkSolvVcycleRange$; it couples active and covered cells by exactly zero; and
the eigenvalues of $\precond\sysmat$ on the volume-mean-free space lie in
$[\chkSolvLambdaMin,\chkSolvLambdaMax]$, with condition numbers
$\kappa(\precond\sysmat)=\chkSolvKappaUniformA$, $\chkSolvKappaUniformB$
(uniform), $\chkSolvKappaAmr$ (composite), $\chkSolvKappaDrop$ (drop) and

$\lceil\ln(2/\reltol)/\ln(1/q)\rceil$ iterations, within one of the measured

every level of these and of the fixtures of \cref{tab:solvers:counts}
$\gershbound{}=2$, so $\jacobiweight{}=\chkSolvOmegaUniform$.

\subsection{Refresh and reuse}\label{sec:solvers:reuse}
\emph{Coefficient refresh.} On a fixed mesh a new face coefficient, as for a
two-phase pressure operator after the interface has moved, refreshes the
hierarchy in place: the coarse activity (without geometry), the restricted
coefficients and boundary coefficients of every level and the values of the
coarse matrices, the smoothed transfers~\cref{eq:solvers:smoothed-transfer}
with their restriction~\cref{eq:solvers:active-transfer}, the Jacobi weights
and diagonals, the components, and the coarse factorisation, refactorised in
place. The two-phase step refreshes the pressure hierarchy in every step when
the densities differ, because a hierarchy built for an earlier interface
position loses effectiveness at a large density ratio. A change between a
pressure ($\reactcoef=0$) and a shifted operator, of the inactive-cell
condition or of the open sides requires a new hierarchy.

\emph{Reuse after a regrid.} A hierarchy for the new mesh may be built with the
previous one: after each coarsening step the new coarse mesh is compared with
the meshes of the old hierarchy (the same base mesh, blocks, faces, dual
volumes and boundary faces), and at the first match the old levels from there
on, with their transfers, restriction maps and coarse factorisation, are
appended and refreshed as above. This requires the same pruning, $\reactcoef$,
$\diffcoef$, open sides and inactive condition and no more than
\code{max_levels} levels; a hierarchy with geometry shares only the coarse
mesh objects and rebuilds its levels. In a flow the pressure hierarchy is
rebuilt this way after every regrid.

\emph{Checks} (\code{solv_reuse_check}). After moving the refined band of the
composite mesh of \cref{tab:solvers:counts} (32 base cells per side), the
new hierarchy of $\chkSolvReuseLevels$ levels reuses $\chkSolvReused$, and its
V-cycle equals that of a hierarchy built from scratch to
$\chkSolvReuseDiff$. After moving the drop on a fixed $64\times64$ mesh, the
refreshed hierarchy keeps its coarse factorisation object and its V-cycle
equals that of a new hierarchy to $\chkSolvRefreshDiff$ (both relative to the
largest entry).

\section{Shifted viscous systems}\label{sec:solvers:shifted}
\emph{One phase.} The implicit midpoint step~\cref{eq:viscosity:single-midpoint}
solves, for each velocity component $k$ separately,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:shifted-single}
  \dualvolmat\roundpar{\idmat-s\,\lapstag{k}}\velo^{\stage{2}}=\dualvolmat\,\mathsf{w},
  \qquad s=\tfrac12\dt\,\visc/\dens,
\end{equation}
with the explicit terms and wall sources of~\cref{eq:viscosity:single-midpoint}
in $\mathsf{w}$. The matrix $\dualvolmat(\idmat-s\lapstag{k})$ is symmetric
for every $s$ iff $\dualvolmat\lapstag{k}$ is. The automatic Krylov selection
uses CG when this weighted symmetry is guaranteed for both components and
BiCGSTAB otherwise. The corrected rows of~\cref{eq:viscosity:laplacian} at
level transitions are nonsymmetric ($\dualvolmat\lapstag{k}$ misses symmetry
by $27\,\%$ of its largest entry on the composite fixture), so on composite
meshes without a solid the one-phase viscous step uses BiCGSTAB (\issue{80}).
The embedded operator has no corrected band: $\dualvolmat\lapstag{k}$ is
symmetric to round-off ($4\times10^{-17}$ on a composite mesh with a solid),
so its weighted system uses CG rather than BiCGSTAB (\issue{128}). The default
preconditioner is, per component,
a tree multigrid with the coarsening of \cref{sec:solvers:hierarchy} (counted
in faces of the axis), rediscretisation with the same viscosity, boundary
conditions and shift on every coarse mesh, no Galerkin product, the
smoother~\cref{eq:solvers:jacobi}, and a dense LU coarse solve for at most
$256$ unknowns, a sparse LU otherwise. For a symmetric fine system the coarse
matrices use the conservative symmetric stiffness without the AMR correction,
so that the V-cycle remains symmetric for CG even on composite coarse meshes.
For a nonsymmetric fine system they retain the corrected operator. Its transfer
is the linear staggered interpolation from the coarser tree: a fine face on a
coarse face line takes the length-weighted mean of the coarse faces that it
overlaps, a new face interpolates linearly between the two neighbouring coarse
face lines of its axis, a missing line at a physical wall contributes the
homogeneous boundary value, and periodic distances wrap; restriction is the
transpose, and the transfer is not smoothed. A new step size changes only $s$:
the values of every level are rewritten on the fixed pattern (once extended by
the diagonal if $s$ was zero), the Jacobi weights recomputed and the coarse
factorisation refactorised; an unchanged $s$ returns at once. After a regrid
the hierarchy is rebuilt with the reuse of \cref{sec:solvers:reuse}, which
here also requires the same boundary type and the same $s$: \apifun{simulate!}
builds the new operator with the step just taken, so runs with a fixed step
reuse the coarse levels. Matrix preconditioners (\code{:ilu}, \dots) are built
from the symmetric part $s\viscstiff{k}+\dualvolmat$ without the corrected
rows.

\emph{Two phases.} The coupled system~\cref{eq:viscosity:phase-midpoint},
$(\massmat+\tfrac12\dt\,\strainmat^{\transpose}\stressw\strainmat)\velo^{\stage{2}}=\rhsvec$,
is symmetric positive definite and solved by CG with the factorised action of
\cref{sec:viscosity:time}. Its preconditioner is a tree multigrid for the
coupled system of all faces: the finest level is the assembled matrix; each
coarser tree (coarsened to at most $2\,\code{coarse_size}$ face unknowns above
the root level and \code{coarse_size} cells below it) rediscretises the stress
operator with the logarithm of the cell viscosity averaged over the children
(a geometric mean of the viscosity), the staggered density averaged as
in~\cref{eq:solvers:coarse-operator}, and the same $\dt$; the transfer is the
linear staggered interpolation above on all faces; smoothing and the sparse
LU coarse solve are those of \cref{sec:solvers:vcycle}. Because the density,
the viscosity and $\dt$ change in every step, the hierarchy is refreshed on
every solve. With two velocities there is one such solve for the mass-weighted
mixture velocity (\cref{sec:viscosity:time}).

\section{Projection systems}\label{sec:solvers:projection}
\emph{One velocity.} The projection~\cref{eq:projection:one-system} is a cell
solve with $\reactcoef=0$, $\diffcoef=1$ and the stop
level~\cref{eq:projection:one-tolerance}, from a zero increment, by CG with
the tree multigrid; with a solid the hierarchy is built with the solver's
geometry. The liquid-carrier projection of \cref{sec:vof:projected} solves a
\code{:dirichlet} cell system, updated in place in every call, by CG without
preconditioner, with \code{compatibility=:project} if its operator is singular
and \code{:error} otherwise.

\emph{CMDGFM.} The two-block system~\cref{eq:projection:cmdgfm-system} is
nondimensionalised before the iteration with the largest domain extent $L$
and the largest density $\dens_0$: the unknowns are scaled by
$\dens_0(L/\dt)^2$ (pressure) and $L/\dt^2$ (jump), the rows by $(\dt/L)^2$
and $\dt^2/L$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:cmdgfm-scaling}
  \hat{\sysmat}=\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\sysmat\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{c}},\qquad
  \hat{\rhsvec}=\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\rhsvec,\qquad
  \mathsf{x}=\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}\hat{\mathsf{x}},\qquad
  \hat{\precond}=\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{c}}^{-1}\precond\,\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}^{-1},
\end{equation}
so that $\hat{\sysmat}\hat{\precond}=\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}\sysmat\precond\mathsf{D}_{\mathrm{r}}^{-1}$
is similar to $\sysmat\precond$, so the preconditioned spectrum is unchanged.
BiCGSTAB itself is not invariant under a nonuniform $\mathsf D_{\mathrm r}$: on
the scaled system it is BiCGSTAB on the unscaled right-preconditioned system
in the inner product $\mathsf u^{\transpose}\mathsf D_{\mathrm r}^{2}\mathsf v$
(shadow residual, residual-minimising step and stopping norm), so the scaling
weighs the pressure and the jump rows in these. BiCGSTAB is
right-preconditioned; each pass requests the relative reduction
$\min(\presstol,\presstol/e)$ of the scaled residual, with $e$ the largest
current defect of \cref{sec:projection:defects}, and up to eight passes
restart from the true residual until the defects are met. The default block
preconditioner is lower block-triangular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:block-preconditioner}
  \precond\begin{bmatrix}\resvec_{\pres}\\ \resvec_{\gradjump}\end{bmatrix}
  =\begin{bmatrix}\mathsf{z}_{\pres}\\ \mathsf{z}_{\gradjump}\end{bmatrix},\qquad
  \mathsf{z}_{\pres}=\mathsf{G}\,\precond_{\mathrm{c}}\,\mathsf{G}^{\transpose}\resvec_{\pres},\qquad
  \mathsf{z}_{\gradjump}=\tilde{\jumpblock}^{-1}\roundpar{\resvec_{\gradjump}-\jumpblockp\,\mathsf{z}_{\pres}},
\end{equation}
where $\precond_{\mathrm{c}}$ is one V-cycle of the tree multigrid of the
pressure operator on all cells (\cref{sec:solvers:multigrid}),
$(\mathsf{G}\mathsf{x})_c=\mathsf{x}_c-\mathsf{x}_{a(c)}$ maps a cell function
to the retained cells $\cellsret$ with $a(c)$ the anchor of the component of
$c$ (so $\mathsf{G}^{\transpose}$ adds a residual to its cell and subtracts it
from the anchor), and $\tilde{\jumpblock}$ is the Crout ILU with drop
tolerance $10^{-3}$ of the jump block $\jumpblock$. The ILU factor is reused
while the jump block is unchanged, and without a solid the one-velocity
operator and hierarchy are shared when their settings agree. The alternatives
are \code{:ilu}, one ILU with drop tolerance $10^{-3}$ of the whole scaled
matrix $\hat{\sysmat}$, and no preconditioner. The first guess is zero or the
previous increment with a jump regenerated from the current residual,
accepted only if it reduces the largest defect below $0.95$ times that of
zero and lowers the Euclidean residual (\cref{sec:projection:defects}).

\emph{Mimetic.} The symmetric indefinite system~\cref{eq:projection:kkt} is
scaled by a congruence (rows proportional to columns), which keeps it
symmetric, and solved by MINRES~\cite{paige1975}, left-preconditioned with the
symmetric positive definite block-diagonal matrix of \cref{sec:projection:mimetic}
whose pressure and multiplier blocks are factorised by sparse Cholesky
(CHOLMOD); there is no first guess, and up to eight passes restart from the
true residual.

\begin{deviation}{Float32 mimetic projection (open)}\label{dev:solvers:float32}
In single precision the mimetic projection of a mixed-circle case does not
converge: at the tolerance $10^{-3}$ MINRES stops after $22$ iterations with
$12$ of $17$ constraint rows independent and a normal velocity defect of
$0.55$ (\code{converged=false}), whereas the sparse-factor solves and the
pure-phase projections converge in single precision. The rank and fit
tolerances are scaled for double precision; whether they or the conditioning
of the fitted rows cause the failure is not determined (\issue{45}). The code
accepts \code{Float32} for the mimetic projection without a warning.
\end{deviation}

\section{Measured convergence}\label{sec:solvers:measured}
\Cref{tab:solvers:counts} gives, for representative systems, the iterations
$\itcount$ to the stop level and the mean reduction of the strong residual per
iteration,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:factor}
  \convfactor=\roundpar{\frac{\strongtwo{\resvec_{\itcount}}}{\strongtwo{\resvec_0}}}^{1/\itcount},
\end{equation}
with the default preconditioner and without one. The fixtures are the unit
square with walls and $8\times8$-cell blocks; the composite meshes refine
twice the blocks within $0.02$ of the circle of radius $0.2371$ centred at
$(0.513,0.468)$; the drop is the liquid inside that circle with densities
$(1000,1)$, so that the face coefficient $1/\stagdens_f$ varies by three
decades; the split domain is the solid band of \cref{sec:solvers:systems} on
$128\times128$ cells. The cell systems have a right-hand side of
deterministic noise, $\rhsvec_c=\operatorname{frac}(43758.5453\sin(12.9898\,c))-\tfrac12$,
and stop at $\reltol=10^{-10}$; the one-phase viscous systems are those of
unit viscosity with the shift $s=\sigma h^2$, $h$ the smallest cell width,
for both components (the larger count), with free-slip walls; the two-phase
viscous systems take the drop with viscosities $(0.1,0.002)$ and $\dt=1$, for
which $\tfrac12\dt\,(\strainmat^{\transpose}\stressw\strainmat)_{ff}/(\massmat)_{ff}$
reaches $\chkSolvVariableRatio$ (uniform) and $\chkSolvVariableAmrRatio$
(composite); both stop at $\soltol=10^{-10}$.

\emph{Cell systems.} With the multigrid preconditioner CG needs
$\chkSolvUniformACycles$--$\chkSolvUniformCCycles$ iterations on
$64\times64$ to $256\times256$ uniform cells, independently of the mesh size,
while without preconditioner the count doubles with every refinement, as the
condition number $\mathcal{O}(h^{-2})$ of the thesis predicts for CG. The
composite meshes, the density ratio $1000$ and the two-component cut-cell
domain keep the count within
$\chkSolvUniformACycles$--$\chkSolvDropAmrCycles$; the drop is the slowest,
with $\convfactor=\chkSolvDropAmrFactor$, where CG without preconditioner
needs $\chkSolvDropAmrPlain$ iterations. Each multigrid iteration costs one
V-cycle with four Jacobi sweeps per level.

\emph{Viscous systems.} The shifted one-phase systems are well conditioned for
small shifts ($\sigma=1$: $\chkSolvShiftACycles$ against
$\chkSolvShiftAPlain$ iterations) and the multigrid keeps the count low at
$\sigma=100$ on the uniform mesh ($\chkSolvShiftBCycles$ against
$\chkSolvShiftBPlain$). On the composite mesh, where the corrected rows make
the system nonsymmetric, multigrid-preconditioned BiCGSTAB needs
$\chkSolvShiftAmrCycles$ iterations with $\convfactor=\chkSolvShiftAmrFactor$,
against $\chkSolvShiftAmrPlain$ without preconditioner; this preconditioner,
with its unsmoothed linear transfer, is much less effective there than on the
uniform mesh. For the coupled two-phase stress the counts are
$\chkSolvVariableCycles$ and $\chkSolvVariableAmrCycles$ against
$\chkSolvVariablePlain$ and $\chkSolvVariableAmrPlain$.

\emph{CMDGFM projection} (\code{solv_cmdgfm_cases}). One projection of a
divergent predictor with a phase jump, density ratio $1000$, $\dt=0.01$,
$\presstol=10^{-10}$ and the drop above, from a zero guess: on $64\times64$
cells ($\chkSolvCmdUniformUnknowns$ unknowns, of which
$\chkSolvCmdUniformJumps$ jumps) BiCGSTAB needs $\chkSolvCmdUniformCycles$
iterations with the block preconditioner~\cref{eq:solvers:block-preconditioner},
$\chkSolvCmdUniformIlu$ with the whole-system ILU and $\chkSolvCmdUniformPlain$
without preconditioner; on the composite mesh with base $32\times32$
($\chkSolvCmdAmrUnknowns$ unknowns, $\chkSolvCmdAmrJumps$ jumps) the counts
are $\chkSolvCmdAmrCycles$, $\chkSolvCmdAmrIlu$ and $\chkSolvCmdAmrPlain$
(\textsuperscript{\dag}: not converged). The block system has no single
volume norm; its reduction is measured with the residual defect that the
projection compares with $\presstol$ (\cref{sec:projection:defects}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solvers:cmdgfm-defect}
  e(\resvec)=\dt^2\max\curlypar{\max_{c\in\cellsret,\ \meas{\opencell{c}}>0}\frac{\abs{\resvec_c}}{\meas{\opencell{c}}},\ \max_{f\in\facesjump}\frac{\abs{\resvec_f}}{h_f}},
  \qquad
  \convfactor=\roundpar{\frac{e(\resvec_{\itcount})}{e(\rhsvec)}}^{1/\itcount},
\end{equation}
with $\rhsvec$ the right-hand side, the residual of the zero guess
($e(\rhsvec)=\chkSolvCmdUniformInitial$ on the uniform fixture), and
$\resvec_{\itcount}$ the final true residual. The block preconditioner reduces
$e$ by $\convfactor=\chkSolvCmdUniformFactor$ (uniform) and
$\chkSolvCmdAmrFactor$ (composite) per iteration, the whole-system ILU by
$\chkSolvCmdUniformIluFactor$ and $\chkSolvCmdAmrIluFactor$, and BiCGSTAB
without preconditioner by $\chkSolvCmdUniformPlainFactor$ and
$\chkSolvCmdAmrPlainFactor$.

\begin{deviation}{Whole-system ILU for the CMDGFM}\label{dev:solvers:ilu}
With \code{preconditioner=:ilu} the CMDGFM projection of the composite fixture
above stops at $\presstol=10^{-10}$ after $\chkSolvCmdAmrIlu$ iterations in
total with a volume defect of $\chkSolvCmdAmrIluDefect$
(\code{converged=false}): since budget remained, the eight BiCGSTAB passes of
the restart loop ended without meeting the defects. The same fixture converges
with this preconditioner at $10^{-9}$ and at $10^{-11}$, and with the default
block preconditioner at all four tolerances. The whole-system ILU also
allocates up to $\chkSolvCmdIluMiB$\,MiB per projection on these fixtures.
The default is unaffected; the cause of the stagnation is not determined; it
is a known issue at the audited commit (\issue{126}).
\end{deviation}

\emph{Allocations.} A repeated cell solve allocates nothing, with and without
the multigrid, on every fixture of \cref{tab:solvers:counts}, as does one
V-cycle. A repeated one-phase viscous solve allocates $\chkSolvStaggeredBytes$
bytes per call, independently of the mesh, when \apifun{solve!} iterates over
the two components of different types to zero their Dirichlet faces; the
contract test that asserts zero bytes fails at the audited commit
(\issue{127}). The two-phase
viscous solve allocates up to $\chkSolvVariableBytes$\,KiB per call with the
multigrid, for the refresh of its hierarchy and coarse factorisation, and
nothing without it; a repeated CMDGFM projection with the block
preconditioner allocates $\chkSolvCmdUniformBytes$ and
$\chkSolvCmdAmrBytes$\,KiB on the two fixtures.
